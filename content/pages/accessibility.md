Accessibility Statement
Marketing Powered is committed to making this website usable for the widest possible range of visitors, including people who use screen readers, keyboard navigation, magnification, and other assistive technology. We treat accessibility as an ongoing operating practice, not a one-time project. This accessibility statement explains the standards we work toward, how we review the site, and how to reach a person when something does not work.

Our Commitment to Accessibility
Accessibility sits with ordinary site maintenance, next to page speed, measurement, and security. The people who open marketingpowered.ai are not one audience. A hospital procurement lead may be comparing vendors on a desktop between committee meetings. A practice administrator may be reading on a phone in a hallway. A compliance officer may be checking whether digital access is treated as a real operating control. Another visitor may be using a screen reader, enlarging text, or moving through every control with a keyboard and no mouse. Those sessions should reach the same information.
The setting we design for is ordinary professional work: a clear page, a visible focus state, and controls that do not depend on a precise pointer. We build websites and landing pages as part of our [web development work](/services/web-development/) for hospitals, healthcare organizations, professional services, and other regulated markets. A firm that asks those organizations to care about usable digital experiences should hold its own site to the same expectation. This page is a description of that practice. It is not a legal opinion, and it is not a claim that every barrier has already been removed.
The Standards We Design Toward
We use the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) as the framework for design and development decisions on this site. WCAG is developed through the W3C process as a shared standard for web content accessibility. WCAG 2.2 organizes 13 guidelines under four principles: content should be perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust. Testable success criteria sit at three levels, A, AA, and AAA. Meeting those success criteria is what determines conformance ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/)).
WCAG 2.2 was published as a W3C Recommendation on 5 October 2023. W3C encourages use of the latest version. Content that meets WCAG 2.2 also meets WCAG 2.1 and WCAG 2.0, because later versions add success criteria rather than rewriting the earlier ones, with the noted removal of the obsolete 4.1.1 Parsing criterion ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/)).
We treat WCAG 2.2 Level AA as our design target. We do not claim that every page currently meets every applicable success criterion. Level AA is the benchmark we measure against and work toward, not a badge we attach to the site.
The Americans with Disabilities Act applies to state and local governments under Title II and to businesses open to the public under Title III. In guidance dated March 18, 2022, the Department of Justice said businesses and governments have flexibility in how they meet nondiscrimination and effective communication requirements, and it named WCAG as helpful technical guidance. That page states that it does not reflect Title II web requirements published in the Federal Register on April 24, 2024 ([ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)).
For state and local governments, the Department’s small-entity guide to that rule names WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the technical standard for web content and mobile apps. Compliance dates in the guide are April 26, 2027 for governments serving 50,000 or more persons, and April 26, 2028 for smaller governments and special district governments. The same guide says a public entity remains responsible when a contractor provides web content for it ([ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/small-entity-compliance-guide/)). Marketing Powered is a private business. This statement covers marketingpowered.ai. It does not transfer a public entity’s Title II duty to this company, and it does not assert that this site meets the Title II technical standard.
Support for Assistive Technology
We aim for this site to work with the assistive technology people already use: screen readers, keyboard-only navigation, browser zoom and text resizing, and operating-system contrast settings. In practical terms, a visitor who never touches a mouse should be able to move through navigation, read each page in a logical order, see where keyboard focus is, complete a form, and reach both the contact details on this page and the audit-request page without being trapped in a component.
The Department of Justice describes common barriers in plain terms: poor color contrast, information conveyed by color alone, missing text alternatives on images, videos without captions, forms without labels or useful error messages, and mouse-only navigation ([ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)). Those examples shape what we check. We use semantic HTML, descriptive page titles and headings, text alternatives for meaningful images, labeled fields, and link text that makes sense out of context.
We do not publish test results for named screen readers or versions. Compatibility depends on the browser, the assistive technology, and the page. If a combination fails here, the reporting path below is the fastest way to tell us.
How We Review and Test the Site
Review is part of publishing, not an annual cleanup. When a page is created or substantially changed, we check the elements that most often block use: heading order, keyboard path, focus visibility, color contrast, link purpose, form labels, error recovery, and alternative text. The same pass covers new components, not only long articles.
We pair automated checks with manual review. The Department of Justice notes that automated checkers can help, and that a clean automated report does not mean a site is accessible. It also notes that a report with a few errors does not, by itself, prove a barrier. Pairing automation with a manual check gives a clearer picture ([ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)). Automation surfaces machine-detectable patterns quickly. It cannot judge whether alt text is meaningful, whether reading order matches the task, or whether an error message tells someone how to recover. A person makes those calls.
The working cycle is simple. We review the changed pages, identify what blocks reading, navigation, or completing a form, fix those issues first, and re-check the affected pages before treating the item as closed. Findings are logged and ranked by how severely they block real use, not by how easy they are to close. A visitor report enters the same queue. A reported barrier is a confirmed one, so it is weighted ahead of theoretical issues of the same type.
Known Limitations and Ongoing Work
Some parts of this site may not yet meet every WCAG 2.2 Level AA success criterion. Older pages, older images, and files published before the current review practice can lag behind newer pages. We work through that backlog by the same rule used for new findings: fix first what blocks reading, navigation, or form completion.
We also expect gaps we have not yet found. A statement can list only the limits we know. If you hit a barrier that is not described here, that is a gap in our knowledge. Report it. We would rather correct the page than defend an incomplete list.
### How this statement stays current
Accessibility feedback is used to set fix order. This statement is reviewed as the site changes and as remediation moves, so it describes current practice rather than a frozen snapshot. We do not attach a conformance date to this page, because we are not publishing an audit certificate.
Third-Party Content and Integrations
This site includes components from outside vendors, including embedded forms, scheduling tools, call tracking, maps, and analytics. We select and configure those tools with accessibility in mind. Their internal behavior is still controlled by the vendor, and their accessibility can differ from the pages around them.
Where a third-party component blocks a task, we will provide another path to the same request. Email and phone, listed below, are the standing alternatives when a form, widget, or embedded map cannot be used. We do not control a vendor’s release cycle, and we will not describe a vendor component as meeting our design target unless we have checked the specific flow a visitor must complete.
How to Report a Barrier
If any part of this site is difficult or impossible to use, contact a person. Email [info@marketingpowered.ai](mailto:info@marketingpowered.ai) or call (561) 560-6804. Details you send about a barrier are handled under our [privacy policy](/legal/privacy-policy/).
A useful report is specific. Include the page URL, what you were trying to do, what happened instead, the browser and device, and any assistive technology involved, such as a screen reader or keyboard-only navigation. A short note is enough if that is all you can send. If a form on the site is the barrier, do not struggle with the form. Use email or phone, and we will take the request from there.
We aim to acknowledge accessibility reports and to tell you what we found and what we changed. If the barrier stops you from finishing something on the site, we will offer another way to finish it, including handling the request by email or phone. We do not publish a fixed response clock. Severity decides order: a blocked form or a trapped keyboard path comes before a cosmetic mismatch.

FAQ
Q: Does this site fully conform to WCAG?
A: We design and test against WCAG 2.2 Level AA as a target. We do not assert full conformance on every page. We state the standard, review against it, and fix gaps as they are found or reported.
Q: Who reads an accessibility report?
A: A member of the team, not an unattended inbox. Reports enter the same queue as internal findings and are ranked by whether they block reading, navigation, or completing a task.
Q: Is this statement a legal compliance certificate?
A: No. The Department of Justice’s business guidance points to WCAG as helpful technical guidance and leaves businesses flexibility in method. It does not turn an accessibility statement into a finding that a site meets the ADA ([ADA.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/)). This page explains our practice and gives you a direct contact path.
Q: Can you review another organization’s website?
A: Accessibility review is part of our web development and site-audit work, including for healthcare organizations and other regulated markets. The work focuses on where a site stands against WCAG and on a prioritized order of fixes. Scope depends on the site.
