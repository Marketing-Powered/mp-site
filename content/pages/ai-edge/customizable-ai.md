What Customizable AI Means Here
Customizable AI is not a promise of unlimited customization. It is the practice of configuring software and models we operate to do one defined job with data your organization has approved for that job. The configuration is written down, tested, and owned by a named person on each side.
Three layers make up every workflow. The model configuration covers the instructions, approved reference material, and output format. The approved business data covers which fields and records the workflow may read. The workflow itself covers the trigger, the steps, the approval point, and the action that follows approval.
This is the same approach described on the homepage under [technology shaped around your business](/#data-control). The useful question is whether the workflow improves control, fits your operation, and supports a decision your team needs to make. If it does not, we do not build it.
We define the use case, the available data, the responsibilities, and the limits before building. That order matters. A workflow designed around what the data can actually support is easier to test, easier to explain to a reviewer, and easier to switch off.
A Scoped Workflow, Step by Step
Every workflow we build follows the same shape: an approved input, a model configuration, an output, a human approval, and an action. The example below shows the pattern for inquiry follow-up. It describes how we scope this kind of work, not a report of a specific engagement.
### Illustrative example: inquiry follow-up drafting
* Approved input. A new inquiry record arrives in your CRM. The workflow reads only the fields approved for this use, such as service line of interest, location, preferred contact method, and the time of the inquiry. Clinical details, insurance identifiers, and free-text notes are excluded unless your review process approves them for this purpose.
* Model configuration. A reviewed instruction set, your approved service descriptions, and an output template define what a draft may contain. The configuration cannot introduce claims that are not in the approved reference material.
* Output. The workflow produces a draft reply and a short summary for the reviewer: what the inquiry asked, which approved content the draft used, and anything the model could not resolve.
* Human approval. A named person on your team reviews the draft inside the CRM. They can edit, approve, or reject it. Nothing sends without that step, and the record shows who approved it and when.
* Action. The approved message goes out through the system you already use for email or messaging. The workflow writes the outcome back to the CRM so reporting reflects what actually happened.
The approval step is not a formality we add for appearance. It is the point where responsibility stays with a person who knows your organization. Automation prepares the work; a person decides.
Where It Fits: Intake, Reporting, and Follow-Up
We scope customizable AI for three kinds of operational work. Each has a clear boundary.
### Intake support
The workflow can summarize inquiry details for the intake team, suggest routing based on approved rules such as location and service line, and flag inquiries that need a person immediately. It does not perform clinical screening, decide eligibility, or respond to a person in crisis without a human in the loop. Those decisions belong to your intake and clinical staff.
### Reporting support
The workflow can draft the narrative sections of a weekly operating check from numbers that come from your reporting system. The numbers are not generated by the model. It explains what the measured results show, and a reviewer confirms the explanation before it goes to leadership. This connects to our [analytics and attribution](/services/analytics-and-attribution) work, where conversion definitions are agreed before anything is reported.
### Follow-up support
The workflow can prepare drafts for approved follow-up sequences, propose timing based on rules you set, and keep records current. Sending stays inside your existing system, and a person approves changes to the sequence. [Workflow integrations](/services/web-development/integrations-and-tracking) connect the pieces so the task does not become one more disconnected tool.
Where a workflow touches calls, our [intake attribution](/services/call-tracking-and-intake-attribution) work defines what is recorded, what is transcribed, and what is excluded, with the notices your organization requires.
Integration Limits We State Up Front
Every integration has limits, and we would rather name them during scoping than discover them after launch.
* We integrate with the systems you already operate, using supported connections or scheduled exports. We do not replace your CRM, scheduling platform, or email system.
* Some systems only allow one-way data movement. When a result cannot be written back, the workflow records it elsewhere and we say so in the plan.
* Field mappings, rate limits, and permissions constrain what can be automated. A field that is not approved for use is designed out of the workflow, not worked around.
* Where a system has no supported connection, the step stays manual. The plan shows which steps are automated and which are not.
* External services remain part of delivery. Ad platforms, analytics, call tracking, CRM, and email delivery continue to process the data they need to do their jobs. Our [local processing](/ai-edge/local-processing) page describes what runs on infrastructure we control and what does not.
Acceptance Tests Before Anything Goes Live
A workflow is not live until it passes an acceptance test that both sides sign. The test is written during scoping, not after the build.
* A set of representative inputs drawn from real patterns, with identifying details removed or replaced, and the expected output for each.
* Edge cases: missing fields, ambiguous requests, inquiries that mention something outside the approved scope, and inputs the model should refuse to act on.
* A failure path. When the workflow cannot produce an acceptable output, it routes the item to a person with a note explaining why. It does not guess.
* Sign-off from your named owner and ours, recorded with the configuration version that was tested.
Testing before deployment is one of the approval points described on our [AI governance](/ai-edge/ai-governance) page. The others cover use-case approval, change approval, and the approval to send or publish.
Maintenance, Monitoring, and Change Control
A workflow that is not maintained drifts. Reference material goes stale, systems change their fields, and models get updated by their providers. We treat each of those as a change that needs review.
A named operator on our side monitors the workflow. Inputs, outputs, approvals, and errors are logged within the limits of the approved data list, so a reviewer can trace what happened without the logs becoming a second copy of sensitive records.
Changes are proposed in writing, tested against the acceptance set, and approved before they take effect. A model update counts as a change. If a change produces worse results, we roll back to the previous configuration and say what happened.
We propose a weekly operating check for active workflows and a monthly review with your leadership, with the cadence defined in your engagement. The review covers what the workflow handled, what it routed to people, and what should change.
Ownership Boundaries
Clients retain ownership of their data. Access to our services does not automatically transfer our technology, workflows, or configurations.
In practice, that means your records stay yours and you can export them. The instruction sets, integration code, and configurations we build remain ours unless an agreement says otherwise. When an engagement ends, you receive your data and the records of approvals and changes, and we remove your data from our environment according to the agreement.
We do not train models on your data without written approval. Where a workflow uses an outside model provider for a specific task, the data list and the provider are named in the plan. Our [data sovereignty](/ai-edge/data-sovereignty) page explains how storage, processing, access, and outside providers are made explicit, and our [owned infrastructure](/ai-edge/owned-ai-systems) page explains why we operate our own systems.
What We Do Not Promise
We do not promise unlimited customization. A workflow is scoped to a defined need, and expanding it is a new scope.
We do not promise that automation replaces judgment. People remain responsible for priorities, approvals, and the changes that affect your organization.
We do not promise that technology alone resolves your security or compliance obligations. The controls we describe support responsible execution; your policies and your reviewers decide what is acceptable.
We do not promise outcomes. A workflow can remove repetitive handoffs and shorten the time between an inquiry and a considered response. Whether that improves your results depends on your operation, and we measure it rather than assume it.
How an Engagement Starts
The first conversation is about the operational need, not the technology. We ask what repetitive work slows your team, which systems hold the data, who would approve outputs, and what a good result looks like.
From there we write the scope: the use case, the approved data, the responsibilities on each side, the integration limits, the acceptance test, and the review cadence. You see the plan before anything is built.
If the need does not justify a workflow, we say so. Sometimes the better answer is a cleaner handoff between two people, or a reporting change, rather than automation.
FAQ
Q: What does "customizable" mean if you do not offer unlimited customization?
A: It means we configure the model, the approved data, and the workflow around one defined operational need, and we document that configuration. You can request changes, and each change is tested and approved before it takes effect. What we do not do is promise that any request can be automated or that the scope can grow without limit.
Q: Which tasks can a workflow handle today?
A: We scope workflows for intake support, reporting support, and follow-up support: summarizing inquiries and suggesting routing, drafting narrative sections of reports from measured numbers, and preparing drafts for approved follow-up sequences. Each has a human approval step, and each excludes decisions that belong to your intake, clinical, or leadership staff.
Q: Does a person review every output?
A: Yes, for any output that is sent, published, or changes a record. The approval step is built into the workflow, and the record shows who approved the output and when. Outputs that fail the workflow's own checks are routed to a person with a note explaining why.
Q: Which systems can you integrate with?
A: We integrate with the systems you already operate through supported connections or scheduled exports, including CRM, email, call tracking, analytics, and ad platforms. Integration discovery during scoping identifies which connections exist, which direction data can move, and which steps stay manual.
Q: Who owns the workflow when the engagement ends?
A: You own your data and receive it along with the records of approvals and changes. The configurations, instruction sets, and integration code we build remain ours unless an agreement transfers them. We remove your data from our environment according to the agreement.
