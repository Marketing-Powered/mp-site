Guest contribution guidelines for Marketing Powered
These guest contribution guidelines define the editorial standard for Marketing Powered, not a link-exchange marketplace. We publish bylined articles from practitioners with direct operating experience in paid media, attribution, healthcare marketing, and regulated-industry compliance. If you have built, audited, or managed the systems you write about and can back up every claim, this page explains how to pitch.

What we publish
Marketing Powered publishes operational marketing articles for leaders at hospitals, health systems, behavioral health organizations, multi-location businesses, and professional services firms. Every accepted piece addresses a concrete decision affecting demand quality, acquisition economics, measurement accuracy, or compliance boundaries.
Submissions fit within four core subject areas:
* Paid media and conversion systems. Practical analysis of Google and Meta campaign structure, measuring paid search against conversion value per cost, landing page architecture, and multi variant optimization for high-intent traffic.
* Attribution and first-party measurement. Connecting ad spend to qualified opportunities, call tracking, CRM workflows, and downstream outcomes, including admissions in healthcare organizations where authorized data supports it.
* Healthcare and multi-location operations. Marketing governance, intake alignment, local visibility, and review acquisition for hospitals, clinical groups, behavioral health providers, and multi-site operators.
* Regulated-industry compliance. Advertising policies, privacy boundaries, and risk controls explained by legal, compliance, or intake professionals who work alongside marketing teams.
A simple filter applies before you pitch: could a marketing director at a hospital or multi-location business use your argument to change a budget, workflow, or audit checklist next week? Articles that pass that test fit our editorial calendar.
Out-of-scope submissions are declined without full review. We do not publish generic tool roundups, high-level listicles, AI hype essays without an operational thesis, unrelated industry topics, or drafts built primarily to carry an outbound link.
Who we want to hear from
Marketing Powered accepts pitches only from named authors with verifiable experience in the subject they propose. Strong contributors include hospital and health system marketing directors, behavioral health operators, compliance officers, healthcare attorneys, regulated-industry agency owners, and specialists in paid media, analytics, or conversion engineering.
This bar reflects how Marketing Powered was built. The founder is a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations, a background outlined on the [About Marketing Powered](/company/about/) page. The founder is also a court-certified expert witness in advertising strategy. Guest drafts are held to the same test used internally: every argument should hold up under direct questioning from an experienced operator.
We decline anonymous submissions, ghostwritten SEO placements, and pitches sent on behalf of unnamed clients. A byline on this site means a real practitioner shaped the argument and stands behind it.
Our editorial standard
Every guest article passes the same verification checks applied across our [media and resource library](/media/). Accepted drafts must meet four standards before entering production.
* Verifiable sources for every number. Benchmarks, industry figures, and policy claims require a primary source an editor can inspect, such as a government publication, a peer-reviewed study, or official platform documentation. First-party findings from your own operating work are welcome when you state the exact scope, unit, and timeframe.
* Accurate regulated-industry language. Healthcare drafts must use precise, non-stigmatizing terminology and avoid medical claims, implied diagnoses, or unverified compliance assertions. Writers covering behavioral health should understand Google's Health sensitive interest category: advertiser-curated audiences, including your data segments (remarketing), are not allowed when the ads or landing pages fall in that category, as stated in the [Google Ads Health in personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en). Marketing Powered is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware, and healthcare contributions must reflect real platform and privacy constraints.
* Editorial links only. Links inside the body exist solely to point readers to primary sources, official policies, or supporting technical references. Buying or selling links for ranking purposes is classified as link spam under [Google Search Central spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), and we do not publish link-placement copy.
* Confirmed author identity. Editors verify each contributor's name, current role, and public professional record prior to scheduling publication.
The review process
Every Marketing Powered guest contribution moves through four sequential stages: pitch, review, edit, and publish. No draft skips a stage, and no edited article goes live without written approval from the author.
* Pitch. You email a focused topic angle, a brief section outline, your credentials, and two or three links to prior work. Editors review structured pitches and respond to proposals that match current editorial priorities. Because submission volume varies, we cannot reply to every email and do not set a fixed response-time window.
* Review. An editor evaluates topic fit, operational depth, and originality for healthcare and regulated-industry readers. Proposals that repeat generic industry summaries or fall outside our four topic categories stop here.
* Edit. Accepted drafts receive a line edit for structure, plain language, and regulated-industry accuracy. Editors tighten sentences and remove promotional phrasing while preserving your central argument, then send the revised draft back for your sign-off.
* Publish. After you approve the final copy, we publish the piece with your byline, author bio, and contextual profile link, and notify you when the page is live.
What a byline includes
A published guest article includes your full name, a concise author biography describing your role and relevant credentials, and one contextual link in the bio pointing to your organization's website or professional profile. That link lets readers check your background and evaluate your perspective in context.
A guest byline here functions as authority positioning for practitioners who want defensible work attached to their name in front of healthcare leaders, multi-location operators, and regulated-industry executives. Acceptance is an editorial decision, not a commercial transaction. No payment moves in either direction, and we do not negotiate anchor text, link attributes, or link placement inside the article body.
Pitches that lead with link specifications rather than a substantive topic tell our editors immediately that the piece is built for link acquisition rather than readers.
What to avoid
Most declined submissions trigger one of four specific editorial rejections during screening: unreviewed AI filler, unsourced percentages, client results cited without permission, or press-release tone. Clearing these four issues before pitching saves time for both writer and editor.
* Unreviewed AI filler. Marketing Powered has been AI-native since 2022, so we evaluate the quality of the finished thinking rather than banning drafting tools. What gets rejected is unedited machine output packed with generic openings, hedged summaries, and confident errors no human practitioner checked.
* Unsourced percentages and performance figures. Invented traffic lifts, unsupported conversion rates, and claims attributed to unnamed studies are grounds for immediate rejection. If a statistic cannot be traced to a named primary source with a publisher and date, remove it.
* Client results without documented permission. Named employers, client outcomes, and customer quotes require documented written permission before submission. Never include confidential former-employer data, unauthorized account screenshots, or patient-identifying information.
* Press-release tone. Product launches, vendor feature announcements, and company self-promotion belong in a newsroom distribution channel, not in a bylined editorial article.
How to submit
Email your pitch to info@marketingpowered.ai with the subject line "Guest contribution pitch." Email is the sole intake channel for guest submissions, and keeping your pitch under 300 words allows an editor to assess fit quickly.
Include four elements in your email:
* Your proposed headline and central argument in two to three sentences.
* A brief outline listing the operational decisions, constraints, or trade-offs the piece will cover.
* A short credential line stating your current role, relevant operating background, and any primary sources you plan to cite.
* Links to two or three published articles, talks, or posts that show how you develop an argument.
Do not attach a completed manuscript on first contact unless an editor requests one. Structured pitches get read first because they show directly how the finished article will serve the reader.
