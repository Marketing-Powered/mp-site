Why paid media waste starts before launch
Picture a typical account launch. A media buyer opens a keyword planner, pulls thirty high-volume terms, rewrites competitor ads, and sets a daily budget.
Six weeks later, the dashboard shows clicks and a cost per lead, yet admissions or signed engagements barely move. Nobody separated buying intent from informational searches, priced what a new customer is worth, or checked platform targeting rules.
The account did not underperform. It was never aimed. Research is the aiming step, and it costs less than the spend it protects.
What paid media research covers before launch
Pre-launch research maps commercial demand, unit economics, competitive positioning, and first-party records before ad spend begins. The output is a documented economic model measured by conversion value per cost, not vanity lead counts.
* Demand mapping: Separating commercial queries from informational searches using local click costs and realistic volume.
* Unit economics: Setting allowable acquisition cost from contribution margin per admission, patient visit, or signed engagement.
* Search results review: Auditing which competitors own top positions, what offers they run, and where their landing pages fall short.
* First-party data audit: Reviewing CRM and intake records to identify which services and geographies actually convert.
How platform policy shapes your media plan
In regulated verticals, platform policy is a research input, not a footnote. Under Google's [personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465), health is a sensitive interest category, restricting advertisers promoting health services from using advertiser-curated audiences such as remarketing lists.
In the United States, advertising recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services requires LegitScript's Addiction Treatment Certification plus Google certification, per Google's [healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031).
A plan built on prohibited retargeting fails in review. Marketing Powered operates with awareness of LegitScript and HIPAA requirements and builds for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted, so plans start from what platforms will run.
Market research for multi-location and regulated organizations
Multi-location research is market-by-market map work. Each facility sits in its own demand radius with distinct search volume, local competitors, payer mix, client economics, and drive-time tolerance.
Scoring each site's capacity against local query volume shows where incremental budget buys growth and where locations bid against one another. Plans assign spend by market instead of spreading one blended budget nationally.
Marketing Powered's founder previously served as a healthcare CMO for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations and managed Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts. That background drives the method: allocate budgets from location-level evidence.
Message and landing page research
Ad platforms test headlines quickly, yet click metrics cannot explain why a buyer calls or leaves. Message research reviews authorized call transcripts and intake notes to catalog real objections from prospects comparing providers.
Landing page research applies the same discipline after the click. It identifies which claims require substantiation, what form length visitors tolerate, and which pages directly answer the intent behind each query.
Where volume supports testing, those insights guide multi variant optimization so visitors land on the best-matched page. The result is a message hierarchy built on buyer evidence.
Attribution research defines the finish line first
Decide what you will measure before spending, because retrofitting attribution leaves gaps across calls and intake stages. Attribution research maps the path from click to call or form, qualified contact, show, and admission or signed engagement.
Marketing Powered tracks attribution to admission, not just lead, tying ad platforms to CRM and call tracking so budgets optimize toward conversion value per cost.
