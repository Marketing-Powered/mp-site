Peptide Ad Suspensions: What Public Evidence Can Establish
Vendors selling remediation describe peptide account suspensions as close to universal. Operators who have never been touched describe them as a myth. Neither position is currently supported by a public, countable dataset, and that gap is what this peptide ad suspension research review sets out to map.

Define the research question before counting anything
Most arguments about peptide advertising collapse four different events into one word. They are not the same event, they do not have the same cause, and they do not have the same remedy.
* Ad or asset rejection. What changes: A single ad is blocked before or shortly after it serves. The account keeps running. Documented reversal route: Edit and resubmit, or request review.
* Restricted delivery or restricted measurement. What changes: The account runs, but a targeting option, audience or conversion event is unavailable. Documented reversal route: Platform-specific resolution guidance.
* Account suspension. What changes: Ads stop serving and the account becomes read only. Documented reversal route: Appeal, sometimes conditional on identity verification.
* Permanent exclusion. What changes: The advertiser is not permitted to advertise again, and related accounts are exposed. Documented reversal route: None documented for egregious findings.
These are distinct states in platform documentation, not rhetorical shades. Google describes a strike system for repeat violations in which the first and second strikes apply temporary account holds of three and seven days, and the third strike results in account suspension, with a warning sent at least seven days before suspension action for repeat violations ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Egregious violations sit in a separate track: Google states these cause immediate suspension without prior warning and that the advertiser will not be allowed to advertise again ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)).
A buyer who hears "suspension" and pictures permanent exclusion will overpay for remediation. A buyer who hears it and pictures a disapproved ad will underinvest in account structure, landing page claims and verification readiness. Any peptide advertising restrictions study that does not classify events at this level of resolution cannot produce a usable number, because its numerator is a mixture of four unlike things.
How these sources were gathered, dated and filtered
This review uses a narrow inclusion rule: platform policy and developer documentation published by the platform itself, and enforcement actions published by a federal agency. Each source was retrieved directly and dated at retrieval on November 23, 2026. Nothing in the conclusions rests on a secondary summary of a policy page.
Excluded from the evidence base: forum threads, screenshots of account notices, vendor blog posts, agency case studies without a named and retrievable source, and reseller claims about approval rates. Those materials can be useful as field signals and as leads for later verification, but they cannot function as primary peptide ad suspension evidence. A screenshot does not show the policy cited, the account history, the landing page content at the time of review, or whether an appeal was filed.
Two scope limits matter. First, platform policy documentation changes without public notice, so any peptide policy research with a stale retrieval date should be treated as a historical observation rather than a current rule. Second, an FDA warning letter documents one firm's conduct at one point in time, not a general standard applied to a category. The warning letter reviewed here states that the cited violations are "not intended to be an all-inclusive statement of violations" ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)), which is a direct instruction against treating one case as a category-wide ruling.
This is the same sourcing standard applied across our [marketing research for paid media](/research): a claim is either traceable to a dated primary source or it is labeled as a hypothesis.
What the primary sources actually document
The public record supports mechanism-level findings in four separable categories. Mechanism means the route by which enforcement happens, not how often it happens.
Product and claim basis. FDA reviewed one firm's website from January to March 2026 and found that, despite labeling marketing the products for research use only and not for human consumption, evidence obtained from the website established that the products were intended to be drugs for human use, making them unapproved new drugs ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)). The agency also treated the sale of bacteriostatic water alongside injectable peptide products as evidence of intended combined use ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)). The operative lesson for marketers is that surrounding site content, product adjacency and benefit language were read as intent. A disclaimer did not isolate the label from the marketing around it.
Identity and verification basis. Google states that certain selected advertisers must complete advertiser verification successfully in order to appeal an account suspension, and that if identity cannot be verified after three attempts they will not be allowed to appeal ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Verification readiness is therefore part of the appeal path itself, not a formality after the fact.
Measurement and health-condition basis. Meta's developer documentation states that beginning September 2, 2025 it began rolling out more proactive restrictions on custom conversions, and that a custom conversion suggesting specific health conditions or financial status will be flagged and prevented from being used to run ad campaigns, with the API field is_unavailable returning true for flagged conversions ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-conversion/)). That is a measurement restriction, not an account action, and conflating the two inflates perceived enforcement.
Circumvention and related-account basis. Google lists circumventing systems, promotion of unauthorized pharmacies and unacceptable business practices among the policies treated as egregious, and states that accounts related to a suspended account may be suspended and that new accounts an advertiser tries to create may also be suspended ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Evasion is handled as its own violation class, which is why rebuild-the-account offers carry risk that the original issue never did.

Why documented cases cannot produce a suspension rate
A rate needs two numbers. The numerator is confirmed, classified events. The denominator is the total population of active peptide advertisers during a defined window. The second number does not exist publicly. No platform publishes the count of active advertisers in this category, and no registry exists that would let an outside researcher construct one. Without it, every percentage circulating in sales decks is an assertion wearing a decimal point.
The collection problem compounds the counting problem. Consider an illustrative scenario: a researcher gathers forty suspension reports from industry forums over six months. Those forty are self-selected. An operator whose account was shut down has a reason to post, ask for help and name a vendor. An operator running without incident has no reason to post at all, and a competitive reason not to describe what is working. The sample is drawn from the complaint population, so it will overstate incidence no matter how carefully each individual report is checked. The arithmetic is clean and the inference is still wrong.
Causal attribution is a third problem. Google states that in determining whether an advertiser is violating policy it reviews information from a variety of sources, including ads, accounts, websites, content, user complaints, consumer reviews, regulatory warnings and rulings ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). An account can therefore be actioned on signals the advertiser never sees. Self-reported cause in a survey response is the advertiser's belief, not the platform's finding, and peptide suspension prevalence evidence built on belief is not measurement.
This is why diligence should focus on documented process rather than claimed outcomes. A systematic review of claim language, destination content and consent handling, of the kind set out in the [healthcare marketing compliance checklist](/resources/healthcare-marketing-compliance-checklist), produces evidence you control. A vendor's recovery percentage does not.
What a credible dataset would have to include
A valid peptide advertising restrictions study is buildable. It has not been built publicly, and the requirements are demanding enough to explain why.
* A defined sampling frame. A list of active advertisers in the category assembled before any outcome is known, recruited through trade associations, payment processors or certification registries rather than through complaint channels.
* Consented, anonymized incident reporting. Participants report events on a fixed schedule with no personal data, no account identifiers and no client names.
* Null reporting as a first-class record. Businesses that experienced nothing must be counted. A dataset that only records incidents can never produce a denominator.
* A fixed taxonomy. The four-event classification above, applied consistently, with the cited policy recorded verbatim from the notice rather than paraphrased.
* A stated window and a stated platform. Enforcement posture differs by channel and changes over time, so pooling three years and two platforms destroys the signal.
* Documented verification. Reported notices checked against the platform notice text where the participant can supply it, with unverifiable reports retained and labeled, not deleted.
* Pre-registration of the analysis. The questions and cut points set before the data arrives, so the finding is not selected after the fact.
Credentialing belongs in such a dataset as a variable, not as a conclusion. Certification processes address specific categories of eligibility risk, and the mechanics are covered in the [LegitScript certification guide](/resources/legitscript-certification). Holding a credential is not the same as product lawfulness, and neither is the same as channel eligibility or account standing. A serious study keeps those four variables in separate columns.
Conclusions this evidence supports, and the ones it does not
* Specific enforcement mechanisms exist and are documented. Status: Supported. Basis: Platform documentation and a federal enforcement action.
* Research-use labeling does not by itself settle intended use. Status: Supported for the cited case. Basis: FDA findings based on surrounding website content.
* Appeal and review routes exist. Status: Supported. Basis: Published appeal process and verification requirement.
* Appeal outcomes are discretionary. Status: Supported. Basis: Reinstatement described as limited to compelling circumstances.
* A given share of peptide accounts gets suspended. Status: Not supported. Basis: No public denominator.
* Suspensions are rising or falling in this category. Status: Not supported. Basis: No dated, representative time series.
* A vendor recovers a stated share of suspended accounts. Status: Not supported. Basis: No independently verifiable register.
On the discretion point, Google's own language is explicit that accounts are "only reinstated in compelling circumstances, such as in the case of a mistake," and that advertisers have at least six months from the suspension date to appeal ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Any offer that treats reinstatement as a product feature is selling against the published process.

Questions executives ask about this evidence
### Can public warnings measure an industry suspension rate?
No. A warning letter is a record of one firm's conduct, and the letter reviewed here states that its citations are not an all-inclusive statement of violations ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)). Counting published letters measures agency publication activity, not category prevalence, and says nothing about advertising accounts at all.
### Are blocked conversions counted as suspended accounts?
They should not be. Meta's documentation describes flagged custom conversions as unavailable for use in campaigns, with an API flag signaling the state ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-conversion/)). The account continues to operate. Treating a measurement restriction as an account action is the single most common inflation in informal regulated advertiser enforcement research.
### What would a representative dataset require?
A sampling frame built before outcomes are known, consented and anonymized reporting from participants with and without incidents, a fixed event taxonomy, a defined window and platform, verification of notice text where available, and pre-registered analysis. Absent those conditions, the output is a collection of anecdotes with a percentage attached.
### Can vendor testimonials establish recovery success?
Not on their own. A recovery claim needs the total number of appeals attempted, the classification of each original violation, the verification status of each advertiser, and the outcome of every case including failures. Testimonials supply the successes and omit the denominator, which is the same structural error described above.
### What can the existing evidence support?
It supports mechanism-level risk assessment. You can establish that claim language and site context can create regulatory exposure, that identity verification can gate an appeal, that health-condition measurement is restricted, that evasion is its own violation class, and that reinstatement is discretionary. You cannot responsibly derive a probability from any of it, and you should discount any proposal that does.
