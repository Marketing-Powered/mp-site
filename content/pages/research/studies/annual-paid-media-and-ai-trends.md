Annual Paid Media and AI Trends Report 2026
Paid media in hospitals, health systems, and other regulated markets still turns on captured intent and an outcome the operator can defend, not on a broader promise from an automated audience. Where prior behavior cannot be reused as a targeting list, the useful work is the first qualified click, a clean handoff into intake, and a record that reaches admission, a kept visit, or a signed engagement. This report treats 2026 as a planning year for those controls.

Paid media constraints that actually matter in sensitive verticals
The constraints that change a budget are not the feature list. They are the rules that decide which audiences an account may build, which claims a landing page may carry, and which certifications must be in place before a campaign is eligible to serve. A useful annual review starts there, in the account and on the destination, before anyone debates a new model.
Google classifies content related to health as a sensitive interest category. That category includes physical or mental health conditions, including diseases, sexual health, and chronic conditions; products, services, or procedures used to treat or manage those conditions; health issues tied to intimate body parts or functions; invasive procedures, including cosmetic surgery and injections; and disabilities, including content aimed at a primary caretaker. Google's examples include treatments for chronic conditions such as diabetes or arthritis, and counseling for mental health issues such as depression, anxiety, and addiction. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
For products and services in a sensitive interest category, advertisers cannot use advertiser-curated audiences. Google's stated reason is that those audiences may contain sensitive user signals. Predefined Google audiences can still be used, because Google configures them without those signals. The unsupported set includes Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments. The supported set includes in-market segments, affinity, demographics with stated exceptions, detailed demographics with exceptions, life events, location targeting, and custom segments under a further limit. [Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465)
That limit on custom segments is easy to miss in a planning deck. If the creative or the landing page is sensitive, custom segment audiences serve only in Display campaigns, and only to non-sensitive audiences or contextually. Other campaign types using custom segments with sensitive creative or sensitive landing pages are not eligible to serve. Users under 18 are not eligible for personalized advertising of any kind, including serving based on predefined Google audiences. Discovery and Demand Gen campaigns use advertiser-curated audiences by default and may be restricted when the products or services fall in a sensitive interest category. [Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465)
Marketing Powered is built for that environment. It is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware, and it is built for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted. The practical reading for an operator is narrower than a slogan: do not plan a healthcare acquisition program around website visitor lists, uploaded patient or customer files, lookalikes, or audience expansion when the ad or the page promotes a covered health service. Search intent, location, and other allowed predefined controls remain the working surface. Confirm the current policy text against the live ads and destinations before a launch, because the category follows the content, not the industry label on the contract.
Certification is a separate gate from audience rules. Google restricts promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. In the United States, an advertiser must be certified by LegitScript's addiction treatment certification program and must also be certified with Google. Not every drug and alcohol addiction service is eligible for LegitScript certification, and services that are not eligible are not allowed to advertise those services on Google. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) LegitScript certification and Google certification are sequential requirements in that policy text. One filing does not replace the other. Location rules differ. In February 2025 Google posted that its healthcare and medicines policy would be updated in March 2025 to allow recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services in Canada for advertisers certified by LegitScript, who could then apply for Google certification. [Update to healthcare and medicines policy (March 2025)](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15857948?hl=en-AU) [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15857948?hl=en-AU&ref_topic=16083443) Read the country row that matches the campaign before treating any cross-border plan as eligible.
Other healthcare lines carry their own limits. Google restricts prescription drug terms in ads, landing pages, and keywords, with location-specific rules. In the United States, online pharmacies and telemedicine providers that fall under the prescription drug services policy must meet LegitScript or National Association of Boards of Pharmacy accreditation paths stated on the policy page, and they must also be certified with Google. Non-government advertisers must be certified, including through G2RS where the policy requires it, before advertising health and medical insurance in the United States, with a further certificate for Affordable Care Act keyword promotion. Government advertisers have a different path on that same page. When a campaign targets allowed locations and the domain is properly certified, Google says the ad is labeled Eligible (limited) and can run in allowed locations. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)
A November 11, 2026 update clarified a different point: advertisers are not allowed to target users based on an interest in categories prohibited in Google's ad network. [Update to the Personalized Advertising Policy (November 2026)](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/18873108?hl=en) That clarification is not a new healthcare retargeting rule. It is a reason to review interest-based and custom-segment inputs against prohibited content, then keep the health review on the health page.
Professional services sit under a different set of limits when the offer is housing, employment, or consumer finance in the United States or Canada. Those access-to-opportunities categories restrict targeting by gender, age, parental status, marital status, and ZIP code. Radius, city, and country targeting remain available, with a minimum radius stated in the policy. [Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) On Meta, Special Ad Categories cover housing, employment, and financial products and services, with limited audience selection tools. Meta replaced the Credit category with Financial products and services in October 2024. From January 21, 2025, that category is required for financial products and services campaigns from advertisers based in the United States or showing ads to audiences in the United States, and ads may be rejected if an appropriate category is not chosen. Meta's examples include insurance, bank accounts, investment services, and payment services. [Special Ad Categories have expanded and now include financial products and services](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/510724041294968)
The commercial implication is simple. A media plan copied from an unrestricted category will overstate what the account can do. Budget should sit on queries and locations the organization can serve, on pages whose claims match the certified scope, and on measurement that does not depend on a remarketing pool the policy will not allow.
That review is a working meeting, not a policy PDF left in a shared drive. A marketing director on a call with regional operators should be able to point at one screen and say which service lines are in a sensitive category, which domains are certified, and which locations have open capacity. If the call cannot answer those three questions, the account is not ready for a larger budget.
First-party data and attribution requirements when retargeting is unavailable
When advertiser-curated audiences are off the table, first-party data does not mean a quieter version of the same list. It means the organization records what a person did in response to an ad, on systems it controls, for a purpose it can explain. The ad platform still reports clicks, cost, and the conversion actions it was given. The business record is the admission, the kept appointment, the scheduled procedure, or the signed engagement.
The founder of Marketing Powered is a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations. The founder managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts. That history is personal, from roles before this firm. It is the reason the measurement standard here is attribution tracked to admission, not just lead. A lead is an opening. An admission, or the equivalent revenue event in a hospital or a professional firm, is the point at which spend can be discussed with operations.
Build the record in the order the work actually happens.
The query or the placement is the first-touch intent signal. Store the campaign, ad group, keyword or asset group, location, and time. Do not enrich that click with a diagnosis inferred from the page they viewed, and do not turn that page view into a customer list for later ads. The policy problem and the data-handling problem arrive together.
The landing page is the second record. It should match the query, name the service without unsupported clinical claims, and offer a path a person can complete: a call, a form, or a scheduled next step. In a sensitive category, that page is also a policy surface. Google tells advertisers flagged under personalized advertising to review the site or app as well as the ad, and one fix path is to edit or remove content that falls in the policy. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) A tracking template that assumes every visitor can be followed later will fight the page design. Design the page for the visit in front of you.
The intake event is the third record. Call tracking, form routing, and CRM status are useful only if someone owns the definition of qualified. A hospital scheduling team and a professional-services intake team will not use the same words. Write the definition down: who counts, what status in the system of record proves it, and which statuses are excluded because they were duplicates, out of area, or outside the service the ad offered. Platform conversion columns should be mapped to those definitions, not the other way around.
The outcome event is the fourth record. For a treatment provider, that event is admission. For a hospital service line, it may be a kept visit or a completed scheduling event that operations already trusts. For a regulated professional firm, it may be a consultation that met the qualification rules, or an engagement the finance system can see. Marketing Powered's measurement focus is the outcome, not the form fill. Joining those events requires authorization to use the underlying data. It does not require sending a patient list back to an ad platform.
HIPAA and ad policy are not the same rule set. The HIPAA Privacy Rule requires an individual's authorization before a covered entity uses or discloses protected health information for marketing, with two stated exceptions: a face-to-face communication between the covered entity and the individual, and a promotional gift of nominal value. If a third party provides remuneration for the marketing, the authorization must say so. [HHS: When is patient authorization required for marketing?](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/when-is-patient-authorization-required-for-marketing/index.html) HHS defines marketing, in this context, as a communication about a product or service that encourages recipients to purchase or use it, and it points covered entities to 45 CFR 164.508 for what an authorization must contain. [HHS: Marketing](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html) Nothing in an ad account setup replaces that analysis. Counsel and the privacy officer decide whether a given export is permitted. The marketing operator's job is to avoid building a workflow that assumes the export is fine.
A workable attribution review asks five questions and stops when any answer is missing.
* What is the business event, and which system is the source of truth for it?
* Which identifier joins the click to that event without placing protected health information into an ad platform tag?
* Who is allowed to see the joined file, and for how long is it retained?
* What does the ad platform receive back, if anything, and is that payload limited to a conversion flag the policy and the privacy review both allow?
* How will finance and operations read the same number the media buyer reads?
If the fifth answer is "they will not," the dashboard is an internal artifact. It is not a management report. Offline conversion imports, server-side collection, and CRM status updates can support the join. They do not, by themselves, make the join appropriate. Scope the fields before the integration is built.
This is also where first-party data earns its name. The asset is the consented or otherwise permitted operational record: capacity by location, which services are open, which calls were answered, which inquiries met the definition, and which became the outcome. It is not a borrowed audience from a platform, and it is not a file of former patients uploaded so a model can find people who look similar. Customer Match is in the unsupported column for sensitive interest promotion. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Treat that as a design constraint, not as a temporary disapproval to route around.
Hospitals and professional firms should apply the same discipline even when the word admission is not theirs. A service line that buys clicks for a chronic-condition program and reports only cost per form will not see whether scheduling could absorb the demand. A firm that buys clicks for a regulated offer and reports only cost per lead will not see whether the consultations were in the practice area the partners will actually take. The annual planning question is which event will be the management metric next year, who owns the definition, and which fields are forbidden in the ad platform regardless of how useful they would be to a model.
What AI-native execution looks like under data-sovereignty rules
AI-native, in this firm, dates to 2022. It describes how the work is executed, not a claim that a model replaces policy, clinical review, or the person who owns the budget. Under data-sovereignty rules, the useful question is which tasks may run on infrastructure the operator controls, which tasks may run inside an ad platform, and which tasks should not be automated at all.
Owned, local AI infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference is part of that setup. It is not a certification. It does not mean every campaign runs on a local model, and it does not mean every data set stays offline. Client data ownership is separate from the hardware an agency operates. A local inference path can keep drafts, classifications, and internal notes inside an environment the operator governs. It does not, by itself, decide whether a use of protected health information is permitted. That decision stays with the covered entity, its privacy officer, and counsel.
The execution layer is narrow on purpose.
Query and search-term review can be assisted. A model can group queries by service line, flag terms that imply a condition the account is not certified to advertise, and surface mismatches between the query and the page. A person still accepts or rejects the negative and the new keyword. In addiction services, a term that pulls the account outside recovery-oriented drug and alcohol treatment, or into a service LegitScript does not treat as eligible, is not a growth opportunity. It is a policy exposure.
Claim review can be assisted and must stay governed. Generative drafts are suggestions. They are not approved copy. Healthcare content should not imply a diagnosis, and it should not imply that a treatment works. Restricted drug terms in ads, keywords, and landing pages are a separate Google restriction, with location-specific exceptions. Content directed at healthcare professionals in their professional capacity is excluded from the restricted-drug-terms sensitive interest category for advertisers in the United States, Canada, and New Zealand. Advertisers planning to use personalized targeting tools to promote restricted drug term products to those professionals after July 1, 2025 must apply for Google's Restricted Drug Term Personalization Certification. [Restricted drug terms in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701653?hl=en) A consumer service-line campaign does not inherit that exclusion. Mixing a professional audience and a patient audience in one asset group is how a narrow exception gets applied to the wrong page.
Intake summarization is the task most likely to cross a line. Call transcripts and form notes can contain identifiers and clinical detail. If that material is summarized, the path should be the owned inference environment, with access limited to people who already handle the record, and with retention written down. Sending the same transcript to a general consumer chat tool because it is faster is not an execution improvement. It is a change in who holds the data.
Budget and bid suggestions are the task most likely to be overtrusted. A platform bid strategy can be appropriate on a search campaign whose conversion action matches the agreed definition. It is not appropriate as a reason to ignore capacity. More qualified demand into a scheduling team that cannot answer is spend without a path to the outcome. An internal model can rank markets by open capacity, answer rate, and outcome rate. It should not invent a performance lift, and it should not be fed a file the privacy review has not cleared.
The founder is a court-certified expert witness in advertising strategy. That credential speaks to how advertising decisions are examined after the fact: what the account was built to do, what the evidence shows, and whether the strategy matches the constraint. It is not a legal opinion, and it is not a substitute for the advertiser's own counsel. Use it as a standard for documentation. If a choice would be hard to explain from the change history, the policy excerpt, and the outcome definition, it is not ready to ship.
Data sovereignty in daily work looks like a short register, not a slogan.
* Search-term grouping and policy flags. This can run on owned inference, or on a reviewed export with no clinical notes. A person accepts or rejects each change.
* Draft headlines inside an approved claim set. This can run on owned inference, then in the ad account. A person gives clinical, legal, and brand approval.
* Bid strategy on an allowed search campaign. This can run on the ad platform, using a cleared conversion action. A person checks capacity before budget increases.
* Call or form summarization that may include identifiers. This can run only on owned, access-controlled inference, and only if the privacy review allows it. Nothing is pasted into general external tools.
* Audience building from condition-page visitors. This is not a candidate for sensitive health promotion. A person removes the list and does not recreate it under another name.
The register will be shorter than a vendor's feature page. That is the point. An annual trend review that adds tools without adding a row to this register has not made execution more native. It has added a place where data can leave.
Multi-location scaling considerations beyond single-site tactics
A single location can hide structural problems. One phone number, one license, one scheduling queue, and one landing page can be watched by the same two people. A network cannot. The founder ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations. The lesson from that operating history is that scale is a governance problem before it is a media problem. Each added location multiplies certification scope, capacity, and the chance that a national ad points at a local service the site cannot deliver this week.
Read that flow left to right, without labels in the graphic, because the labels are the part operators argue about. A person clicks an ad. The click carries a location and a service line. The page offers a next step that belongs to one site, not to a national average. Intake records whether the inquiry was in area, in service, and answerable. The system of record later shows whether that inquiry became an admission or the equivalent kept event. If any step is national when the constraint is local, the report will flatter the wrong market.
Four differences show up as soon as a second site is live.
Licensure and certification scope. An addiction-services certification that covers one legal entity and one domain does not automatically cover a new location, a new domain, or a sister brand. Google's United States rule is specific: advertise recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services only with LegitScript certification and Google certification, and do not advertise services that are not eligible. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Before a location is added to a campaign, confirm that the entity, the domain, and the services on the page are inside the certification that Google and LegitScript currently recognize. Do not treat a shared template as evidence.
Capacity. A market with open beds, open chairs, or open appointment slots can take more qualified demand. A market that is full cannot. Moving budget toward the full market because its cost per click looks efficient pushes people into a queue the site cannot honor. The weekly meeting between marketing and operations should include a capacity note by site. If operations will not share that note, media should not pretend the network is one funnel.
Local proof on the page. Address, phone, hours, and the services actually offered at that site have to match the ad's location target. A national page with a locator widget can be the right path for some queries. It is the wrong path when the query names a city and the widget drops the caller into a different state's intake. Track the site that received the inquiry, not only the campaign that paid for the click.
Reporting grain. A network score hides a site that spends without admissions and a site that cannot spend because its page is disapproved. The management pack should show spend, qualified inquiries, and outcomes by market and by site, using one definition. Creative and bids can still be shared where the offer is the same. The outcome table should not be shared into a single blended row if leaders use that row to move money.
Hospitals with employed physician groups and community locations face the same pattern under a different vocabulary. A service-line campaign for a chronic condition is health content under Google's examples, so the audience limits apply even when the brand is a health system rather than a treatment provider. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) The scaling choice is which campuses have the service, which scheduling pools can take the visit, and which landing pages name that campus without borrowing claims from a flagship the patient will never see.
Professional services firms with multiple offices have a lighter clinical burden and a similar control problem. If the offer touches consumer finance, housing, or employment, the United States and Canada personalization limits on age, gender, parental status, marital status, and ZIP code still apply. [Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) Office-level landing pages, local numbers, and a definition of a qualified consultation keep the firm from buying city-level demand it cannot staff. A financial firm advertising to US audiences also needs the correct Meta Special Ad Category where the offer is a financial product or service. [Special Ad Categories have expanded and now include financial products and services](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/510724041294968)
Single-site tactics that do not survive this test include one conversion action for every location, one phone number forwarded blindly, one budget paced to a national daily cap, and one creative set that names a service a given site does not offer. The replacement is not a separate account for every street address. It is a structure in which location is a field on the click, the page, the call, and the outcome, and in which budget moves only when that field is populated.
How owned infrastructure changes deployment and governance decisions
Owned infrastructure changes the questions a buyer asks before a tool is turned on. A rented seat answers "who has a login." An owned inference environment answers where the job runs, who can reach the prompts and the outputs, how a model is updated, and how the work leaves when the contract ends. Those are deployment decisions. They are also governance decisions, because each one decides whether sensitive operational detail stays with the operator.
Marketing Powered operates owned, local AI infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference. The business value is control: a configurable boundary around tasks that should not sit in a general external tool, and a place to run process work without treating a vendor chat window as the system of record. It is not a promise that costs fall by a stated amount, and it is not a statement that client data becomes the agency's data because the hardware is owned. Hardware ownership and data ownership are different facts. Say both plainly in any vendor review.
Deployment choices that actually change risk:
Where the prompt is sent. A classification of public ad copy can tolerate a different path than a summary of an intake note. Write the split down. If a workflow cannot name the endpoint, it is not ready for material that might identify a patient or a client.
Who can invoke the job. Access should follow the same roles that already see the underlying record. A shared team login makes the later audit useless. The founder' s court-certified work in advertising strategy is a reminder that change history is part of the evidence. If the history shows only a vendor account, the organization cannot explain who approved the output.
What is retained. Prompts, outputs, and logs are records. Retention should match the privacy notice and the contractual schedule, not the default of the tool that was easiest to connect. Local infrastructure makes a shorter retention choice possible. It does not make an undocumented choice acceptable.
How models are updated. An open or locally hosted model can be pinned to a version. A hosted product can change behavior between Mondays. For claim-sensitive drafts, pin the version used in production and re-review outputs after a material change. Do not assume yesterday's approved pattern still holds because the prompt text did not change.
What the exit looks like. Export formats, deletion evidence, and a named owner for the cutover belong in the evaluation, not in a renewal argument. A tool that cannot return the definitions, the negative lists, the claim log, and the outcome map is a poor system of record even if the interface is fast.
Governance is the meeting in which those choices are accepted or refused. A practical cadence is monthly for access and retention exceptions, and at each campaign launch for any new field moving toward an ad platform or an external model. The launch record should name the data class, the endpoint, the human approver, and the policy or privacy source the approver relied on. Marketing Powered does not treat a workflow as certified because it uses local inference. HIPAA-aware means the design expects a privacy review. It does not mean the review has been completed for a given client file.
Hospitals should be especially plain about business associates and covered-entity duties. If a vendor will handle protected health information, the agreement and the permitted purpose come before the integration. Professional services firms with confidentiality duties, including legal and financial practices, should apply the same gate to client files even when HIPAA is not the statute in play. The infrastructure question is identical: does this job need to leave the environment the firm controls?
Owned infrastructure does not replace the ad platform. Bidding, ad serving, and policy review still happen in the platforms that sell the inventory. What ownership changes is the layer around that work: the drafts, the classifications, the internal scoring, and the notes that would be reckless to paste into a general tool. Teams that skip that distinction either over-claim what local hardware does or under-use it because a seat license was faster to buy.
Practical limits of current platform AI features for healthcare advertisers
Platform AI is a set of features with different permissions, not a single switch called automation. The useful 2026 review is a standing-table comparison: what the platform recommended, what the policy allows, and what the printed outcome report says happened after intake. If those three pages disagree, the recommendation does not win by default.
Audience features are the first limit. Audience expansion and lookalike segments are advertiser-curated. They are not available for promotion in a sensitive interest category, including health. Your data segments, the current name for what many buyers still call remarketing lists, sit in the same unsupported column, as does Customer Match. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) A feature that "finds more people like your converters" is the wrong feature when the converters came from a condition or treatment page. Predefined audiences remain available because Google excludes sensitive signals from them. That is a narrower tool than most unrestricted accounts use. Plan for the narrower tool.
Campaign types that assume advertiser-curated audiences are the second limit. Discovery and Demand Gen use those audiences by default and may be restricted for sensitive interest promotion. [Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) A healthcare account that copies a demand-generation structure from a consumer brand should expect a serving limit, not a learning phase. Other automated campaign types need the same test: open the audience settings, name every list and expansion control, and remove anything in the advertiser-curated column before judging performance. A restricted campaign is not a failed test of creative. It is a failed reading of the policy.
Creative automation is the third limit. Asset combinations can write sentences no clinician and no counsel reviewed. In a sensitive category, the landing page is in scope along with the ad. A generated line that implies a diagnosis, promises a result, or names a prescription drug can create both a policy issue and a claims issue. Google restricts prescription drug terms in ads, landing pages, and keywords, with location-specific exceptions, and it does not allow ads promoting prescription opioid painkillers, aside from limited exceptions that include certified addiction treatment providers in the United States. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Turn off combination settings that can assemble unreviewed claims, or confine automation to a claim set a human has already approved line by line.
Bidding automation is the limit buyers most often confuse with strategy. A bid strategy can allocate within a campaign whose traffic is allowed and whose conversion action is clean. It cannot supply the missing audience, repair a page that is out of certification scope, or decide that a form fill equals an admission. If the conversion action is a click on a phone number, the strategy will buy phone clicks. If the action is an offline event limited to qualified admissions, and the privacy review allows that flag to return, the strategy has a chance to follow the business. The printed report on the standing table should show both numbers. Leaders who fund only the platform number will scale the wrong event.
Healthcare professional campaigns are not a back door for patient campaigns. The restricted-drug-terms exclusion for professional audiences is limited to content directed at healthcare professionals in their professional capacity, in the United States, Canada, and New Zealand, and personalized targeting of those products after July 1, 2025 requires a separate certification. [Restricted drug terms in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701653?hl=en) Keep professional pages, professional creative, and professional conversion actions in their own campaigns. Do not point a patient service-line ad at a professional exception because the click was cheaper.
There is a further limit that no platform feature fixes. Google's healthcare and medicines policy expects ads and destinations to follow applicable laws and industry standards, and some healthcare content cannot be advertised at all in a given location. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) An optimization score does not override that. Neither does a model that has seen a large number of unrestricted accounts. The operator's advantage is the refusal: do not adopt a feature whose documented mechanism requires an audience or a claim the account is not allowed to use.
For professional services, run the same comparison with the category that actually applies. A firm promoting loans, insurance, or other financial products to US audiences is in Meta's financial products and services category, with limited audience tools, not in an open prospecting setup. [Special Ad Categories have expanded and now include financial products and services](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/510724041294968) A firm promoting housing, employment, or consumer finance on Google in the United States or Canada cannot lean on age, gender, parental status, marital status, or ZIP code targeting. [Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) Platform automation that quietly reintroduces those controls is a defect, not a lift.
The standing-table habit is the annual practice worth keeping. Print or export the recommendation, the policy excerpt, and the outcome report. Mark each recommendation as allowed and measured, allowed and unmeasured, or not allowed. Fund only the first group. Revisit the second group after the definition and the join exist. Delete the third group from the roadmap so it does not return under a new product name next quarter.
Operator checklist for evaluating new tools or partners
Use this checklist when a platform release, an agency pitch, or an internal request arrives with the word automation attached. A tool passes only if the operator can answer every item from documents, not from a demo.
* Name the category. Is the ad or the page promoting health, a restricted drug term, addiction services, insurance, housing, employment, consumer finance, or another restricted offer? If yes, open the current policy page for that category before the demo continues.
* Name the audience mechanism. Does the tool require Customer Match, your data segments, lookalikes, audience expansion, or another advertiser-curated audience to produce the result it showed? If the offer is in a sensitive interest category, that mechanism is not available. [Restricted targeting in Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465)
* Name the campaign type. If the plan depends on Discovery or Demand Gen for a sensitive health offer, expect the default audience restriction. Do not accept "it will learn" as a serving plan.
* Name the certification scope. For US recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, confirm LegitScript certification and Google certification, and confirm the service is eligible. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Repeat the check for every location and domain, not only the flagship.
* Name the outcome. What event will leadership manage, who owns the definition, and which system is the source of truth? If the answer is a platform lead, the tool has not been tied to admission or its equivalent.

### Can a hospital or clinic remarket to people who visited a treatment page?
Not with advertiser-curated audiences if the ads promote a sensitive health interest. Google does not allow Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, or lookalike segments for that promotion. Predefined Google audiences can be used because sensitive signals are excluded from them. Custom segments with sensitive creative or landing pages are limited to Display, and only to non-sensitive audiences or contextually. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
### Does LegitScript certification allow addiction treatment ads on Google by itself?
In the United States, Google also requires its own certification. Services that are not eligible for LegitScript certification are not allowed to advertise drug and alcohol addiction services on Google. Confirm the country row, the entity, and the domain before a campaign is built. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)
### Is owned local AI infrastructure the same as a HIPAA determination?
No. Owned, local infrastructure can support HIPAA-aware inference by keeping selected jobs inside an environment the operator governs. It does not certify a workflow, it does not move ownership of client data, and it does not replace an authorization analysis under the Privacy Rule. [HHS: Marketing](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html)
### Should next year's plan be judged on cost per lead?
Use cost per lead only as an operating diagnostic. The management event should be admission, a kept visit, or the equivalent signed outcome, joined through a path privacy review allows. A lower cost per lead that never reaches that event is not evidence the plan improved.
### What should a 2026 planning review decide in one sitting?
Decide which offers are in restricted categories, which audiences are therefore unavailable, which locations are inside certification and capacity, which outcome definition finance will accept, and which AI jobs stay on owned infrastructure. Feature adoption is a later choice. It is not the decision.
