Google Ads Compliance for Mental and Behavioral Health
Most behavioral health accounts do not lose Google Ads because the bids were wrong. They lose the ability to serve because the offer, the audience settings, or the destination crossed a rule the team treated as a copy note. This guide separates the certification bar for drug and alcohol recovery from the personalization bar that covers mental health advertising in general, and it shows how to build search campaigns that can keep running.

Two policies set the floor
Picture a Monday operations meeting. The media buyer has a disapproval notice open. The clinical director is asking whether an anxiety intensive outpatient page "counts as addiction." Finance wants to know why remarketing was switched off after it had been funding a large share of branded return visits. Those three questions are not one problem. They sit on two different Google Ads policies, and mixing them up is how teams either over-restrict a counseling practice or under-restrict a treatment program.
Google's Healthcare and medicines policy is about what may be advertised at all, where, and by whom. Some healthcare content cannot be advertised. Other content can run only in certain locations, and only after the advertiser has applied and been approved. If a campaign targets allowed locations and the domain is properly certified, Google says the ad is labeled Eligible (limited) and can run in those allowed locations. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Display & Video 360 buyers are expected to follow the same healthcare policy. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)
The second policy is personalized advertising. Health is a sensitive interest category. Google's examples include physical or mental health conditions, products and procedures used to treat or manage chronic conditions, and, by name, counseling services for mental health issues like depression, anxiety, and addiction, including gambling addiction and alcohol addiction. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) That example is the line most outpatient practices miss. You do not need an addiction certification to fall under the health personalization rules. Counseling for depression is already inside the category.
For ads promoting in that category, advertiser-curated audiences cannot be used, because they may contain sensitive user signals. Google's non-exhaustive list of those audiences includes Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments. Predefined Google audiences, such as in-market, affinity, demographics with stated exceptions, life events, and location targeting, can still be used, because Google says sensitive user signals are excluded from them. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Users under 18 are not eligible for personalized advertising of any kind. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
There is a narrow exclusion, not a workaround. In a May 2025 clarification, Google said the health sensitive interest category excludes content directed at healthcare professionals acting in their professional capacity. The same update said this did not change the existing prohibition on targeting users based on sensitive health conditions. [[3]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16258024?hl=en) A campaign recruiting clinicians is not the same campaign as one offering care to patients. Do not reuse the professional-audience exclusion to retarget people who read a condition page.
Custom segments need their own reading. If a custom segment uses sensitive creative assets or points at a sensitive landing page, Google says it will only serve with Display campaigns to non-sensitive audiences or contextually. All other campaigns using that combination are not eligible to serve. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) A Display plan built on "people who visited the depression page" is not a clever segment. It is a segment the policy is written to stop.
The commercial point is simple. A media plan copied from a dental group, a law firm, or a home-services account assumes remarketing, customer lists, and lookalikes. In this vertical those tools are the first thing to remove, not the first thing to test. Marketing Powered is built for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted. The rest of this guide is the operating detail behind that constraint.
Classify the offer before you spend
Compliance starts with a service map, not a keyword list. One website often holds several offers. Each offer can sit in a different policy box. A single domain that mixes them will be reviewed as the whole destination, not as the one ad group you hoped would pass.
Use this classification before anyone builds a campaign. It is a working map of published rules, not a legal opinion, and local licensure still has to be checked separately.
* Outpatient counseling or therapy for anxiety, depression, or similar conditions; Healthcare and medicines posture: Not listed as its own certification category on the healthcare policy; Personalization posture: Named health content. No advertiser-curated audiences. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
* In-person psychiatry that does not promote online prescribing or drug sale; Healthcare and medicines posture: Same as counseling, unless prescription-drug or telemedicine rules are triggered; Personalization posture: Health content if the ads concern a mental health condition or its treatment
* Telemedicine that involves online prescribing, dispensing, or sale of prescription drugs; Healthcare and medicines posture: Restricted. In the United States, online pharmacies and telemedicine providers need LegitScript Healthcare Merchant Certification or the stated NABP accreditation, and Google certification. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031); Personalization posture: Health content, plus restricted-drug-term rules if drug names are used
* Recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services; Healthcare and medicines posture: Restricted. Allowed only in listed locations, and only if the advertiser is approved. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649); Personalization posture: Health content. Counseling for alcohol addiction is an explicit example. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
* Sober living, referral or lead-gen sites, assessment-only services; Healthcare and medicines posture: Inside Google's examples of recovery-oriented services, which include sober living, mutual help, lead generators, referral agencies, and crisis hotlines. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) LegitScript generally does not treat sober living or referral services as eligible. [[5]](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/); Personalization posture: Still health-related if the creative is about addiction or mental health
* Impulse-control disorders, behavioral addiction, nicotine addiction; Healthcare and medicines posture: Out of scope of the addiction services policy. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649); Personalization posture: Gambling-addiction counseling is still named in the health examples. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Do not treat "out of scope" as permission to build condition audiences.
* Health insurance promotion in the United States; Healthcare and medicines posture: Separate certification path, including G2RS for non-government advertisers. Dental, vision, and travel health insurance are not under that restriction. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031); Personalization posture: Depends on the creative. Insurance is not a free pass around health personalization if the ad is about a condition.
The failure mode in multi-service organizations is the quiet second offer. A licensed treatment program puts a sister sober living house, a cash-pay referral page, or an unlicensed assessment funnel on the same domain. Google's addiction services examples already include recovery support such as sober living and lead generators or referral agencies. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) LegitScript's eligibility FAQ then narrows who can actually be certified: clinical substance use disorder treatment in a licensed setting or through compliant telehealth, including professionally licensed in-home clinical care; credentialed interventionists providing in-person services; mutual support groups, with family-or-caregiver groups excluded; and crisis hotlines that may inform and support but may not promote or direct people to specific treatment providers. Sober living environments are generally not eligible. Referral services and assessment-only services are listed among organizations that are generally not eligible. Virtual intervention services are not eligible. [[5]](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/)
Read those two pages together. Being named in Google's restricted category does not mean LegitScript will certify you. And if you are not eligible for the certification Google requires in the United States, you are not allowed to advertise those drug and alcohol addiction services on Google. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)
Hospitals and health systems hit the same split. A behavioral health unit advertising outpatient therapy is not in the addiction-services certification lane unless the ads and pages promote recovery-oriented drug and alcohol services. A telepsychiatry offer that promotes online prescribing is in the prescription drug services lane. A service-line campaign aimed at patients is still health content for personalization purposes. Classify the service line before the media team inherits a suspended asset group.
Drug and alcohol recovery rules
Google restricts the promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Advertisers who have applied and been approved may advertise those services in Australia, Canada, France, Ireland, New Zealand, and the United States. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) The location tables are stricter than that list suggests. In Australia, France, Ireland, and New Zealand, Google allows this promotion by government entities only, and those advertisers must also be certified with Google. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Private providers should not plan a paid-search launch in those countries on the strength of the country name alone.
In the United States, the rule is direct: to advertise recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, you must be certified as an addiction services provider by the LegitScript Certification program, and you must also be certified with Google. Google states that not all drug and alcohol addiction services are eligible for LegitScript certification, that those not eligible are not allowed to advertise those services on Google, and that LegitScript charges a fee for processing and monitoring, with fee waivers possible in some circumstances. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Canada uses the same two-step structure: LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification, then Google certification. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Canada was opened to this private-provider path in a March 2025 policy update. [[6]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15857948?hl=en-AU)
LegitScript is explicit that certification may be required for certain advertising programs and that platform advertising decisions are separate. [[5]](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/) A seal on the website is not Google approval. Apply to Google only after the third-party certification the location requires is actually in hand, and apply in the category that matches the business. Google's application instructions say not to apply for a category that does not match the business model, to make the application at the child account level rather than the manager account (MCC) level, and to submit a separate application for each location or group of locations if you want to advertise in more than one place. The site should be fully functional and globally accessible. An agency applying for an advertiser should send documentation of the relationship with the advertiser or license holder. [[7]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595717)
That location rule is an operations problem, not a formality. The founder of Marketing Powered is a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations. A network of that shape cannot treat certification as a single PDF attached to a manager account. If a facility is promoted or referenced on the certified domain, it has to be inside the certification scope. If a location is added, LegitScript's FAQ says you do not file a wholly new application to add it, and that significant changes should be reported within 30 days. [[5]](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/) Google still wants the advertising application aligned to the child account and to the locations being targeted. [[7]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595717)
Two drug policies sit beside addiction services and are easy to trip while writing a medication-assisted treatment page.
First, Google does not allow ads promoting or selling prescription opioid painkillers. Medication-assisted treatment drugs for opioid use disorder that meet the other prescription-drug requirements on the healthcare policy are not included in that ban. Certified addiction treatment providers in the United States are named as a limited exception, where local law permits, alongside a short list of other exceptions such as certain public-health campaigns. Violations of the opioid painkiller policy are treated as egregious. Google says that suspension can occur on detection, without prior warning, and that reinstatement is limited to compelling circumstances. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Do not let a blog post, a sidebar, or a stock photo caption promote opioid painkillers on a page you are sending paid traffic to.
Second, restricted drug terms are a different switch. For campaigns targeting Canada, New Zealand, or the United States, Google allows prescription drug terms for promotional purposes in line with local law, including content about branding, use, sale, and distribution. You do not need certification merely to use those terms in ads and landing pages. You do need certification to keyword-target them. Online pharmacies, telemedicine providers, and pharmaceutical manufacturers still need their own certification. Outside those three countries, promotional use of prescription drug terms in ads and landing pages is not allowed, with limited non-promotional exceptions such as regulatory warnings, legal notices, public-health campaigns, and academic publications. [[7]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595717) A treatment center can mention a medication on a US landing page and still be blocked from bidding on that medication name until the keyword certification is in place. Plan the keyword list against that distinction before launch, not after the first wave of disapprovals.
Violations of the addiction services policy itself are not in the "immediate suspension, no warning" class. Google says it will issue a warning at least 7 days before suspending the account for that policy. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) Seven days is not a grace period to keep spending through a known violation. It is a notice window. Use it to remove the ads, fix the destination, or file a real appeal. Leaving disapproved addiction ads in the account while you "see if they serve" is how a warning becomes an account event.
Build search without remarketing
The workable campaign, for a counseling practice and for a certified treatment program, is built around search intent and location, not around a list of people who already revealed a health interest. The workflow below is what a careful launch looks like when a media buyer, an intake lead, and someone who owns licensure sit in the same room before anything is enabled.
Start with the offer memo. One page, written in plain language: who is licensed to provide the service, in which states or provinces, at which addresses, whether any page promotes drug and alcohol recovery, whether any page promotes online prescribing, and whether any page sends callers to a third-party program. If the memo cannot be written without hedging, the campaign is not ready. The ad and the destination will be reviewed together.
Confirm certification against that memo. For a counseling practice with no drug-and-alcohol recovery offer and no online prescribing offer, do not file an addiction-services application "just in case." Google tells applicants not to apply for a category that does not match the business model. [[7]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595717) For a recovery program, confirm LegitScript status for the domain and the facilities actually named on it, then confirm Google's own approval on the child account that will spend. Eligible (limited) is the status you are aiming to understand, not a failure label: it means the ad can run in allowed locations when the domain is properly certified. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)
Build Search campaigns around service and place. Keywords should describe the care someone is already looking for and the geography you are licensed to serve: the level of care, the city, the insurance or payment language you can truthfully support, and the brand name. Do not import a remarketing list, a customer list, a lookalike, or audience expansion onto those campaigns. Google's fix for a personalized-advertising flag is to remove advertiser-curated audiences from the ad, the ad group, the asset group, and the campaign. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Check all four. A list left on at campaign level will keep limiting the account after the ad group looks clean.
Predefined audiences are allowed even when you are promoting in a sensitive category, because Google excludes sensitive signals from them. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) That is not an invitation to rebuild condition targeting under another name. Use location and, where it fits, a predefined audience as an observation layer you can read. If a custom segment points at a condition landing page, expect the footnote: Display may serve it only to non-sensitive audiences or contextually, and other campaign types using that segment with sensitive assets or pages will not be eligible to serve. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
Write the ad as an identification of the provider, not as a clinical promise. Say who you are, where you provide care, what level of care is available, and what the first call or visit is for. Do not claim a cure, a specific recovery timeline, or a result the reader should treat as the likely outcome. Google's unreliable-claims rules do not allow inaccurate claims, claims that present an improbable result as the likely outcome, or unproven cure claims for medical ailments. For health-related claims, including testimonials, Google may look to local regulatory guidelines. [[8]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?_gl=1%2A2mu9uq%2A_ga%2AMTMyNjU4MTEyLjE3NjQ0MzUzMDE)
Match the landing page to that ad and to nothing extra. If the ad is for outpatient therapy, the page should not open with a residential drug-treatment offer, a sober living application, or a partner-referral form. If the ad is for a certified program, the page should name the certified entity and the locations that certification covers. Pop-ups that block the content, broken forms, and destinations that are hard to navigate are their own policy problem. Google's destination requirements expect the landing page to be functional, useful, and easy to navigate, and Google says a warning is issued at least 7 days before suspension for that policy. [[9]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16427615)
Set conversion tracking in a system you control. The click, the call, and the form can be counted. The audience cannot be rebuilt from the people who converted if those people are patients or prospective patients of a health service. That is the structural reason attribution has to be joined downstream, in the CRM or intake system, to a qualified inquiry and then to admission. Marketing Powered tracks attribution to admission, not just to the lead. The Google Ads interface is the acquisition record. It is not the clinical record, and it should not be asked to become one.
Compare that with the account a non-health advertiser would build in the same week. They would upload customers, expand the audience, remarket to pricing-page visitors, and let Performance Max find similar people. Each of those steps is an advertiser-curated audience or a close cousin of one. In this category they are the steps to delete. The performance consequence is real: you will not get the cheap return visits those tactics produce in other industries. The alternative is not a clever audience. It is better query coverage, cleaner locations, faster intake, and a page that tells the truth about who answers the phone.
Landing pages, claims, and drug terms
Policy review does not stop at the ad. Google reviews the destination, and misrepresentation rules cover both. You cannot make it seem that you are affiliated with another organization or a government body when you are not. You cannot offer a service you do not have, including by lacking the licenses or qualifications. You cannot present yourself as providing medical help you do not provide. The business name in the ad has to represent the business being advertised. [[8]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?_gl=1%2A2mu9uq%2A_ga%2AMTMyNjU4MTEyLjE3NjQ0MzUzMDE) For a multi-location behavioral health organization, that means the ad, the page title, and the location asset should resolve to the same legal operator, not to a marketing brand that no clinician works under.
Location assets add a physical-world check. Advertising a business location without the owner's approval is not allowed. A location that does not match the business running the ad is not allowed. A closed location is not allowed. [[10]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/144649?hl=en&ref_topic=1310862) If a network has paused a site, remove it from ads and from the page before the campaign keeps sending people there. Affiliate-style location listings do not rescue a mismatch for the primary business.
Drug terms deserve a line-by-line pass, especially on medication-assisted treatment and psychiatry pages. In the United States, Canada, and New Zealand, a prescription drug term can appear in ad text and on the landing page for a promotional purpose if local law allows it, without a separate certification just for that appearance. Bidding on the term is the step that requires certification. [[7]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595717) Build two lists. One is language the page may use because a clinician needs to describe care. The other is keywords the account is actually certified to target. Do not let a keyword tool merge those lists.
Telemedicine is the other place psychiatry and addiction treatment get conflated. Booking an in-person visit is not, by itself, the prescription drug services policy. Promoting services related to the online prescribing, dispensing, or sale of prescription drugs is. Google's examples of businesses subject to that policy include online pharmacies and telemedicine providers. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) In the United States those advertisers need the stated LegitScript or NABP credential and Google certification. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) If the growth plan depends on virtual prescribing, put that offer on its own certification track and its own destination. Do not bury it in a general "mental health services" campaign and hope the review model reads only the headline.
Copy that tends to survive review is specific and boring in the right ways. Name the license type the organization actually holds. Name the city and the level of care. Say what happens on the first call: insurance questions, a clinical screening, or a scheduled visit, whichever is true. Say who the program is for in service language, not in a diagnosis you are targeting as an audience. If you use a patient story, do not present one person's result as the result a new caller should expect, and remember that Google may defer to local rules on health testimonials. [[8]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?_gl=1%2A2mu9uq%2A_ga%2AMTMyNjU4MTEyLjE3NjQ0MzUzMDE)
Copy that creates both policy and trust problems is the language of certainty. Cure claims, "in 30 days," before-and-after implications, and invented scarcity around bed availability are unreliable claims even when a similar result has happened for someone. They also train the call center to inherit expectations the clinical team cannot meet. The same standard applies to partner pages. If the ad is yours and the page belongs to a network of programs you do not operate, the user still has to be able to tell who will receive their information. Hiding that is a misrepresentation problem, and for drug and alcohol recovery it is often an eligibility problem as well, because referral services are generally outside LegitScript's addiction treatment certification. [[5]](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/)
A practical page review, done by a person and not only by an automated policy scan, checks five things: the legal name and license, the addresses that match location assets, every mention of drugs or medications, every link to a sister facility or partner, and every sentence that states an outcome. Fix those before you raise budgets. A clean account structure cannot outrun a destination that contradicts it.
Protect the account and measure admissions
The review that matters is not a results slide. It is a working session: certification status, disapproval reasons, search terms added since the last check, locations still open, and whether new pages have been published under the same domain. The image of that meeting should look like an account review, because that is what keeps a behavioral health Google Ads account available next month.
When an ad is disapproved under addiction services, Google's own options are to apply if you meet the requirements, to edit the ad or asset so it complies, or to appeal if you believe the decision is wrong. After an edit, Google says it automatically re-reviews the ad or asset, and that this typically takes 24 to 48 hours. If you cannot fix the violation, Google's guidance is to remove the ad, which helps prevent a later suspension for repeated violations. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) Appeals from the Ads page let you say you dispute the decision or that you made changes to comply. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) An appeal with no change, filed because the team is impatient, is not a strategy.
Do not open another account to get around a disapproval or a suspension. Google's circumventing-systems policy treats multiple-account abuse as not allowed. The examples include creating new accounts after a suspension to keep breaking the rules, and using several accounts to promote products or services for which you lack the required Google Ads certifications. [[11]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15938075?hl=en-AU) Cloaking, which means showing Google a different destination from the one users get in order to hide a policy problem, is treated as egregious. Suspension for that violation can happen on detection, without prior warning. [[12]](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15936969) A staging site that looks compliant and a live site that funnels callers into an uncertified offer is not a test. It is the pattern those rules describe.
Separate the warning clocks so the team does not panic, and does not relax. Addiction services and destination-requirements violations come with a stated warning of at least 7 days before suspension. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) [[9]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16427615) Unauthorized pharmacies and prescription opioid painkiller violations are in a different class: egregious, and eligible for suspension without that warning. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Know which notice you are holding before you decide how long you have.
Measurement has to respect the same boundary as targeting. You can know which campaign produced a call. You should not upload a list of people with a diagnosis, or people who visited a condition page, back into Customer Match or a similar advertiser-curated audience. That is the personalization rule, applied to "optimization" rather than to prospecting. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Join the click or call to the intake record in a system you operate, then mark whether that person reached a qualified clinical conversation and, where the data relationship allows it, an admission. That is a business measurement design. It is not a Google audience.
Calls need an extra privacy reading. Google's Call Ads Supplemental Terms say Google may monitor and record a sample of calls, texts, and similar communications started through the programs. The terms also say Google does not intend use of those communication features to create HIPAA obligations and makes no representation that the features satisfy HIPAA. If you are a covered entity or business associate, or you advertise for one, and you would be using the features in a way that involves protected health information, the terms say you should not accept them and should ask Google for exclusion from monitoring and recording. Accepting the terms includes a warranty that you are not in that position and will not use the features for a purpose involving protected health information. [[13]](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16914745?hl=en) Call recording is also skipped by default for advertisers in sensitive industries such as specific healthcare categories unless the advertiser explicitly enables it by accepting those terms. [[14]](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16913427?hl=en-AU)
Marketing Powered is HIPAA-aware. That is not a certification claim, and it is not a reason to put clinical detail into an ad platform. The practical setup is owned, local AI infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference where inference is actually needed, and a conversion path that stops at admission status rather than at a diagnosis code inside Google Ads. The founder managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts, and is a court-certified expert witness in advertising strategy. Those are the founder's history. They are useful because this work is account governance under published rules, not a creative exercise. They are not a promise about any current account's results.
What to review every week, in order:
* Certification and domain status for every child account that is spending, including any location added to the site since the last check.
* Disapprovals and limited statuses, with the policy name written down, not paraphrased.
* Audience settings at campaign, ad group, and asset group level. Advertiser-curated audiences should not be on.
* Search terms that pulled in drug names, unverified partners, or offers the certification does not cover.
* Destination diffs: new pages, new phone numbers, new location pages, new pop-ups.
* Intake outcomes joined outside Google: qualified conversation, scheduled assessment, admission. Spend that cannot be read past the lead is spend you cannot govern.
That review is slower than refreshing a cost-per-lead dashboard. It is also the review that keeps the account able to buy the next click.

FAQ
Q: Do outpatient therapists need LegitScript to run Google Ads?
A: Not for general mental health counseling. Google's Healthcare and medicines policy does not list counseling for anxiety, depression, or similar conditions as an addiction-services or prescription-drug category. The personalization policy still applies. Counseling for depression, anxiety, and addiction is a named example of health content, so Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalikes are not available. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) If the same practice starts advertising drug and alcohol recovery, or online prescribing, a different certification path opens.
Q: Can we remarket to people who visited an anxiety or depression page?
A: No. A remarketing list built from your site visitors is an advertiser-curated data segment. Those segments cannot be used when you are promoting in a sensitive interest category, and Google's health examples include that counseling. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) The fix Google describes is to remove those audiences from the ad, the ad group, the asset group, and the campaign, or to change the content so it is outside the policy. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Changing the button color is not the fix.
Q: We have several facilities. Is one certification enough?
A: LegitScript allows many organizations to certify multiple locations, and organizations with 10 or more facilities may fall under an enterprise path. Significant changes are supposed to be reported within 30 days. [[5]](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/) Google's advertising application is separate: file it at the child account, not only at the manager account, and file a separate application for each location or group of locations you want to advertise in. Q: What if we offer therapy and addiction treatment on one site?
A: Split the analysis even if you do not immediately split the domain. Therapy pages still sit under health personalization. Drug and alcohol recovery pages sit under addiction services and, in the United States and Canada, under the LegitScript-plus-Google requirement. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) If uncertified facilities or referral offers are linked from the certified destination, expect the review to see them. The clean operating choice is one destination per certification scope, with navigation that does not quietly merge them.
Q: Can we bid on medication names?
A: In the United States, Canada, and New Zealand, using a prescription drug term in an ad or on a landing page is not, by itself, the step that requires certification. Keyword-targeting that term is. [[7]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595717) Confirm you hold the certification that covers keyword targeting before those terms go into the account. Keep opioid painkiller promotion off the page entirely, aside from the narrow exceptions Google lists, including the separate treatment of qualifying medication-assisted treatment drugs. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)
Q: Are sober living homes or referral sites able to advertise recovery services in the US?
A: Google includes sober living environments, lead generators, and referral agencies in its examples of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, which means those promotions are inside the restriction. [[4]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) LegitScript says sober living environments are generally not eligible, and lists referral services among organizations that are generally not eligible. [[5]](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/) Google then says services that are not eligible for LegitScript certification are not allowed to advertise drug and alcohol addiction services on Google. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) A licensed clinical program can still have a housing partner. It should not send paid Google traffic to an offer LegitScript will not certify and Google will not approve.
Q: What should we do in the first day after a disapproval?
A: Read the policy name on the disapproval. If it is addiction services and you are not certified for that offer, pause or remove the ads rather than editing around the words. If you are certified and the ad was flagged in error, appeal from the account and say whether you dispute the decision or have changed the ad to comply. Q: Does call tracking from Google Ads create a HIPAA safe harbor?
A: No. Google's Call Ads Supplemental Terms say Google does not intend those features to create HIPAA obligations and does not represent that the features satisfy HIPAA. Covered entities and business associates are told not to accept the terms if use would involve protected health information, and to request exclusion from monitoring and recording instead. [[13]](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16914745?hl=en) Treat call content as something your own intake system must handle under your own obligations. Use Google's call reporting for operational counts, not as a clinical record.
