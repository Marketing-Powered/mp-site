Google Ads Match Types 2026 Strategy Guide
Match type is a budget decision, not a keyword label you set once and ignore. Exact, phrase, and broad match decide which searches can spend your daily cap and how much control you hand to Google's query matching. In 2026 the practical question is where tight control still outperforms broad automation, especially in healthcare and other regulated accounts that cannot buy the click back later.

Match type is a budget allocation
Picture a Tuesday budget review between a hospital service-line marketer and a paid search buyer. Brand terms in exact match are pacing behind, while a broad-match ad group for a general service name has spent most of the daily cap. Nothing in the Google Ads interface flags an error because the auction simply spent wherever the settings granted permission.
A match type is spending permission. Exact match permits searches with the same meaning or intent as your keyword, phrase match permits searches that include that meaning, and broad match permits related searches that may not contain the keyword's direct meaning, according to [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en). Because broader match types also cover the searches of narrower types, repeating one idea in three match types creates three doors into the same budget rather than three separate strategies.
The commercial trade-off is direct. Narrow matching leaves demand unseen, while wide matching spends on demand you did not name. Under a capped daily budget, the wider door often spends first, starving the high-intent keyword you wanted to protect.
This constraint hits harder when recovery tools are off limits. For ads about physical or mental health conditions, treatments for chronic conditions, invasive procedures, or disabilities, [Google's health in personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) prohibits advertiser-curated audiences, including Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments. Predefined Google audiences such as in-market segments remain permitted because Google states that sensitive user signals are excluded from them. When you cannot retarget a visitor who leaves a condition page, match type, negatives, and landing-page relevance carry the full weight of acquisition quality.
What exact, phrase, and broad match do now
Google documents three positive keyword match types using familiar syntax: unquoted text for broad match (the default), quotation marks for phrase match, and square brackets for exact match. What changed relative to older training decks is the matching standard. Exact match no longer requires identical text, and phrase match no longer requires a fixed word order.
* Exact; Syntax: [keyword]; What Google says can match: Searches with the same meaning or intent; Primary trade-off: Lowest reach among the three match types
* Phrase; Syntax: "keyword"; What Google says can match: Searches that include the meaning, including implied or more specific forms; Primary trade-off: Can pull adjacent queries that share a topic but not your offer
* Broad; Syntax: keyword; What Google says can match: Related searches, including queries without the keyword's direct meaning; Primary trade-off: Least predictable query mix from the keyword alone
These rules come directly from [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en). The same documentation notes that near-duplicate keywords like "red car" and "car red" are treated as duplicates, with only one bid entering the auction. Stacking reordered variants across ad groups adds reporting clutter without creating extra control.
Broad match also reads signals beyond the keyword string. Google may use the searcher's recent activity, the content of your landing page and assets, and other keywords in the ad group to identify related queries. That helps when a dedicated page describes one service, but it wastes spend when a multi-location hospital points a broad "spine surgery" keyword at a general services directory that lists ten other departments.
Google states that using Smart Bidding with broad match is critical because every query carries different auction-time context, and while Smart Bidding works with all match types, it performs best with broad match's larger learning pool per [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en). Running broad match on manual bids without conversion signals is not a discovery test. It is an open-ended interpretation of your website paid at a static price.
Phrase match sits in the middle to reach more searches than exact while staying closer to your offer than broad. Because "include the meaning" allows word-order changes and implied intent, inspect the search terms report every week rather than trusting legacy assumptions about quotation marks.
Where tight control still earns its keep
Automation works well when bounded by clear commercial rules. Unchecked automation writes a budget policy your leadership team never approved.
Exact match still earns a dedicated budget when the query represents core demand. Facility names, provider brands, specific location-plus-service queries, and scarce high-intent terms belong here. Same-meaning matching grants a smaller permission than related-search matching, which protects spend where wrong clicks are expensive and qualified searches are limited.
Phrase match fits structured modifiers you are staffed to serve: a city, an insurance term, a care modality, or a credential. A professional-services firm offering three legal practices can use phrase match to capture location-modified employment counsel queries without letting broad match expand into unrelated HR how-to searches.
Broad match earns budget only after three prerequisites are in place:
* Conversion tracking measures a real business event rather than a page view or unqualified click.
* The campaign uses a conversion-based Smart Bidding strategy so bids adjust to query intent.
* An operator reviews search terms frequently enough to add negative keywords before waste compounds.
There is also a structural reason to retain exact and phrase coverage alongside broad tests. Under [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en), an eligible exact-match keyword identical to the user's query is preferred over other broad or phrase keywords and over Performance Max. An identical phrase or broad keyword shares priority with an identical Performance Max search theme, after which AI relevance and Ad Rank decide among remaining candidates. Deleting an identical exact keyword removes that priority shield and pushes the query into a broader auction contest.
Audit the mix before you widen it
A useful match-type audit happens in a working session with two people and one exported search-terms file. One person operates the Google Ads account, and the other owns intake or revenue and knows immediately which inquiries the team will reject. Run these seven steps in order so each change stays readable.
* Export active Search keywords with match type, cost, and primary conversion action. If the account optimizes to clicks, scrolls, or unvetted form fills, fix measurement first. Reallocating match types around the wrong conversion event scales the wrong demand.
* Segment keywords by intent before checking cost. Group rows into brand, non-brand commercial, location-modified, and informational buckets. Cost alone makes every line look expensive; intent clarifies which searches deserve budget.
* Compare search terms against the matching keyword, not the campaign label. Campaign names reflect your plan, while search terms show what the auction actually bought during a complete reporting window.
* Sort queries into three actions: tighten, widen, or block. Add blocked terms as negatives the same day. Hold tighten-and-widen edits until you inspect campaign-level overrides.
* Check the campaign-level broad match setting. Per [Google Ads Help on the broad match keywords campaign setting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13389795?hl=en), turning this setting on converts existing exact and phrase keywords to broad match, forces new keywords to broad match, and requires conversion-based Smart Bidding. Editing individual match types inside a campaign with that override enabled wastes the effort.
* Review Performance Max and AI Max overlap. A Search keyword can lose an identical query if its campaign is budget-limited, has low search volume, faces disapproved ads or landing pages, or misses targeting rules, as outlined in [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en). Changing a match type cannot rescue an ineligible keyword.
* Rebuild in deliberate layers. Keep must-own terms in exact or phrase, isolate broad match where Smart Bidding and negative lists are active, and separate brand from non-brand where reporting requires clean visibility. Google documents brand inclusions as a specific control for keeping expanded brand campaigns tied to defined brand queries in [Google Ads Help on the broad match keywords campaign setting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13389795?hl=en).
Avoid widening match types in the same week you swap landing pages, bid strategies, or conversion actions. Isolating one variable lets you attribute changes in conversion value per cost to the decision that caused them.
How the auction picks a keyword
Match type controls whether a keyword can enter the auction. Prioritization rules decide which eligible keyword or campaign represents your account when multiple candidates qualify.
For identical queries, Google documents a clear preference order in [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en). An eligible exact-match keyword identical to the search takes priority over other phrase or broad keywords and over Performance Max. An identical phrase or broad keyword shares priority with an identical Performance Max search theme, while an identical search theme beats a non-identical phrase keyword. When multiple ad groups remain eligible, AI relevance narrows the field and Ad Rank breaks ties.
Two planning rules follow from this hierarchy. First, identical exact coverage acts as a defensive moat for brand names and core service lines, provided the Search campaign stays eligible with approved ads, valid targeting, and sufficient budget. If Search runs out of budget at noon, Performance Max or a looser ad group can absorb the afternoon queries.
Second, campaign settings alter how identical queries are prioritized. Under [Google Ads Help on the broad match keywords campaign setting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13389795?hl=en), campaigns with that setting enabled prioritize their broad keywords on identical searches as if both a broad and an exact copy existed. If you later migrate that campaign to AI Max, broad keywords lose that exact-equivalent priority unless you add exact-match copies, and converting an existing broad keyword to exact can remove historical keyword stats. Review that mechanics note before restructuring campaigns ahead of a migration.
Negatives are a different matching system
Positive match types expanded to cover intent and related themes, but negative keywords stayed literal. Assuming negatives behave like mirror images of positive keywords is the most common reason excluded topics keep spending.
Negative keywords do not match close variants. As shown in [Google Ads Help on negative keywords](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972?hl=en), excluding the negative broad keyword "flowers" blocks "red flowers" while still allowing "red flower" to serve. Casing and misspellings are handled, but singulars, plurals, and synonyms must be added explicitly.
Each negative match type follows strict token rules documented in [Google Ads Help on negative keywords](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972?hl=en):
* Negative broad match (default): Blocks searches containing all negative terms in any order, but allows searches containing only part of the phrase.
* Negative phrase match: Blocks searches containing the exact terms in the same order, even when extra words appear before or after.
* Negative exact match: Blocks searches containing the exact terms in the same order with no extra words, allowing queries with additional words through.
Account-level negatives apply across eligible Search and Shopping inventory, making them useful for universal exclusions and risky for mixed-objective accounts. A hospital running nurse recruitment ads cannot block "jobs" at account level without halting its careers campaign. Note two mechanical limits from the same help page: Google may still show an ad if your negative appears after the 16th word of a long query, and symbols like ampersands or accented characters are treated as distinct keywords.
Build negative lists from paid search terms and intake rejections rather than recycled agency templates. Tie review frequency to spending velocity: daily or twice-weekly checks for active broad-match and AI Max ad groups, and lighter periodic checks on exact-match brand campaigns.
Regulated accounts and the 2026 setting change
Place two live builds side by side to see why match-type rules cannot be copied across industries. On one side, a multi-location hospital promotes a sensitive health service line without remarketing lists or patient-list Customer Match. On the other, a B2B professional-services firm advertises an unrestricted commercial offer with full audience layering. Moving the firm's broad-match structure into the hospital account either triggers policy violations or strips away the audience guardrails and leaves open-ended query expansion.

Canada has the same pair of requirements. Other countries differ, including government-entity-only rules, so check the location table before treating certification as a keyword problem.

Planning for 2026 also requires checking platform upgrades already on the calendar. According to [Google Ads Help on the broad match keywords campaign setting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13389795?hl=en), starting in September 2026 campaigns using that setting will automatically upgrade to AI Max. Audit active campaigns now rather than designing a structure around a deprecated toggle.
AI Max introduces distinct permissions. Per [Google Ads Help on how AI Max for Search campaigns works](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15910187?hl=en), enabling AI Max activates search term matching by default (toggleable at the ad group level), combining broad match and keywordless matching across keywords, creatives, and URLs, with an AI Max match type and source breakdown reported in the search terms report. Text customization and final URL expansion are separate controls. Google states in [Set up AI Max for Search campaigns](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15909989?hl=en) that search term matching, final URL expansion, and text customization need conversion-based Smart Bidding to function effectively, and new Search campaigns turn AI Max on by default.
Regulated advertisers should treat those controls as three separate approvals. You may test search term matching while keeping final URL expansion off so sensitive condition pages never swap to generic pages, and keeping text customization off where medical or legal copy requires pre-approval.
A practical account split for 2026 balances control with discovery:
* Keep brand names and must-own commercial queries in an adequately funded Search campaign with exact match on identical terms.
* Assign location-modified and core service terms to phrase match aligned to staffed offerings.
* Isolate broad match or AI Max search term matching inside campaigns with conversion-based Smart Bidding, single-offer landing pages, and active negative lists.
* Evaluate Performance Max by whether it captures identical Search queries rather than by blended account averages.
* Never attempt to backfill weak query control with restricted remarketing audiences on sensitive health campaigns.
Watch language settings as well. Under [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en), Google may serve an ad in a different language than the search query when confident the user understands it. Health systems serving bilingual regions should configure dedicated language campaigns and landing pages intentionally instead of catching mismatches on recorded intake calls.

FAQ
Q: What are the three Google Ads match types?
A: The three positive Google Ads match types are broad, phrase, and exact. Broad is the default and matches related searches, phrase matches searches that include the keyword's meaning, and exact matches searches with the same meaning or intent across a narrower query pool, per [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en).
Q: Does exact match still mean the exact words?
A: No. Exact match covers searches with the same meaning or intent rather than an identical character string, so close wording variations still match. Check the search terms report regularly to verify what your exact-match keywords triggered.
Q: Should every Search campaign move to broad match?
A: No. Broad match requires conversion-based Smart Bidding, reliable conversion tracking, and active negative keyword maintenance to avoid wasted spend. It is a weak default for brand protection, narrow service scopes, or landing pages covering multiple unrelated offers.
Q: Why did a negative keyword fail to block a search?
A: Negative keywords do not match close variants, so missing a singular, plural, or synonym allows related searches through. Confirm you chose the right negative match type, because negative exact permits extra words and negative broad only blocks queries containing every listed term, per [Google Ads Help on negative keywords](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972?hl=en).
Q: Can healthcare advertisers replace tight match types with remarketing?
A: Not on sensitive health topics. Under [Google's health in personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en), advertiser-curated audiences including your data segments, Customer Match, audience expansion, and lookalike segments are prohibited for sensitive health ads and destinations, leaving query control as your primary lever.
Q: What happens to the campaign-level broad match setting in 2026?
A: Starting in September 2026, Google will automatically upgrade campaigns using the campaign-level broad match setting to AI Max, according to [Google Ads Help on the broad match keywords campaign setting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13389795?hl=en). Audit campaigns using that toggle before the transition so prioritization and URL controls match your compliance rules.
Q: How does Performance Max interact with Search match types?
A: Performance Max competes in the same auction under a documented priority order rather than operating in isolation. An eligible exact-match keyword identical to the query takes priority over Performance Max, while an identical Performance Max search theme can beat a non-identical phrase keyword, per [Google Ads Help on keyword matching options](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en).
