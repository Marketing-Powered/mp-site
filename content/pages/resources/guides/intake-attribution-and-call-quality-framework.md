Intake Attribution and Call Quality Framework for Healthcare
An intake attribution framework is the shared set of definitions, fields, and owners that connect a marketing source to a call, a qualified inquiry, and an admission outcome. It tells a provider which campaigns produced admissions the organization has agreed to count, not merely which campaigns produced phone activity. It will not prove causation by itself, and it only joins records where consent and data-handling rules allow the join.

What providers should expect from it
Picture a Tuesday operations meeting at a multi-location provider. Marketing brings a call export. The admissions lead brings a disposition log. Finance brings a census tied to arrival or billing. Each report can be internally consistent and still useless beside the others, because each team is counting a different event. The call file mixes new people seeking care with existing patients, family members, referral partners, insurance verification, vendors, and wrong numbers. The admissions log uses free text. Finance uses a later stage that marketing never sees.
A working intake attribution framework produces three things, and only those three things, until the organization is ready for more.
First, a written definition of a qualified inquiry and a written definition of the admission stage that will be used in acquisition reporting. Second, one record per inquiry, with a stable identifier, a source of record that is not overwritten on transfer, and a disposition code from a closed list. Third, a monthly view that shows source, inquiry volume, qualified inquiries, and the agreed downstream outcome side by side, including an explicit unknown bucket.
That is the expectation to set with a CEO, a COO, a CMO, and an admissions director before anyone debates software. The framework is a record-keeping structure and a governance routine. It is not a single score, and it is not a claim that a click caused a person to enter care. Providers that want the measurement system built and maintained, rather than only the definitions, can look at [analytics and attribution](/services/analytics-and-attribution). This guide owns the implementation detail those service pages do not.
What a provider should not expect is a clean causal story from the first month, a replacement for clinical judgment, or a file that can be uploaded anywhere. Unknown source will remain. Some admissions will have no marketing record. Some marketing records will never become admissions. The useful output is a ledger the three teams will sign, not a chart that hides the disagreements.
When behavioral programs need the join
The break shows up early in behavioral and mental health programs because the phone is the front door and the front door is noisy. A residential program, an outpatient clinic, and a referral line can share a brand and still produce calls that should never sit in the same conversion bucket. A parent calling about a young adult, a probation officer asking about bed availability, an alumni checking a schedule, and a person seeking care for the first time can all reach the same tracked number in the same hour. If those calls share one conversion action, paid media will optimize toward whatever is easiest to generate, not toward an admission the organization can defend.
Inquiry-to-admission tracking matters here for a second reason. Platform rules limit how health-related demand can be chased after the click. Google treats health as a sensitive interest category. Its examples include counseling services for mental health issues such as depression, anxiety, and addiction [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en). When an advertiser is promoting in that category, advertiser-curated audiences are not available. That list includes Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en). Predefined Google audiences can still be used, because Google states that sensitive user signals are excluded from them. However, custom segments that use sensitive creative or point to sensitive landing pages will only serve on Display to non-sensitive audiences or contextually, and other campaigns using those custom segments will not be eligible to serve [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en]. Marketing Powered is built for Google’s sensitive healthcare vertical, where advertiser-curated audiences are restricted. The practical consequence is that the first session and the first qualified inquiry have to be captured correctly. There is no later remarketing list to paper over a lost source.
Addiction-services advertising has a separate certification layer, and it is location-specific. Google restricts promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Services for impulse control disorders, behavioral addiction, or nicotine addiction are out of scope of that policy [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en). The location table is not one rule. On the current [healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en), the United States row requires certification as an addiction services provider by the LegitScript Certification program, and Google certification. The Canada row requires LegitScript’s Addiction Treatment Certification and Google certification. The Australia, France, Ireland, and New Zealand rows limit promotion to government entities and require Google certification. They do not state a LegitScript requirement. Other locations may not allow the promotion. Read the live row for every country in the campaign before treating a certification as current. Marketing Powered is LegitScript-aware. Certification status is the advertiser’s to hold and to renew. It is not a substitute for the intake ledger.
Additional confidentiality rules may apply to substance use disorder records. Under 42 CFR 2.12, Part 2 restrictions cover information, including information on referral and intake, about patients receiving diagnosis, treatment, or referral for treatment for a substance use disorder, when that information is created by a federally assisted part 2 program and the other applicability tests are met [42 CFR 2.12](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/2.12). Whether a given program is a part 2 program, and which fields in a marketing join would identify a person as having a substance use disorder, is a legal determination. It is not a setting in a call platform. Counsel should make that call before any outcome status moves toward an agency, an analytics tool, or an ad account.
The same pattern appears in mental health service lines that are not addiction treatment. The calls are still mixed. The disposition list is still the control. The difference is which advertising and confidentiality rules apply. A [behavioral health](/who-we-help/behavioral-health) operator should assume the phone file is contaminated until a taxonomy proves otherwise. That assumption is operational, not a judgment about the people calling.
Definitions before any dashboard
Attribution fails at definitions and handoffs before it fails at technology. A healthcare marketing attribution model that starts with a tool choice will reproduce whatever the tool already counts. Call duration, answer rate, and form fills are convenient. They are not an admission definition.
Write two definitions on one page and get them signed by admissions, marketing, and finance. Each provider sets its own. The template below is a starting worksheet, not a clinical standard and not a rule imposed by any platform.
Qualified inquiry. A new contact, from a person seeking care or from a support person acting for that person, about starting care at a named location or service line, that is not an existing episode, a vendor, a wrong number, or a pure insurance-verification call with no intent to start care. The contact has enough information to continue: a callback path and a service line or location. A call that ends before those facts exist can still be logged. It is not qualified.
Qualified admission, for acquisition reporting. Pick one stage and use it everywhere the media report is discussed. A practical ladder, which the provider may shorten, is: inquiry logged, assessment scheduled, assessment completed, benefits verified, arrival confirmed, admitted, still in care after an agreed early window. The reporting stage might be "arrived" for one organization and "admitted" for another. The error is using the word admission in a meeting to mean three of those stages at once.
Source of record. The source written at the first qualified inquiry, and not replaced by a later internal transfer, a callback, or a second campaign touch inside the same episode. Rules that keep the source honest:
* Store the click or session identifier, or the fact that one existed, on the call record before the call is transferred.
* If there is no click identifier, use the tracked-number pool mapped to the campaign or location, not the extension the caller reached after the transfer.
* Direct, unmarked, and untracked stay unknown. Do not assign unknown volume to the largest campaign.
* Professional referrals, alumni, and walk-ins get a referral or offline code. They are not paid-search conversions.
* A re-admission window, if the organization wants one, is a written number of days. It is an organizational choice. It is not a default in the ad platform.
What is not a definition. Call length is not quality. A long call can be an insurance dispute with an existing patient. A short call can be a clear new inquiry that is transferred cleanly. A form fill is not an inquiry until a person confirms intent. A booked assessment is not an admission unless the signed definition says so.
Put the signed page in the same folder as the disposition list. If the page and the list disagree, the dashboard is not ready. This is the healthcare marketing attribution model in operational form: a rule for which event earns the source, a rule for which event counts as qualified, and a rule for which later event finance will accept. Software implements those rules. It does not invent them.
An illustrative ledger and schema
The scene that follows is illustrative. It is not a client result, not a benchmark, and not a description of any named organization. Use it to see the people and the fields, then replace every label with your own.
Two locations share a brand. One is residential. One is outpatient. A person seeking care clicks a location page, calls a tracked number, and reaches an intake coordinator. The coordinator hears that the caller is a sibling asking about outpatient care for an adult who has agreed to the call. The coordinator logs a new inquiry, codes the disposition, and books an assessment at the outpatient site. Later that day a clinician calls back from a different extension. A week after that, the person arrives. Finance records arrival against the outpatient location. Marketing still needs to know that the original click, not the clinician callback, is the source of record.
The stage ledger for that inquiry has one row, not three.
* Session. Who writes it: Site or call tracking. What changes: Click or session present, location page category, campaign. What must not change: Clinical reason, caller name in any ad-platform field.
* Call received. Who writes it: Intake coordinator. What changes: Call identifier, time, location, party type. What must not change: Source of record.
* Disposition set. Who writes it: Intake coordinator, same shift. What changes: Code from the closed list, qualified flag. What must not change: Free-text as the only code.
* Handoff. Who writes it: Intake coordinator. What changes: Next step, owner, time. What must not change: Campaign, replaced by the transfer extension.
* Arrival or admission stage. Who writes it: Admissions or finance. What changes: Agreed stage and date. What must not change: Diagnosis, clinical note, recording.
An anonymized event schema, fields and definitions only, can live in the CRM or in a governed export. Keep the marketing export thinner than the clinical record.
* inquiry_id. Definition: Internal identifier created at first log. Leaves the covered environment?: Only if counsel allows a join key.
* call_id. Definition: Identifier from the call platform. Leaves the covered environment?: Inside the call platform and CRM.
* occurred_at. Definition: Timestamp of first contact. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes, as a time bucket if needed.
* location_id. Definition: Facility or program code. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes, as a code.
* channel. Definition: Paid search, organic, direct, referral, unknown. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes.
* campaign_id. Definition: Present only when source of record is paid. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes, as an ID, not as a condition name.
* click_id_present. Definition: Yes or no. Not the raw identifier in a loose export. Leaves the covered environment?: Raw value stays in the system that captured it.
* path_category. Definition: Service line or location type, not a condition URL. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes, if the category itself is not identifying.
* disposition_code. Definition: Closed-list code. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes.
* quality_flag. Definition: Human score against the rubric. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes, as pass or review.
* qualified_inquiry. Definition: Derived from disposition and rubric. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes.
* admission_stage. Definition: The signed stage only. Leaves the covered environment?: Yes, as a stage name the privacy review has cleared.
* source_locked. Definition: Boolean. True once source of record is written. Leaves the covered environment?: Internal.
Sample disposition taxonomy, also illustrative. Retire overlapping free-text labels rather than adding codes forever.
* NEW_SEEKER: person seeking care, first inquiry in the window
* NEW_SUPPORT: family or other support person, new inquiry, caller has a path to continue
* PRO_REFERRAL: referral partner, with partner code
* EXISTING: current patient, alumni, or open episode
* BENEFITS_ONLY: insurance or benefits question with no new-care intent
* VENDOR: sales, recruiting, or other non-care call
* WRONG_OR_DROP: wrong number, immediate disconnect, or no usable facts
* DUPLICATE: same person, same open inquiry
* NOT_A_FIT: new inquiry, organization cannot serve, documented reason that is not a diagnosis code in the marketing export
The data-ownership split is part of the schema, not a footnote. The ad platform holds cost, clicks, and a conversion signal that has been stripped to what counsel allows. The call platform holds audio and transcripts only if recording is permitted and the vendor relationship has been reviewed. The CRM holds the inquiry, the disposition, the owner, and the locked source. The EHR or billing system holds the clinical and census record. A status flag may return to the CRM. The clinical note does not travel to the ad account. An agency may receive coded, aggregated fields the provider authorizes. Client data ownership stays with the provider. Owned hardware or local inference, where an organization uses it, does not transfer ownership of those records and does not mean every workload runs there.
In the illustrative scene, the analyst joins on inquiry_id, not on the caller’s name. The intake lead can see the recording if the recording policy allows it. The media buyer sees channel, campaign_id, qualified_inquiry, and the agreed stage. Nobody in that chain needs a diagnosis to decide whether the campaign is producing qualified demand.
Roles, scoring, and the monthly close
Name one owner for each handoff. Shared ownership is how source fields get overwritten.
The CEO or COO accepts the admission stage that will be used in spend reviews and refuses any parallel definition in board materials. The CMO owns source capture, the campaign taxonomy, and the marketing export specification, including the list of fields that must never appear. The admissions or intake director owns the disposition list, coder training, and intake call quality scoring. Privacy or compliance owns the field list, retention, recording posture, and vendor agreements. Finance owns the census or billing flag that confirms the agreed stage, and owns the refusal to send clinical detail back upstream. An analyst owns the join logic and an exception log. The analyst does not invent a source for unknown calls.
Intake call quality scoring is a human review against a rubric, applied after the disposition is set. It is not a substitute for the disposition, and it is not a model score treated as fact. A useful rubric has four checks. The provider should edit the words to match how the desk actually works.
* Right party. The caller is a person seeking care, or a support person on a new inquiry, not an existing episode or a vendor.
* Right intent. The conversation is about starting care, not a status check, a billing dispute, or a wrong number.
* Enough to route. Location or service line is known, and there is a way to continue.
* Handoff complete. A next step is booked, or the reason it was not booked is a code, not a blank note.
Do not score warmth, accent, or call length. Duration can help routing and staffing. It does not define qualification. If a transcript tool suggests a code, a person still sets the code. Marketing Powered has been AI-native since 2022 and can run HIPAA-aware inference on owned, local infrastructure where that design fits. A suggestion is not a disposition. Human governance stays on the code that enters the ledger.
Implementation, in order:
* Privacy and counsel review the fields, any recording, retention, and whether Part 2 or other confidentiality rules constrain the join. Stop field design until that review names what may leave each system. This page is not legal advice.
* Draft the two definitions. Admissions, marketing, and finance sign the same page.
* Freeze the disposition taxonomy. Free text can remain as a note. It cannot be the system of record.
* Map the call record to the CRM with a stable inquiry_id before any transfer. That call-to-CRM mapping is the handoff most often lost.
* Capture source at the session and lock it. Document the rule for callbacks and internal transfers.
* Train intake on the codes with examples counsel has cleared. If recordings are not cleared for training, use scripts.
* Dual-code for a short period. Two people code the same sample. Fix codes that they apply differently. Do not publish a dashboard during that period except as a draft with the disagreements visible.
* Publish the monthly view: media cost where available, inquiries, qualified inquiries, and outcomes at the signed stage. Keep unknown visible. Keep untracked admissions visible beside any cost figure so the figure is not mistaken for the full census.
* Hold a short monthly close. One person chairs it. The agenda is exceptions, not a tour of every campaign. Change a definition only by rewriting the signed page.
The intake handoff deserves its own line in that sequence. A transfer to a clinician, a second site, or a callback queue is where source, party type, and next step usually die. The coordinator who first qualifies the inquiry writes the code before the transfer. The receiving person may add a note. The receiving person does not get a new inquiry_id unless the signed rules say the contact is a new episode.
Admissions conversion attribution, in this framework, means the share of qualified inquiries that reach the signed stage inside a written window, split by source of record and location. It does not mean the share of all phone calls that someone later called an admission. Report the denominator in the same cell as the rate. A rate without a denominator is how the Tuesday meeting goes wrong again.
The downloadable artifact is a one-page worksheet, not a software install. Rebuild it in a spreadsheet with these tabs: definitions and signatures, disposition codes, event fields and where each field lives, role map, monthly close checklist, and an exception log. Complete that worksheet before asking for an outside review. Related implementation notes sit with other [resources](/resources). Service pages remain the place for buying an analytics engagement. This page remains the place for the definitions.
Hospitals and other regulated buyers
The same ledger applies outside residential treatment, with different stage names. Hospital and health-system service-line leaders usually do not lack data. They lack a single definition that survives a handoff among marketing, the access center, a clinic scheduler, and a facility finance partner. A booked slot, a completed visit, and an inpatient admission are different events. Facility-level measurement fails when a central call center overwrites the location the person originally selected, or when a referral office and a digital campaign both claim the same visit.
For a hospital service line, the qualified inquiry is often a new-patient request for a named service, not a nurse-line symptom call and not a prescription refill. The downstream outcome used in acquisition reporting might be a completed new-patient visit, or a scheduled procedure, depending on what the service-line leader and finance will both defend. Write that choice down. Do not let the access-center software’s default "scheduled" stand in for it.
Professional services have a narrower analog. A qualified consultation is the event the firm defines: right matter type, right party, conflict status clear enough to proceed, next step owned. Form fills and answered calls are inputs. They are not the event a partner should use to judge a campaign. Multi-location professional firms have the same source-lock problem as multi-site providers. The office that receives the transfer is not a new source.
Regulated buyers should expect extra constraints, not a different theory. Consent, retention, and platform policy still govern which events can be joined and which can be sent anywhere outside the organization. Platform rules are changeable. A measurement design that depended on a remarketing list, a customer upload, or a conversion payload full of page URLs will break, or should never have been turned on, when the content is health-related. Build the ledger so the marketing export can survive the removal of those tactics.
What does not change is the ownership split. Marketing owns acquisition source. Intake, access, or scheduling owns the disposition. Finance or the service-line operator owns the outcome stage. Privacy owns the boundary. If those four do not sign the same definitions, a hospital attribution model is a dashboard with a disputed denominator.
Limits, mistakes, and data handling
Set the two reports next to each other before the monthly close. On the left, the call platform’s default: answered calls over a duration threshold, source taken from the last number that rang, recording stored with the vendor, conversion sent because the call was long enough. On the right, the ledger: disposition from the closed list, source locked at first inquiry, qualified flag set by a person, outcome stage written by finance, unknown left unknown. The people in that comparison are specific. The analyst maintains both columns. The intake lead can explain every code on the right and almost none of the thresholds on the left. The CFO will use the right-hand stage and will ignore a conversion the call platform marked qualified. If the meeting still argues from the left-hand column, the framework is not in use yet.
The data cannot prove several things operators sometimes ask it to prove.
It cannot prove that the credited campaign caused the admission. First inquiry, or last click before the inquiry, is a reporting rule. It is not an incrementality test. People see more than one message. Referrals happen in conversations that never hit a tracked number. A rise in qualified admissions after a budget change is a pattern to investigate, not a measured effect.
It cannot prove clinical quality, appropriateness of care, or what happened for the person after the agreed stage. Those questions belong to clinical and quality teams, with their own definitions. Marketing language should not imply diagnosis or treatment effect.
It cannot see demand that never creates an inquiry record. Brand searches that become a walk-in, a physician referral with no digital touch, and calls to a number that was never pooled will sit outside the join. Show them as untracked. Do not force them into a campaign.
It cannot justify sending protected information to an advertising system. Google states that these supplemental terms are currently applicable to advertisers in the United States. Google’s Call Ads Supplemental Terms state that Google may monitor and record a sample of communications initiated through those program features [Call Ads Supplemental Terms](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16914745?hl=en). Accepting the terms includes a representation that notice was given and express consent obtained from people who may participate. The same terms state that Google does not intend use of the Communication Features to create obligations under HIPAA and makes no representations that those features satisfy HIPAA requirements [Call Ads Supplemental Terms](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16914745?hl=en). A covered entity or business associate that wants to use the features is told not to accept the terms, and instead to request exclusion from monitoring and recording. Accepting the terms includes a warranty that the advertiser is not a covered entity or business associate and will not use the features in a way that involves protected health information [Call Ads Supplemental Terms](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16914745?hl=en). Read the current terms. They are a vendor contract, not a compliance determination for your program.
Recording and transcription for intake quality are a separate legal decision. State notice and consent rules differ. This guide does not list them and does not advise a recording setup. Counsel decides whether to record, what notice is required, how long audio is kept, and who may hear it. OCR guidance on audio-only telehealth, which is not an intake-marketing manual, still states a useful boundary: a telecommunications vendor with only transient access to protected health information may be a conduit, but a vendor that creates, receives, or maintains that information, including by storing recordings or transcripts, is more than a conduit and requires a business associate agreement before use [HHS audio-only telehealth guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-audio-telehealth/index.html). Apply that question to the actual call-tracking vendor. Do not assume a telehealth arrangement covers a marketing recording archive.
The HIPAA Privacy Rule requires authorization for uses or disclosures of protected health information for marketing communications, with limited exceptions, including certain face-to-face communications and promotional gifts of nominal value [HHS marketing authorization FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/when-is-patient-authorization-required-for-marketing/index.html). An internal attribution join is not the same act as a marketing communication. It still needs its own privacy review. A cookie banner is not that review.
Mistakes that recreate the Tuesday argument:
* Treating every answered call as a conversion in the ad platform.
* Letting a duration threshold define qualification.
* Overwriting source when the call is transferred or returned.
* Putting condition or treatment names in URLs that analytics or ad tags can collect.
* Uploading patient or inquirer lists into Customer Match for health promotion.
* Allocating unknown source in proportion to known source.
* Using one word, admission, for assessed, arrived, and still in care.
* Sending recordings to an agency "for optimization" without a defined purpose, a retention limit, and an agreement.
* Building the dashboard before the signed definitions exist.
* Hiding undispositioned calls so the qualified rate looks stable.
Marketing Powered tracks attribution to admission, not just to the lead, where authorized data supports the join. The founder’s own history is the reason that line is literal: a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations, and who managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts. That is founder history, not an agency client result. It is also why the constraint section is as long as the schema section. Scale without a locked definition produces a larger argument, not a clearer one.

FAQ
Q: What should a provider expect from an intake attribution framework?
A: Expect a signed definition of a qualified inquiry, a signed definition of the admission stage used in acquisition reporting, one record per inquiry with a locked source, a closed disposition list, and a monthly view that keeps unknown and untracked volume visible. Expect a named owner for intake, for source capture, for the outcome flag, and for the privacy boundary. Do not expect a causal proof, a clinical outcome measure, or a file that is safe to upload to an ad platform by default. The framework works only where consent, retention, and confidentiality rules allow the join.
Q: How should an intake attribution framework be measured against qualified calls or admissions?
A: Measure it with denominators written next to every rate. Qualified-inquiry rate is qualified inquiries divided by inquiries that have a disposition, not divided by every row in a raw call file if some rows were never coded. Inquiry-to-admission rate is outcomes at the signed stage divided by qualified inquiries, inside a window the organization has written down. Cost per qualified admission, when media cost is known, uses that same signed stage as the denominator and is shown beside admissions that have no marketing record. Compare those figures to the call platform’s duration-based "qualified" count only as an exception report. If the two counts match, confirm that someone did not simply copy the duration rule into the CRM.
Q: Does call duration define a qualified call?
A: No. Duration is a staffing and routing signal. A qualified call, in this framework, is a qualified inquiry: right party, right intent, enough information to continue, and a disposition from the closed list. Intake call quality scoring then checks the handoff. A long call with an existing patient is not a new acquisition. A short call that books the right next step can be.
Q: Can admission outcomes be sent back to an ad platform?
A: Only after privacy review, and only as a minimized signal the platform’s current terms allow. Do not send recordings, clinical reasons, diagnosis, or page URLs that reveal a condition. Google’s call-feature terms tell covered entities not to accept a setup that would involve protected health information, and to request exclusion from monitoring and recording if they use those features [Call Ads Supplemental Terms](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16914745?hl=en). Health promotion also cannot use advertiser-curated audiences such as Customer Match or your data segments [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en). A neutral conversion name and a join that stays inside the provider’s systems is the safer default until counsel and the current platform policy say otherwise. Policies change. Check them before a new upload.
Q: Is this the same as a healthcare marketing attribution model?
A: The intake attribution framework is the part of a healthcare marketing attribution model that most teams skip. The model still needs cost, clicks, and a source rule. Without the definitions, the disposition taxonomy, and the locked handoff, the model reports activity. With them, the model can report qualified inquiries and an agreed downstream stage. It still cannot, by itself, prove incrementality.
Q: Who should own the intake handoff?
A: The admissions or intake director owns the disposition and the quality rubric. The coordinator who first handles the inquiry writes the code before any transfer. Marketing owns the source field and the rule that later callbacks do not replace it. Privacy owns whether a recording, a transcript, or an outcome flag may move. Finance confirms the stage used in the spend review. One analyst maintains the join and the exception log. If a vendor’s default "qualified" flag conflicts with the signed definition, the signed definition wins. A completed definition worksheet is the right artifact to bring into a review of source capture, call-to-CRM mapping, and the admission stage your finance team will actually use.
