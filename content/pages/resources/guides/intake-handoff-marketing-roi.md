Intake Optimization for Treatment Centers
Intake optimization for treatment centers is the work of making the path from inquiry to admission visible, owned, and repeatable. Paid media can produce the call or the form. It cannot finish the admission if the handoff drops the context, the owner, or the next step. This guide covers the operating details that decide whether that spend is readable as cost per admission.

The desk that receives the inquiry
Picture the admissions desk at 8:10 a.m. A coordinator has a headset on, a location schedule on one screen, and a queue that already holds a parent who called from a car, a form that arrived overnight, and a missed call from a second campus whose line overflows into the same team. The person on the phone is not shopping for a campaign. They are trying to decide whether this program can take their family member, whether insurance will be checked before they drive across the state, and whether anyone will still be there if they have to call back.
That desk is where marketing ROI is settled. The media invoice is already real. The admission is not. If the coordinator cannot see which location the person asked about, which page they used, or whether a verification request is already open, the conversation restarts. The caller repeats the story. The second telling is shorter, and often more guarded, than the first.
The handoff is an operating event, not a color in a CRM. It has a time, a person, and a record of what was promised next. When those three things are missing, adding budget does not repair the loss. It sends more people into the same undefined transition. A qualified caller who reaches a full census is a capacity fact. A qualified caller who reaches voicemail, then a generic callback two days later, is a handoff fact. Those two outcomes should not share a row labeled "lead."
Operators who run more than one location feel this first. The same campaign can look efficient in the ad account and uneven on the floor, because one campus answers live and another routes after-hours calls into a shared inbox no one owns before noon. The useful comparison is not which ad group had the lower cost per click. It is which location received a complete record, and which location received a phone number with no context.
What the handoff has to carry
A usable handoff is short enough that a coordinator can read it before saying hello, and complete enough that the caller is not asked to rebuild the inquiry. Agree on the fields before you argue about software.
Minimum context for a new inquiry:
* How the person reached you: call, form, chat, or referral, plus the location or service line they selected.
* When the inquiry arrived, and when a person first responded.
* Who owns it now, and who covers if that person is in another call.
* What the caller already provided: name or relationship, preferred callback window, payer name if offered, and the level of care they asked about.
* The next step, written as an action with a time, not as "follow up."
* The current status, using a label admissions and marketing both accept.
Leave clinical detail out of places that do not need it. A group text, a shared spreadsheet on a personal drive, and an ad-platform note field are poor homes for a caller's story. The coordinator needs enough to continue the conversation. The media buyer needs a source and an outcome code. Finance needs a disposition it can reconcile. None of those jobs requires pasting a full intake narrative into a chat channel.
Scripts help when they are a checklist, not a recital. The caller may be the person seeking care, or a spouse, parent, or clinician calling for them. The tone should change. The information standard should not. Every live answer should cover fit for the program you actually offer, how benefits will be checked, what happens if a bed or slot is not open today, and how the person will hear back. A coordinator who improvises those four points will sound warmer on some calls and incomplete on others. Incomplete is what shows up later as "we are not sure what happened to that lead."
Write the handoff rules for the missed call as carefully as for the answered one. A returned call that starts with "I see you asked about our evening program in the city closest to you" is a different conversation from "Can I have your name and what you are looking for?" The first version is only possible if the handoff carried the context forward.
Stage math, not a single rate
One conversion rate hides the leak. Split the path into stages the team can count without a debate: inquiry received, person reached, screened for program fit, benefits checked, scheduled or accepted, and admitted or arrived. Define the exit rule for each stage in one sentence. "Verified" should not mean "we left a message for the payer" in one office and "we have a written benefit summary" in another.
Cost per admission, for a defined pool of spend, is that spend divided by the admissions tied to it. If the denominator rises while the spend stays the same, cost per admission falls. If inquiries rise and admissions do not, cost per inquiry can look better while the admission economics get worse. That relationship is arithmetic. It is not a forecast, and it is not a result from any center.
Use a worksheet with your own numbers, and label it as a worksheet. Suppose a month of defined media cost is $100,000 and the admission log ties 40 admissions to that cost. Cost per admission on that worksheet is $2,500. If the same $100,000 is tied to 50 admissions, the figure is $2,000. The click price did not change. The handoff, the capacity, or the fit of the inquiries did. Do not treat $2,500 or 40 as a benchmark. They exist so a leadership meeting can see that the denominator is an operations number, not a platform column.
Track answer rate and speed to first human contact beside the later stages, without pretending the first metric is the ROI metric. A team can answer every call and still lose people in verification. A team can verify quickly and still have no open slot. Report those separately so the budget conversation does not "fix" a capacity problem by pausing a campaign that was producing appropriate inquiries.
For a multi-location network, keep the stages at location level. A blended admission rate will flatter the campus that is staffed and hide the campus that is not. The founder of Marketing Powered previously ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations, and managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts. At that kind of spread, a single network conversion rate is too coarse to decide where the next dollar goes.
Finance should see the same admission once. If marketing counts a scheduled arrival and admissions counts a completed admission, publish both, and do not add them. Contribution, payer mix, and length of stay belong to finance. Marketing's job in the meeting is to show which sources reached which stage, and where the record stopped.
A morning on the intake line
Follow one inquiry through a ordinary Tuesday, because the failures are sequential.
At 7:42 a.m. a parent calls a number tied to a specific campus page. The call rings to a coordinator who can see the location, the time, and any form fields already submitted. The parent is driving. The coordinator confirms the program the parent asked about, says whether that campus offers it, and books a benefits check rather than offering a vague "someone will call you." The record now has an owner and a next time.
At 8:05 the verification specialist opens that record, not a forwarded voicemail. If the payer portal is slow, the status stays "verification in progress," with the time the request started. The parent is not left to assume silence means rejection. If your team cannot return a benefit summary inside the window you promised, say so on the first call and give a real window. Callers compare your silence with the next center that answers.
At 9:30 a clinician, or a trained screener working from clinical criteria your medical leadership approved, reviews fit. This is not a media task. Marketing should not rewrite clinical inclusion rules to make a campaign look fuller. The handoff into this step needs the caller's stated need and the program they were offered, so the screener is not starting from a blank note.
At 10:15 the campus confirms whether a bed or an outpatient slot exists. If it does not, the status is "no capacity," with the date a seat might open, or "referred out," with the reason. Those are successful handoffs even when they are not admissions. Counting them as failures teaches the media account to avoid appropriate demand you cannot serve.
At 11:00 the coordinator calls back with the next concrete step: arrival window, what to bring, or the time of a clinical assessment. The parent can repeat the plan. That repetition is the quality check. If they cannot, the handoff was not finished.
After lunch, a supervisor listens to two recorded calls from the morning, under whatever consent and access rules your compliance lead has set. The review is specific: Did the coordinator name the next step? Did anyone promise a clinical result? Did the record match what was said? Coaching from a real call beats a slide about "being empathetic."
After hours, the same path has to exist in shorter form. A night voicemail with no owner until the next business morning is a designed loss, especially when the person called because the situation at home changed that evening. Staff the return, or narrow the hours you invite calls, and match the ad schedule to the hours a person actually answers. Inviting demand you will not meet is an intake decision, not a bidding decision.
Rules that change follow-up
Follow-up is where intake optimization and advertising policy meet. The constraint is not a reason to stop measuring. It is a reason to choose channels and data flows that can survive a review.
Google treats content related to health as a sensitive interest category. The category includes physical or mental health conditions, and Google's examples include counseling for mental health issues such as depression, anxiety, and addiction, including alcohol addiction. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) When you promote in that category, Google's personalized advertising rules say you cannot use advertiser-curated audiences, because those audiences may contain sensitive user signals. The disallowed set includes Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments. Predefined Google audiences can still be used, because Google excludes sensitive signals from them. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) "Your data segments" is the list type operators often mean when they say remarketing. Building a recovery plan around following people who visited a condition or treatment page is the wrong plan for this vertical. Marketing Powered's work in this category is built for that limit: retargeting is not permitted, and the acquisition path has to stand on search intent, location, and the intake record rather than on a trail of the same person across the web.
There is a further limit on custom segments. If the creative or the landing page is sensitive, Google says those custom segment audiences serve only in limited Display situations, to non-sensitive audiences or contextually, and other campaign types using that combination are not eligible to serve. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Read the live policy against the specific campaign before you treat a warning as either harmless or fatal. Account structure, destination, and creative all matter.
For recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, Google restricts promotion and, in the United States, requires certification by LegitScript's addiction treatment certification program plus Google's own healthcare certification. Google states that not every drug and alcohol addiction service is eligible, and that services outside that scope, such as impulse-control disorders, behavioral addiction, or nicotine addiction, are not covered by that section of the policy. [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) Certification is a condition of advertising those services. It is not a substitute for an accurate landing page or a careful intake script. The founder is a court-certified expert witness in advertising strategy, which is relevant to how these rules get argued. It is not a substitute for your counsel's read of your own ads, sites, and licenses.
Privacy rules shape the same handoff from the other side. The HIPAA Privacy Rule defines marketing, in general, as a communication about a product or service that encourages the recipient to purchase or use it, and it requires individual authorization before protected health information is used or disclosed for marketing, with limited exceptions. [[3]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html) Communications for treatment, and certain communications about the covered entity's own health-related services, are treated differently from a paid push of someone else's product. Do not collapse "we are calling this person back about the assessment they requested" into "we may upload this list anywhere." If a third party is paid, directly or indirectly, so that it can market its own service using protected health information, HHS describes that arrangement as marketing that requires authorization, with no exception in that part of the definition. [[3]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html)
Online tracking has its own bulletin, and part of it has been narrowed by a court. HHS has said regulated entities may not use tracking technologies in a way that impermissibly discloses protected health information, and that disclosing protected health information to a tracking vendor for marketing without a valid authorization is an impermissible disclosure. [[4]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html) On June 20, 2024, a federal district court vacated the bulletin to the extent it said HIPAA obligations are triggered merely by connecting an IP address with a visit to an unauthenticated public webpage about specific health conditions or providers. HHS noted that it was evaluating next steps. [[4]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html) That order is not a reason to pipe appointment details, call recordings, or portal activity into an advertising pixel. On user-authenticated pages, HHS says tracking technologies generally have access to protected health information. If a person books an appointment on a covered clinic's site and that site sends appointment information plus an IP address to a vendor, HHS's bulletin treats that vendor as a business associate and expects a business associate agreement. [[4]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html) A cookie banner is not a HIPAA authorization.
A business associate, in HHS's terms, is a person or entity that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits protected health information for certain functions or services on behalf of a covered entity. [[5]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html) If a call-tracking, transcription, texting, or hosting vendor does that work, the agreement has to be in place before the data moves, and the agreement cannot authorize a use the Privacy Rule would not allow the covered entity to make. Marketing Powered is HIPAA-aware in how this work is scoped. That is not a certification, and it does not make a vendor relationship compliant by itself. Where model-assisted summaries are used, ask where the audio and the notes are processed and who can retrieve them. Marketing Powered maintains owned, local AI infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference, and has been AI-native since 2022. That infrastructure is for control and deployment choice. It does not mean every workload stays on it, and client data ownership is separate from any hardware the firm operates.
On the call itself, train for plain limits. Describe the program, the steps, and the logistics. Do not promise a clinical result, a cure, or a ranking against another program. Those sentences create both a trust problem and a compliance problem, and they are unnecessary for a person who needs to know what happens next.
Lead counts beside admission records
Put two documents on the table in the weekly meeting. On the left, the platform report: impressions, clicks, calls, forms, cost. On the right, the admission log: the same week, with a source code, a location, a stage, and a disposition. The people in the room should include whoever buys the media, whoever runs the desk, and whoever will sign the revenue number. If those three cannot point to the same inquiry, you do not have an ROI figure yet. You have two exports.
Read them as a comparison, not as a verdict on the agency or the coordinators. A row that exists only on the left is an inquiry the floor never absorbed, or a call the platform counted and the CRM never opened. A row that exists only on the right may be a referral, a walk-in, or a direct call that never touched the tracked number. Both gaps are useful. Mixing them into one "conversion" column is how a budget review rewards the loudest spreadsheet.
A working disposition list, which you should edit to match your programs, is enough to start: reached, not reached, not a fit, verification pending, verification complete, scheduled, admitted, no capacity, referred out, declined, duplicate. Publish the list. Retire labels nobody uses. Credit each admission once, even if three campaigns touched the household. Assisted influence can be noted. It should not create a second admission in the cost math.
The meeting question then changes. Instead of "Which campaign had the cheapest lead?" it becomes "Which source produced people we could reach, screen, and admit, at a cost finance will stand behind, given the slots we actually had?" That is the commercial use of attribution tracked to admission, not just to the lead. It will make some efficient-looking campaigns look expensive, and some expensive-looking campaigns look appropriate, once no-capacity and not-a-fit are visible.
The same comparison travels. A hospital service line may end at a completed visit rather than a residential admission. A professional-services firm may end at a retained matter. The labels change. The discipline does not: one definition of done, one owner after the inquiry, and a record finance can reconcile without rebuilding it from call recordings.
Questions operators ask
### What should marketing hand to the desk with every inquiry?
Source, location or service line, arrival time, and whatever the person already submitted, plus a named owner. The desk should not need the ad account login to know why the person called. The media team should not need the clinical note to know the source and the ending status.
### Why is cost per inquiry a weak read on marketing ROI?
It stops before the handoff. Two sources can share a cost per inquiry and diverge at answer rate, verification, or admission. Cost per admission, using spend and admissions you have actually tied together, tells you whether the path held. It still needs a capacity note, or you will punish a source for a full census.
### Can we build an audience from people who visited a treatment page?
Not as an advertiser-curated audience if the ads and pages fall in Google's health category. Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments are not available for that promotion. [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) Follow-up by phone or another channel you control is a different question, and it still has to respect authorization rules if protected health information is involved. [[3]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html)
### When does a call-tracking vendor need a business associate agreement?
When the vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits protected health information for the center. [[5]](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html) A recording or transcript in which a caller gives a name and the care they are seeking is the usual case, not a rare one. Confirm the agreement, the access rules, and what is stripped from any export before the first tracked patient call.
### What should the weekly report contain if we only fix one thing?
A stage table by location and source: received, reached, screened, verified, admitted, and the non-admission dispositions. Pair it with spend for the same dates. Leave creative tests and keyword trivia out until that table is stable enough that a missed status is obvious.
If you want that stage table built from the inquiries you already pay for, start with a review of the handoff rather than another line of spend.
