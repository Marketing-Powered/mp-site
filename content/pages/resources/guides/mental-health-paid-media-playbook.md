Mental Health Paid Media: The Complete Guide for Marketing Leaders
Most underperformance in mental health paid media is misdiagnosed. Teams blame the algorithm, or treat every healthcare rule as one fog, for ceilings that are often an attribution gap or an account built as if a retargeting pool would sort the traffic later. This guide separates what Google and Meta currently fix by policy from the structure, measurement, and creative decisions a marketing leader can still change.

What Google and Meta Actually Restrict
Picture a Tuesday review that many marketing directors inherit rather than design. A media lead has a disapproval notice open. An admissions director wants to know why last month's inquiry volume did not show up as admissions. Someone asks whether the empty remarketing list is a bug. It is usually not a bug. It is two different rule sets, and they do not fail in the same way.
On Google, health is a sensitive interest category under the personalized advertising policy. The category covers physical or mental health conditions, including diseases, sexual health, and chronic conditions that need long-term care. It covers products, services, or procedures used to treat or manage those conditions. It covers intimate health issues, invasive procedures, and disabilities, including content aimed at a caregiver. Google's own examples include counseling for mental health issues such as depression, anxiety, and addiction, including gambling addiction and alcohol addiction. Read the current wording on [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) before you treat any sentence in this guide as the live rule. Policies change, and the English help page is the version Google uses to enforce them.
When a promotion falls in that category, Google draws a hard line on audiences. You can use predefined Google audiences. You cannot use advertiser-curated audiences, because those lists may contain sensitive user signals. The prohibited column includes Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments. The allowed column includes in-market segments, affinity, demographics with stated exceptions, detailed demographics with exceptions, life events, location targeting, and custom segments, again with a stated limit. Users under 18 are not eligible for personalized advertising of any kind. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
That is the retargeting constraint in operational language. A remarketing list built from people who visited a depression, addiction, or psychiatry page is an advertiser-curated audience. Customer Match built from an intake export is the same class of object. Marketing Powered builds for this vertical on that premise: standard retargeting is not available, and the account has to work without it. Search keywords are a different mechanism. They match a query the person typed. They are not a substitute label for a prohibited list, and they are not banned by the audience table.
Custom segments are the detail teams miss. Google says custom segment audiences that use sensitive creative assets, or that point to sensitive landing pages, will only serve with Display campaigns to non-sensitive audiences or contextually. Other campaign types using those custom segments with sensitive creative or landing pages will not be eligible to serve. A custom segment is not a quiet way to rebuild a treatment-page audience. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
If an ad is flagged, Google lists three fixes, not a hidden fourth path. Remove advertiser-curated audiences from the ad, the ad group, the asset group, and the campaign. Or edit the site, app, or ad so the content no longer falls in the policy. Or appeal if you believe the classification is wrong. Repeated violations that you do not fix can put the account at risk of suspension. The practical response is to remove the audience object, not to rename it. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
Meta does not use the same switch. Housing, employment, and financial products and services sit in Special Ad Categories, with limited audience tools. Mental health treatment is not that category. Copying a housing-ad setup will not solve a therapy account, and it will not explain a disapproval. Meta's personal-attributes standard is the rule that shapes ordinary mental health creative: ads must not assert or imply a person's race, ethnicity, religion, disability, or physical or mental health, including medical conditions. The standard also covers implying those attributes for the person's family. [transparency.meta.com](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes)
Drug and alcohol addiction treatment on Meta is a separate gate. Advertisers who want to run those ads to people in the United States must be certified with LegitScript and must have written permission from Meta. Meta's scope includes clinical treatment and sites that describe in-person treatment, online and in-person support groups, crisis hotlines, and online treatment. Physical rehabilitation, such as kinesiology, is called out as a type of ad that can run without that permission. Confirm the live text at Meta's [Drug and Alcohol Addiction Treatment](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drug-alcohol-addiction-treatment/) standard before you brief a campaign.
Hold the two platforms apart in the briefing document. Google's sensitive-interest rule removes advertiser-curated audiences from health promotions, including counseling for depression, anxiety, and addiction. Meta's personal-attributes rule restricts what the ad may say about the person seeing it. Meta's addiction-treatment rule, for U.S. targeting, is a certification and permission problem. None of those rules is a reason to stop measuring admissions. None of them is fixed by a new bid strategy.
LegitScript and Account Eligibility
Certification is an eligibility gate. It is not a quality score, and it is not proof that the campaigns are pointed at admissions. Treat it as a fact about which domains, facilities, and service lines are even allowed to enter the auction.
Google restricts promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Approved advertisers can promote those services only in listed locations, and the country table decides who can be approved. The overview page names Australia, Canada, France, Ireland, New Zealand, and the United States. Services unrelated to drug and alcohol addiction, including impulse control disorders, behavioral addiction, and nicotine addiction, are outside that policy. A violation of the addiction-services policy does not trigger an immediate suspension without warning. Google states it will issue a warning at least 7 days before suspending the account. That warning is not a grace period for scaling uncertified ads. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en)
The country rows are the part operators skip. For the United States, Google requires certification as an addiction services provider by the LegitScript Certification program, and a separate certification with Google. Google states that not all drug and alcohol addiction services are eligible for LegitScript certification, and that services which are not eligible are not allowed to advertise those services on Google. For Canada, the requirement is LegitScript's Addiction Treatment Certification program, plus Google certification. For Australia, France, Ireland, and New Zealand, Google allows promotion of those recovery-oriented services by government entities only, and those advertisers must also be certified with Google. A U.S. playbook copied into another country is a policy error, not a localization task. Check the live country table on [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en).
LegitScript describes its Addiction Treatment Certification as the certification and monitoring program recognized by Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Nextdoor to vet addiction treatment advertisers. On its certification page, organizations that may fit include licensed addiction treatment in the United States or Canada, in person or remote, covering clinical services such as detox, residential or outpatient treatment, counseling, or medication-assisted treatment, including co-occurring programs. Mutual support groups and crisis hotlines that do not promote specific treatment providers are described separately. Organizations that may not fit include lead generators, call centers, or marketers who refer people to other providers for compensation, and sober living or recovery residences without licensed clinical services. Read the current eligibility notes on [LegitScript's certification page](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/) rather than relying on a vendor summary. A fuller walkthrough of the application sequence sits in the [LegitScript certification guide](/resources/legitscript-certification-guide/). [legitscript.com](https://legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
The standards change how the account is built, which is the point marketing leaders miss when they treat certification as a PDF in a shared drive.
Licensure and facility disclosure come first. The applicant must be licensed for the services offered in the jurisdictions served, and must be able to document ownership, lease, or permission to use the locations where services are offered. Every physical property and corporate entity under the applicant's control, and entities it refers participants to, must be disclosed. Properties that themselves provide addiction treatment services must meet the standards. A campaign that sends "near me" traffic to a sister location that was never disclosed is not a routing shortcut. It is a certification problem. [legitscript.com](https://legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
Website and advertising standards come next, and they are media standards. The site must conspicuously show the physical address where services are rendered and the specific nature of those services. Photographs must depict the applicant's facilities. Content must not misrepresent scope or location. Advertisements must identify the advertiser, must not use a third-party business name, must reflect the services available at a given location, and may not claim a location where services are not provided. If paid search is geo-modified for a city where the organization has no clinical service, the ad and the page are both in scope of that standard. [legitscript.com](https://legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
Commercial structure is in scope too. Co-ownership of, or a formal or informal affiliation with, a lead generator is prohibited and may be grounds for denial. LegitScript's examples of informal affiliation include sponsoring or answering the hotline for a lead generator. Incentives such as paying for travel are generally not eligible, and other incentives, including paying rent or health insurance premiums during treatment, are called out as problematic. Affiliates and partners have to meet the program standards, and the applicant must provide every domain it controls. An agency landing-page domain that is not on that list is not a neutral tracking choice. [legitscript.com](https://legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
There is a sequencing rule that should sit in the launch checklist. LegitScript states that applicants seen advertising on Google, Bing, or Facebook without certification, or in apparent violation of a platform's terms, may be treated as trying to circumvent the program, and that such advertising may be grounds for denial. Turning ads on "while the application is in review" can damage the application itself. [legitscript.com](https://legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
Build the account from the eligible entity outward. One brand does not equal one applicant. A hospital adding a substance-use service line, a multi-site network, and a telehealth program can be three different eligibility facts. Separate campaigns, domains, and conversion actions by the entity and the service that the certification actually covers. General mental health services are not automatically inside the addiction-services policy. Do not import addiction-treatment keywords into a psychiatry campaign to "test demand," and do not import a certified treatment domain into an uncertified sister brand. Marketing Powered is LegitScript-aware in that operational sense: certification status decides what is eligible to run, and the account structure should show that decision without a scavenger hunt.
The Attribution Mistake Behind the Ceiling
Platform policy explains disapprovals and missing audiences. It does not explain a dashboard that looks busy while the census does not move. That second failure is usually a definition problem, and it is fixable.
A lead, in most mental health accounts, is whatever the ad platform was told to count. A form submit. A click-to-call. A call longer than a threshold someone set two years ago. An admission is a later operational event: a person accepted into a level of care, at a facility that can provide it, on a date admissions will stand behind. Those are not two views of the same number. They are different events, often recorded in different systems, by different teams, with different incentives. When the bid strategy optimizes to the first event, it will find more of the first event. It has no reason to prefer the second unless you teach it, carefully and only with data you are authorized to share.
The mask works like this. Campaign A produces many short calls and duplicate forms. Campaign B produces fewer contacts, and a larger share of those contacts become scheduled assessments and then admissions. If the only shared report is cost per platform conversion, Campaign A wins the weekly meeting. Budget moves toward A. B looks expensive. Six weeks later, admissions asks why the paid channel is louder and less useful. Nothing in Google's audience policy caused that. The conversion action did.
Connecting spend to admission is an operations project, not a column in a spreadsheet.
Start with a written definition that admissions and marketing both sign. What counts as an inquiry. What counts as a qualified inquiry for that service line. What counts as a scheduled assessment. What counts as an admission, and at which level of care. If two facilities use the word "admit" for different moments, stop comparing them until the word means one thing. A hospital service line and a residential program should not share a conversion name if the downstream event is different.
Then attach the source to the contact without putting clinical detail in the advertising system. Dynamic numbers or a controlled call-tracking pool can tie a call to a campaign, ad group, and landing page. A form can carry a click identifier or a first-party campaign parameter. The identifier that joins to the record of care should not be a diagnosis, a medication, or a free-text clinical note. The conversion name imported back to the ad platform should say "admission" or "scheduled assessment," not the condition. URL parameters should not encode the condition either. If the only way to get a clean join is to paste chart text into a public analytics property, do not build that join.
The import itself has a boundary. Offline conversions are useful when the organization has decided which events it is authorized to send, who approves the feed, and how long the identifier remains available. They are not useful as a silent copy of the electronic health record. Marketing Powered tracks attribution to admission, not just lead, where authorized data supports that join. The work is the join and the definition, not a promise about what the next quarter's census will do.
Report three numbers side by side, and refuse to manage the channel on only one of them. Platform conversions, qualified inquiries as intake defines them, and admissions or scheduled assessments by facility and service line. Cost per platform conversion can stay on the media tab. It should not be the number that moves the budget if admissions data exists. Where admissions data does not exist yet, say so in the report. A gap is a better management fact than a substituted proxy that everyone pretends is an admission.
This is also why "the algorithm" is a weak diagnosis. Automated bidding will hunt the conversion you give it. If that conversion is a 30-second call, you will buy 30-second calls. If the conversion is an authorized admission event with enough volume to learn, the bid can move toward that event, still inside the audience limits above. Low volume is a real constraint. Inventing a target cost per admission to force a strategy is not a measurement system. Wait until the definition is stable, then decide whether the volume supports automated bidding or whether manual control is the honest setting.
The commercial argument for the extra work is capacity, not a prettier chart. Intake can only return so many calls. A campaign that fills the queue with mismatches consumes the same staff as a campaign that fills it with people the facility can assess. [Analytics and attribution](/services/analytics-and-attribution/) is the layer that makes that difference visible. Without it, every later debate about creative and structure is a debate about a number nobody in admissions recognizes.
Account Structure Without Retargeting
In an unrestricted account, sloppy structure has a safety net. Someone visits, does not call, and a remarketing pool sorts them later by page, by video view, or by a customer list. In this category, that net is not there. The first session has to carry the segmentation that the pool used to carry. That is a design constraint, not a temporary outage.
The workable unit is intent, not a single "mental health" campaign with every keyword in it. Split by the decision the searcher is already making, because you will not get a second paid touch that re-segments them after they leave.
Service line is the first split. Outpatient therapy, psychiatry and medication management, intensive outpatient or partial hospitalization, residential or inpatient addiction treatment, and a hospital behavioral unit are not one ad group with shared ads. They imply different licensure, different pages, different intake scripts, and often different eligibility to advertise at all. A query about a therapist near a suburb should not enter an auction against a query about medical detox unless the same facility, the same page, and the same certified entity can honestly answer both.
Geography is the second split, and it has to be real. Location targeting is on Google's allowed side of the personalized-advertising table. That does not mean every radius is a strategy. A radius that crosses into a city where you have no staff, no beds, and no disclosed facility will buy calls you cannot accept, and it can conflict with the location-accuracy standard described above. For a multi-site provider, city or facility campaigns with controlled overlap beat one national campaign that lets the system decide which phone number appears.
Query theme is the third split, and it replaces the story sequence people try to run in remarketing. Brand queries, condition-plus-local queries, level-of-care queries, insurance or payment queries, and family-member queries ("help for my son," "program for my spouse") can each have their own ads and pages. You are not following the person from article to article. You are answering the query in front of you with the page that matches it. If the page is the homepage for every theme, you have rebuilt the problem the remarketing pool used to hide.
Negatives are the exclusion tool you still have. Employment queries, training and licensure queries, free crisis resources you do not operate, drug names you are not allowed to bid on, and locations you do not serve should be excluded on purpose, before launch, and reviewed from the search-term report on a set cadence. This is not a trick to recover a prohibited audience. It is how you stop paying for queries the intake team will correctly reject.
What you should not do is smuggle the old audience model back in through a new campaign type. Performance Max and Demand Gen still sit under the same personalized-advertising rules. Audience expansion, lookalikes, Customer Match, and your data segments remain on the prohibited side when the promotion is in the sensitive category. Asset-group signals do not restore a treatment-page remarketing list. If a campaign type cannot run without those objects, it should not be the core of the account. Search, with explicit keywords, locations, and pages, remains the control surface. Where predefined Google audiences are eligible, they can support non-search formats, with the custom-segment limit kept in view. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
A practical structure for a regional provider often looks like a set of portfolios, not a single tree:
* One portfolio per eligible legal entity and domain, so an uncertified page cannot inherit a certified campaign by accident.
* Inside it, campaigns by facility or market, with budgets tied to that site's intake capacity rather than to a brand-level ambition.
* Inside those, ad groups by service and query theme, each with a matching landing page that states the service, the address, and how to reach intake.
* A shared negative list for jobs, education, and out-of-scope substances, plus market-level negatives for cities you do not cover.
* Brand campaigns separated from non-brand, so a rise in brand searches is not mistaken for new demand created by non-brand spend.
Bidding follows the conversion you can defend. If the only stable event is a qualified call, bid to that, and keep admissions in a parallel report until the offline event is clean enough to import. Do not point a new bid strategy at a conversion action that still includes wrong numbers, staff testing the form, and spam. Cleaning the action is the strategy.
The people who feel this change are the media buyer and the intake lead, sitting in the same room with the search-term report and last week's call dispositions. The buyer wants another ad group. Intake wants fewer calls that start with "do you take my plan, and are you actually in this city?" That conversation is the account structure. It is slower than launching one campaign and a remarketing tag. It is also the structure that still works after the tag is correctly removed.
Multi-Location and Multi-Facility Design
Scaling paid media across sites is a routing problem wearing a media costume. The founder of Marketing Powered is a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations, and who managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts. That is the founder's operating history, not an agency client result, and it is not a template you can paste onto a health system. It does say what breaks when the location count leaves the range where one person can keep every phone number in their head.
At a handful of sites, a shared brand campaign can look efficient. One budget, one set of ads, one phone number, a page that lists "locations" in a footer. The inefficiency is hidden in the call. The person reached a number. Intake then discovers the caller is two hours from the only campus with the needed level of care, or that the nearby campus does not take the payer, or that the nearby campus is a sober living residence that cannot advertise the clinical service the ad described. The platform records a conversion. The campus records a turnaway. Multiply that by two dozen sites and the brand average becomes a story nobody at a single facility recognizes.
Facility-level measurement is the correction. Each location that can admit, or that can complete the appointment the ad offered, needs its own landing page, its own tracking number or number pool, and its own line in the weekly report. The page states the address, the services at that address, and the hours a person will actually answer. The report shows inquiries, qualified inquiries, and admissions or scheduled assessments for that site, next to the spend that site consumed. A network total can sit above those rows. It should not replace them.
Cannibalization is the second failure, and it is easy to create with good intentions. Two facilities in the same metro bid on the same city-modified keywords, in the same account, with overlapping radii. They raise each other's cost, they split the impression, and the caller gets whichever ad rank won, not whichever site had a bed or an opening. The fix is an ownership rule. One facility owns a city, or the campaigns use location targeting and exclusions so the overlap is deliberate and small. Shared brand terms can stay central. Non-brand local terms should have an owner. When a site is full, its budget pauses or shifts. Leaving it live because "the account needs volume" buys calls the site will not accept.
The same pattern shows up when a hospital adds a behavioral health service line to an existing system account. Orthopedics, imaging, and primary care may still have remarketing options the behavioral line does not. If those campaigns share asset groups, audience lists, or a single landing domain that mixes condition pages with general hospital pages, the sensitive-interest rules can flag more than the new service line. Keep the behavioral campaigns, pages, and conversion actions distinct. Route the local query to the campus that offers that service, not to the flagship URL because it has the stronger domain rating. [Hospital PPC](/services/hospital-ppc/) and [healthcare lead generation for multi-location providers](/services/healthcare-lead-generation/) are the adjacent problems: capacity, routing, and a disposition by site.
Certification multiplies the issue for addiction treatment. LegitScript's facility and advertising standards require disclosure of the locations where services are rendered, and they prohibit claims of locations where services are not provided. A location page for a campus that only offers outpatient therapy cannot wear a residential headline because the residential campus is in another state. A tracking number that forwards to a central call center is acceptable only if the call center can place the caller at the location the ad named, and if that arrangement does not create the lead-generator affiliation the certification standard prohibits. Write the forwarding rule down. If the center's job is to sell the caller to whichever facility pays, you are no longer looking at a call-routing detail. [legitscript.com](https://legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
A workable multi-site design has a short list of artifacts, owned by name:
* A location record that operations accepts as current: name, address, phone, services, hours, payers if you state them, and whether the site is on the certification disclosure.
* A page per location that uses that record and does not invent services for symmetry.
* A campaign or budget owner per market, with a rule for what happens when the schedule is full.
* A disposition code intake will actually use, including "wrong location" and "service not offered here," so those calls do not remain inside a blended conversion.
* A monthly read of search terms by market, looking for cities and services the location record does not support.
The founder is also a court-certified expert witness in advertising strategy. That credential is relevant to how claims, locations, and offers get argued, not to a performance figure. The operating lesson from a 3-to-24 footprint is narrower and more useful: local intent has to be captured locally, measured locally, and refused when the location cannot deliver the service the query asked for. Brand averages hide that refusal until the census, or an auditor, makes it obvious.
Creative and Messaging Within the Rules
Creative in this category is a constraint problem with a commercial cost. The ad has to be specific enough to earn the right click, and plain enough that it does not assert a diagnosis about the person reading it, invent an outcome, or describe a service the page cannot deliver.
Meta publishes the distinction in examples, which is more useful than a paraphrase. Under the personal-attributes standard, "Bulimia counseling available," "Depression counseling," and "New diabetes treatment available" are shown as acceptable. "Do you have diabetes?", "Depression getting you down? Get help now.", and a line telling someone not to wait to get a spouse treated for cancer are shown as violations. The difference is the subject of the sentence. Name the service. Do not tell the viewer, or their family, that you know their condition. A question that diagnoses the reader is the pattern to remove, even when a consumer copy test would say the question "performs." Confirm the examples on Meta's [Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes) page, because example sets move.
That standard still leaves room for useful ads. Access, process, and facts convert without implying you have read someone's chart. Licensed clinicians. A stated level of care. The city. Whether new assessments are being scheduled. Evening hours, if they exist. Insurance or self-pay, if the statement is true for that location and you are willing to have intake repeat it. What the first call covers, in ordinary language. These are the details a person needs before they will dial, and they are also the details that separate two providers who are otherwise bidding on the same query.
Avoid the motivational register. "You do not have to do this alone" is common because it feels kind. It also says nothing a competing ad cannot say, and it slides toward implying the reader's state. Prefer the operational sentence: who you are, what service this page is for, where it is delivered, and how to reach a person. If a family member is the searcher, let the query carry that fact. An ad group for family queries can speak to someone seeking care for another person, as a description of the service, without asserting that the viewer's household has a diagnosis.
Outcome language is where regulated copy usually breaks. Do not promise recovery, a timeline, or a result. Do not let a testimonial on the landing page do that work if you would not put the same sentence in the ad. Google reviews the destination with the ad. A compliant headline sitting on a page of before-and-after claims, undisclosed stock photography, or a list of cities you do not serve will fail a review that the headline alone would have passed. LegitScript's advertising standard points the same direction for certified addiction treatment advertisers: accurate, identified, and limited to services at the location named. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) [legitscript.com](https://legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
Write to the query theme, then stop. A residential ad should not borrow outpatient language to widen the net. A therapy ad should not borrow detox language because the cost per click looked lower in an old account. The click you do not want is more expensive than the click you lose. Landing pages should match in the first screen: service, place, and a way to call or request an assessment. A form that asks for clinical history before anyone has explained the next step will suppress the very contacts you are trying to measure, and it creates a data-handling problem the next section covers.
Test message, not diagnosis. Practical versus warmer tone is a fair test if both versions stay inside the personal-attributes examples and neither version invents a result. Test the offer you can fulfill this week, such as an assessment window, against a generic "learn more." Judge the test on qualified inquiries and downstream admissions, not on click-through rate alone. A higher click-through rate on a line that implies the reader's condition is not a win. It is a policy incident with a good interim metric.
Creative production needs a reviewer who is allowed to say no. The reviewer checks three things before an ad is uploaded: the sentence does not assign a condition to the reader or their family; the service and location match the page and the certification record; the page does not add claims the ad avoided. That review is shorter than an appeal.
Privacy-Aware Measurement Infrastructure
Measurement in this vertical fails in two directions. Too little join, and you manage to leads. Too much clinical detail in the advertising and analytics stack, and you have created a privacy problem in order to solve a reporting problem. The design goal is a join that admissions recognizes, built from the minimum data the advertising platforms need.
Decide what is allowed to leave the clinical environment before you pick a tool. A click identifier, a campaign name, a location code, a call duration, and a disposition such as "scheduled" or "admitted" can be enough. A diagnosis, a note, a medication list, or a recording of a clinical assessment is not a media input. If a vendor's default setup stores full call recordings in a shared inbox that also syncs to an ad platform, that default is the wrong setup, even if the dashboard looks complete. Marketing Powered is HIPAA-aware in this limited, operational sense. That is not a certification claim, and it is not a statement that a workflow is compliant because a vendor used the word.
URLs and conversion names are the leaks teams stop noticing. A landing-page path that includes the condition is readable in analytics, in referrer logs, and sometimes in the ad platform's page report. A conversion action named for a specific disorder does the same job in a place more people can export. Use neutral names. Keep the condition on the page content, where it belongs for the reader and for policy review, not in the tracking label.
Call handling needs an owner. Who hears the recording. How long it is kept. Whether a transcription job sends audio to a third party. Which dispositions are written back, and by whom. A weekly sample of calls, reviewed by intake and marketing together, will tell you whether the conversion action still means what the definition says. It will also tell you whether ads are buying the wrong city or the wrong level of care, which no platform column will phrase that clearly.
First-party data is the join key you can govern. The click or call arrives with a source. Intake records a disposition in the system it already uses. A controlled export, approved for that purpose, matches the two on an identifier that is not a clinical fact. Where that match is authorized, admissions can be imported as offline conversions. Where it is not, keep the admissions report beside the media report and manage to both. Do not close the gap by pasting protected information into a shared sheet. [First-party measurement](/resources/first-party-data-strategy/) is this chain, not a newsletter program.
Owned infrastructure matters at the point where someone wants a model to read intake notes, call transcripts, or creative tied to a condition. Marketing Powered has been AI-native since 2022 and uses owned, local AI infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference. Which workloads run there depends on the engagement and on the client's data-handling requirements. Owning the infrastructure does not transfer ownership of client data, and it does not mean every campaign or every prompt stays on that hardware. The business reason to care is control: a person can decide that clinical-adjacent text is not sent to a consumer cloud model in order to draft an ad or summarize a call. A person still approves anything that will be published or that will change spend.
Put the measurement review on a calendar, in a room, with the artifacts visible. One screen shows platform conversions by campaign and location. One screen shows intake dispositions for the same period. A third note lists what was not joined, and why. The meeting ends with a decision: shift budget, fix a page, fix a routing rule, or leave the campaign alone because the admissions line does not support a change. A dashboard without that decision is decoration.
What to Audit in the Account
Run this as a quarterly working session, not as a slide. You need the ad account, the certification record if addiction treatment is in scope, the location list operations trusts, the last month of search terms, and a disposition export from intake. Two people should sit in it: whoever changes campaigns, and whoever can say whether a call became an assessment or an admission. A compliance or privacy reviewer joins if recordings, imports, or new pages are in scope.
Separate the findings into fixed and fixable before you recommend a tactic. Fixed means a current platform or certification rule. You design around it. Fixable means a choice in the account, the page, the definition, or the handoff. You change it. Mixing the two produces the usual bad plan: a request to "get remarketing turned back on," followed by a bid-strategy change that optimizes the same polluted conversion.
Policy and eligibility, first.
* Open [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) and confirm whether your ads and destinations still fall in the health category. Then check every campaign, ad group, and asset group for Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, lookalikes, and custom segments pointed at sensitive pages. Remove what the current table prohibits.
* If you promote recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, open the country row that matches your targets on [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en). Confirm LegitScript status where it is required, Google certification, and that every domain and facility in the account is inside that status. In the United States and Canada those are different sentences. In several other countries the row is limited to government entities.
* For Meta, read the personal-attributes examples against live ads, and, for U.S. drug and alcohol addiction treatment, confirm both LegitScript certification and written permission before anything is active. [transparency.meta.com](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drug-alcohol-addiction-treatment/) [transparency.meta.com](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes)
* List disapprovals and limited-serving ads. Note whether the fix is an audience removal, a copy edit, or an eligibility gap. Do not appeal a flag you have not read.
Structure and local truth, second.
* Map each live ad group to a service, a location, and a page. Anything that points at the homepage, or at a location where the service is not offered, is a finding.
* Read search terms for jobs, education, free crisis resources, substances you do not treat, and cities you do not serve. Add negatives. Pause geo targets that intake marks as wrong-location.
* Compare radii and city bids across facilities. Where two sites compete for the same query, assign an owner or document the overlap.
* Check that phone numbers on ads match the location record, and that after-hours calls have a path a person will answer. Spend during hours intake cannot cover is a capacity finding, not a media finding.
Measurement, third.
* Write the current conversion actions in one column and the admissions definition in the next. If they are not the same event, say which one the bid strategy uses.
* Confirm that conversion names, URLs, and imported fields do not carry clinical detail.
* Sample calls or forms against the disposition list. Count wrong numbers, duplicates, and mismatches. If that share is large, do not change bids until the action is cleaner.
* If an offline import exists, name the approver and the event. If it does not exist, keep the side-by-side report and do not pretend the platform number is an admission.
The output of the session is a short list: what is prohibited and will stay prohibited, what will be changed this month, who owns the change, and which number you will read next month to see whether the change mattered. That list is more useful than a score. [Paid media management](/services/paid-media/) is the ongoing version of this review, built around the same split between policy limits and account choices. The founder history above is context for why the review includes facilities, certification, and admissions, not only keywords.

FAQ
Q: Does every mental health advertiser need LegitScript?
A: No. Google's addiction-services policy covers recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, not every counseling or psychiatry offer. In the United States, advertisers of those addiction services must be certified by LegitScript's program and certified with Google. Services that are not eligible for that certification are not allowed to advertise those services. Canada has its own LegitScript requirement plus Google certification. Other listed countries limit promotion to government entities. General mental health promotions can still fall under the health sensitive-interest rules even when LegitScript is not the gate. Read both policies for the service you actually sell. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
Q: Can we rebuild remarketing if we collect the audience on a general page?
A: Do not plan on it. Google's fix for a sensitive-interest flag is to remove advertiser-curated audiences, or to edit the ad and destination so they no longer fall in the policy, or to appeal a classification you believe is wrong. A list built to stand in for visitors to a treatment page is the object the policy is written to stop. Predefined Google audiences and location targeting remain on the allowed side, with the custom-segment limit if the creative or landing page is sensitive. Search intent does not require that list. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
Q: Why can cost per lead look acceptable while admissions do not?
A: Because those are different events. The platform counts what you defined as a conversion, often a form or a call over a duration threshold. Admissions counts a later decision at a facility with capacity and the right service. If the bid optimizes to the first event, volume can rise while the census does not. The fix is a shared definition, a join that does not move clinical detail into the ad platform, and a report that shows both numbers by location. It is not a new audience.
Q: Are Meta mental health ads a Special Ad Category?
A: Housing, employment, and financial products and services use Special Ad Categories and limited audience tools. That is a different mechanism. Mental health creative is governed first by the personal-attributes standard, which bars ads that assert or imply the viewer's physical or mental health. Drug and alcohol addiction treatment ads aimed at people in the United States also require LegitScript certification and written permission from Meta. Use the standard that matches the ad, and check it again before a launch. [transparency.meta.com](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes) [transparency.meta.com](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drug-alcohol-addiction-treatment/)
Q: How should a hospital system treat a new behavioral service line?
A: Keep it distinct from service lines that are not in the sensitive category. Separate campaigns, pages, and conversion actions. Point local queries at the campus that offers the service. Apply the addiction-services rules only where the service is recovery-oriented drug and alcohol treatment, and apply the sensitive-interest audience rules wherever the ads or pages promote mental health conditions or treatment. Measure inquiries and scheduled assessments or admissions for that campus, not a system-wide lead total.
Q: How often should the policy pages be reread?
A: On a set cadence, and again whenever an ad is disapproved or a new service line, domain, or country is added. Google and Meta revise these pages. A quarterly audit that cites last year's screenshot is how fixed rules and fixable mistakes get swapped. The links in this guide are the places to start. They are not a substitute for the page on the day you launch. The operators who hold a ceiling in this category are the ones who treat the restrictions as the design brief. Advertiser-curated audiences are not the growth plan. Certification decides which addiction-treatment entities can run. Structure, local truth, and a join to admission decide whether the spend that is allowed to run produces a census admissions will recognize. The rest is a bid change on a number that was never the business. [Request an acquisition audit](/get-started/request-an-audit/)
