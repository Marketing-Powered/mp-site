Meta Ads for Treatment Centers: A Policy-Safe Operating Playbook
Meta ads for treatment centers can create inquiries, but only when the account is run as a policy-reviewed system that ends at a verified admission. The unit of control is not the ad; it is the chain from message to landing page to tracked inquiry to intake outcome. Most providers lose money in the handoffs between those parts, not inside Ads Manager.

What to expect before spend
Picture a Tuesday admissions huddle. The marketing lead has Ads Manager open. The admissions director has the call log. The COO is asking which number the board should fund. One report counts people who submitted a form or clicked to call. The other counts families who reached a person, asked about a level of care the program actually offers, and fit the catchment and payer path the program can serve. Those lists rarely match. The gap is the business problem this playbook closes.
A treatment center should expect four operating facts, not a promise of volume. First, drug and alcohol addiction treatment ads aimed at people in the United States are a restricted category. Meta's Advertising Standards require LegitScript certification and written permission from Meta before those ads run [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830). Certification alone does not authorize the account, and if the business is not eligible, Meta's guidance says it may not advertise those services in the US.
Second, the ad is not the unit of control. A policy-safe ad that feeds a vague intake script still wastes spend. A well-targeted ad that implies the viewer has a condition can be disapproved even when the offer is legitimate. Both failures are operating problems fixed by a documented pre-launch review and a definition of a qualified inquiry that admissions signs before funding begins.
Third, rehab Facebook ads are not a separate system from Instagram ads. Both run through Meta under the same Advertising Standards Meta Advertising Standards. Splitting brand or geography is an operating choice; treating "Facebook policy" as different from "Instagram policy" is not.
Fourth, judge the account on cost per qualified inquiry and verified admissions, not on the lead column. Cost per qualified inquiry is spend divided by inquiries that meet a written definition. It is an internal figure Meta will not calculate for you. Platform-reported cost per lead can fall while admissions stay flat, or the reverse. Neither number alone tells a CEO whether to add budget.
When this model applies and who is excluded
Use this model when the organization wants Meta, including Facebook and Instagram placements, to produce admissions demand for substance use treatment, and when someone inside the organization can own compliance, creative, tracking, and intake as separate jobs. It applies to single-site programs and multi-location networks. A network adds a practical question: which location owns the inquiry, which address appears on the page, and whether the certificate and domain actually cover that site. LegitScript's certification standards prohibit co-ownership of, or formal or informal business affiliation with, lead generators, and may treat that affiliation as grounds for immediate denial [LegitScript Certification Standards](https://www.legitscript.com/addiction-treatment-certification-standards). An agency, directory, or call center cannot borrow a treatment center's certificate to run ads as if it were the provider.
With certification and permission, covered promotions include in-person clinical treatment, addiction hotlines, mutual support groups, and online addiction treatment including remote programs Meta Advertising Standards. Physical rehabilitation, such as kinesthesiology, does not need that permission. That exception is narrow and is not a workaround for a substance use program.
Mental health services are adjacent, not identical. Meta mental health campaign guidelines, in practice, mean the personal-attributes rules, the ban on requesting health information in ads, and the rules on health data sent through Meta's business tools Personal attributes. A counseling practice that does not promote drug or alcohol treatment is not automatically inside the addiction-treatment permission path. It is still inside the rules that stop an ad from telling a stranger they are depressed, and inside the rules that stop a pixel from shipping a diagnosis as event data [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809). If an ad promotes a prescription medication, review Meta's separate drug advertising rules before launch; addiction-treatment permission does not clear drug promotion Drugs and pharmaceuticals.
Some organizations should not plan on this channel at all. LegitScript's FAQ states that sober living environments are generally not eligible, virtual or online intervention services are not eligible, and support groups intended primarily for family members or caregivers are not eligible [LegitScript FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/). Lead generators and uncertified call centers are also outside the application path per Meta's guidance [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830).
The same ownership pattern is useful beyond treatment centers. Hospitals and other regulated healthcare organizations face the same fracture: marketing counts a conversion, intake counts a schedulable patient, and privacy counsel asks what the tag just sent. The fix is still a separate reviewer, a written definition of a qualified inquiry, and event design that does not export health details. How this work is scoped for treatment providers is described on the [behavioral health](/who-we-help/behavioral-health) page.
Search sits beside Meta, not inside it. Google's healthcare and medicines policy requires advertisers promoting recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services in the United States to be certified by LegitScript as an addiction services provider, and states that services not eligible for that certification are not allowed to advertise those services on Google [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031). Advertisers must also be certified with Google. A LegitScript certification does not replace that step, and a Google certification does not replace Meta's written permission. This page does not build the search account. The shared discipline is policy-reviewed creative, message-to-call relevance, and offline conversion quality defined with admissions.
Policy checkpoints before spend
Policies change. Read the live text before every launch and after any disapproval you cannot explain. What follows is an operating summary, not a policy table. The restriction wording, and how to read it, lives in the [rehab advertising guide](/resources/rehab-advertising-guide).
Permission. For US-targeted drug and alcohol addiction treatment ads, confirm two artifacts: an active LegitScript addiction treatment certification for the business and domains you will use, and written permission from Meta. Meta's help steps are sequential. Become certified first. LegitScript reviews background, qualifications, licensing, and privacy practices, and charges a fee for processing and monitoring [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830). Not every service is eligible. If it is not, stop. After certification, request authorization in the Authorizations and Verifications area of Business Settings, and select the country the certification covers. Keep the certification active. Meta states that if it determines the certification is no longer active, authorization may be revoked [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830).
Creative. Ads must not assert or imply a person's physical or mental health, including a medical condition. Meta's own examples allow "Depression counseling" and "New diabetes treatment available." They do not allow "Do you have diabetes?" or "Depression getting you down? Get help now." "You" is acceptable when it does not attach a personal attribute Personal attributes. Ads also must not request health information without prior permission. Meta's standards cite lead-form fields that ask whether a person has experienced depression or anxiety as not allowed Advertising Standards, lead ads.
Data. Meta's business-tools terms treat health information as prohibited information. The list includes diseases and medical conditions, mental health and psychological states, medical procedures and testing, prescription medications, and physical locations that identify a health condition or places of treatment and counseling [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809). That information must not be shared in Meta Business Tools data, including URL parameters, custom audiences, custom conversions, custom event names, and custom data. Sending the same information through the pixel, the Conversions API, or a server does not remove the prohibition [Data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698). Renaming an event from "DetoxLead" to "Lead" does not fix a rule that is about the information, not the label.
Meta may also classify a data source and apply sharing restrictions: a core setup that blocks custom parameters and the part of a URL after the domain, limits on certain standard events, or full restrictions that block events for ads use [Data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698). Core setup is a control Meta offers. Meta states that it is not a substitute for your own compliance work. If Events Manager shows removed parameters or blocked events, treat that as a defect to fix, not as a filter you can lean on.
Privacy, separate from platform policy. If the organization is a HIPAA covered entity or business associate, HHS Office for Civil Rights states that regulated entities are not permitted to use tracking technologies in a manner that would result in an impermissible disclosure of protected health information [HHS bulletin](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html). A disclosure of PHI to a tracking vendor for marketing, without a HIPAA-compliant authorization, is an impermissible disclosure under that bulletin. On June 20, 2024, a federal court vacated the portion of the bulletin that treated an IP address plus a visit to an unauthenticated public webpage about a health condition or provider as enough, by itself, to trigger HIPAA [HHS bulletin](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html).
The rest of the bulletin was not vacated. User-authenticated pages, such as a portal, generally give tracking tools access to PHI, and a vendor that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI for a covered function can be a business associate [HHS bulletin](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html). Unauthenticated pages can still involve PHI. HHS's examples include appointment scheduling and symptom-checker tools that collect an email address or a reason for seeking care, and login or registration pages that collect credentials [HHS bulletin](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html). A cookie banner is not a substitute for a privacy review. This page is not legal advice. Counsel should review the specific pages, forms, and call recordings, and should separately review substance use disorder confidentiality rules.
Targeting. Housing, employment, financial products and services, and social issues, elections, or politics have their own special-ad rules [How to choose a Special Ad Category](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/298000447747885). Addiction treatment is not one of those categories. Do not select a special ad category to make a treatment ad safer. What did change, on January 19, 2022, is detailed targeting. Meta removed options that relate to topics people may perceive as sensitive, including health causes, with examples such as "Lung cancer awareness" and "Chemotherapy" [Meta developer post](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2021/12/08/updating-metas-detailed-targeting-options/). Build audiences from geography you can serve, age ranges appropriate to an adult program, and broad placements. Do not build an audience whose name or rule says the person has an addiction, takes a medication, or visited a counseling URL.
The handoff that decides value
Follow one inquiry across a room, not across a dashboard. A person sees the ad on a phone at a kitchen table after work. The ad names a service the program offers and a city it serves. It does not tell the viewer what is wrong with them. The click opens a page on a domain covered by the certification. The page repeats the same offer, shows a physical address where services are rendered, and gives two next steps: a staffed phone number and a short form. The form asks for a way to call back. It does not ask for a diagnosis, a substance, a medication, or a symptom list. Those questions belong on a call with a person who is allowed to ask them, under a script admissions and compliance have both read.
The call does not land in a general voicemail tree that also handles job applicants and vendor pitches. It reaches someone who can tell a fit inquiry from a mismatch, and who records a disposition the same day. The disposition uses the words admissions agreed to: seeking treatment for self or as an authorized family decision-maker, level of care offered here, geography served, payer or private-pay path the program can accept, and a reason code when the answer is no. That note is the conversion. The platform lead is a receipt that someone clicked.
Most treatment centers do not lose the account inside the bid. They lose it in this handoff. Marketing publishes a page written to win the click. Admissions answers a different offer than the page described. Analytics fires an event named for a clinical step, or appends the landing path, which may itself name a condition, into the URL sent with the event. Compliance reviews a PDF of the ad policy while the live form still asks a health question. Each group is locally reasonable. The chain is not.
Campaign quality assurance is the habit of reading the chain as one object. Before an ad is eligible for spend, one person who did not write it compares four artifacts side by side: the ad, the page, the form or call script, and the event names. If any artifact implies a personal health attribute, requests health information, or would send a place of treatment or a condition to Meta, it does not launch. Message-to-call relevance is the same test heard out loud. If the person who answers cannot fulfill the sentence in the ad, the ad is ahead of operations.
Policy-reviewed creative is not a legal memo stapled to a finished ad. It is a short review with a named reviewer and a date. An illustration of the line, not approved copy for any account:
* Closer to the allowed pattern: "Outpatient alcohol treatment is available in this city. Call admissions to ask about the program."
* Closer to the disallowed pattern: "Your drinking is out of control. We already know you need detox."
The second sentence asserts a condition and implies knowledge of the viewer. Meta's examples reject that structure even when the underlying service is real Personal attributes. Do not use patient photos, recovery stories, or outcome claims without a documented authorization, and do not use them to imply what will happen to the next caller. Non-stigmatizing language is an operating rule, not a style preference. Fear, shame, and urgency aimed at a stranger's health status are both a policy risk and a poor admissions signal.
Owners and launch sequence
Assign four owners. One person may hold two seats in a small program. No person should review their own claim or their own ad.
* Certification, domains, Meta permission. Owner: Compliance lead. Separate reviewer: Marketing lead confirms the ad account matches the authorized business.
* Offer, creative, landing page. Owner: Marketing lead. Separate reviewer: Compliance reviewer who did not write the ad.
* Pixel, Conversions API, URL parameters, call tracking, CRM fields. Owner: Analytics or IT owner. Separate reviewer: Privacy counsel or the compliance lead.
* Qualified-inquiry definition and disposition codes. Owner: Admissions lead. Separate reviewer: CEO or COO, before spend.
* Weekly read of inquiries against that definition. Owner: Marketing lead prepares the sheet. Separate reviewer: Admissions lead confirms or corrects the dispositions.
The sequence is the control. Skipping ahead to "just test a small budget" is how restricted accounts get more restricted, and how unqualified calls train staff to ignore the channel.
* Confirm eligibility in writing. List every service you intend to mention: residential, outpatient, detox, medication, family education, hotline. Mark which of those Meta's policy and LegitScript's eligibility rules can cover. Drop the rest from the ad plan. Record certificate status, covered domains, covered facilities, and the Meta authorization status. If any item is pending, the campaign waits.
* Write the inquiry definition with admissions, not after the first bad week. Include who may inquire, which levels of care count, geography, payer constraints, and exclusions such as vendors, job seekers, wrong numbers, and clinical needs you do not treat. Set the window in which a disposition must be recorded. If admissions will not sign it, you do not have a measurement plan. You have a lead goal.
* Build the page to the definition. One primary offer per page. Address and phone visible. No symptom quiz. No field that asks the visitor to disclose a condition in order to submit. No URL path that names a diagnosis if that URL will be sent with an event. If you need clinical education on the site, decide with counsel which pages carry tracking and which do not. HHS draws that distinction between authenticated and unauthenticated flows [HHS bulletin](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html). Your pages may not.
* Design events that could be read aloud in a privacy review. Prefer a small set of bland events tied to actions that are not themselves a diagnosis: a click to call, a submitted contact request, a connected conversation if the call platform can pass that without a health label. Do not send admission as an event named for a level of care, a drug, or a facility type. Offline conversion quality means the outcome you send back is accurate, consented where required, and stripped of prohibited information [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809). If a lower-funnel event is restricted in Events Manager, do not route around the restriction with a new pixel, a new name, or a hidden parameter. Judge that outcome in your own sheet instead.
* Review creative as a packet. Primary text, headline, image or video, button, lead form if any, and the page it opens. The reviewer checks personal attributes, health questions, claims of a cure or a timeline, and consistency with the offer admissions can fulfill Personal attributes. Record pass, revise, or stop. An ad the author marks "probably fine" is not a review.
* Staff the phone before the ad. Publish the hours a person answers. Define what happens after hours. A form that promises a same-day call and a queue that calls in three days will manufacture the complaint that "Meta leads are junk." Speed is an operations choice, not a bid strategy.
* Launch narrow, then read dispositions, not just spend. Start with the offer and geography you can fulfill. Do not add lookalikes built from a clinical list, a patient file, or a page audience whose rule is "visited the detox URL." Customer lists and site audiences are allowed only when the names, rules, and underlying data do not suggest a health condition [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809). A list of people who inquired, labeled and filtered as people with a substance use disorder, fails that test even if the file came from your own CRM.
* Hold a weekly quality review. Marketing brings spend, delivery, and the raw inquiry list. Admissions brings dispositions. Compliance brings any disapproval or diagnostic warning. The meeting ends with one change: creative, page, routing, definition, or pause. A meeting that only notes "leads were down" is not quality assurance.
Pre-launch checklist. Copy it into the working doc the four owners share. Do not treat a blank box as optional.
* LegitScript certification active for this legal entity, these facilities, and these domains
* Meta written permission active for the country you will target
* Services in the ads are services the certification and the program both cover
* No lead-gen, sober-living, or uncertified call-center offer in the account
* Ad account, page, and domain match the authorized business
* Inquiry definition signed by admissions and by the CEO or COO
* Landing page offer matches the ad and shows a real address and a staffed number
* Form and lead ads ask for contact, not symptoms, diagnoses, or medications
* No patient story, photo, or outcome claim without documented authorization
* Reviewer who did not write the ad has signed the creative packet
* Pixel and Conversions API inventory completed: pages, events, parameters, URL strings
* No event name, audience name, or parameter describes a condition, medication, or place of treatment
* Scheduling, portal, login, and registration pages reviewed with counsel before any tag is placed
* Call tracking does not write a diagnosis into the source label or the CRM campaign name
* After-hours path and callback standard are staffed
* Weekly review is on the calendar, with admissions in the room
Measure past the platform lead
Platform leads answer a platform question: did someone complete an action Meta was told to count? Admissions answers a different question: did a person we can serve ask for care, and did they admit? Meta ads for treatment centers should be measured on the second question. The first question is a diagnostic, useful for spotting a broken form or a dead number, and a poor guide for budget.
Define the rows before you need them. Leave the numbers blank until the week produces them. Do not backfill from memory.
* Spend. What it is: Amount billed in the account for the period, for the campaigns in scope. Who records it: Marketing.
* Platform-reported results. What it is: The action you optimized for or reported, labeled with Meta's name for it. Who records it: Marketing.
* Connected conversations. What it is: Calls or chats that reached a person, not a ring-out. Who records it: Admissions or call platform.
* Qualified inquiries. What it is: Connected conversations or forms that meet the signed definition. Who records it: Admissions.
* Not qualified, with reason. What it is: Vendor, employment, geography, level of care, payer, clinical mismatch, other. Who records it: Admissions.
* Verified admissions. What it is: Admissions the program counts in its own census, tied back to the inquiry ID where the link is real. Who records it: Admissions, confirmed by finance or the census owner.
* Cost per qualified inquiry. What it is: Spend divided by qualified inquiries in the same period. Who records it: Calculated, not imported.
* Disputed rows. What it is: Inquiries marketing and admissions coded differently. Who records it: Both, resolved in the weekly review.
That sheet is the dashboard. A sample wireframe has six blocks and no invented totals: spend, qualified inquiries, cost per qualified inquiry, verified admissions, top three disqualification reasons, and a list of disapproved or limited ads. If a vendor dashboard cannot show the admissions block, it is a media report. Keep using it for delivery. Do not let it be the board report.
Offline conversion quality is the decision about what, if anything, you send back to the platform. Send only an outcome you can define without a health label, and only if counsel agrees the transmission is permitted [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809). A bland "qualified conversation" event can still be prohibited information if the criteria are "person seeking treatment for opioid use" or the URL is a counseling path. When you cannot send a clean event, do not send a dirty one and hope the filter catches it. Meta says its filters are not your compliance program [Data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698). Optimize to a permitted, non-clinical action if the account allows it, and do the quality math in the sheet.
Two comparisons matter, and both are internal. Compare qualified inquiries with platform results for the same dates. A wide gap means the form, the offer, or the routing is attracting people outside the definition, or the platform action is easier than a real conversation. Fix the chain before you raise the bid. Compare verified admissions with qualified inquiries. A wide gap here is usually not a creative problem. It is capacity, payer mix, clinical fit, speed to contact, or a definition written too loosely. Cutting the cost per lead will not close it.

That is the founder's history, not an agency client result. The measurement rule taken from that operating history is that attribution is tracked to admission, not just to the lead. What the data cannot prove
Put the two reports for the same week on one table and read them as a comparison, not as a verdict. The media report can show spend, impressions, clicks, the actions you chose to count, and the cost of those actions. It can show which ad and which placement received delivery. It cannot show that a family admitted because of one ad when they also called a directory, searched the brand, and were referred by a clinician. It cannot show that a missed call was unqualified. It cannot show clinical fit. If the account's attribution setting changes, last period and this period are not the same series. Do not treat a change in a modeled column as a change in demand until admissions has counted the same weeks in its own log.
The admissions report can show who was reached, who met the definition, who admitted, and why others did not. It cannot, by itself, show which headline caused the call. It cannot repair a missing source tag. It cannot prove a channel "does not work" from a week in which the phone was short-staffed. When the two owners disagree on a row, the disagreement is the data. Resolve it in the review. Do not average it into a prettier cost.
Recurring mistakes, in the order they usually appear:
* Treating a LegitScript certificate as permission to run any offer, on any domain, in any ad account [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830).
* Writing ads in the second person that name a condition, a relapse, or a family's crisis. The service can be named. The viewer's health cannot be asserted Personal attributes.
* Putting the clinical screen on the form because "admissions wants better leads." The policy limit and the privacy limit both point the other way. Better leads come from a tighter offer and a staffed screen, not from a symptom field in the ad Advertising Standards, lead ads.
* Naming events, audiences, and URL parameters after the condition or the unit. Core setup may strip part of the URL. It will not absolve the design [Data sharing restrictions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/511197658391698).
* Retargeting people who visited a treatment page, or uploading a patient or prospect list filtered by diagnosis. The audience rule is the violation, even with a neutral name [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809).
* Optimizing to a purchase, schedule, or complete-registration event that either does not happen or encodes health information. A restricted event is a signal to change the measurement plan, not to disguise the event.
* Reporting cost per lead to the board and calling it acquisition performance. A lead that admissions will not accept has a cost and no contribution to census.
* Letting the media buyer and the admissions director use different definitions for a month and then debating which vendor failed.
* Assuming a cookie banner, a new pixel, or a server-side pipe makes the same data acceptable to Meta or permissible under HIPAA [HHS bulletin](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html).
* Pausing after a disapproval and rewriting only the headline, without checking the page, the form, and the event.
None of these mistakes is fixed by a new interest stack. The sensitive-cause targeting options Meta removed in 2022 are not coming back as a targeting strategy you should try to recreate with lookalikes of patients [Meta developer post](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2021/12/08/updating-metas-detailed-targeting-options/). Geographic reach you can clinically serve, a true offer, a clean page, and a fast human screen are the controls you still have.
What a careful account can show a board, after a few review cycles, is narrower and more useful than a platform export. It can show whether qualified inquiries are arriving at a cost the program is willing to pay, whether those inquiries admit at a rate operations can explain, and whether disapprovals and data-restriction warnings are rising or falling. It cannot show a permanent cost, a required budget, or a close rate you can take to a lender. If someone offers those figures without your census and your signed definition, they are offering a story.

FAQ
Q: What should a provider expect from meta ads for treatment centers?
A: Expect a permissioned, certified account if you are advertising drug and alcohol treatment to people in the United States [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830). Expect creative that describes the service without assigning a condition to the viewer Personal attributes. Expect disapprovals when the ad, the form, or the page crosses those lines. Expect platform lead counts to run ahead of qualified inquiries. Expect to fund the phone and the review meeting, not only the media. Do not expect the channel to rank clinical quality, to replace referrals, or to produce a stable cost you can forecast from someone else's account.
Q: How should results be measured against qualified calls or admissions?
A: Write the definition of a qualified inquiry with admissions before spend. Count connected conversations and form inquiries against that definition. Divide spend by qualified inquiries for cost per qualified inquiry. Count verified admissions from the program's own census, linked to an inquiry ID only when the link is real. Review the gap between platform results and qualified inquiries, and the gap between qualified inquiries and admissions, as separate problems. Send an offline outcome to Meta only when the event is accurate, free of health labels, and cleared by counsel [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809). If you cannot send it cleanly, keep the math in your sheet.
Q: Do rehab Facebook ads follow a different rule than Instagram?
A: No. Facebook and Instagram placements are bought in the same Meta system and reviewed under the same Advertising Standards Meta Advertising Standards. A Facebook-only campaign does not escape the certification requirement, the personal-attributes rule, or the data rules. Splitting brand, offer, or geography can be an operating choice. Splitting "Facebook policy" from "Instagram policy" is not.
Q: Do mental health campaigns need the same permission as addiction treatment?
A: The LegitScript and written-permission requirement applies to drug and alcohol addiction treatment ads targeting people in the United States [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830). A mental health campaign that does not promote those services is not automatically on that path. It is still subject to Meta mental health campaign guidelines in the form that matters operationally: do not imply the viewer has a condition, do not request health information in the ad or the lead form Advertising Standards, lead ads, and do not send mental-health information through Meta's business tools [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809). If the campaign mentions medication or a controlled service, check the drug rules and your certification scope before you assume you are outside them Drugs and pharmaceuticals.
Q: Can the lead form ask about symptoms, substances, or medications?
A: Meta's standards do not allow ads to request health information without prior permission, and they flag lead-form questions about conditions such as depression or anxiety Advertising Standards, lead ads. Keep the form to a callback. Put clinical questions on a staffed call, under a script compliance has read. Have counsel review any field beyond name, phone, and a general request to be contacted. A richer form can look like lead quality inside the ad account and still be the reason the ad, or the data connection, is limited [Prohibited information](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/361948878201809).
Q: What should we do if the account or the ad is restricted?
A: Stop editing at random. Export the disapproval or restriction reason, the ad, the page, the form, and the event diagnostics. Check certification and Meta permission before you rewrite copy [How to Apply for Permission](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787684018620830). Q: Who should sit in the weekly review?
A: The marketing lead, the admissions lead, and someone who can speak for compliance or privacy. The CEO or COO does not need every meeting. They do need the signed definition, and they need the sheet when budget is the question. A vendor may attend. The vendor should not be the only person who can define a qualified inquiry. If the chain from permission to admission is not written down, more budget will not make the report clearer.
