Microsoft Ads for Behavioral Health: A Complete Guide
Treatment marketers who already run Google often look at Microsoft Ads for Behavioral Health as spare demand: the same queries, a second auction, and a faster way to add volume. That framing skips the part that decides whether the account can run at all. This guide covers the policy differences that matter, the setup work an import does not finish, and the measurement standard that counts an admission rather than a click.

Why Microsoft Ads Matters for Treatment Marketers
Picture a paid media lead and an admissions director at the same table, a Microsoft Advertising account on one screen and the Google account on the other. The useful question is not whether Bing "is cheaper." It is which queries never enter the Google auction, which locations are under-served on the primary account, and whether a second search network can be run without creating a second compliance problem.
Microsoft Ads matters when a treatment organization is concentrated on one auction. Concentration is an operating risk: a policy disapproval, a budget cap, or a sudden rise in competition on the primary platform shows up as empty inquiry slots, not as a chart in a media report. A second search network can cover incremental queries from people researching a level of care, a city, or a program name. It cannot replace the primary account, and it should not be sold as a workaround for rules the organization already has to meet on Google.
The commercial test is admissions capacity, not click volume. A multi-location network can add Microsoft in markets where intake can actually answer, and leave it off in markets where the census is full or the certified URL is not ready. A single-site provider can use it to test whether desktop and partner-network searchers inquire about the same level of care the Google account already funds. In both cases the account is a supplement. If the team cannot say which facility, which level of care, and which inquiry source produced a scheduled assessment, extra clicks only add noise.
That review should happen before budget moves. Compare search-term reports, not audience lists. Look for queries the Google account is already restricting, for locations with no certified landing URL, and for campaigns that were built to remarket. Those three checks decide whether Microsoft is an expansion or a policy incident waiting on a new logo.
How Microsoft Policy Differs from Google
Google and Microsoft both restrict addiction-treatment advertising. They do not use the same gate, the same geography, or the same extra approval.
On Google, promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services is restricted. Advertisers targeting certain locations, including the United States, can run those ads only if certified by Google. For the United States, Google also requires certification as an addiction services provider through the LegitScript Certification program, and it states that services not eligible for that certification are not allowed to advertise drug and alcohol addiction services. Google's own certification still has to be completed on top of LegitScript. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) Services unrelated to drug and alcohol addiction, such as impulse-control disorders, behavioral addiction, or nicotine addiction, sit outside that addiction-services policy. They are not a free pass. Google treats health as a sensitive interest category, including physical or mental health conditions and products or services used to treat them, and its examples include counseling for depression, anxiety, and addiction. In that category, advertiser-curated audiences are not available. Predefined Google audiences can still be used. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
Microsoft's healthcare policy, updated October 5, 2026, is narrower in a few places operators miss. Advertising for drug and alcohol addiction recovery is only allowed in the United States. Advertisers promoting rehab facilities, group therapy, sober living, halfway houses, or similar offerings must be certified by LegitScript. Landing pages and any URLs used in the ad must be certified. Lead generation ads are not allowed. Healthcare advertising must target users 18 and older, and advertisers may not target consumers based on sensitive health information. Microsoft also does not accept ads for clinical trials, experimental treatments, or medical research solicitations. [Healthcare and Pharmaceutical](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/3/en/60379)
* Drug and alcohol recovery ads; Google Ads, United States: Restricted; LegitScript plus Google certification; Microsoft Advertising: Allowed only in the United States, with LegitScript certification
* URLs in the ad; Google Ads, United States: Domain and certification status are reviewed; Microsoft Advertising: Landing pages and any URLs in the ad must be certified
* Lead generation; Google Ads, United States: Lead generators and referral agencies are inside the restricted addiction-services examples; Microsoft Advertising: Lead generation ads are not allowed for this category
* Remarketing and similar lists; Google Ads, United States: Advertiser-curated audiences are not allowed for sensitive health content; Microsoft Advertising: Targeting based on sensitive health information is not allowed
* Clinical trial recruitment; Google Ads, United States: Not allowed in most locations; allowed in some with restrictions; Microsoft Advertising: Not accepted
* Separate platform application; Google Ads, United States: Google certification is required in addition to LegitScript; Microsoft Advertising: The recovery section does not describe a second Microsoft preapproval form; pharmacy and telemedicine preapproval is a different track
LegitScript describes its addiction treatment certification as the third-party certification recognized by Google, Meta, Microsoft (Bing), and Nextdoor, and it states that Meta, Microsoft Bing, and Nextdoor currently recognize that certification in the United States only, while Google recognizes it for providers in the United States and Canada. [Addiction treatment certification fact sheet](https://www.legitscript.com/daatc-certification-factsheet) A Canadian certification that unlocks Google does not, on Microsoft's own policy, unlock drug and alcohol recovery ads outside the United States.
Two operating implications follow. First, a certified provider site is not a license to run a call-broker or directory URL beside it. If the URL in the ad is not the certified URL, the ad is outside the rule Microsoft published. Second, mental health counseling that is not drug and alcohol recovery may fall outside the LegitScript recovery section and still be barred from condition-based targeting. Write the account to the stricter rule that actually applies to the service line, not to a blended "behavioral health" label.
Setting Up a Compliant Treatment Account
Setup is a sequence, not a toggle. The account should exist before campaigns are imported, and the certified URL list should exist before keywords go live.
Start with the legal advertiser. The business that holds the state licenses and the LegitScript certification should be the advertiser of record. A media vendor, a call center, or a sister brand that is not on the certification should not be the entity behind the ads. Microsoft's recovery rule is about who is advertising and which URLs are certified, not about which agency logs in.
Confirm geography next. Drug and alcohol recovery ads on Microsoft are limited to the United States. A campaign inherited from a Google account that also targets Canada, or that uses a broad location set "because the import brought it over," is already misaligned. Location targeting should name the states and cities the certified facilities actually serve, with exclusions for states where the organization is not licensed to treat.
Then inventory every URL that could appear in an ad, a sitelink, a location asset, or a call-to-action path. Microsoft requires those URLs to be certified, not merely the homepage. A 12-facility network that sends each city ad group to a unique page needs each of those pages on the certification, or it needs a single certified URL that can represent the offer without sending people to an uncertified microsite. Location pages spun up for a campaign, insurance-verification quizzes, and "verify your benefits" forms on a separate domain are the usual failures.
Only after that inventory is clean should anyone import from Google. Microsoft documents an import from Google Ads that brings over most campaign information, with exceptions. Political campaigns are skipped. Auto-generated assets are not imported. Audience campaigns can come across with a Target CPA or Maximize conversions bid strategy when conversion goals are set up, and with Enhanced CPC when they are not. [What gets imported from Google Ads](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/3/jp/50851) Import is a time saver for keywords, ads, and negatives. It is also how a disallowed audience, an uncertified final URL, or a Canada location setting arrives intact.
After import, a person should open every campaign and remove anything the recovery policy will not carry: lead-form assets, remarketing lists built from treatment pages, customer lists that include patients or inquirers, and final URLs that are not on the certification. Bids and budgets copied from Google are a hypothesis, not a plan. Pause the imported campaigns until that pass is done. Launching "to see what happens" is how a restricted category gets its first strike.
Keyword and Audience Targeting Limits
Search intent is the allowed core of this category. Someone typing a query about detox, residential care, outpatient treatment, or a facility in a specific city is raising a hand. An audience built because someone visited a condition page is a different act: it tells the platform the person is likely to have, or to be researching, a health condition.
Microsoft's healthcare policy prohibits targeting consumers based on sensitive health information and points advertisers to its privacy and personalization rules. Those rules say advertisers may not use Microsoft Advertising to process sensitive personal data in order to personalize advertising or to create or enhance user profiles. [Privacy and Personalization](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/3/en/60212) A remarketing list of visitors to a detox, residential, or medication-assisted treatment URL is that kind of list. So is an upload of people who called about a specific substance. The practical rule for a treatment site is simple: do not build audiences from condition pages, insurance-verification pages, or inquiry lists, and do not turn on audience expansion that tries to find more people who look like those visitors.
Keyword work still has to be tight, because search is doing the job remarketing cannot.
* Group keywords by level of care and by facility, not by a single national ad group. A query for outpatient care in one city should not inherit the bid and the landing page of a residential program three states away.
* Keep match types intentional. Broad match will pull job seekers, free hotline queries, and adjacent medical terms. Those negatives belong in the account before spend does.
* Separate brand queries for the organization's own names from non-brand category queries. They have different economics and different landing pages.
* Treat medication names as a policy review, not as a keyword idea. Prescription-drug language can move an ad into a different Microsoft and Google rule set than the recovery section. If a program offers medication-assisted treatment, have compliance read the ad, the keyword, and the page together before that language goes live.
* Do not buy competitor brand terms as a shortcut to volume unless counsel has cleared the trademark and the landing page does not imply an affiliation the organization does not have.
LinkedIn profile targeting is the Microsoft feature teams ask about next, and it is easy to misuse. Microsoft Advertising documents targeting by company, industry, and job function, and states that on Search campaigns this targeting is bid-only: ads can still show to people who do not match, with a bid adjustment for those who do. [LinkedIn profile targeting](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/3/en/56905) A separate help article limits that targeting to PC and the United States. [LinkedIn profile targeting availability](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/22/en/60032) Job function is not a diagnosis. Raising a bid for people whose profiles list healthcare or executive roles can be a rational adjustment for an executive program. It does not identify who has a substance use disorder, and it should never be described that way in a brief, a slide, or an ad.
In-market and similar platform segments need the same reading. If a segment is defined by a health condition or by treatment intent that Microsoft would treat as sensitive health information, leave it off. Demographic bid adjustments that do not encode a condition, such as age over 18, are a different matter. Healthcare ads on Microsoft must already target users 18 and older.
Landing Pages and Conversion Requirements
The page is part of the ad. Microsoft reviews destinations, and the recovery section says the landing page and any URL in the ad must be certified. A compliant headline pointed at a page that overclaims, collects more health detail than the inquiry needs, or routes into an uncertified call flow will still fail.
What the page should do is narrower than most treatment landing pages attempt.
State the service, the setting, and the geography. Residential, outpatient, detox, and sober living are different offers and, on Microsoft's examples, all sit inside the recovery category when they are drug or alcohol services. The page should make the level of care obvious without diagnosing the reader. Address the organization, not the person's condition. "Programs for alcohol withdrawal in Tucson" describes a service. "Your addiction is destroying your family" addresses a stranger as a patient. The second line is a poor ad even before policy review, and it conflicts with the duty not to target people on the basis of sensitive health information.
Claims need the same restraint Microsoft writes into the healthcare policy generally. Health-related claims must be supported. Exaggerated or unsupported statements that create unrealistic expectations are out. Exaggerated before-and-after images are out. Comparative or superlative lines need factual support and a cited third-party study where the policy requires it. [Healthcare and Pharmaceutical](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/3/en/60379) Outcome percentages, cure language, and time-boxed recovery claims do not belong in the ad or on the page. Describe staffed services, hours, levels of care, and how to reach admissions. Leave efficacy claims out.
The setup path that holds up in review looks like this.
* Confirm LegitScript certification covers the legal entity and every URL the ads will use.
* Create the Microsoft Advertising account in that entity's name, with billing and business verification that match the certification.
* Limit drug and alcohol recovery campaigns to the United States, and to states where the facilities are licensed.
* Import structure from Google only as a draft. Strip uncertified URLs, lead forms, and any audience built from health pages.
* Rewrite ads around service, place, and level of care. Remove outcome claims.
* Place the UET tag, then create conversion goals with neutral names.
* Capture the Microsoft Click ID on the inquiry and pass qualified calls and admissions back as offline conversions.
* Launch a small set of exact and phrase keywords against one certified URL, and read the search-term report before expanding match types.
Conversion setup is part of compliance, not a later analytics project. Universal Event Tracking is how Microsoft records on-site conversions: the tag is created in the platform and added to the site, including an industry selection that can be set to health and wellness. [UET tag setup](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/3/en/56913) The tag should not receive condition names in event labels, page paths that spell out a diagnosis, or form fields that contain clinical detail. Name goals for the business event: inquiry submitted, qualified call, assessment scheduled, admission. A goal named for a substance is a health label stored in an ad platform.
Forms should ask for what admissions needs to call back, not for a clinical history. Phone numbers on the page should be tracked without putting the caller's story into the ad system. If a chat tool stores transcripts, those transcripts stay in the system authorized to hold them. They are not a remarketing seed.
Budget and Bid Strategy Trade-offs
There is no published Microsoft price list for this category, and there is no responsible way to promise a cost-per-click gap versus Google. Auction prices move with competition, match type, geography, and Quality Score inputs the advertiser does not fully control. Treat any benchmark deck that quotes a fixed discount as unverified until it is measured in the account, against admissions, over a period long enough to include slow intake cycles.
Budget should be set from the question the organization can answer. How many additional qualified inquiries can admissions handle this month, in which facilities, without stretching response time past the window in which people still answer? A small daily budget on a narrow keyword set in two cities will teach more than a national campaign copied from Google at full scale. The point of the first month is to learn query mix, disapproval risk, and whether inquiries reach a person. It is not to "scale Bing."
Bid strategy follows measurement, not the other way around. Enhanced CPC and other automated strategies need a conversion goal that represents a real business event. If the only goal is a page view or a click on a phone number, automation will buy more of that weak event. Microsoft's import behavior makes the same point: without conversion goals, imported audience campaigns fall back to Enhanced CPC. Set the goal before trusting a smart bid.
A workable sequence for most treatment accounts:
* Weeks one and two: manual or enhanced CPC, exact and phrase match, one certified URL per ad group, search-term reviews every few days.
* After enough qualified inquiries exist to see a pattern: test a conversion-based strategy on the non-brand campaigns that already have clean queries, using the qualified-call or assessment goal, not the raw click.
* Keep brand and non-brand apart so a cheap brand click does not teach the bidder that the category is inexpensive.
* Cap geography and schedule to the hours admissions answers. A click at 1 a.m. that reaches voicemail is a media cost and an intake failure.
Shared budgets across facilities hide the facility that is overspending. If the organization operates in more than one market, give each market its own budget and its own conversion action, even if the daily amounts are small. That is how a network sees that one city produces assessments and another produces job-seeker clicks. It is also how a later admissions upload can be tied to the right campus.
Do not move budget off Google because a Microsoft dashboard shows a lower cost per click in week one. Cost per click ignores answer rate, insurance fit, and whether the person was a fit for the level of care on the page. The comparison that matters is cost per qualified inquiry and cost per admission, over a window that matches how long intake actually takes. Until those numbers exist, Microsoft is a test budget, not a reallocation thesis.
Tracking Admissions, Not Just Leads
A lead is an unfinished fact. In treatment marketing, the decision the budget should inform is whether a person reached admissions, was appropriate for the program, and was admitted. Click-through rate, impression share, and even cost per form fill stop short of that decision. Marketing Powered tracks attribution to admission, not just to the lead, and the same standard belongs on Microsoft.
The mechanics are ordinary, and they fail in ordinary ways. Microsoft's conversion-tracking documentation covers on-site goals through UET and offline goals, including imported call conversions. Offline events depend on capturing the Microsoft Click ID from the ad URL and associating it with the later event. [Conversion tracking](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en-us/solutions/tools/conversion-tracking) Auto-tagging has to be on, or the landing URL never receives the click ID. The intake CRM, call-tracking platform, or form database has to store that ID against the person record at the moment of inquiry. If the ID is dropped at the chat handoff, the call transfer, or the second form on a different domain, the admission cannot be posted back.
Microsoft's Campaign Management API makes the timing constraint concrete. An offline conversion is sent against an offline conversion goal, using the Microsoft Click ID. The conversion time has to be later than the click, inside the goal's conversion window, and, for that API path, within the last 90 days or the upload fails. After the goal is created, the documented guidance is to wait before the first upload. Duplicate rows with the same click ID and the same conversion time are ignored. [OfflineConversion](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/campaign-management-service/offlineconversion?view=bingads-13) An admissions cycle that runs past the window you configured will look like "Microsoft does not convert," when the real fault is a window set for ecommerce.
Build the record the way an admissions coordinator actually works. The coordinator does not need a real-time dashboard of every click. The coordinator needs a call or a form that names the facility, the level of care, and the source, then a later status: reached, screened, admitted, or not a fit. Marketing needs the click ID sitting on that same record so the admitted status can be uploaded without anyone pasting a patient narrative into an ad platform. Neutral status values are enough. Clinical notes, diagnoses, and medications stay out of the advertising system. That separation is the practical meaning of being HIPAA-aware. It is not a certification badge, and it is not created by placing a tag.
Owned, local infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference can support internal review of call outcomes and intake coding without sending that material to a consumer ad platform. The ad platform still only needs the click ID, the time, the goal name, and, if you use value-based bidding, a value your finance lead is willing to stand behind. Value should be an internal planning figure, labeled as such, until the organization has a measured contribution per admission. Do not upload a guessed lifetime value and then describe it as revenue the channel produced.
Multi-location networks should pass a facility identifier in the CRM, not in the ad-platform event name. One offline goal for "admission" plus a facility field in the source system is cleaner than a goal per condition. Facility-level reporting then happens in the system that is allowed to hold the operational record. The media report answers a narrower question: which campaign, which click, and which subsequent admission status.
If call extensions are part of the account, decide in advance which calls count. A tap on a number is not an admission. Import the calls that admissions marks as qualified, and keep the rest out of the bidding goal. Otherwise automated bidding will chase misdials and job seekers with the same enthusiasm it chases a scheduled assessment.
Where Microsoft Fits in the Paid Mix
Microsoft belongs in the mix when four conditions are true at once. The organization is a provider, not a lead seller. The LegitScript certification and the URLs are current. Admissions can answer. Someone owns the click-ID path from the ad URL to the admission status. If any one of those is missing, the right spend is zero until it is fixed. Adding a network does not repair a broken intake desk or an uncertified page.
It does not belong in the mix as a place to run the remarketing Google will not allow. The founder built this practice for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted. Microsoft's rule against targeting on sensitive health information leads to the same operating choice. Search, tight geography, and certified pages. Not patient lists, not condition audiences, not "people who almost filled out the form."
It also does not belong as a copy of the Google account with the logo changed. Import can move the structure. It cannot move judgment about match types, negatives, location limits, or which conversion the bidder should see. Teams that schedule an automatic import and never read the Microsoft search terms will import Google's mistakes on a delay and add a few of Microsoft's own.
For hospitals, health systems, and professional-service organizations outside drug and alcohol recovery, the recovery section may not be the binding rule, but the sensitive-health rule still is. A service line built around depression, eating disorders, or dual-diagnosis care should assume condition-based audiences are off, even when LegitScript's addiction certification is not the gate. Pharmacy, telemedicine that sells prescription drugs, and health insurance sit on still other certification tracks. Do not let a treatment-center playbook leak into those accounts, and do not let a hospital playbook leak into a recovery account that Microsoft limits to certified US URLs and bans lead generation for.
The founder of Marketing Powered is a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations, and managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts. He is a court-certified expert witness in advertising strategy. That history is a reason to treat policy text as an operating constraint, not as a footnote under a media plan. It is not a promise about what a new Microsoft account will return.
Use Microsoft when the primary account is healthy, the certification file is current, and the organization wants a measured test of incremental search demand. Keep the test small enough that a disapproval or a weak inquiry week does not starve the Google programs that already feed admissions. Expand only after qualified inquiries and admissions can be tied to the click, by facility, without sending clinical detail to the ad platform.

FAQ
Q: Do we need LegitScript to run drug and alcohol recovery ads on Microsoft?
A: Yes, for the category Microsoft describes. Advertisers promoting rehab, group therapy, sober living, halfway houses, or similar drug and alcohol addiction services must be certified by LegitScript, the landing pages and any URLs in the ad must be certified, and the ads are only allowed in the United States. Lead generation ads are not allowed. [Healthcare and Pharmaceutical](https://help.ads.microsoft.com/apex/index/3/en/60379) Google, for the United States, requires that same LegitScript certification and a separate Google certification. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) Certification is eligibility, not a substitute for ad-level review.
Q: Does mental health counseling follow the identical rule?
A: Not always. Google's addiction-services policy is scoped to recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, and it says services unrelated to that scope, including some behavioral addictions, fall outside it. Counseling for depression, anxiety, and addiction is still listed among health examples in personalized advertising, where advertiser-curated audiences are not allowed. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) On Microsoft, the named recovery section is drug and alcohol, while the broader healthcare policy still bars targeting based on sensitive health information. Read the service line against both texts before you assume a therapy practice can skip certification or that a recovery provider can skip audience limits.
Q: Can we remarket to people who visited a treatment page?
A: Do not. A list of people who visited a condition or treatment URL is a health inference. Microsoft prohibits targeting consumers based on sensitive health information and prohibits using the platform to process sensitive personal data for personalization. Google does not allow advertiser-curated audiences, including remarketing lists built from your own visitors, for sensitive health content. Build the account on search queries and certified pages.
Q: If we import Google campaigns, is the Microsoft account compliant?
A: No. Import copies structure. It does not certify a URL, remove a lead form, or know that an audience was built from a detox page. Microsoft will not import every asset, and audience campaigns may arrive with a bid strategy that assumes conversion tracking you have not set up. Review locations, final URLs, audiences, and ad claims before anything is enabled.
Q: How far back can an admission be tied to a click?
A: Depends on the goal window you set and the upload path you use. On the Campaign Management API, the conversion time must fall inside the offline conversion goal's window, must be after the click, and must be within the last 90 days or the upload fails. Set the window to the real path from click to admission, store the Microsoft Click ID at inquiry, and upload a neutral admission status. Do not put clinical notes in the payload.
Q: Should budget move off Google once Microsoft is live?
A: Not on the basis of early cost per click. Keep Microsoft as a capped test until qualified inquiries and admissions are visible by facility. The primary account remains the one admissions already depends on. Expand the test only when the certified URLs, the answer rate, and the click-ID path are all holding. An acquisition audit is the right next step when the question is whether a second search network belongs in the plan, or whether the current account is carrying uncertified URLs, health-based audiences, or goals that stop at the lead.
