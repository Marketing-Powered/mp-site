Healthcare PPC Advertising: Agency Operator Guide
Healthcare ppc advertising is paid search and related paid media that reaches people looking for care and routes them to a call or form intake can act on. Evaluate it against qualified inquiries and intake outcomes, alongside cost, response capacity, and policy constraints. Platform-reported leads alone do not establish inquiry quality.

Where this guide applies
Picture three rooms on the same Tuesday morning. In a treatment center admissions office, a coordinator is on a recorded line with a parent asking about a level of care the program does not offer, while two other lines ring from outside the licensed states. At a hospital service-line desk, a scheduler is trying to place an orthopedic inquiry against the wrong campus, with the campaign report still pooled at the system level. At a multi-location clinic, the front desk has a form submission for a service that location does not staff this month. The auction mechanics are similar. What differs is who answers, what that person can accept, and which platform rules apply to the service being advertised.
This guide is written for treatment center CEOs, COOs, CMOs, and admissions leaders. The same operating frame applies, with adjustments, to hospitals, health systems, multi-location healthcare services, and other regulated organizations. It is a working document a marketing lead and an admissions or scheduling lead can use in the same meeting. It does not replace counsel, a privacy officer, or a clinical decision.
### Treatment and behavioral health
For treatment providers, inquiry quality depends on service fit, geography, payment arrangements, and intake capacity. An inquiry is useful only if the team can move it forward: the level of care is one the program provides, the caller is in a geography the facility can serve, there is a payment path the facility can work with, and the right person can be reached. Search demand can be urgent, and a click is not the same object as an admissible inquiry.
Platform rules are narrower than "behavioral health" as a category. Google restricts promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Advertisers targeting the United States may promote those services only if certified by Google, and U.S. advertisers must also be certified as an addiction services provider by LegitScript's Certification program. Not every drug and alcohol addiction service is eligible for that certification, and services that are not eligible are not allowed to advertise those services on Google. Google's examples of in-scope services include clinical addiction treatment providers, recovery support such as sober living environments and mutual help organizations, lead generators or referral agencies, and crisis hotlines for drug and alcohol addiction. Promotion of services unrelated to drug and alcohol addiction, such as impulse control disorders, behavioral addiction, or nicotine addiction, is outside that policy. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) [Addiction services](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en)
Do not treat that certification rule as a requirement for every mental health or behavioral health advertiser. A counseling practice, a psychiatric hospital service line, and a substance use program can sit under different rule sets. Read the current policy for the service you actually advertise, and record the date you read it.
### Hospitals, health systems, and other regulated markets
Hospital and health-system marketers usually face a different shape of the same problem: several facilities, several service lines, scheduling friction, referral demand beside paid demand, and measurement that has to be split by location. The definition of a qualified inquiry will differ by service line, so the organization keeps several definitions, not one. A booked appointment may be the outcome stage rather than an admission.
For multi-location practices, diagnostics, urgent care, and professional services in regulated categories, the questions are the same. Which queries map to a service that location can deliver? Who verifies the inquiry? What happens in the system after the call or form? What data may be recorded, joined, or sent to an ad platform under current platform rules and applicable law? The worksheet later in this guide is built to be copied once per service line or location.
The qualified inquiry definition
A reporting mismatch can begin with the lead definition. If the platform conversion differs from intake's definition, marketing and admissions will bring different numbers to the same meeting. One side is counting what the ad platform was told to count. The other side is counting inquiries the facility can actually work.
Google Ads can count a phone call as a conversion when the call lasts at least a minimum length the advertiser sets, for calls from ads and for calls to a forwarding number on the website. Call reporting has to be enabled for that duration method to work as documented. For calls from ads, Google also describes a path that analyzes call recordings with its own AI when recording is available, and falls back toward duration when recording is not. Importing call conversions from another system can give the advertiser more control over which calls are counted, for example only calls that include a defined business outcome. None of those methods is an admissions qualification. A long call, an AI quality label inside the ad platform, or a form fill is a platform event. It is not, by itself, evidence that the person was in the service area, seeking a service the program provides, or able to move forward. [About phone call conversion tracking](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6100664?hl=en-AU) [Set up calls to a phone number on a website](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883) [Measure calls from ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095882?hl=en)
Smart Bidding uses the conversion goals selected for the campaign. A conversion action is eligible for bidding when it is set as a primary action and the campaign optimizes toward the goal that contains it. A secondary action is tracked for observation and is excluded from the conversions column used for bidding, unless it is pulled into a custom goal. If the selected goal is "a call longer than the duration you set," the bidding system is pointed at that goal. It has not been given intake's definition of a qualified inquiry. [About conversion goals](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10995103?hl=en)
The operating fix is a written definition of a qualified inquiry, with one owner, thresholds, an exclusion list, an "unknown" category, and a review date. That artifact is what connects keyword, ad, landing page, call or form, intake disposition, and outcome. Without it, each stage can be optimized to its own metric. With it, the stages can be judged against the same target.
The primary measure in this guide is cost per qualified, admissible inquiry. Qualified means the inquiry meets the written definition. Admissible means intake can move it forward given service fit, licensing, payment path, and capacity. That classification is an operational reporting category, not a clinical determination. Do not place patient-level records in the definition document, and do not assume that stripping clinical narrative makes inquiry data non-sensitive. The HIPAA Privacy Rule protects individually identifiable health information held or transmitted by a covered entity or its business associate when that information relates to an individual's past, present, or future physical or mental health or condition, the provision of health care, or payment for that care, and identifies the individual or could reasonably be used to do so. Identifying information listed with an indication of treatment at a facility can be protected health information. [HHS de-identification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html)
For an economics view of how qualified demand connects to payback, see [healthcare marketing ROI](/resources/healthcare-marketing-roi). This page owns the operating definition and the measurement chain. It does not try to be the ROI model.
Implementation steps and owner roles
Each step has one accountable role, an output, and a failure sign. Titles will map differently in a hospital than in a single-site program. The point is a named person, not a committee that owns the gap.
### Step 1: Write the qualified inquiry definition
Owner: Head of admissions, intake, or scheduling, with the marketing lead as co-signer.
Output: A one-page definition with inclusion criteria, exclusion criteria, and an unknown category.
Start with the people who answer the phone or work the form queue. Ask them to list the common reasons an inquiry is not accepted: out of area, wrong service or level of care, payment path the facility cannot work, looking for a different service, not the person seeking care, or not ready to continue the conversation. Those reasons become the exclusion list. Write inclusion criteria in plain language a marketing system can hold: geography, service fit, payment path, whether the person can be reached, and whether the caller is the person seeking care or an appropriate contact. Keep clinical detail out of the marketing definition. Add a review date. Capacity, contracts, licensed geography, and service lines change, and the definition should change with them.
Failure sign: Marketing and admissions can each describe a qualified inquiry, and the descriptions differ.
### Step 2: Map keyword-to-call relevance
Owner: Paid media manager, with admissions or scheduling input.
Output: A keyword intent map that sorts search themes by how well they match what intake can accept.
Keyword-to-call relevance asks whether the person searching a theme is a person the team can help. Group themes by intent: a specific level of care or service, a comparison of providers, a payment or insurance question, information-seeking with no request to enter care, employment, or a different service entirely. Then check each group against call or form outcomes. A theme with high inquiry volume and low acceptance is a relevance problem. Narrow it, add negatives, restructure it, or stop buying it.
For hospitals and multi-location groups, add geography. A query can match a service line and still miss the location. Structure campaigns so budget, ad copy, and landing pages can be managed by location and service line rather than one pooled account. Review search terms against outcomes, not only against cost.
Failure sign: Search term reports are reviewed for cost and not for disposition.
### Step 3: Structure the account for control
Owner: Paid media manager, with a compliance reviewer.
Output: A short structure note that lists campaigns, ad groups, landing pages, conversion actions, and which policy source was last checked for each service type.
Separate campaigns where that improves budget ownership and diagnosis. If sensitive-category services, general information, and unrelated services share one campaign and one asset set, a disapproval is harder to trace, and budget is harder to assign to a service line or a facility. Separation does not contain enforcement. Google can suspend an account for policy violations, and a suspension stops ads in that account from running. Some violations, including certain egregious policies, can suspend an account on detection without a prior warning. Other policies, including the addiction services policy, state that violations will not lead to immediate account suspension without a warning issued at least 7 days prior. Campaign structure is an operating control. It is not a firewall. [Account suspensions overview](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9841640?hl=en) [Addiction services](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en)
Failure sign: After a disapproval or a limited-serving status, nobody can name the asset, the policy source, or the person who will resolve it.
### Step 4: Review creative and landing pages on a cadence
Owner: Content or creative lead, with compliance review. Admissions or the service-line owner confirms that the page matches what the facility delivers.
Output: A reviewed library of ads and landing pages, with review date, reviewer, and the policy source checked.
Treat copy and landing pages as account-health inputs, not a launch task. A claim on a page can become a disapproval, a mismatch with what intake can deliver, or a privacy problem if the page collects more than the inquiry process needs. Describe the program as it is, including what it does not provide, because an accurate page is also a filter. Avoid outcome promises. Do not imply a diagnosis. Use accurate, non-stigmatizing language. When a platform policy page changes, the review log shows what must be rechecked.
Failure sign: Pages were reviewed at launch and not again.
### Step 5: Capture calls and forms in a form intake can use
Owner: Marketing operations or analytics lead, with intake.
Output: A capture setup that can associate an inquiry with source, campaign, and keyword where that association is authorized, and a disposition field that maps to the written definition.
Where calls are a primary inquiry channel, use approved call-source tracking and a short, mandatory disposition list. A controlled list of roughly six to ten dispositions can be reported. Free-text notes are harder to classify consistently than controlled dispositions. Use notes for context, and use the disposition list for routine reporting.
Do not enable recording or transcription by default. Document the applicable authorization, notice, consent, access, and retention requirements before any recording or transcript is turned on. Google's own call-recording feature for calls from ads is described as limited to calls where both parties are in the United States or Canada, and that is a platform feature, not a determination that recording is lawful for your program. [Measure calls from ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095882?hl=en)
Covered entities and business associates may not use tracking technologies in a manner that results in an impermissible disclosure of protected health information. On June 20, 2024, a federal court vacated a portion of HHS's online tracking bulletin, specifically the statement that HIPAA obligations are triggered merely because an online technology connects an IP address with a visit to an unauthenticated public webpage addressing specific health conditions or healthcare providers. Read the current bulletin with that order in mind. This guide is not a privacy determination. [HHS: online tracking technologies](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html)
Failure sign: Intake records outcomes in a system that cannot be joined, even in aggregate and under an approved method, to campaign data.
### Step 6: Decide what, if anything, goes back to the platform
Owner: Analytics lead, with the privacy or compliance reviewer and the paid media manager.
Output: A written decision on which outcome signals, if any, are sent to an ad platform, the product used, and the legal and contractual basis.
Offline conversion quality is the question of whether the platform is learning from what happens after the click, or only from the click and the call duration. Do not use enhanced conversions or store sales uploads for conversions involving health or medical information. Google's customer data policy, which applies to enhanced conversions for web, enhanced conversions for leads, store sales uploads, and Google-engaged audiences, states that conversions related to sensitive categories cannot be used for measurement in enhanced conversions or store sales uploads. Health or medical information is listed among those categories, with examples that include purchases of medical services, prescription drugs, or medical devices. The same policy says advertisers may not upload conversion information related to sensitive categories. Consent is not an exception to that product restriction. Evaluate any other measurement method separately against its current platform rules and legal requirements. [Customer data policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709?hl=en)
When a signal cannot be sent, the same classification can still be used inside the organization. Keyword themes, campaigns, and landing pages can be judged on qualified-inquiry rates in internal reporting, and budget decisions can be made from that reporting rather than from the platform's conversion column alone.
Failure sign: Either health-related conversion data is uploaded into a prohibited product, or nothing after the click is analyzed because someone decided measurement was impossible.
### Step 7: Run campaign quality assurance on a calendar
Owner: Paid media manager runs the routine. The account lead signs off.
Output: A recurring QA log.
Campaign quality assurance is a routine with a scope, not a checklist used only at launch. A workable weekly pass includes search-term review against the intent map, with negatives added where the query cannot be accepted; a check of disapprovals and limited serving; a sample of calls or forms classified against the definition; a landing-page check for phone-number accuracy, form errors, mobile behavior, and unreviewed copy changes; and budget pacing against capacity. More paid demand into a team that cannot answer is spend without a path to an outcome.
A monthly pass should add a conversion-action audit, a definition review with admissions or scheduling, and a reread of the policy sources that apply to the services in the account.
Marketing Powered has been AI-native since 2022, and owned, local AI infrastructure can support HIPAA-aware inference where that deployment fits the data-handling decision. If a team uses assisted classification to review calls or search terms at volume, a person still owns the rules, samples the output, and signs the result. That infrastructure does not make an account compliant, does not transfer ownership of client data, and does not mean every campaign runs on a local model.
Failure sign: QA happens only after results drop.
### Step 8: Write scaling rules before spend moves
Owner: CMO or COO, with admissions and paid media.
Output: A one-page rule set for increasing and reducing spend.
Scaling is a capacity decision as well as a media decision. Decide in advance what must be true before budget increases: the qualified-inquiry rate has held over a window you choose, intake can respond within the target time, benefit verification or scheduling capacity can absorb more volume, and the next dollar is aimed at comparable queries. Decide what triggers a reduction. Without written rules, increases tend to happen in the week the platform report looks strongest.
Failure sign: Spend moves because cost per click fell, while answer rate and qualified-inquiry rate were not in the same review.
Metrics to review when scaling
Put the weekly review in one room, on one screen, with marketing and admissions or scheduling both present. The left side of the screen is what the ad platform reports. The right side is what the people who took the calls recorded. The distance between those two views is the quality gap. The layout below is an illustrative meeting screen, not a measured result and not client data. Every figure is a field you fill from your own systems.
Panel 1, funnel by campaign group. Spend, clicks, inquiries, qualified inquiries, and admits or scheduled patients, with rows by service line or location.
Panel 2, cost ladder. For each campaign group: cost per click, cost per inquiry, cost per qualified inquiry, and cost per admit or scheduled patient. The step from cost per inquiry to cost per qualified inquiry is the quality gap. A low cost per click can sit on top of a wide gap.
Panel 3, disposition mix. For each theme, the share of inquiries classified as qualified, out of area, wrong service, not a prospect, and unknown.
Panel 4, response health. Answer rate, time to first response, and missed calls or abandoned forms by hour.
Panel 5, account health. Open disapprovals, last policy-source review date, last definition review date, and whether the live ads and pages have a current review-log entry.
Panel 6, decisions. Three decisions, an owner for each, and a due date. If the meeting ends without a decision, the dashboard was a display, not a review.
Read the layers in order when you are deciding whether to increase spend. None of them is enough alone.
Demand. Impressions and impression share on core themes, click-through rate, cost per click, and whether impression share is lost to budget or to rank. These describe the auction. They do not describe whether the traffic can be accepted.
Relevance. Share of spend on themes the intent map marks as acceptable, share of inquiries classified as wrong service, wrong area, or non-prospect, and negatives added in the last review.
Response. Answer rate, time to first response, missed-call or abandonment rate, and callback completion. A scaling plan that ignores response will buy more inquiries into a bottleneck.
Qualification. Qualified-inquiry rate by campaign, theme, and landing page; cost per qualified inquiry; and the share sitting in unknown. A high unknown share is a disposition problem before it is a media problem.
Outcome. Verification or assessment completion where that stage exists, admission or scheduled-appointment rate from qualified inquiries, cost per admit or scheduled patient by campaign group and, where relevant, by facility, and time from first inquiry to that outcome.
Account health. Open disapprovals, limited serving, and the age of the last policy and creative review.
A theme with a higher cost per click, a stable qualified-inquiry rate, and a response team that can answer is a clearer scaling candidate than a theme with a low cost per click and a poor acceptance rate. Do not publish a third-party keyword-tool cost per click as the price a provider will pay. Those tools produce planning estimates. They are not your account's cost per click, and they are not your cost per qualified inquiry.
Measure calls against admissions
The working chain is keyword, ad, landing page, call or form, intake disposition, outcome. Each link has to be joined to the next by an identifier your systems can hold without exposing more than the approved use requires.
Attribute the inquiry. Where authorized, call-source tracking or form parameters retain campaign and, when available, keyword information. This front half of the chain is usually the most mature, and it is also the half most likely to be mistaken for the whole measurement system.
Classify the inquiry. Intake assigns a disposition from the controlled list: qualified, not qualified with a reason, or unknown. Classification should happen as soon as practical, not at month end, or the weekly media review is always looking at a stale definition of quality.
Record the outcome. For qualified inquiries, track whether the person moved to the next defined stage and, eventually, whether they were admitted or scheduled. Many organizations hold that record in an EHR, a CRM, or an admissions tool. Whether and how that record may be joined to marketing data is a privacy and contractual decision. Where a row-level join is not appropriate, a reconciled report of counts by campaign can still inform budget choices. Do not assume an aggregate report is de-identified. HHS describes two methods that meet the Privacy Rule's de-identification standard: expert determination, and safe harbor removal of specified identifiers plus no actual knowledge that the remaining information could identify an individual. Even properly de-identified data retains some risk of identification. Apply approved aggregation and small-cell controls before sharing. A report that only states an average, and does not identify any person, is a different object from a campaign table with counts small enough to point back to a person. [HHS de-identification guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html)
Calculate cost per qualified inquiry and cost per admit or scheduled patient. Divide spend by qualified inquiries, and spend by admits or scheduled patients, for the same campaign grouping. Use a cohort. Google Ads reports conversions by the date of the ad interaction, and the delay between click and conversion can make recent cost per acquisition look higher than it will look once later conversions arrive. That platform explanation does not establish how long a healthcare admission takes. Outcomes may occur after the inquiry. Use the lag you observe in your own records, and do not compare this month's spend with this month's admits as if they were the same cohort. [About conversion lag reporting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9347141?hl=en)
Review and adjust. Take the results back to the shared meeting. Adjust the intent map, the negatives, the creative, the landing pages, and, when capacity or contracts have changed, the definition itself.
The chain is a decision tool. It is better than platform-reported leads alone. It does not prove that paid search caused every admit. People may search several times, call more than one provider, be influenced by family, a referral, a review, or another channel, and convert on a path tracking does not capture. Last-click or last-call attribution assigns credit. It does not establish cause.
A practical audit can start by comparing intake dispositions with the conversion actions used for bidding, then tracing mismatches through search terms, ads, and landing pages. An account can look orderly and still be pointed at the wrong target. The mismatch is often faster to see from the call log than from the campaign tree.

Policy as an operating input
Healthcare advertising rules are set by platforms, and in some categories by certification programs the platform requires. They differ by country, service type, and product. They change. The notes below are Google Ads examples retrieved for this page. They are not a universal healthcare rulebook, and they are not a substitute for reading the current policy page before you launch or expand.
Certification is service-specific. For U.S. recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, Google requires LegitScript addiction-services certification and Google certification. Services outside that scope are not covered by that sentence. In the United States, advertising health and medical insurance coverage also has its own certification path, with a different certifier, and government advertisers are handled differently from other advertisers. Online pharmacies and telemedicine have still other requirements. Match the rule to the service. Do not copy an addiction-services certification requirement onto a service line it does not cover. [Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en)
Personal health content restricts some audiences. Google treats health as a sensitive interest category in personalized advertising. The category includes physical or mental health conditions and products, services, or procedures used to treat or manage them. Counseling services for mental health issues such as depression, anxiety, and addiction are listed among the examples. For that category, advertiser-curated audiences cannot be used, including Customer Match, audience expansion, and lookalike segments, because they may contain sensitive user signals. Predefined Google audiences can be used, because Google states that sensitive user signals are excluded from them. Custom segments that use sensitive creative or point to sensitive landing pages have further limits: on Display they are described as serving only to non-sensitive audiences or contextually, and other campaign types using those custom segments with sensitive assets or landing pages are not eligible to serve. These are Google rules for a sensitive interest category. They are not a statement that every audience feature on every platform is closed, and they are not a statement that search itself is prohibited. [Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
Marketing Powered is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware, and built for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical. That positioning is an operating stance. It is not a certification of the firm, of a workflow, or of a client's account, and it is not a claim that a tool produces compliance.
Build policy into the week rather than into a static memo.
* Keep a policy source register. For each platform and service type, record the policy page, the date it was last read, the reviewer, and any change made to ads, keywords, or pages.
* Recheck on events: a new service line, a landing-page rewrite, a new state, a disapproval, or a limited-serving status.
* Label a platform requirement differently from an internal practice. A preference is not a rule, and a rule is not optional because it is inconvenient.
* Name an escalation owner. When serving is limited or an account is suspended, one person owns the response, and the revenue implication is reported to the COO or CEO the same day. For repeat violations of a Google Ads policy, Google describes a warning at least 7 days before suspension, and a strike system that can end in suspension. Egregious violations are handled differently and can suspend an account immediately. Know which path you are on before you plan the week as if spend will continue. [Account suspensions overview](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9841640?hl=en)
No workflow, review log, or model makes an account compliant. Compliance decisions belong to the advertiser, its counsel, and its compliance reviewers.
Limits, mistakes, and the worksheet
Set the same conference table twice. In the first meeting, marketing arrives with platform conversions and a cost per lead. Admissions arrives with a call log full of out-of-area and wrong-service dispositions that were never coded, only described in notes. Nobody can say which conversion action bidding is using. The meeting argues about volume. In the second meeting, the qualified inquiry worksheet is on the table, the disposition list matches the worksheet, and the screen shows the cost ladder beside the disposition mix. The argument moves to a specific theme, a specific exclusion, and a decision with an owner. The second meeting is the point of the instrument below. The figures in it are blank on purpose. Enter your own. Do not borrow another organization's rates.
### Mistakes that show up in the account
Treating the platform conversion as the business outcome. A call that meets a duration setting is a call. It is not an admit, and it may not be a qualified inquiry.
Writing the definition once. Capacity, payment arrangements, geography, and service lines change. A definition without a review date drifts.
Letting marketing own the definition alone. Marketing can draft. Admissions or scheduling has to co-own, because that team bears the cost of a bad classification.
Buying the cheapest click. A low cost per click can come from queries intake cannot accept. A higher cost per click on a theme with a stable qualified-inquiry rate can be the better purchase. The platform report will not make that comparison unless you add the disposition.
Scaling into a bottleneck. If intake cannot respond within the target time, more demand produces more missed calls and more unresolved forms.
Assuming structure contains a policy problem. Separation helps diagnosis and budget ownership. It does not stop account-level enforcement.
Treating tracking choices as purely technical. Recording, retention, identifiers, and signals sent to platforms are privacy and legal choices. Involve the reviewers before the tag is live.
Over-reading a short window. Admit or appointment counts for a single campaign group can be small. A few cases can move cost per admit sharply. Show the sample size beside the rate, and use a longer window before you treat a swing as a trend.
Reporting only a blend. A blended cost per qualified inquiry can hide a strong theme paying for a weak one. Break results out by campaign group, theme, and, where relevant, location.
Uploading health-related conversions into a product that prohibits them. A retail enhanced-conversion setup is not a healthcare default. Read the customer data policy before any upload.
### What the numbers do not prove
Executive readers should know the limits before they use the chain in a budget decision.
* The chain does not prove causation. A qualified inquiry that arrived through paid search may also reflect a referral, a review, a prior visit, or a family conversation. Attribution assigns credit.
* The chain does not capture every path. Callers may use a main number instead of a tracked number, switch devices, or call after a delay. Some of that loss is structural.
* A small sample is not a stable rate. Short-period cost per admit for one campaign group can move on a handful of outcomes. Report the count with the rate.
* An operational label is not a clinical judgment. A qualified inquiry does not describe anyone's clinical needs and must not be used as if it did.
* Technical possibility is not permission. The fact that a field can be sent to a platform does not mean it should be sent. That decision sits with the advertiser and its advisers.
* History is not a forecast. Auctions, competitors, capacity, and policy changes move. A past qualified-inquiry rate is a guide for the next review, not a prediction of next quarter.
* A keyword-tool estimate is not your acquisition cost. Use it for planning context. Use your measured cost per qualified inquiry for decisions.
Stating these limits makes the report easier to defend, because the decision is tied to what the data can support.

FAQ
Q: What is healthcare PPC advertising?
A: Healthcare PPC advertising is paid search and related paid media used to reach people looking for care, with the advertiser paying when someone clicks or under the pricing model the platform applies to that campaign type. In healthcare it sits under platform policies that vary by service and location, and under privacy and advertising laws that also vary. The practical goal is an inquiry intake can act on, not a click or a form fill by itself.
Q: What metrics should be reviewed when scaling healthcare PPC?
A: Review them in layers. Demand metrics include impression share and cost per click. Relevance metrics include how inquiries classify by theme. Response metrics include answer rate and time to first response. Qualification metrics include qualified-inquiry rate and cost per qualified inquiry. Outcome metrics include cost per admit or scheduled patient, with the sample size shown. Account-health metrics include open disapprovals and the date of the last policy-source review. Increase spend on a theme when qualification and response still hold, not only when cost per click looks low.
Q: How should healthcare ppc advertising be measured against qualified calls or admissions?
A: Join keyword, ad, landing page, call or form, intake disposition, and outcome. Use one written definition so dispositions stay consistent. Calculate cost per qualified inquiry and cost per admit or scheduled patient by campaign group, on a cohort, because the outcome may occur after the inquiry and recent platform cost per acquisition can look worse until delayed conversions arrive. Report the count beside the rate. Treat last-click credit as an assignment of credit, not as proof of cause.
Q: How is healthcare PPC management different from general PPC management?
A: The auction is familiar. The constraints are not. Some treatment advertisers need certification before they can promote recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, and that requirement should not be generalized to every behavioral health advertiser. Personal health content restricts advertiser-curated audiences on Google. Measurement products that accept customer data prohibit health or medical information in enhanced conversions and store sales uploads. The operating routine has to include a policy-source register, sign-off on creative, and a written decision about what is recorded and what is shared.
Q: Does this framework apply outside treatment centers?
A: Yes. Hospitals, health systems, multi-location practices, and other regulated organizations can use the same chain. Expect several definitions, one per service line or location. Intake may be a scheduling team or a contact center. The outcome stage may be a booked appointment rather than an admission.
Q: Can offline conversions be used in healthcare PPC?
A: Do not use enhanced conversions or store sales uploads for conversions involving health or medical information. Google's customer data policy prohibits using sensitive-category conversions, including health or medical information, for measurement in those products, and it prohibits uploading conversion information related to sensitive categories. Evaluate any other measurement method separately against its current platform rules and legal requirements. Where an upload is not appropriate, qualified-inquiry rates can still be analyzed internally and used to adjust campaigns by hand. [Customer data policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709?hl=en)
Q: Should a provider publish cost per click as a benchmark?
A: Be careful. A keyword tool's cost per click is an estimate for planning. It is not what a specific account will pay per click, and it is not cost per inquiry or cost per qualified inquiry. Use your own measured figures for decisions, and label estimates as estimates.
Q: How often should the qualified inquiry definition be reviewed?
A: At least monthly in the joint review, and whenever capacity, payment arrangements, service lines, or licensed geography change.
Q: Can a workflow assure platform approval or compliance?
A: No workflow, tool, or review log can assure platform approval. No workflow makes an account compliant by itself. Policy review is a documented routine. Compliance decisions rest with the advertiser and its advisers. An acquisition review should focus on qualified demand, intake capacity, attribution, and policy governance.
