Who should use these resources
Picture a Tuesday afternoon in a conference room that is slightly too small for the people sitting around the table. The marketing director has a monthly media export open on a laptop. The intake lead has a call disposition log and a stack of web forms that never turned into booked assessments. A clinical operator holds the current census sheet, open bed count, or clinician schedule. A finance lead asks why last month's search spend did not show up as admitted volume or kept visits.
A working resource is a document those four people can place in the center of that table and use before approving the next invoice. It is not a category overview written to sound polished from a distance. Because that Tuesday meeting looks different depending on the care model and corporate structure, this library separates guidance by operational reality.
### Residential and outpatient addiction treatment operators
Addiction treatment teams must connect paid and organic demand to a real staffed facility, a verified level of care, and a recorded admission. The hard questions in that room are concrete. Which sites have licensed clinical staff today, which services are eligible to appear in paid search, whether a caller matched the payer and clinical profile, whether the inquiry reached benefits verification, and whether the person actually arrived. Any guide that treats click volume or raw form counts as the endpoint misses the business problem.
### Outpatient mental health practices
Outpatient therapy and psychiatry groups face a related acquisition challenge on a different operational clock. Capacity is usually defined by a clinician's open appointment blocks rather than a residential bed board. The person making the inquiry might be the prospective patient, a parent, or a referring physician. Here, marketing works when it converts open clinician hours into qualified inquiries and attended first visits without mistaking a third-party directory listing for a complete acquisition system. The location, routing, and measurement frameworks on this page apply directly, while residential admissions desk steps should be swapped for scheduling and kept-visit records.
### Hospitals and health systems
Hospital marketers often enter this material while supporting a behavioral health service line or adapting high-intent acquisition controls for another clinical department. The shared operational risk is paying to generate demand that central scheduling, clinic intake, or inconsistent campus listings fail to convert into kept appointments. The added layer in a health system is internal governance. A single hospital campaign can cross multiple legal entities, IT security reviews, electronic health record handoffs, and vendor contracts. Treating a multi-hospital service line as if it were a standalone treatment center creates friction with compliance and scheduling teams unless you translate the structure first.
### Professional services and other regulated organizations
Law firms, accounting practices, and other regulated B2B or consumer firms often examine healthcare marketing systems because the commercial mechanics overlap. High-intent search, long consideration windows, strict claim boundaries, and a recurring temptation to optimize for cheap top-of-funnel conversions show up in both worlds. The transferable parts of this library are offer clarity, office-level accuracy, documented claim review, and measuring marketing against a qualified business event rather than a raw click. What does not transfer is addiction-advertising certification. Never copy drug and alcohol treatment platform procedures into a general hospital service line or a professional practice that does not advertise substance use disorder services.

### A plain test before using any guide
Before asking your team to adopt any checklist from this library or from an outside vendor, apply a direct Monday-morning test. Can a named person in your organization point to one concrete decision they will change next week because of the document. Can they identify the specific log they will inspect, the unsupported claim they will pull down, or the misleading metric they will remove from the executive packet. If the document only confirms familiar opinions without altering a record, a campaign setting, or a handoff, set it aside.
Read in dependency order
Most wasted effort in healthcare marketing begins at the channel layer. A team reads a bidding tactic, an ad-copy framework, or a landing-page tip, then starts executing before verifying whether the account is allowed to run the offer, whether two campuses share a single call-tracking line, or whether marketing and intake share a written definition of a qualified inquiry.
Working through this library in dependency order prevents teams from tuning tactics on top of a broken foundation. Follow these four steps in sequence before editing bids or rewriting ad copy:
* Document every service offered and the staffed sites where patients can actually receive each service.
* Record real clinical or scheduling capacity for the next thirty days from the operator who controls the schedule.
* Lock the downstream commercial event and intermediate disposition codes that finance, intake, and marketing will use in monthly reviews.
* Build or revise search campaigns, landing pages, and call routing inside those documented limits.
### Define the offer and staffed sites
Write down exactly what the organization provides, where a person can receive care, and who has authority to approve public descriptions of that care. For a substance use disorder provider, that means mapping detox, residential, partial hospitalization, and intensive outpatient levels of care to the specific street addresses licensed and staffed to deliver them. Pasting a national menu of services onto every regional page creates immediate operational and regulatory risk.
For an outpatient mental health group, offer truth means listing which offices provide psychiatry, therapy, psychological testing, or medication management, along with whether clinicians are currently accepting new patients at each site. For a hospital system, it means naming the exact service line, the participating campuses or clinics, and the real scheduling door a patient will enter when they call or submit a form.
Location truth sits ahead of search rankings and ahead of paid media budgets. An advertisement or landing page that implies a staffed clinic where you have no physical team creates two failures at once. It exposes the organization to advertising-policy enforcement, and it corrupts performance data. Inquiries arrive for a city you cannot serve, the ad platform logs a phone click as a conversion, intake spends staff time turning people away, and finance sees media spend with zero patient volume. No automated bidding rule can fix a false location claim.
### Confirm capacity before buying demand
Buying demand when the organization cannot accept new patients produces hold queues, dropped inquiries, frustrated families, and negative public reviews about phone responsiveness. Before any paid-media scaling guide becomes useful, the clinical or operations leader who owns the schedule must state what the organization can realistically intake over the next month.
That capacity figure is never a marketing estimate. It is grounded in open staffed beds, active group sizes, clinician hours, payer contract rules, or hospital clinic appointment blocks.
Capacity statements must match the delivery model. A residential addiction program may have open residential beds while remaining constrained by detox beds required for initial stabilization. An outpatient psychiatry office may have open therapy slots across the week while having zero new-patient prescriber openings until next month. A hospital behavioral health department operates under different throughput limits than an orthopedic surgery line down the hall. Writing the constraint down in plain numbers prevents media buyers from pouring spend into a bottleneck that intake cannot clear.
### Lock the measurement definition before the media plan
Agree in writing on the single commercial event that governs budget conversations. Ad-platform conversions, raw web forms, and total inbound phone calls are operational inputs. They show that a marketing asset prompted an interaction, not that the business gained a patient or client.
For addiction treatment programs, the downstream commercial event is a recorded admission, supported by intermediate checkpoints such as qualified inquiry and verified insurance benefits so teams can locate where inquiries drop out. For outpatient mental health practices, the matching commercial event is usually an attended first appointment rather than a booked slot that later no-shows. For a hospital service line, the governing event is a scheduled and kept clinical visit within the advertised service line.
Use the operational event your finance and clinical teams already trust and record every week. Creating a custom marketing metric that looks smoother on a slide deck only guarantees an argument during quarterly budget reviews.
### Match the sequence to your organization type
Once site truth, capacity, and the governing commercial event are written down, channel work becomes a controlled engineering exercise rather than guesswork.
A treatment center operator should move in this exact sequence: verify platform certification and country rules for the services offered, align facility pages and phone numbers to staffed sites, establish intake disposition codes tied to admissions, and then construct search campaigns inside those policy boundaries.
An outpatient mental health operator should confirm real office rosters and open clinician hours, audit response time from form submission or call to live scheduling, verify local profile accuracy across offices, and then fund high-intent local search and paid search where capacity exists.
A hospital marketing team must insert a formal governance check between capacity and channel launch: confirm which department approves clinical claims, which security team clears tracking tools and pixels, and which scheduling desk answers campaign-specific phone numbers. Sending paid traffic to a shared hospital switchboard without a verified handoff turns acquisition spend into abandoned transfers.
Platform rules that bound the work
Behavioral health marketing is not general healthcare marketing with a different keyword sheet. Recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services sit inside dedicated platform certification regimes. General mental health counseling that does not treat substance use disorders operates under a different rule set. Naming prescription medications or opioids triggers separate policy checks altogether. A playbook that collapses these categories into one checklist leads teams straight into account suspensions, wasted creative production, or unsupportable public claims.
### Addiction services scope and geographic rules
Google restricts the promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, allowing advertisers in listed locations, including the United States, to promote those services only with Google certification, according to the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines: Addiction services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en-AU). Google's in-scope examples include clinical addiction treatment providers, recovery support services, lead generators or referral agencies, and crisis hotlines, while services unrelated to drug and alcohol addiction, including impulse control disorders, behavioral addiction, and nicotine addiction, fall outside that policy, as stated in the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines: Addiction services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en-AU).
Operators must keep one distinction sharp when reading those examples. Google's list of in-scope examples, including recovery support services and referral agencies, is not the same list as LegitScript eligibility. Do not assume a sober living residence, a recovery coach, or a referral company can obtain LegitScript certification simply because Google names those categories inside its addiction-services scope.
Eligibility and certification rules also vary strictly by country, so never apply a United States workflow blindly to international campaigns. On the United States row of the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en), advertising recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services requires certification as an addiction services provider through LegitScript, and services that are not eligible for that certification are not allowed to advertise those services on Google. Canada has its own LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification requirement plus Google certification, following the [Google Ads March 2025 healthcare and medicines update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15857948?hl=en-AU&ref_topic=16083443) and the Canada row of the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en). Australia, France, Ireland, and New Zealand allow government entities only, and still require Google certification under the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en). Check the live location table on Google's policy page before treating any two countries as the same compliance path.
* United States; Recovery-oriented addiction ads eligibility on Google: Eligible addiction services providers; ineligible services are not allowed to advertise those services on Google; Certification requirement: LegitScript certification as an addiction services provider plus Google certification
* Canada; Recovery-oriented addiction ads eligibility on Google: Eligible organizations under the March 2025 expansion and live Canada policy row; Certification requirement: LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification plus Google certification
* Australia, France, Ireland, New Zealand; Recovery-oriented addiction ads eligibility on Google: Government entities only; Certification requirement: Google certification
* Unlisted locations; Recovery-oriented addiction ads eligibility on Google: Not listed for standard certified promotion; Certification requirement: Confirm live location table in Google Ads before building campaigns
Even between the United States and Canada, where both countries now have a LegitScript-plus-Google certification path, campaigns are not automatically interchangeable. Each market requires its own verification steps, location-accurate landing pages, and account-level confirmation before media spend goes live.
### Medication terms and opioid language follow separate rules
Treatment programs and medical practices that discuss specific pharmaceuticals, including medication-assisted treatment options, can encounter prescription-drug advertising rules that operate independently from addiction-services certification.
For campaigns targeting Canada, New Zealand, or the United States, an advertiser does not need certification merely to use prescription-drug terms in ads and landing pages, but must be certified to keyword-target those terms, according to the [Google Ads October 2025 healthcare and medicines update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16606361?hl=en-AU&ref_topic=16083443). Certification is also required for specific business categories, including online pharmacies, telemedicine providers, and pharmaceutical manufacturers, and promotional use of prescription-drug terms is not allowed in campaigns targeting locations other than Canada, New Zealand, and the United States under the [Google Ads October 2025 healthcare and medicines update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16606361?hl=en-AU&ref_topic=16083443).
Opioid terminology carries an even narrower boundary. Google does not allow ads promoting or selling prescription opioid painkillers, though that exclusion does not cover medication-assisted treatment drugs for opioid use disorder that satisfy Google's other prescription-drug requirements, according to the [Google Ads July 2024 opioid policy update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15215456?hl=en) and the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en). Limited exceptions permit certain advertisers, including certified addiction treatment providers in the United States, to refer to opioids where allowed under the [Google Ads July 2024 opioid policy update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15215456?hl=en).
The operational lesson is direct. Treat medication names in keyword lists, ad headlines, and page headings as policy decisions rather than copywriting variations. Review the exact search term, the destination page copy, the target geography, and the account's active certifications inside the same sign-off workflow.
### What a LegitScript certificate does and does not settle
LegitScript describes Addiction Treatment Certification as intended for organizations that provide substance use disorder treatment or recovery services and meet its standards, according to the [LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/). Its public FAQ states that certification may be required for certain advertising programs, while certification alone does not establish automatic ad approval because each platform enforces its own policies and decisions, as noted in the [LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/).
Once certified, organizations still have separate platform steps to finish. The [LegitScript certification onboarding guide](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/welcome-to-legitscript-certification/) directs certified organizations to register with Google and to complete Meta's Addiction Treatment Center Ads authorization path, noting that ads must still follow each platform's own rules. Treat those onboarding instructions as your starting checklist, then verify live approval status inside each ad account before scheduling launch dates.
Certification also governs ongoing advertising behavior, not just a one-time application packet. Standard 13 of the [LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification standards](https://www.legitscript.com/addiction-treatment-certification-standards) requires accurate, transparent ads: applicants must identify themselves clearly, must not use third-party business names in ads, must accurately reflect the services available at a given location, and may not claim to operate where they do not provide services. Under that same standard, advertising on Google, Bing, or Facebook without certification, or in apparent violation of platform terms, may be treated as an attempt to circumvent the program, according to the [LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification standards](https://www.legitscript.com/addiction-treatment-certification-standards). Standard 14 adds an explicit operational boundary on patient inducements, stating that incentives such as paying for patient travel generally make an applicant ineligible under the [LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification standards](https://www.legitscript.com/addiction-treatment-certification-standards).
Put Standard 13 and Standard 14 directly into your creative review sheet. Checking ad copy and landing pages against those rules after an account warning arrives is far more expensive than catching a third-party brand bid or an inaccurate city claim before launch.
### Designing paid media without retargeting
Marketing Powered is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware, and it is built for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted. That restriction shapes campaign architecture from the very first spreadsheet. It is an operating boundary for healthcare paid media, not a claim that any software tool, pixel setting, or agency workflow confers HIPAA certification.
HIPAA awareness in marketing starts before any script is placed on a website. It means classifying what data a marketing platform is permitted to receive before deploying a form, call-tracking script, chat widget, or analytics tag. Never push patient-identifying details or treatment-seeking health information into an advertising platform on the assumption that a vendor's marketing brochure resolves your legal obligations.
In day-to-day campaign management, building without retargeting forces healthier discipline. Prospecting and high-intent search must earn the inquiry on the first visit through clear service alignment, verifiable facility details, fast mobile load times, and an accessible phone or intake form. Separate drug and alcohol addiction campaigns from general mental health campaigns whenever certification requirements, clinical offers, or landing pages diverge. Discard any growth playbook that relies on remarketing audiences, unrestricted lookalike expansion built from sensitive patient lists, or unstaffed city-name landing pages. Inside regulated healthcare, respecting the platform constraint is what keeps the account running month after month.
Compare inquiry counts with admissions
Set three reports side by side on the conference table and refuse to let them share a single column header. Blending ad-platform events, intake logs, and clinical admissions into one dashboard number is the fastest way to hide a broken acquisition funnel.
The first report is the ad-platform export. It tracks media spend, impressions, clicks, and whatever technical event the account is configured to record as a conversion. That event might be a click on a mobile phone link, a completed web form, a live chat start, or an inbound call lasting longer than sixty seconds. The ad platform is not sitting at your admissions desk. It cannot tell whether the caller needed a level of care you actually deliver, whether the insurance plan was accepted, or whether a bed or clinician slot was open that week.
The second report belongs to intake or central scheduling. It records inquiries that a trained staff member classified as in scope across clinical need, geography, payer fit, and timing. Just as important, it records out-of-scope inquiries along with standardized reason codes. A ninety-second phone call is not automatically a failure if the person booked an assessment quickly, and a twelve-minute call is not automatically a win if the caller needed a service your clinic does not offer. Reason codes turn subjective complaints about lead quality into diagnosable system defects.
The third report belongs to admissions, billing, or clinical scheduling. For a treatment provider, the closing event is a confirmed admission. For an outpatient mental health practice, it is an attended first appointment. For a hospital service line, it is the kept clinical visit or procedure that department already tracks in its operational reporting. Marketing Powered focuses on attribution tracked to admission, not just lead, where the organization is a treatment provider and authorized data paths support that connection. Outside addiction treatment, the same discipline applies by tying acquisition spend to the clinical or commercial event the operator already uses to run the business.
* Report 1: Platform export; Operational owner: Paid media buyer; Core fields recorded: Spend, clicks, queries, tag-fired conversions, conversion value per cost; What it accurately measures: Auction pressure, query intent, ad relevance, landing-page engagement; What it cannot prove on its own: Clinical fit, payer acceptance, staffing capacity, real patient volume
* Report 2: Intake log; Operational owner: Intake or scheduling lead; Core fields recorded: Qualified inquiries, out-of-scope inquiries, standardized reason codes, response time; What it accurately measures: Offer accuracy, routing accuracy, call quality, form usefulness; What it cannot prove on its own: Whether benefits cleared or whether the person arrived for care
* Report 3: Admissions or kept visits; Operational owner: Admissions director, clinic manager, finance; Core fields recorded: Confirmed admissions or kept first visits, site, service line, matched source ID; What it accurately measures: Real commercial output and true acquisition economics by site; What it cannot prove on its own: Which specific keywords or ads wasted spend before intake intervened
### Measure paid search by conversion value per cost
Raw cost per click tells you how competitive the search auction is, and raw cost per lead only measures how cheaply your tracking tag fires. When you evaluate PPC performance across sites, levels of care, or service lines, measure against conversion value per cost, where higher is better.
Using conversion value per cost forces two practical habits. First, it requires the team to assign realistic relative value to verified downstream stages rather than treating every phone click as equal. A qualified residential assessment or a kept psychiatry consultation does not carry the same business value as an unverified general contact form. Second, it stops media buyers from chasing cheap, low-fit clicks simply to make a cost-per-lead chart look lower while admissions stay flat.
Never collapse the three reports into one blended number and present it as audited fact. Cost per platform conversion describes the tag. Cost per qualified inquiry reflects intake's written criteria. Cost per admission reflects the full path only when admissions are reconciled back to acquisition sources using rules finance and marketing both signed off on. If half your admissions lack a matched source record, report the unmatched share plainly. A modeled estimate is a planning aid, not a measured outcome.
### Diagnose the two conversion gaps
Separating the three reports exposes two distinct operational gaps, and each gap has a different fix.
The first gap sits between Report 1 (platform conversions) and Report 2 (qualified intake inquiries). Common causes include a tracking number that rings the wrong campus, a mobile form that omits the screening question intake needs, a landing page that lists a level of care that site does not deliver, or a tag firing on button clicks instead of completed inquiries. This is a marketing, routing, and conversion-system problem. Adding media budget before closing this gap simply buys more misrouted traffic.
The second gap sits between Report 2 (qualified intake inquiries) and Report 3 (admissions or kept visits). Common causes include slow call-back speed, weekend or after-hours coverage holes, slow insurance verification, payer contract limits, or full beds and clinician schedules. This is primarily an intake and clinical operations constraint, though marketing still needs visibility into it so budgets can be shifted toward sites and services that have real open capacity.
Any resource or agency recommendation that tells you to optimize ad creative without first identifying which gap is leaking volume will spend your time on the wrong layer of the business.
### Run a twenty-inquiry manual trace before buying software
Before signing a new analytics contract or rebuilding a CRM, run a manual audit for one week. Select twenty recent inquiries from one active campaign and trace each record by hand across all three reports. Write down the timestamp and campaign from the platform, the intake disposition and reason code, and the final admission or kept-visit status. Twenty traced records reveal more operational truth than an automated dashboard built on unverified assumptions.
Look for four concrete failure patterns during that trace:
* Tag fired with no intake record: The ad platform counted a conversion, but intake has no matching call or form. Owner: web developer and analytics lead fixing trigger logic or call routing.
* Reached intake but out of scope: The person connected with staff, but needed an unoffered service, lived outside the service area, or carried an unaccepted payer. Owner: media buyer and content lead tightening keyword exclusions, ad copy, and landing-page eligibility statements.
* In scope with no timely next step: The caller matched clinical, location, and payer criteria, yet waited hours for a callback or dropped during benefits verification. Owner: intake supervisor and operations lead reviewing staffing hours and handoff speed.
* Admitted or attended with lost source: The patient arrived for care, but the clinical or scheduling record carries no link back to the original inquiry. Owner: CRM administrator and operations lead repairing intake source capture without exposing protected health information to ad platforms.
Hospitals can execute this exact twenty-record walk by placing central scheduling or clinic visit status in the third column instead of residential admissions. Professional services firms can place accepted partner consultations in the third column rather than white-paper downloads. The method stays constant because the goal is always reconciliation against an event the organization already trusts.
Records to assemble before launch
A compliance and readiness review that begins after headlines are drafted starts too late. Assemble four compact working records before turning on paid media or publishing major site updates. These records do not replace legal counsel or licensure review; they give legal counsel, clinical leaders, platform reviewers, and media buyers one shared factual baseline.
### Maintain a sourced claims and location log
Every public statement about clinical services, accreditations, staff credentials, accepted insurance relationships, or physical locations belongs in a version-controlled claims log. Each entry needs three fields: the exact approved wording, the internal owner who verified it, and the primary source document supporting it, such as a state facility license, an active accreditation record, a current payer roster, or a clinical director's written sign-off.
If the only justification for a claim is that a previous agency wrote it on an old brochure, pull the claim until someone verifies it. Healthcare copy must remain accurate, specific about available services, and non-stigmatizing. Never imply clinical diagnosis, never imply that a marketing message or intake call delivers a treatment effect, and never make outcome promises about recovery or clinical results.
Pair the claims log with a location truth sheet. For every physical site named in ads, on the website, or inside local business profiles, verify that five elements match across every surface: street address, live intake or scheduling hours, the dedicated phone number reaching staff authorized for that site, the exact levels of care or clinical specialties available there, and active licensure for those services. A city name inside a search keyword is not a physical facility. Even for outpatient practices or professional firms with regional service areas, describing on-site presence where no office exists breaks trust and triggers platform enforcement.
### Document advertising permissions by country and service
When a campaign touches recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, build a permissions file before uploading keywords. Record whether the entity is eligible for LegitScript certification, the current certification status, the public verification URL, the date someone last checked that status, and confirmation that Google Ads and Meta authorizations are active for every country in the media plan.
Do not omit this file if your organization operates as a lead generator or referral agency under the theory that only inpatient facilities face review. Google explicitly lists lead generators and referral agencies among its in-scope examples in the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines: Addiction services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en-AU), while United States advertisers that are ineligible for LegitScript certification cannot advertise those services on Google under the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en).
If campaigns mention or keyword-target prescription medications, add a separate section documenting prescription-drug certification status and confirming that target countries stay inside permitted locations under the [Google Ads October 2025 healthcare and medicines update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16606361?hl=en-AU&ref_topic=16083443). If opioid terms appear anywhere on destination pages, document how those references remain within the medication-assisted treatment distinction and limited exceptions outlined in the [Google Ads July 2024 opioid policy update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15215456?hl=en). Keep general mental health ad groups cleanly separated from addiction-treatment ad groups so one permission change never breaks unrelated campaigns.
### Map every vendor, field, and data path
List every technology vendor that touches a visitor or an inquiry: call-tracking providers, web form tools, chat widgets, analytics scripts, advertising tags, CRM databases, email automation tools, and SMS platforms. For each tool, document four facts:
* Exactly which data fields the tool collects or receives.
* Whether any collected field can contain health information, clinical questions, or treatment-seeking status.
* Which internal executive or compliance officer approved that data path and vendor agreement.
* How long the vendor retains the record and who controls deletion.

### Write a one-page launch note with named owners
Before approving launch spend, summarize the pre-launch file on a single page and circulate it to marketing, intake, clinical operations, and finance. Include seven items:
* Campaign objective, monthly budget cap, and active date range.
* Specific locations and clinical services included in scope, plus those explicitly excluded.
* Active certification and platform authorization status by country, or written confirmation that the campaign does not promote addiction services or restricted medications.
* Approved public claims and prohibited phrasing for ad copy and landing pages.
* Exact conversion events recorded in the ad platform and the downstream commercial event governing budget decisions.
* Standardized intake disposition codes and callback time targets.
* Named owners responsible for the monthly three-report review.
Without a one-page launch note, teams end up debating scope, clinical language, and reporting definitions inside ad-account change logs after money has already been spent.
Paid media, landing pages, and conversion systems
Once site truth, capacity, permissions, and measurement rules are fixed, execution inside paid media, landing pages, and call systems determines how much qualified demand each dollar produces. In regulated healthcare, disciplined structure outperforms aggressive automation every time.
### Structure search campaigns around verified intent and site boundaries
Because retargeting is not permitted in Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, search campaigns carry heavy responsibility for capturing high-intent demand cleanly on the first interaction. Structure accounts around three clear separation lines: clinical permission tier, physical location or service area, and level of care or specialty.
Never mix recovery-oriented addiction keywords and general outpatient mental health keywords inside the same campaign. Their platform permissions, landing-page compliance disclosures, payer profiles, and intake routing paths differ. Mixing them forces automated bidding to optimize toward whichever bucket fires tags more cheaply, usually dragging budget away from higher-value clinical needs.
Apply strict negative keyword governance before launch and during weekly query reviews. Exclude job seekers, academic researchers, free public hotlines where your organization only provides private clinical care, out-of-footprint states, and unoffered levels of care. Every irrelevant search term removed before the click protects both conversion value per cost and intake staff bandwidth.
When reviewing campaign performance, judge account changes by whether they raise conversion value per cost and increase qualified intake volume at sites with open capacity. A campaign restructure that doubles click-through rate while increasing out-of-scope calls is a regression, not an improvement.
### Use multi variant optimization and strict message match on landing pages
A landing page in behavioral health or hospital marketing has one job: confirm immediately that the visitor reached the right clinical service at a real location they can access, then make the next step unmistakable.
Match the landing-page headline directly to the ad and search query that brought the visitor to the page. If a prospective patient or family member searches for outpatient psychiatry in a specific metro area and clicks an ad naming that clinic, the headline above the fold should confirm outpatient psychiatry at that named location, not greet them with a broad corporate mission statement. Strong message match reduces bounce waste and directly improves conversion value per cost.
Put verifiable proof above the fold where a hurried visitor can read it in five seconds: real location details, clinical accreditations or licensure where approved in your claims log, accepted insurance categories if verified, and clear hours of operation. Keep intake forms short enough to finish on a phone without collecting unnecessary clinical essays, and place a tappable phone number prominently for mobile visitors who prefer speaking to intake directly.
For organizations running high-volume paid traffic across multiple service lines or locations, ML-driven landing page routing, known as multi variant optimization, helps pair incoming search context with the most effective approved page variant. Keep one hard boundary around multi variant optimization in healthcare: every page variant entering the test pool must pass the exact same claims log, location-truth check, and platform policy review as a static page. Automated routing may decide which approved layout converts qualified visitors more reliably; it must never generate unreviewed clinical wording or send a visitor to a facility that cannot serve them.
### Connect call handling and form intake to downstream records
Paid search and landing pages fail quietly when the telephone and form handoff break down. Treat call tracking and intake routing as core conversion infrastructure rather than an IT afterthought.
Assign distinct tracking numbers to clearly defined acquisition sources and site combinations so intake and marketing can see which campaigns drive calls to which locations without scrambling regional attribution. Verify regularly that every tracking number rings directly to live staff trained on that site's services, rather than looping through an outdated menu tree or dropping into an unmonitored voicemail box after business hours.
Keep privacy boundaries sharp in every call-tracking and CRM integration. Qualify what recording, transcription, and data-storage features are enabled, verify who holds administrative access, and confirm that raw patient-identifying details or clinical disclosures are never transmitted back into advertising platforms. Accurate tracking implementation tells you which campaign, landing page, and keyword group produced a qualified inquiry and downstream admission using authorized, privacy-aware identifiers. It does not require turning an ad platform into a clinical database.
Multi-location operations, hospitals, and operator proof
Growth across multiple facilities exposes every shortcut a single-site organization used to survive. Multi-location marketing typically breaks in one predictable pattern: corporate headquarters writes one broad service promise, each individual site actually delivers a different subset of services, and the website, paid ads, and business profiles broadcast the headquarters version everywhere.
Phone calls then land at offices or treatment centers that cannot fulfill the request. Consolidated corporate reports look active because top-of-funnel call counts rise, while site-level census and kept-visit totals refuse to move.
### Centralize standards while keeping facility facts local
Scaling acquisition across sites requires dividing governance cleanly between what headquarters must lock down and what each staffed location must control.
* Brand and compliance claims; Headquarters standard (centralize): Approved corporate facts, prohibited medical claims, claims-log review rules; Site-level reality (keep local): Site-specific licenses, accreditations, and approved specialty descriptions
* Platform permissions; Headquarters standard (centralize): Account-level certifications, country policy tracking, negative keyword baselines; Site-level reality (keep local): Exact services eligible to advertise for that specific address
* Measurement definitions; Headquarters standard (centralize): Written definitions of qualified inquiry, conversion value per cost, and admission or kept visit; Site-level reality (keep local): Local intake disposition logging, bed or schedule capacity updates
* Website and local listings; Headquarters standard (centralize): Page templates, technical performance standards, schema structure, analytics hygiene; Site-level reality (keep local): Street address, real intake hours, site phone number, clinicians or levels of care on site
* Call and form routing; Headquarters standard (centralize): Tracking governance, vendor security review, reporting hierarchy; Site-level reality (keep local): Live staff answering for that location and site-specific arrival instructions
Pointing a national keyword list at one corporate homepage forces visitors to figure out your footprint themselves and misroutes inquiries. Publishing dozens of cloned city pages that swap out the town name over identical boilerplate text creates inaccurate location claims that fail both user trust and platform review. The durable alternative is narrower and stronger: publish a dedicated location page wherever a staffed facility exists, describe only the care delivered at that site, route inquiries to staff who can act for that site, and measure spend against that site's own qualified demand and downstream volume.
### Apply facility truth to hospital service lines and professional offices
Hospital systems face the same multi-site problem under more complex organizational labels. Inpatient units, outpatient clinics, employed physician groups, joint ventures, and community behavioral health programs may all sit under one health-system brand while running on different scheduling systems and legal entities.
If a six-hospital health system offers inpatient and outpatient behavioral health at only two campuses, paid search and landing pages must name those two campuses explicitly and route callers straight into their scheduling workflow. Advertising behavioral health across the footprint of all six hospitals creates misdirected calls that other campuses cannot schedule. Likewise, do not force a hospital behavioral health service line into a standalone residential treatment center page layout, and do not evaluate hospital service-line campaigns with residential census language if the department measures kept outpatient appointments.
Multi-office professional services firms can borrow this exact governance split without touching healthcare compliance files. Keep brand standards, tracking hygiene, and qualified-consultation definitions centralized, while tying every office page and local search profile to real staffed offices and practice areas delivered on site.

### Does LegitScript certification mean addiction treatment ads can run?
No. For the United States, Google's policy requires LegitScript certification for recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, and services that are not eligible are not allowed to advertise them on Google under the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en). Other countries on Google's location table do not use that same sentence, so confirm the exact row for every country in your plan. LegitScript notes in the [LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/) that certification may be required for certain advertising programs while each platform controls its own approval decisions, and Standard 13 of the [LegitScript Addiction Treatment Certification standards](https://www.legitscript.com/addiction-treatment-certification-standards) still requires transparent self-identification and location-accurate claims on every ad and landing page.
### Are mental health practices under the same advertising rules as addiction treatment?
Not automatically. Google's addiction-services policy applies specifically to recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, placing impulse-control, behavioral-addiction, and nicotine services outside that policy according to the [Google Ads Healthcare and medicines: Addiction services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en-AU). An outpatient therapy or psychiatry group that does not advertise substance use disorder treatment should not copy an addiction-certification workflow as its entire compliance plan. That practice still needs accurate location records, HIPAA-aware vendor mapping, and prescription-drug certification checks if it keyword-targets medication terms in Canada, New Zealand, or the United States under the [Google Ads October 2025 healthcare and medicines update](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16606361?hl=en-AU&ref_topic=16083443).
