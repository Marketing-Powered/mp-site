Market Intelligence vs Market Research: Choose the Work That Closes the Loop
A monthly media budget can clear every review and still leave admissions unable to name which dollars produced a patient. The market intelligence vs market research choice decides whether that gap is met with a periodic snapshot or with a system that joins spend to an admission. In regulated markets, buying the snapshot when the budget needs the join is how operators keep funding slides.

Why the labels matter less than the loop
Picture a healthcare marketing office on a Monday. Two operators sit at a conference table with a laptop open to last month's media dashboard. On the wall, a printed campaign calendar runs across the quarter, and someone is pointing at a date block for a service-line push. Spend by campaign is visible. Qualified inquiries are partly visible. Admissions, or the professional-services equivalent of a signed engagement, are in another system, under another definition, owned by another team.
That room is where the labels usually fail. A proposal can call the work market research, competitive intelligence, marketing intelligence, or business intelligence and still describe three different jobs: a bounded study, a watch on the outside market, and analysis of records the organization already holds. Vendors overlap the names because buyers overlap them. The useful split is not the vocabulary on the cover. It is whether the output changes the next allocation by linking acquisition activity to a downstream outcome.
A study can be rigorous and still leave the calendar untouched. A dashboard can be current and still count the wrong event. The commercial cost shows up as media that keeps optimizing to whatever the platform can see, usually a click, a form, or a raw call, while finance keeps asking a question the report was never built to answer.
The loop that matters has three stages and no extra theater: media spend, a qualified inquiry, and the outcome the organization already uses to judge growth. For a hospital service line that outcome may be a kept appointment or a completed procedure. For a multi-location healthcare service it is often an admission. For a professional services firm it is a signed engagement. If the meeting ends by commissioning another snapshot and nobody owns the missing column, the organization bought a label.
Market research as periodic input
Market research, used properly, is a project with an end. It starts from a decision that has a date, a signer, and a finding that would change the answer. It picks an evidence base, states what that evidence cannot see, and delivers a memo, a dataset, or a model. Then it stops. That shape is a strength. A lease, a capital request, a fee change, or a go-or-no-go on a new outpatient site does not need a permanent monitoring retainer. It needs a study whose assumptions are still true on the day someone signs.
The inputs are familiar because they are the right ones for a point-in-time unknown. Population and payer mix for a geography. Provider supply and drive time. Referral relationships that do not show up in an ad account. Buyer interviews when the question is why late-stage work is lost. Win and loss notes when the CRM already holds the deals. A message test before a service line is renamed. Each of those has a shelf life. Demographics move slowly. Competitor offers and hiring can move in weeks. A study that does not print an expiry date next to each input invites the organization to treat last quarter's finding as this month's fact.
Research becomes expensive when it is asked to do a job it cannot repeat. Paid media is not a one-time unknown. Bids, queries, creative, intake staffing, and location mix move on an operating calendar, not on a fieldwork calendar. A 60-page readout of a metro can inform where to open. It cannot tell a buyer, six weeks later, which campaigns are producing admissions and which are producing calls that never reach a scheduler. If the only artifact in the budget meeting is the readout, the media plan keeps its own score.
Treat research as an input with a job description. Write the decision, the date, the reversal condition, and the sources that are in or out. If primary interviews are "possible" rather than scoped, they are not in the study. If the finding would not change the lease, the price, or the service mix, the study is optional confidence, and confidence is a poor line item unless it changes the size or the pace of the commitment.
Market intelligence as an operating system
Market intelligence, in the sense that changes a media budget, is not a longer research project and not a synonym for more slides. It is the continuous operating layer that keeps acquisition activity tied to outcomes and gives someone the authority to move money when the tie moves. External watching belongs in that layer. It is not the layer.
A workable system has four parts. The question is an allocation question, not a topic: which campaigns, geos, or locations should receive the next dollar, and which should lose it. The cadence matches how fast the organization can actually respond. A weekly brief for a team that reallocates quarterly is subscription content. The join runs from spend to the named outcome, not to a proxy chosen because it is easy to pixel. An owner, not a distribution list, changes bids, creative, intake coverage, or location mix when the join shifts.
Outside signals still matter. Competitor offers, public capacity moves, hiring, and positioning tell you why a market may have changed. They do not tell you whether your spend produced the outcome. Internal reporting tells you what your own pipeline did. It is silent on demand that never reached you. Intelligence is the practice of holding those streams to one decision, with the limits of each stream written down.
That operating view comes from the founder's history, not from an agency case file. The founder is a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations, and managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts, with attribution tracked to admission, not just lead. The practice is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware, and it is built for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted. Those constraints are why a continuous join matters more than another taxonomy of research products. A network that cannot follow a visitor with a remarketing list has to know, from its own records, which acquisition paths reach an admission.
The same logic travels past behavioral health. A hospital marketing team justifying service-line spend, a professional services firm defending search against a long sales cycle, and a regulated local business with several offices all face the same test. If the output does not change next period's allocation against a real outcome, it is research support or reporting. It is not an intelligence system, whatever the proposal calls it.
Attribution requirements that separate the two
The separation is mechanical. Research can describe a market without ever touching an ad account. Intelligence, as an operating system, fails if it cannot carry an identifier from the click or visit through intake and back to a milestone. The picture is a short path, not a matrix of methods: media spend, qualified inquiry, admission.
Start with the visit. A click identifier or session identifier has to be captured before the person leaves the page. On a form, that means the identifier is written to the inquiry record, not left in a browser cookie that dies at the handoff. On a call, which is often the highest-intent path in healthcare and in other urgent categories, a static published number collapses every campaign into one bucket. Dynamic number insertion, described in [how call tracking works](/resources/how-does-call-tracking-work/), gives the call a session context so the inquiry is not logged as direct traffic. The operating detail lives in [call tracking](/resources/call-tracking/) and in [marketing measurement](/resources/marketing-measurement/): the raw call is an inquiry, and the conversion the business cares about happens later.
Next, the record has to survive intake. Marketing and the desk that answers need one definition of a qualified inquiry, one small set of dispositions, and a rule for who updates the status. A [healthcare intake data dictionary](/resources/healthcare-intake-marketing-data-dictionary/) is useful here because it forces the same words in the ad account, the call platform, and the CRM. Without that, "qualified" means a duration threshold in one system and a clinical or commercial screen in another, and the join is a story.
Then the milestone has to travel back. When an inquiry becomes a scheduled assessment, an admission, or a signed engagement, the CRM should be able to emit a privacy-safe offline conversion: identifier, time, and a value the finance team accepts. [Attribution in Salesforce and HubSpot](/resources/marketing-attribution-in-salesforce/) is the same pattern under two common systems of record. What must not travel back is a diagnosis, a transcript, or any other clinical detail. The platform needs a milestone. It does not need the reason for care.
Platform rules make this join more important, not less. Google treats health as a sensitive interest category. The category includes physical or mental health conditions, products and services that treat or manage chronic conditions, invasive procedures, and disabilities. Counseling for issues such as depression, anxiety, and addiction is an explicit example. For ads in that category, advertiser-curated audiences are not allowed, including Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion, and lookalike segments, because those audiences may contain sensitive user signals. Predefined Google audiences may still be used, because sensitive signals are excluded from them. Custom segments that use sensitive creative or point to sensitive landing pages are limited: they serve only with Display campaigns, to non-sensitive audiences or contextually. ([Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)) [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
That is the practical meaning of a vertical where retargeting is not permitted. The ecommerce pattern of cold traffic, a visitor list, and a lookalike does not transfer to a clinic, a hospital service line, or a counseling practice advertising a covered condition. Search intent, location, and the outcomes you upload under policy still do work. A research deck about "the patient journey" does not replace the missing list. If the organization also spends on other platforms, those rules should be read from the current policy, not assumed to match Google's.
Google also restricts promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Advertisers may promote them in certain locations only if certified by Google. Services unrelated to drug and alcohol addiction, such as impulse-control disorders, behavioral addiction, or nicotine addiction, sit outside that specific restriction. ([Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en)) [[2]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) Certification posture is an operating constraint on what can run, not a substitute for attribution. A certified account that optimizes to raw calls still cannot tell a budget owner which dollars reached an admission.
Data sovereignty in regulated verticals
The join only stays legitimate if the marketing layer is not the clinical record. First-party data, in this setting, means the organization decides what is collected, where it sits, who can see it, and what is allowed to leave. It does not mean every field on an intake form is fair game for an ad platform, a survey vendor, or a public model.
A practical split keeps the systems honest. The marketing record holds a lead identifier, campaign and click context, timestamps, and the commercial milestone. The intake or CRM record holds contact details and whatever the care or client team must know to do their job. The two meet on the lead identifier. They do not meet by pasting a transcript into a spreadsheet that later gets uploaded. [First-party data strategy](/resources/first-party-data-strategy/) is the discipline of building that join on purpose. [Data sovereignty](/ai-edge/data-sovereignty/) is the related question of which services sit in the path and what leaves the environment.
Healthcare makes the split non-optional. Call recordings and chat logs often contain exactly the information that must not reach an advertising endpoint. A form that asks for a condition, a medication, or an insurance member identifier is a different object from a form that asks for a preferred time and a consent to be contacted. An integration that "just syncs the CRM" can move the wrong object if nobody mapped fields before the webhook was turned on. A [HIPAA-aware CRM review](/resources/hipaa-compliant-crm/) is about that mapping and the postback, not about a badge on a vendor slide. Marketing Powered does not claim HIPAA certification. The work is HIPAA-aware: scope what moves, keep clinical content out of ad platforms, and do not treat a workflow as a compliance conclusion.
Owned, local AI infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference fits in the same boundary. Classification of allowed, non-clinical fields, or a review of whether a transcript should be summarized at all, can be run where the organization controls the environment. That is a control over path and exposure. It is not a claim that every workload stays on agency hardware, that client data ownership changes because hardware is owned, or that a model makes a process compliant. AI-native operations since 2022 are useful when they shorten the time from a milestone change to a readable allocation view, with a person still accountable for the rule that fired.
Professional services and other regulated local businesses have a quieter version of the same problem. A matter description, a financial-hardship note, or a client list is not a remarketing file. The intelligence layer should be able to say which offer and which office produced signed work without exporting the file that proves why the client called.
When research is the right tool
Research is the right purchase when the unknown sits outside your records and the decision will be made once. Use it for site selection, for a pricing change that needs competitor packaging and buyer interviews, for a message that has not been in market, and for a market-entry question whose answer will still hold when the board meets. Scope it to the reversal condition. If no finding would flip the recommendation, stop before the proposal.
Intelligence is required when spend is already continuous and someone must move it. That includes a health system running always-on search for several service lines, a multi-location operator comparing offices that share a brand and do not share a market, and a professional services firm whose signed work lands months after the first click. It also includes any account in a sensitive health category that cannot rebuild demand with a visitor list. In those accounts, the cost of a missing join is not theoretical. The platform will optimize to the event you give it. If you give it a call, you buy calls.
The two are not rivals, and buying both by default is how scopes bloat. A study can set the hypothesis: this geography, this service line, this message, this competitive set. The operating system tests the hypothesis against admissions or signed engagements and retires it when it expires. A monitoring retainer is justified when the outside condition changes faster than a study can hold and the team can act inside the monitoring interval. If pricing, staffing, and market entry are annual decisions, weekly competitive memos will mostly accumulate unread.
Read a proposal against that split. A vendor who leads with methodology and ends with a PDF is selling research, which may be exactly what a dated decision needs. A vendor who leads with a dashboard and cannot name the milestone, the identifier, or the field that is forbidden to leave is not selling intelligence. They are selling a view of the top of the funnel with a more expensive label.
Build the minimum intelligence layer
The decision often looks like one person at a desk, a printed study on one side and a tablet of live campaign and pipeline figures on the other. The question is not which document is thicker. It is which artifact tells you what to do with next month's budget, and which one will be wrong by the time you act.
A minimum layer is a sequence, not a platform purchase. Name one outcome that finance already respects. Admission, kept procedure, or signed engagement. Do not invent a softer event so the chart looks full. Capture click or session context on every form and every tracked call, and write it to the inquiry before the conversation starts. Give intake or sales a disposition list short enough to be used on a busy day, and make "source unknown" a visible failure rather than a default. Review spend, qualified inquiries, and the downstream milestone by campaign and by location on a cadence the media owner can act on. Send back to the ad platform only the milestone and a value, never the clinical or matter content. File external research as a hypothesis with an expiry date, and reopen it only when the outcome data contradicts it or the market assumption dates out.
Multi-location work breaks this layer when each office keeps its own definition of qualified. [Multi-location marketing](/resources/multi-location-marketing-strategy/) is an intelligence problem before it is a creative problem: the network total is not comparable if one site logs a booked assessment and another logs a raw call under the same campaign name. The calendar on the wall can still coordinate launches. The join has to use one code set, or the comparison across sites is a research anecdote.
None of this requires a new name for the team. It requires an owner for the identifier, an owner for the disposition, and an owner for the reallocation. Research vendors can still be hired into step one of a specific decision. They should not be hired to stand in for the six steps above. If a current stack already holds the click identifier and the CRM milestone, the minimum layer may be a join and a weekly read, not a new contract. If those fields do not exist, no study will create them.
A checklist for budget owners
Before the next statement of work, a budget owner can score the request in one sitting. The point is to refuse work that cannot change an action, and to refuse a retainer that cannot see an outcome.
Write the decision in the signer’s words, with the date it will be made even if no new evidence arrives. Name the finding that would reverse the current plan. If you cannot name it, you are buying reassurance. Separate outside unknowns from records you already hold, and check the CRM, call platform, and billing or admissions system before you pay for interviews about your own customers. Mark each input with how fast it goes stale. Put research on the items that are external, dated, and decisive. Put the operating layer on anything that must still be true next month when the media invoice arrives.
For any work described as intelligence, require five proofs in the scope. The outcome metric, and the system where it is recorded. The identifier that ties an inquiry to a click or a tracked call. The list of fields that will never be exported. The person who can change spend when the read changes. The cadence, matched to a real reallocation meeting, not to a vendor’s publishing schedule. If those five are missing, the work is a report. Pay for it only if a report is what the decision needs.
For regulated healthcare accounts, add the platform constraint in writing. Which campaigns fall under health in personalized advertising, which audiences are therefore off limits, and which milestones are approved for offline upload. For recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services, confirm the certification path before creative is built. LegitScript-aware planning belongs in that check, alongside Google’s own certification rules for the locations you target. Do not let a research vendor’s competitive tear-down set targeting that the ads policy will not run.
Hold the same standard for hospitals, professional services, and other regulated local businesses. The last stage of the path may not be called an admission. It still has to be the event the organization is willing to fund. A market intelligence vs market research decision that cannot point to that event is not finished, however complete the methodology section looks.
Questions budget owners ask
### Is market intelligence just market research on a retainer?
No. A retainer can still be a series of snapshots. Intelligence, as an operating choice, includes a join from spend to a downstream outcome and a person who changes allocation when that join moves. Ongoing competitor monitoring can be part of the input. It does not, by itself, close the path from media spend to admission or signed engagement.
### Can a strong study replace attribution?
A study can answer a question your records cannot, such as whether a new site has enough demand or how lost buyers describe the decision. It cannot update when bids, queries, or intake capacity change, and it cannot tell the ad platform which inquiries became admissions. Use the study to set a hypothesis. Use the operating layer to test it. If the study is asked to stand in for call tracking, CRM dispositions, and offline conversions, the budget meeting will still be arguing from slides.
### Does the retargeting limit change which work to buy?
Yes, for advertisers promoting covered health interests. Google does not allow advertiser-curated audiences, including your data segments and Customer Match, for that sensitive category, so the visitor-list funnel common in other industries is not available. ([Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)) [[1]](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) That raises the value of first-party outcome data you are allowed to use, and it lowers the value of a research narrative about "nurture" that assumes remarketing. Other platforms have their own current rules. Read those policies before you copy a Google constraint, or a consumer playbook, across accounts.
### What is the smallest layer worth funding?
One shared outcome, capture of click or call context on every inquiry, a disposition list intake will actually complete, a recurring read by campaign and location, and a privacy-safe postback of the milestone. External research is added when a dated decision needs evidence you do not hold. If the identifier and the milestone already exist, fund the join and the meeting that acts on it before you fund another report.
### How should a hospital or a professional services firm adapt the admission standard?
Keep the structure and rename the last stage to the event finance already uses: a kept appointment, a completed procedure, a signed engagement, or another milestone with a real economic meaning. Do not soften the event so media performance looks faster. Apply the same field split. Commercial context can move. Clinical detail, matter detail, and customer lists built on sensitive facts should not. The founder’s attribution practice was tracked to admission because that was the outcome that matched the operating question. Your outcome should match yours, with the same refusal to stop at the lead.
