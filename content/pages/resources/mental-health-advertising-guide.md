Mental Health Advertising: Where to Run Ads and What to Avoid
Mental health advertising is paid promotion that helps people discover a practice, understand its services and request care. For practice owners, the commercial task is connecting appropriate demand with available appointments while controlling claims, targeting and data handling.

The operating principle: qualification before optimization
Start with the operating decisions, not the platform settings: which services need demand, what makes an inquiry qualified, who approves publication and how intake records the outcome.
This guide gives mental health practices, group practices and outpatient clinics a channel-selection framework, a pre-launch review checklist and a measurement worksheet. The objective is accountable acquisition, not activity that stops at a form submission.
Define a qualified inquiry before buying traffic.
A qualified inquiry is a unique new request for care that meets the practice’s documented service, location, age-range and payment criteria, subject to clinical assessment.
This is an operational definition, not a clinical determination. Marketing should not decide whether someone is clinically suitable for treatment.
Use cost per qualified inquiry as a primary acquisition measure. Then examine scheduled and attended first appointments alongside capacity, intake responsiveness and unresolved outcomes.
Publication needs an equally explicit standard. Require named reviewers to approve the claims, destination, targeting and data flow. Treat platform approval as one checkpoint, not permission to skip the practice’s own assessment.
The controlling question is whether the practice can explain the path from advertising expenditure to appropriate requests and appointment outcomes, including where that explanation remains incomplete.
Scope: review the service, not just the practice label
This playbook focuses on outpatient therapy, counseling, psychiatry and related services. Review each advertised service separately.
Google distinguishes recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services from other healthcare categories. In the United States, advertising those addiction services requires LegitScript certification and Google certification. That is not a blanket certification requirement for every counseling practice. [Google Ads healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en).
For programs involving online prescribing, conduct a separate assessment. Google restricts services related to online prescribing, dispensing and prescription-drug sales, and evaluates factors including the advertised offering and website content. [Google Ads prescription drug services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598647).
Do not use “behavioral health” as the entire policy assessment. Document the service, destination, targeted geography and applicable certification category.
Treat this guide as an operating framework. Have an appropriate privacy or legal adviser assess the practice’s obligations and proposed implementation.
Before spending: confirm the practice can receive demand
Prepare a readiness sheet for each advertised service and location.
Capacity: Identify the appointment types to fill, clinicians accepting new patients and scheduling options intake can actually offer. Assign someone to update availability language.
Service fit: Document services offered, ages served, locations and telehealth coverage. Route clinical questions through the clinical team rather than an advertising qualification script.
Payment fit: Confirm approved wording about accepted plans and self-pay arrangements. Avoid broader claims than intake can substantiate.
Response ownership: Assign calls, forms, missed contacts and after-hours requests to named owners. Establish a realistic response standard and a backup.
Outcome recording: Select the approved operational system for inquiry statuses. Define who updates it and how reporting receives aggregate outcomes.
Do not expand spend to compensate for an unexplained handoff problem. First determine whether the constraint is demand, contact handling, appointment availability or missing information.
For multi-location practices, complete this exercise by office and service. A practice-wide availability statement is not a substitute for knowing where a new inquiry can be scheduled.
The acquisition path and its owners
Use this sequence as the reporting backbone:
* Ad interaction: A person interacts with the advertising.
* Inquiry: A call, form submission or scheduling request reaches the practice.
* Qualified inquiry: Intake applies the documented operational criteria.
* Scheduled first appointment: A booking is confirmed.
* Attended first appointment: Attendance is recorded.
At each transition, assign a status owner and identify the system of record.
* What counts as qualified?; Accountable owner: Intake lead; Required evidence: Written stage definitions
* Where should spend go?; Accountable owner: Marketing lead; Required evidence: Service-level channel plan
* Is the message accurate?; Accountable owner: Clinical reviewer; Required evidence: Approved claims and credentials
* Is the data flow acceptable?; Accountable owner: Privacy or legal reviewer; Required evidence: Documented collection and sharing assessment
* Can the practice deliver?; Accountable owner: Practice owner and intake lead; Required evidence: Capacity and routing confirmation
A small practice may assign several responsibilities to one person. It should still record each decision separately.
Step 1: Define qualified inquiries with intake
### Separate contacts from acquisition opportunities
Distinguish new requests from existing-patient contacts, duplicates, employment inquiries, vendor messages and wrong numbers.
Use neutral disposition categories:
* Qualified, scheduling pending.
* Scheduled.
* Service not offered.
* Location or age range not supported.
* Payment arrangement not supported.
* Duplicate or existing-patient contact.
* Unable to determine.
Keep “unable to determine” visible. An unanswered call is not evidence that the caller was unqualified.
For providers using admissions-based PPC, substitute a precisely defined admission or intake-start milestone where appropriate. For outpatient practices, scheduled and attended first appointments may be the more useful endpoints.
### Keep clinical judgment outside marketing qualification
Ask intake to record operational fit without turning the marketing report into a clinical record.
The acquisition team needs to understand whether advertised demand matches the practice’s offering. It does not need diagnoses, clinical notes or questionnaire answers in its dashboard.
Document when the operational qualification occurs. If the status remains provisional until a later intake conversation, show that limitation.
### Establish reconciliation rules
Name an owner and backup for status updates. Decide how to handle duplicate requests, reopened inquiries and conflicting records.
Keep the qualification definition consistent within a reporting cohort. If the practice changes its service or payment criteria, record the effective change and avoid comparing unlike definitions without explanation.
Step 2: Choose channels by patient intent
Use channels for distinct jobs rather than launching everywhere at once.
* Paid search; Proposed job: Respond to an explicit service or provider search; Launch condition: Relevant destination and tested intake route; Evaluation focus: Qualified inquiries and appointments
* Local discovery; Proposed job: Support evaluation of a particular office; Launch condition: Accurate location and service information; Evaluation focus: Qualified location-level contacts
* Paid directories; Proposed job: Reach people comparing providers; Launch condition: Clear placement terms and source identification; Evaluation focus: Qualified inquiries by directory
* Paid social; Proposed job: Introduce a practice, location or service; Launch condition: Reviewed creative, targeting and data plan; Evaluation focus: A defined awareness or inquiry objective
* Video and display; Proposed job: Explain services or build familiarity; Launch condition: Separate budget and evaluation hypothesis; Evaluation focus: Reach, subsequent inquiries and uncertainty
Keep organic local-profile work separate from paid-media expenditure. Both can support discovery, but report them as different activities.
### Mental health Google Ads: begin with a bounded search test
For a practice with capacity and functioning intake, consider a focused search test before adding channels with different objectives.
Organize mental health Google Ads around meaningful service and location differences. Separate reporting when availability, payment arrangements, geography or budget ownership requires a different decision.
Build the test around:
* Services the practice actually provides.
* Relevant locations and approved service areas.
* Accurate payment and scheduling information.
* A landing page matching the advertised offering.
* A working call or appointment-request route.
Review search terms and exclusions on an agreed cadence. Investigate informational searches before excluding them indiscriminately.
Keep landing pages practical: service descriptions, credentials, location, payment information and a clear next step. Avoid making an initial appointment request an unnecessary clinical questionnaire.
### Do not build the plan around visitor-list remarketing
Google treats mental health conditions and related counseling as sensitive health categories. Advertisers promoting these categories cannot use advertiser-curated audiences such as Customer Match and “your data” segments. Do not plan to remarket mental health services through those visitor or customer lists. [Google Ads health personalization policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en).
Google distinguishes those restricted audiences from predefined Google audiences, which remain subject to the policy’s conditions and exceptions. This is not a blanket prohibition on every audience feature. [Google Ads restricted targeting policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465?hl=en).
Policy-reviewed paid search therefore needs targeting review as well as copy review. Check the actual campaign configuration, not just the planning document.
### Give social, video and directories separate objectives
For social campaigns, specify whether the job is introducing an office, explaining a service or generating inquiries. Require a current platform review covering creative, targeting, lead forms and event sharing before launch.
Prefer service-led language such as “Counseling appointments at our downtown office” over copy that presumes the viewer’s condition.
For video and display, ring-fence the test budget. Define what evidence would justify continuing, changing or ending the test. Do not blend awareness results with search inquiry costs and call the combined number acquisition efficiency.
For directories, inspect inquiry routing, cancellation terms, reporting access and source identification before committing.
Step 3: Put publication through a review gate
Before publishing mental health ads, review service claims, credentials, language, targeting, destinations, intake routes and tracking. Record the decision and accountable owner.
Campaign quality assurance should cover the complete journey. Review what the reader sees and what the technology sends.
### Assign approval responsibilities
Marketing: Message consistency, geography, exclusions, budget settings, destinations and version control.
Clinical: Service descriptions, qualifications, treatment language and claims about care.
Privacy or legal: Proposed collection, recipients, contracts, permissions and applicable requirements. Include relevant licensing-board and professional advertising rules in the assessment.
Intake: Availability, payment wording, telephone routing and the next step promised.
Practice owner: Operational trade-offs and launch authorization after required reviews.
Save the approved version and decision date. Reopen review when material changes affect services, claims, audiences, forms, tracking or vendors.
### Write copy that can survive scrutiny
Favor concrete, supportable information: appointment format, office location, clinician qualifications and how scheduling works.
Avoid overstated outcomes and sensational crisis framing. Do not imply that requesting an appointment establishes a diagnosis or treatment plan.
Route testimonials, patient stories, comparative claims and treatment-outcome statements through separate review. Do not treat permission to use a statement as the entire assessment of whether it belongs in an ad.
The trade-off is deliberate: specificity may narrow the message, but it makes the advertised next step easier for intake to deliver.

### Service and message
* Confirm the advertised service and location.
* Check credentials and service descriptions.
* Assign ownership for availability and payment wording.
* Remove stigmatizing language and assumptions about the reader.
* Obtain separate approval for testimonial or outcome claims.
* Match the ad’s promise to the landing page.
### Targeting and destination
* Confirm geography against the approved service area.
* Check applicable platform and certification requirements.
* Review audience settings separately from creative.
* Test telephone, forms and scheduling on mobile.
* Explain what happens after an inquiry.
* Confirm intake can deliver that next step.
### Data and approval
* Document trackers, transmitted fields and recipients.
* Inspect forms, URLs and events for sensitive information.
* Review proposed conversion sharing before activation.
* Assign access and retention responsibilities.
* Record marketing, clinical, privacy and intake decisions.
* Name the person authorized to pause spend.
Keep the completed checklist with the approved campaign version. It supports a repeatable process, not a compliance determination.
Step 4: Instrument the journey without exporting the clinical record
Start with a data-flow inventory: advertising platform, website, forms, telephone system, scheduling tool, intake system and reporting destination.
For each connection, record what is collected, where it goes, who can access it and why it is needed.
### Review tracking in context
HHS states that HIPAA rules apply when covered entities or business associates collect or disclose protected health information through tracking technologies. Its guidance also acknowledges a court ruling vacating the portion that treated an IP address combined with a visit to certain unauthenticated public health pages as sufficient to trigger HIPAA obligations. Avoid classifying every public-page visit as PHI. [HHS online tracking guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html).
Ask the reviewer to assess the actual page context, transmitted fields, recipients, permissions and contracts. Include applicable state privacy and call-recording requirements in that assessment rather than assuming one national workflow resolves everything.
The FTC’s BetterHelp order provides a separate advertising-data example: it prohibited health-data sharing for advertising after allegations involving email addresses, IP addresses and health-questionnaire information disclosed despite privacy promises. [FTC final BetterHelp order announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/07/ftc-gives-final-approval-order-banning-betterhelp-sharing-sensitive-health-data-advertising).
### Separate internal attribution from advertising uploads
Do not make platform uploads the prerequisite for useful internal reporting.
Keep patient-level records in approved operational systems. Give marketing aggregate outcomes or the least detailed approved reporting needed for acquisition decisions.
Before enabling an upload, require field-level assessment of the event, identifiers, context, recipient and permitted purpose. If sharing is not approved, retain outcome reporting internally and document the resulting platform limitation.
Do not treat first-party collection or owned infrastructure as permission for unrestricted reuse.
### Test with synthetic information
Use clearly synthetic test details. Check routing, notifications, duplicates, status updates and failure handling.
Do not enable call recording or transcription by default. Assess necessity, access, retention, notices and permissions first.
Test unanswered calls and unavailable appointments as carefully as successful submissions.
Step 5: Measure qualified demand and appointment progression
Use a defined inquiry cohort with a stated follow-up cutoff. Link scheduled and attended appointments to that cohort rather than dividing current spend by unrelated appointments.
### Cost per qualified inquiry worksheet
Enter aggregate figures only.
* Inquiry cohort; Completion rule: Record inquiry dates and follow-up cutoff
* Acquisition scope; Completion rule: Identify campaign, service and location
* Media spend; Completion rule: Use actual spend for that scope
* Unique inquiries; Completion rule: Deduplicate and remove unrelated contacts
* Qualified inquiries; Completion rule: Apply the intake-approved definition
* Scheduled first appointments; Completion rule: Count linked bookings
* Attended first appointments; Completion rule: Count linked confirmed attendance
* Unresolved outcomes; Completion rule: Report separately
* Cost per qualified inquiry; Completion rule: Media spend ÷ qualified inquiries
* Scheduling rate; Completion rule: Scheduled first appointments ÷ qualified inquiries
* Cost per attended first appointment; Completion rule: Media spend ÷ attended first appointments
When a denominator is zero, report “not calculable,” not zero cost.
Label media-only calculations clearly. If a broader acquisition-cost calculation includes agency fees or other costs, name them rather than silently changing the numerator.
### Operator decision example: investigate the handoff before changing the ad
The following is a hypothetical operating example, not client data.
A practice records inquiries that meet its operational criteria, but their scheduling statuses remain unresolved. The available evidence does not yet establish whether the problem is contact handling, appointment availability, patient preference or incomplete recording.
The next action is reconciliation, not an immediate campaign rewrite.
* Qualification statuses are missing; Decision it supports: Repair outcome recording
* Qualified inquiries await scheduling; Decision it supports: Inspect contact attempts and availability
* Bookings lack attendance updates; Decision it supports: Complete cohort follow-up
* Platform and intake totals differ; Decision it supports: Compare definitions, duplicates and reporting windows
* A location lacks appropriate capacity; Decision it supports: Reallocate or pause that promotion
Use this discipline in both directions. Do not rewrite ads to solve an unexamined scheduling constraint, and do not blame intake before reviewing the demand attracted.
### Keep a decision log
For each material change, record the evidence, action, owner and next review point.
Separate immediate operational checks from cohort evaluation. Broken routing may need action before enough appointment outcomes exist to assess acquisition efficiency.
Allocate budget against qualified demand and deliverable capacity, not inquiry volume alone.
What the data cannot prove
### A conversion label does not establish quality
For this framework, a tracked action becomes a qualified inquiry only after intake applies the agreed criteria. Keep those stages separate.
Use call duration as an investigation signal, not a substitute for qualification. Do not classify a contact solely because it exceeded a time threshold.
### Attribution does not establish incremental demand
Treat source attribution as a record of the connection under your chosen method, not proof that advertising alone caused an appointment.
Keep missing sources and unmatched records visible. Do not force uncertain outcomes into a channel to make reporting look complete.
### Incomplete cohorts do not support final costs
Show unresolved inquiries and the follow-up cutoff. Avoid presenting recent appointment costs as settled while outcomes remain open.
With small samples, make restrained decisions. Examine longer periods where useful and explain changes in capacity, services or measurement before treating differences as campaign effects.
### Keyword CPC is not cost per patient
Do not use a click-cost estimate as a patient-acquisition forecast. A planning model also needs explicit assumptions about inquiries, qualification, scheduling and attendance.
Label those assumptions as estimates. Replace them with measured cohort data when available.
Common mistakes and choosing marketing support
Avoid launching every channel simultaneously, changing qualification criteria mid-report, treating unknown outcomes as failures or letting new tracking bypass review.
For multi-location practices, do not let a blended total hide different service availability or intake constraints.
When evaluating mental health marketing services, ask for:
* A written qualification definition and intake owner.
* A service-specific platform assessment.
* Recorded approval of creative, destinations and tracking.
* Transparent spend and fee reporting.
* Clear account access and export terms.
* A reconciliation process for intake outcomes.
* An explanation of reporting limitations.

FAQ
Q: Can mental health providers advertise on Google?
A: Eligibility depends on the advertised service, destination, geography and targeting. Assess the offering against Google’s healthcare requirements and sensitive-category targeting restrictions rather than relying on the practice’s label. [Google healthcare policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) and [health personalization policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en).
Q: Does every therapist need LegitScript certification?
A: Do not assume a universal requirement. Google specifies certification requirements for particular services, including US recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Review the actual offering and website before applying a certification rule. [Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en).
Q: What should providers review before publishing ads?
A: Review claims, credentials, language, targeting, destinations, intake routing and data transfers. Assign owners and record decisions. Reopen the assessment when a material change affects the approved setup.
Q: How should advertising be measured against calls or admissions?
A: Count unique qualified inquiries, then reconcile scheduling, attendance or the program’s defined admission milestone. Use a matching inquiry cohort and disclose unresolved outcomes. Do not substitute call duration for intake qualification.
Q: Should a practice start with search or social?
A: Consider a bounded search test when the priority is appointment inquiries and the practice has capacity, approved messaging and functioning intake. Give social a separate objective and evaluation plan. This is a sequencing recommendation, not a universal performance claim.
Q: What is a reasonable advertising budget?
A: Build it around service priorities, available appointments and a test the practice can afford to evaluate. State the assumptions explicitly. Avoid setting the budget from click costs alone or expanding spend before understanding the intake constraint.
