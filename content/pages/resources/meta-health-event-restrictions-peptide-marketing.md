Meta Health Event Restrictions: What Peptide Marketers Need to Know
Most teams searching for Meta health event restrictions peptides are looking at one blocked custom conversion, not a disabled ad account. Those are different decisions with different remediation paths. The fix almost always starts with what data reached Meta, not with what the event was called.

Identify which object Meta actually restricted
Before anyone touches an event, write down the object type, the object ID, the date and the exact notice text. Meta's developer documentation states that beginning September 2, 2025 it began rolling out more proactive restrictions on custom conversions that may suggest information not permitted under its terms, naming conditions such as arthritis and diabetes as examples, and that flagged conversions cannot be used to run ad campaigns ([Meta Marketing API, Custom Conversions](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-conversion/)). That is an object-level classification, not a verdict on the advertiser.
For API-managed accounts, the same documentation says the is_unavailable field returns true when a custom conversion has been flagged, and that attempting to modify a flagged conversion returns an error whose user-facing title names the custom conversion as blocked ([Meta Marketing API, Custom Conversions](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-conversion/)). Read the field and the object ID rather than the sentence in the alert, because the generic error text in that same documented response refers to a custom audience.
Audiences, individual ads and the ad account itself surface through separate notices and separate queues. Treating a flagged conversion as an account emergency burns review requests you may need later.
Inspect the meaning your payload carries
Custom conversions are rules, and rules read your site. Meta's own example defines a purchase conversion by matching URLs that contain thankyou.html ([Meta Marketing API, Custom Conversions](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-conversion/)). If the matching URL is a path like /glp-1-weight-management/consult-booked, the conversion carries condition meaning even when the conversion itself is named something neutral.
Build a sanitized map of every signal before you argue about any of them. For an illustrative peptide funnel, that map covers the conversion name, the full rule definition, the event name, content_name and content_category values, quiz or intake answers passed in query strings, the referring URL, and any product or protocol name embedded in the path. Check what the server sends as well as what the browser sends.
Health information is broader than most measurement specs assume. The FTC describes it as anything that conveys information or enables an inference about a consumer's health, including browsing or purchase data, and advises businesses to take a broad view ([FTC business guidance on consumer health information](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/collecting-using-or-sharing-consumer-health-information-look-hipaa-ftc-act-health-breach)). A product page view can qualify. For the tracking-layer view of this problem, see [Meta Pixel HIPAA compliance](/resources/meta-pixel-hipaa).
Evaluate permitted measurement under law and platform terms
Platform terms and legal obligation are two separate analyses, and clearing one does not clear the other. HIPAA rules apply to covered entities, meaning health plans, providers conducting standard transactions electronically and clearinghouses, as well as to business associates ([FTC business guidance on consumer health information](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/collecting-using-or-sharing-consumer-health-information-look-hipaa-ftc-act-health-breach)). Many peptide sellers and direct-to-consumer telehealth brands sit outside that definition and conclude they are unregulated. That conclusion is wrong.
The FTC Act's obligations apply to HIPAA-covered entities and business associates as well as to companies handling health information that are not required to comply with HIPAA, and the agency points to its BetterHelp, GoodRx and Premom matters as establishing that disclosing consumers' health information for advertising without affirmative express consent may be an unfair practice ([FTC business guidance on consumer health information](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/collecting-using-or-sharing-consumer-health-information-look-hipaa-ftc-act-health-breach)). The same guidance warns against false or misleading claims that a business is "HIPAA Compliant" or "HIPAA Certified".
Keep four questions separate: whether the business operates lawfully, what the product's regulatory status is, whether the channel permits the advertising, and whether the account is in good standing. Measurement design follows all four. Outcome-side measurement is covered in [healthcare marketing attribution through admissions](/services/analytics-and-attribution).
Correct the data flow instead of the label
Renaming a flagged event while the same payload keeps flowing is not remediation. It leaves the inference intact, and it reads as concealment rather than correction. Removal is the defensible move.
A workable sequence looks like this. Inventory every event and the fields it sends. Decide, at the source, which values should never leave your systems, including condition terms, protocol names and intake answers. Restructure URLs so the path and query string no longer describe a condition, and strip residual parameters server side before the event is sent. Only then rebuild the conversion with a neutral definition.
Meta's documentation offers three resolution paths for flagged conversions: create a new custom conversion, select a different existing one, or duplicate a running campaign and select a non-flagged conversion before publishing, noting that once the campaign is published you cannot remove or change the custom conversion ([Meta Marketing API, Custom Conversions](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-conversion/)). The same reference caps custom conversions at 100 per ad account, so retiring dead objects is part of the cleanup, not housekeeping you defer. Related channel rules are summarized in [Facebook ads for healthcare](/resources/facebook-ads-for-healthcare).
Use the documented review route correctly
Review exists for disputed classification, not for pushing a sensitive payload through a second time. Meta's documentation states that if you believe a custom conversion was flagged in error and does not include non-permitted information, you can request a review in Ads Manager under the campaigns table, or in Events Manager under the custom conversions page ([Meta Marketing API, Custom Conversions](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/custom-conversion/)).
Before filing, assemble the record: the exact rule definition, a sanitized example of a matching URL, the full parameter list, the date the payload changed and who approved the change. If any of that still carries condition meaning, fix the flow first and create a clean object instead. Resubmitting an unchanged signal spends a review on a case you will lose and can draw further scrutiny.
Document the decision either way. A short internal log of what was removed, when and on whose authority is what makes the next conversation with a platform, a partner or counsel short. A broader operational list sits in the [healthcare marketing compliance checklist](/resources/healthcare-marketing-compliance-checklist).
Explain what measurement you keep and what you lose
Be explicit with stakeholders, because a clean fix still costs signal. What usually survives: standard events that describe a commercial action without implying a condition, aggregated platform reporting, consented and lawful contact matching, geo or holdout testing, and CRM-side reporting that follows an inquiry through to a closed outcome. What does not survive: condition-level conversion granularity, audiences built from sensitive inference, and any optimization that depended on the removed field.
Set expectations on timing too. Rebuilt conversions start with no history, so delivery systems relearn, and week-over-week comparisons across the change are not like for like. Plan a measurement window rather than reading the first few days as damage.
