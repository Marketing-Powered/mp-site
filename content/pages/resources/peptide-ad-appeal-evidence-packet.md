Peptide Ad Appeal Evidence Packet
An appeal is not a persuasive letter with attachments bolted on. Peptide ad suspension appeal evidence works when a reviewer can match the cited policy, the verified business identity and the dated correction in a single pass. This page gives you the packet structure to assemble before you write a word of appeal copy.

State the decision being appealed
Start with the notice itself, not with your explanation of it. Google sends suspended advertisers both an in-account notification and an email, and [the email notification identifies all policies the advertiser has been suspended for violating along with a link to submit an appeal](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). That email is the controlling document for your packet. Everything else in the file exists to answer it.
Open your packet with a one-page decision record. It should capture, in plain fields:
* Account name, account ID and payments profile name as they appear in the interface
* Action type: account suspension, account hold, disapproved ads, or a restriction limited to specific policies
* The exact policy names listed in the notice, copied verbatim rather than paraphrased
* Date and time the notification was received, and who in the business received it
* Whether a prior warning was received, and on what date
* Whether any related or linked accounts also received notices
Precision matters because the enforcement paths are different and the evidence they require is different. Some suspensions follow a warning period: for repeat violations that lead to account suspension, [Google sends a warning outlining the nature of the violation and remedial action, at least 7 days before the suspension action](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). Others do not. [If Google detects violations of its egregious policies and Terms and Conditions, the account is suspended immediately without prior warning](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en), and the published list of policies treated as egregious includes circumventing systems, promotion of unauthorized pharmacies, counterfeit, coordinated deceptive practices and unacceptable business practices, among others.
For peptide advertisers, that distinction sets the entire tone of the packet. A warning-based suspension usually leaves a documented remediation window you can evidence with dated changes. An immediate action on an egregious category is a far narrower conversation about identity, product status and whether the business model itself is eligible on the channel.
Two more facts belong in the decision record because they govern your timeline and your behavior. [Advertisers have at least 6 months from the date of account suspension to submit an appeal](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en), so the common instinct to file within an hour of the notice is rarely the strongest move. And volume works against you: [filing too many appeals for the same account suspension can mean they are not processed, and indications of misuse of the appeals function can suspend processing of certain appeals for 7 days](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). You generally get one well-built attempt at a time. Build the packet first.
Document the actual business
The second section of the packet answers a question the reviewer cannot answer from your ad copy: who is actually advertising, and does that entity exist in a form Google can confirm. This is where most peptide-adjacent appeals quietly fall apart, because the operating reality often involves several entities. A telehealth group, a separate professional corporation, a fulfillment or compounding partner, a marketing LLC that owns the domain, and a payments profile opened years ago under a name nobody has updated.
Google's verification requirements are explicit about matching. [The documents required depend on whether the account type is "Organization" or "Individual," and the details in submitted documents must exactly match the payments profile information, including the organization name](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=en). For U.S. organizations, [two different types of documentation may be required: organization registration documents and a government-issued photo ID from an authorized representative, where the authorized representative is an admin of the Google Ads account and/or the payments profile paying for the ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=en).
The acceptable registration categories are specific. [They include a document, notice or letter issued or stamped by the IRS stating the organization name, such as a CP575 or 147C, a Certificate of Business Incorporation issued by the state where you conduct business, a recent SEC filing, or a business credit report from Experian, Equifax or TransUnion](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=en). For the representative, [acceptable identification includes a passport, state identification, driving license or permanent resident card, and digital IDs are not accepted](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=en).
Build an identity block that lines these up before submission:
* Legal entity name. What you hold: IRS letter or state incorporation certificate. What it must match: Payments profile organization name.
* Authorized representative. What you hold: Government-issued photo ID. What it must match: Account admin or payments profile owner.
* Advertising domain. What you hold: Registrar record and hosting invoice. What it must match: Entity named on the site and in the ads.
* Operating licenses. What you hold: State board, pharmacy or clinical licenses held by the operating entity. What it must match: The entity whose services the ads promote.
* Third-party certifications. What you hold: Certificate and current status page. What it must match: The domain and entity under review.
Treat the mechanics as part of the evidence. [IDs should be valid, in color, clear and well lit, submitted as an original photo rather than a photocopy or screenshot, with front and back submitted separately when requested, uploaded only through the Ads interface rather than email, and agencies should not upload documents for multiple advertisers in the same file](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=en). Verification failures are frequently procedural: [common reasons include an unverifiable document, a name mismatch, or an unsupported document type](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=en). A name mismatch between your incorporation certificate and a payments profile created under a trade name is the kind of problem you should find yourself, not have found for you.
Identity also gates the appeal in some cases. [Certain selected advertisers must complete advertiser verification successfully in order to appeal an account suspension, and if Google is unable to verify their identity after 3 attempts, they will not be allowed to appeal](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). Three attempts is not many when the underlying issue is a stale entity name.
If you want a broader pre-submission sweep of entity, site and claim hygiene, the [Healthcare Marketing Compliance Checklist](/resources/healthcare-marketing-compliance-checklist) covers the same discipline applied across a site rather than a single appeal.
Map findings to corrections
This is the analytical core of the packet, and the part generic advice skips. Each policy line in the notice gets its own row, and each row gets artifacts that a stranger can verify without a phone call. If a cited issue has no artifact, that is a finding, not an omission to paper over.
An issue-to-evidence table, shown here as an illustrative format rather than a description of any specific business:
* Policy line 1. What was actually true: Landing page described outcomes not supported by the product's regulatory status. What changed: Claim language removed and replaced with factual service description. Verifiable artifact: Archived before and after page captures, CMS revision history. Date completed: Date.
* Policy line 2. What was actually true: Certification status referenced on site had lapsed. What changed: Reference removed pending current status. Verifiable artifact: Screenshot of current site section, certification status record. Date completed: Date.
* Policy line 3. What was actually true: Destination URL and display path resolved to different content for some sessions. What changed: Redirect rule removed, single destination enforced. Verifiable artifact: Server configuration change record, redirect test log. Date completed: Date.
* Policy line 4. What was actually true: Product line advertised is not eligible on the channel. What changed: Those products removed from all campaigns and from the advertised domain section. Verifiable artifact: Campaign change history export, sitemap diff. Date completed: Date.
Three rules make the table credible.
First, separate the questions that regulated advertisers routinely collapse. Whether a business operates lawfully, what the regulatory status of a given product is, whether the business holds a third-party certification, whether the product category is eligible for a given advertising channel, and whether the account is in good standing are five separate determinations. A valid state license does not create channel eligibility. A certification does not change product status. An enforcement posture by a regulator is not an authorization to advertise. Build your rows around the specific determination the notice actually made.
Second, distinguish a real correction from a cosmetic one. Removing a claim from one landing page while the same claim lives on four programmatic templates, a blog archive and a comparison table is not a correction. Neither is pausing campaigns without changing the destination. Google's review draws on more than your ads: [in determining whether an advertiser or destination is violating policies, Google reviews information from sources including ads, accounts, websites, content, user complaints, consumer reviews, regulatory warnings and rulings, and other third-party sources](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). Your packet should assume the site, not just the ad, is in scope.
Third, do not document anything that resembles evasion. Rebuilding on a new account, masking the entity or showing different content to review systems than to users creates a far worse record. [Accounts related to a suspended account may be suspended, any new accounts the advertiser tries to create may also be suspended, and Google recommends reinstating suspended accounts before attempting to create new ones](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). There is also a useful mechanic to know: [if an account was suspended because other accounts verified with the same identity documents were suspended, it will be automatically reinstated once those other accounts are successfully appealed](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). That makes sequencing a strategic decision, and it means identifying the root account matters more than filing everywhere at once.
Where certification is genuinely part of the eligibility path for your category, treat the program's own requirements as the standard rather than your interpretation of them. The [LegitScript Certification for Addiction Treatment Advertising](/resources/legitscript-certification-guide) guide walks through how a certification body evaluates an operating model, which is a useful mental model even when your category is different.
Organize attachments
A reviewer is reading many cases. The packet should be navigable in under a minute by someone who has never heard of your business. That is a document-control job, and it is the part owners most often hand to whoever is free.
Use a single dated index as the first page of the file set. Illustrative format:
* 01. Document: Suspension email, full headers. Supports: Decision record. Date of document: Date received. Redacted: No.
* 02. Document: IRS entity letter. Supports: Identity. Date of document: Issue date. Redacted: Tax ID partially masked.
* 03. Document: State incorporation certificate. Supports: Identity. Date of document: Issue date. Redacted: No.
* 04. Document: Domain registrar record. Supports: Identity, destination ownership. Date of document: Export date. Redacted: Registrant contact masked.
* 05. Document: Landing page capture, before. Supports: Issue 1. Date of document: Capture date. Redacted: No.
* 06. Document: Landing page capture, after. Supports: Issue 1 correction. Date of document: Capture date. Redacted: No.
* 07. Document: Campaign change history export. Supports: Issue 4 correction. Date of document: Export date. Redacted: Internal cost data removed.
* 08. Document: Certification status record. Supports: Issue 2. Date of document: Status date. Redacted: No.
Naming conventions should encode the same information, for example 03_identity_incorporation-certificate_2026-01-14.pdf. Consistent ordering between filenames, index rows and the references in your written explanation is the single cheapest credibility gain in the whole exercise.
Redaction deserves its own pass, and for health-adjacent businesses it is not optional hygiene. Send only what the cited issue requires. Appeals do not need patient names, dates of service, diagnoses, order histories, lab results, prescriber notes or anything else that identifies an individual. If an invoice or fulfillment record is genuinely the best proof that a product line was discontinued, redact to the smallest defensible unit before it leaves your systems.
An authorized redaction example, illustrative:
* Original record: Order 88421 | Jane R. Doe | 14 Oak St | BPC product, 2 units | shipped 11/02 | prescriber Dr. A. Smith.
* Submitted version: Order [redacted] | [customer identity redacted] | [product line X] discontinued, final ship date 11/02 | prescriber identity redacted.
Keep an unredacted master copy in your own records with a note of who approved each redaction and when. If a reviewer later asks a narrow question, you want to answer it from an intact file rather than reconstruct one. Note also the handling rule from Google's own documentation: [documents should be submitted only through the Ads interface and not sent over email](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/9872280?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=en). Your internal routing should match that discipline, which means no identity documents in shared drives with open link access and no customer records in a vendor's inbox.
If a certification is part of your evidence set, know its real timeline before you reference it as pending. The practical cost, rules and sequencing involved are covered in [LegitScript Certification: Cost, Rules and Timeline](/resources/legitscript-certification), and a certification in progress should be described as in progress, with its application date, not implied as held.
Write a factual explanation
Only now do you write. The explanation is short, references the index by number, and does exactly one thing per cited issue: it either asserts that the finding was mistaken and shows why, or it accepts the finding and shows the correction. Conflating the two is the most common self-inflicted wound in appeals, because an explanation that disputes a finding while also describing how it was fixed tells the reviewer the advertiser does not know which story is true.
The dispute path is narrow and should be used only when you can demonstrate it. Google is direct about the standard: [accounts are only reinstated in compelling circumstances, such as in the case of a mistake, so advertisers are told to be thorough, accurate and honest](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). A defensible dispute usually rests on something objective, such as a destination that was never live during the cited period, a third-party script that injected content without authorization, a confusion between your domain and a similarly named one, or an account compromise. Supply the artifact that proves it, then stop. Adjectives do not help.
The correction path is more common and more winnable. Write it as a sequence of facts: what the notice cited, what was in fact true, what changed, when it changed, and how it is prevented from recurring. Guidance from Google's own tips is to explain the situation for each violating policy and provide context, with the examples given being things like explaining why different content was shown to Google than to users in a cloaking case, or describing a partnership in a phishing case where you are an official partner of another brand ([Google Ads account suspensions overview](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Context means specifics about your operation, not sentiment about your intentions.
Keep the tone flat. No apology paragraphs, no revenue impact, no staffing hardship, no commitments you cannot evidence. Reviewers are evaluating a record, and the appeal is read alongside automated signals: [detection combines Google's AI with human evaluation, where nuanced determinations are flagged for trained operators and analysts](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). Write for a reader who will spend a short time with a long queue.
A structural suggestion that keeps writers honest: draft the explanation as numbered responses that mirror the issue-to-evidence table exactly, one paragraph each, no more than six sentences per paragraph, every factual sentence pointing to an index number. If a paragraph has no index reference, either find the artifact or delete the sentence.
One caution on who writes it. If an outside agency built the campaigns or the landing pages under review, that agency is a witness to the facts, not a neutral drafter of them. Ask for raw change history and access logs rather than a summary. The vetting questions in [Healthcare Marketing Agency: Vetting and Strategy Guide](/services/healthcare-marketing-agency) are a reasonable way to assess whether a partner can produce that level of record. Marketing Powered's own perspective here comes from operating inside Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted and identity and eligibility questions are routine, including a founder history managing $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts.
Maintain submission history
Close the packet with a log, and keep updating it after submission. The log is what turns a one-off scramble into an asset you can reuse if the first appeal is denied in part, if a second account is affected, or if a regulator, insurer or acquirer later asks how the business responded.
Record the route precisely. [In the account, the Contact Us link in the notification leads to the appeal form, and selecting Contact Us again while an appeal is processing places the account in an "Appeal pending" status, with the outcome delivered by email; a rejected appeal can be re-appealed through the same process](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). Note also that [separate appeals are required for each suspended account when multiple accounts are affected](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en), so your log should have one row per account, not one row per incident.
An illustrative submission and response log:
* Date. Action: Notice received. Channel: Email and in-account. Submitted by: Recipient name. Reference: Message ID. Outcome and open items: Policies cited: list.
* Date. Action: Remediation completed. Channel: Internal. Submitted by: Owner. Reference: Index 05 to 08. Outcome and open items: Issue 3 unresolved pending vendor change.
* Date. Action: Verification documents submitted. Channel: Ads interface. Submitted by: Authorized representative. Reference: Task reference. Outcome and open items: Pending.
* Date. Action: Appeal submitted. Channel: Appeal form. Submitted by: Account admin. Reference: Appeal reference. Outcome and open items: Pending.
* Date. Action: Outcome received. Channel: Email. Submitted by: n/a. Reference: Message ID. Outcome and open items: Partial: two issues resolved, one unaddressed.
Track unresolved items with the same rigor as resolved ones. If an appeal is denied, the useful question is not whether to resubmit but what new fact exists that did not exist at the last submission. Resubmitting the same packet with stronger adjectives consumes a review cycle and risks the appeal-volume limits described above. Note too that a suspended account is largely read-only: [permitted actions include canceling the account, billing actions, appealing the suspension, completing advertiser verification and updating security settings](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en). Plan your acquisition continuity around that constraint rather than around an assumed reinstatement date.
Finally, write a short post-incident note while the detail is fresh: what signal was missed, who owns site claim review going forward, what the pre-launch check is for new landing pages, and how often entity and certification records are reconciled against the payments profile. That note is the difference between an incident and a control.
