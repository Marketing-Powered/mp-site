Diagnosing a Peptide Advertising Restriction
A restriction notice arrives with a policy name, a timestamp and very little else, and the instinct is to edit something immediately. A peptide ad suspension diagnosis begins before any of that, by naming what was actually restricted: an asset, a destination, an identity, a credential or an account. Classify first, remediate second, appeal last.

Preserve the original notice before you change anything
The first thirty minutes decide how much evidence you still have on day ten. Someone on the marketing team sees a red banner in the account or an email in a shared inbox, opens the ad, rewrites a headline and resubmits. The ad now looks different from the ad that was actioned, the change history records an edit rather than a violation, and the person who eventually writes the appeal has to reconstruct a state that no longer exists.
Capture the notice as it was delivered. Google sends suspension notice through both an in-account notification and an email, and the email identifies all policies the advertiser has been suspended for violating along with a link to submit an appeal ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). That email is a primary document. Save it as a file, not as a forwarded summary, and keep the full header and the original policy names rather than a paraphrase someone typed into a project tracker.
A usable preservation set for a peptide restriction looks like this:
* The exact policy name or editorial reason code cited, copied verbatim, including any code number.
* The specific object affected: campaign, ad group, individual ad, keyword, asset, extension or final URL.
* The timestamp of the notice and the timestamp of the last change made before it.
* The account identifier, the manager account above it, and every account linked to the same billing profile or identity documents.
* A current export of change history, user access and billing method history.
* A full-page screenshot and an archived copy of each affected landing page, including any interstitial, pop-up or redirect behavior present that day.
Preserve the destination, not only the ad. If the landing page is edited during triage, the version a reviewer saw is gone. Save the HTML, the rendered page and the page as it appears on mobile.
Access gets narrower after an account action, which is another reason to move early. Suspended Google Ads accounts operate in read-only mode with a limited set of permitted actions, including billing, appeals and verification, security settings and downloading reports ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Pull the reports and exports you will want for the appeal while you can still pull them cleanly, and store them somewhere outside the ad platform.
Classify the restriction before anyone starts remediation
Restrictions are issued at different levels of a platform's enforcement stack, and the level determines the fix. Treating an asset disapproval as a terminal suspension wastes weeks. Treating an account suspension as a copy problem wastes appeal attempts. The classification step takes a few minutes and changes everything downstream.
Five classes cover most notices a peptide or compounding-adjacent advertiser will see.
Asset-level disapproval. One ad, keyword, asset or extension is not serving. The rest of the account continues. The cause is usually in the text, the claim, the image or the destination it points to.
Serving limitation. The asset is approved in principle but restricted in where, to whom or in which formats it can run. Nothing is wrong with the account; the inventory available to it is narrower than expected.
Strike or temporary hold. Google operates a strike-based system for repeat violations of certain policies, with no strike issued for a first violation, a warning first, and a maximum of three strikes. The first and second strikes place the account in a temporary hold for three and seven days respectively, a second strike applies if the same policy is violated again within ninety days of the first, and the third strike results in suspension ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/10922738)). The policies inside that strike system include unapproved substances, which is why peptide advertisers should read a strike notice as a countdown rather than a formality.
Account-level action. The account itself is suspended. Google describes several distinct routes to this, including policy violations, billing and payment reasons such as unpaid balance or suspicious payment activity, unauthorized account activity where credentials appear compromised, and age requirements on the Google Account ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Egregious violations cause immediate suspension without prior warning, while repeat-violation suspensions are preceded by a warning sent at least seven days in advance ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)).
Measurement or tag issue. Conversion tracking breaks, a tag fires on a blocked page, or a feed stops updating. Performance collapses and looks like enforcement. It is not, and no appeal will fix it.
Microsoft Advertising is useful here even for advertisers who run almost entirely on Google, because its editorial reason codes separate causes that Google often reports in a single sentence. Microsoft documents distinct codes for restricted medical content, for missing required site certification or accreditation, for a website that is not accessible at all times, for landing page content that is not relevant to the ad, and for a missing privacy policy when personal data is collected ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/guides/editorial-failure-reason-codes?view=bingads-13)). It also carries several codes whose message is the generic statement that the ad or website contains content that is not allowed ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/guides/editorial-failure-reason-codes?view=bingads-13)). The lesson transfers: identical notice language can sit on top of very different underlying causes, so the code number and the context matter more than the sentence.
The following taxonomy is illustrative. It is a sorting aid, not a claim about how frequently each class occurs.
* Asset disapproval. What the notice points at: One ad, keyword or asset. First question to answer: Which claim or term triggered it?. Reversible by editing copy?: Often.
* Serving limitation. What the notice points at: Delivery scope. First question to answer: Is this geography, format or audience?. Reversible by editing copy?: Sometimes.
* Strike or hold. What the notice points at: Repeat policy behavior. First question to answer: Which policy, and when was the prior one?. Reversible by editing copy?: Only with removal plus acknowledgement.
* Account action. What the notice points at: The account or its identity. First question to answer: Policy, billing, security or verification?. Reversible by editing copy?: No.
* Measurement issue. What the notice points at: Tags and conversions. First question to answer: Did enforcement actually occur?. Reversible by editing copy?: Not an enforcement matter.
For the broader policy landscape that sits behind these classes, including sensitive-category handling across search and social, start with the [healthcare advertising policy guide](/resources/healthcare-advertising) rather than a platform-specific help article.
Trace the evidence behind the notice
Classification tells you where to look. Tracing tells you what is actually there. Work through four evidence surfaces in order, and write down what you find rather than what you assume.
The destination. Open the final URL in a clean browser session and on mobile. Microsoft's codes flag websites that are not accessible at all times, repeat inaccessible website failures, landing page content that is not relevant, sites that do not contain the offer indicated in the ad text, pop-up or pop-under behavior on the landing page, and sites that exhibit disallowed behavior ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/guides/editorial-failure-reason-codes?view=bingads-13)). Each of these is a destination problem, not a copy problem, and each is invisible to anyone who only reviews the ad inside the platform editor.
Claims and category language. Read the page the way a reviewer reads it: headline claims, outcome language, product naming, availability statements, intake forms and anything that reads as a therapeutic promise. Microsoft restricts the promotion of medical content as its own reason code, separate from certification ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/guides/editorial-failure-reason-codes?view=bingads-13)). Keep five things separate in your notes, because conflating them produces bad appeals: whether the business operates lawfully, what the regulatory status of the product is, whether a required credential exists, whether the channel permits advertising for that category, and what state the account is in. A business can be entirely lawful and still be ineligible to advertise a specific product on a specific platform.
Identity, certification and verification state. Check whether advertiser verification is complete and current, whether the legal entity on the account matches the entity on the site and in billing, and whether a required credential or accreditation exists. Microsoft specifies that advertisers promoting prescription drugs must maintain certification for each market where they advertise, giving the example that advertising prescription drugs to customers in the United States requires NABP Verified Internet Pharmacy Practice Sites accreditation, and it carries a separate code for required site certification or accreditation that is missing ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/guides/editorial-failure-reason-codes?view=bingads-13)). A missing credential is not an argument you can win with better copy.
Recent change and security. List everything that changed in the thirty days before the notice: new domain or subdomain, new payment method, new users added, agency access granted or revoked, bulk edits, a CMS or plugin update. Google notes that an unauthorized user who obtains credentials can access an account, and that detection of such access can lead to a temporary suspension intended to protect against unauthorized charges ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). A compromised site or account can produce a notice that reads, from the outside, exactly like a content violation. Malicious software sits on Google's list of egregious policies ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)), which raises the cost of mistaking a security incident for a creative problem.
It also helps to know that reviewers are not only looking at your ad. Google states that in determining whether an advertiser or destination is violating its policies it reviews information from sources including ads, accounts, websites, content, user complaints, consumer reviews, regulatory warnings and rulings, and other third-party sources ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). If a regulatory action or a pattern of consumer complaints exists, the evidence trail has to include it.
Cross-platform tracing is faster when someone already reads both rule sets fluently; the [Microsoft Ads management](/services/microsoft-ads-management) page covers that editorial side in more depth, and [security practices](/company/security) matter whenever a destination compromise is on the table.
Assign the remediation owner by restriction class
Most restrictions stay open longer than necessary because the wrong team is holding them. A marketing manager cannot complete an identity verification that requires the legal entity owner's documents. A developer cannot rewrite a disapproved therapeutic claim. A compliance lead cannot clean a malware injection. Misrouting is quiet, because everyone involved is working, just not on the blocker.
The matrix below is an illustrative routing model, not a description of any specific engagement.
* Asset copy or claim. Primary owner: Marketing, with compliance review. Supporting owner: Legal, where claims are regulated. Typical first action: Rewrite and document the rationale.
* Landing page content, relevance, privacy policy. Primary owner: Marketing and web. Supporting owner: Legal for policy text. Typical first action: Rebuild the page, archive both versions.
* Site accessibility, redirects, malware. Primary owner: IT or development. Supporting owner: Hosting and security vendor. Typical first action: Contain, remediate, then verify externally.
* Advertiser identity or billing verification. Primary owner: Legal entity owner or finance. Supporting owner: Agency, in a support role only. Typical first action: Submit matching entity documents.
* Missing certification or accreditation. Primary owner: Whoever owns credentialing. Supporting owner: Operations and clinical leadership. Typical first action: Confirm eligibility, then apply.
* Measurement or tagging. Primary owner: Analytics or development. Supporting owner: Marketing. Typical first action: Validate tags before touching ads.
Two routing rules prevent most of the waste. First, identity and billing items follow the documents, not the job title. Google states that certain selected advertisers must complete advertiser verification successfully in order to appeal an account suspension, and that if identity cannot be verified after three attempts they will not be allowed to appeal that suspension ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Three attempts is a small budget. The person whose name appears on the business registration should be in the room before the first submission, not after the second failure.
Second, anything that touches related accounts is an executive decision rather than a channel decision. Google notes that accounts related to a suspended account may be suspended, that new accounts an affected advertiser tries to create may also be suspended, and that it recommends reinstating suspended accounts before attempting to create new ones ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). There is a useful detail on the other side of that rule: where an account was suspended because other accounts verified with the same identity documents were suspended, Google states it will be automatically reinstated once those other accounts are successfully appealed ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). That changes sequencing. Fix the root account first.
Marketing Powered's founder ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from three to twenty-four locations and managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts, which is the background behind this routing discipline. Our practice is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware and built for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted. Choose correction or dispute on the facts
Once the class and the owner are settled, there are three honest paths, and the choice should follow the evidence rather than the urgency.
Correct. Choose this when the evidence shows a real, current violation that you can remove. For a strike, Google requires that violations be corrected and an acknowledgement form submitted to resume serving, and if an appeal of the strike is unsuccessful the violations still have to be removed or fixed and the strike acknowledged before the temporary hold is released ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/10922738)). The acknowledgement form also asks the advertiser to affirm that creating additional accounts or attempting to circumvent policy enforcement is prohibited ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/10922738)). Correction is unglamorous and it is frequently the fastest route.
Dispute. Choose this when you believe the action was applied in error and you can show it. Google's appeal route runs from the Contact Us link in the in-account notification, the account shows an appeal pending status while it is reviewed, the outcome arrives by email, and a rejected appeal can be re-appealed through the same process ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Pace matters: Google states that filing too many appeals for the same suspension may mean they are not processed, recommends submitting one appeal at a time, and notes that indications of appeals misuse can suspend processing of certain appeals for seven days ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). You also have time to prepare properly, since advertisers have at least six months from the date of account suspension to submit an appeal ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)).
Set expectations honestly before anyone commits budget to remediation. Google states that accounts are only reinstated in compelling circumstances, such as in the case of a mistake ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)), and for egregious violations it states that the account is suspended immediately without prior warning and the advertiser will not be allowed to advertise again ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)). Reinstatement is discretionary. No agency, consultant or internal team can promise it.
Qualify. Choose this when the blocker is a credential or an eligibility question rather than a mistake. Certification is a program with its own requirements and timeline, and it belongs on a separate track from the appeal. The [LegitScript certification guide](/resources/legitscript-certification) covers how that kind of credentialing process is structured and what it demands of the business. Keep the logic clean while you work: regulatory enforcement discretion regarding a product category is not the same thing as a platform granting advertising permission, and holding one credential does not create blanket eligibility for every product, claim or channel.
There is a fourth option that is not an option. Do not build a replacement account, obscure the destination or submit identity information that does not match the operating entity. Circumventing systems appears on Google's list of egregious policies ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9841640?hl=en)), and the practical effect of evasion is to convert a solvable problem into a permanent one.
Maintain the incident record through resolution
Treat the restriction as an incident with a file, not as a ticket that closes when ads resume. The file is what lets a second appeal be better than the first, and it is what prevents the same cause from recurring under a different label six months later.
A workable record has five running sections. First, the preserved evidence from day one, untouched. Second, a hypothesis log: each suspected cause, the evidence for and against it, and who tested it. Third, an action log with timestamps, owner and outcome for every change made to ads, destinations, access, billing or credentials. Fourth, an open-uncertainty list, written plainly, including anything the team could not verify. Fifth, a checkpoint schedule with named owners and dates.
The uncertainty list earns its place. It is the section that stops a team from writing an appeal that asserts more than it knows. If nobody can confirm whether a plugin update introduced a redirect, that belongs in writing rather than in an appeal narrative that claims a clean site.
While the account is restricted, the commercial question is not only reinstatement. It is what the business does about demand in the meantime, and whether the acquisition model being defended can carry the cost of defending it. Model the revenue gap, decide which channels absorb volume, confirm that intake and call handling still capture what arrives, and check that measurement will still work when serving resumes. Attribution that tracks to admission rather than to lead makes that judgment much easier, because it shows what the restricted channel was actually producing rather than what it was reporting.
Finally, build the recurrence controls into the normal operating rhythm: a pre-publication claim review, a monitored uptime and malware check on every advertised destination, a calendar reminder for verification and credential renewals, and a documented owner for each. Platform rules change, so re-read the primary policy sources before relying on a prior interpretation.
