Reviews and Testimonials in Peptide Marketing
Most peptide marketing testimonials get a business into trouble at the point of reuse, not the point of collection. A customer's unsolicited words on a third-party platform and the same words pulled into an ad are two different regulatory objects, and they need two different sets of controls.

Separate reviews from advertising testimonials
A review is feedback a customer publishes. A testimonial is feedback a business selects, frames and republishes to persuade someone to buy. That shift matters: the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule explains that the exemption for merely hosting consumer reviews does not apply once reviews are featured in a promotional context, at which point they function as testimonials ([FTC: Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).
Note what the rule does not do. The Commission declined to finalize the proposed section on consumer review reuse or repurposing, and the final rule centers on fake or false reviews and testimonials, insider reviews, company-controlled review sites, review suppression and buying reviews conditioned on sentiment ([Federal Register: Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)). So reposting is not automatically a rule violation. The exposure comes from what the reposted quote now claims on your behalf.
* Who chose it. Public review you host: The customer. Testimonial you publish in marketing: Your marketing team.
* Claim responsibility. Public review you host: Reviewer's own words. Testimonial you publish in marketing: Yours, including implied claims.
* Disclosure duty. Public review you host: Insider relationships still disclosed. Testimonial you publish in marketing: Material connections disclosed.
* Substantiation duty. Public review you host: None created by hosting alone. Testimonial you publish in marketing: You need support for the claim conveyed.
Treat selection as a publishing act with a named owner.
Collect feedback without manipulation
Neutral solicitation is the control that keeps your review base usable. Ask for an honest review. Do not ask for a good one, do not route unhappy customers away from public platforms, and do not tie anything of value to the sentiment expressed. The FTC prohibits incentives conditioned on reviews expressing a particular sentiment, and has clarified that the prohibition applies whether the condition is express or implicit ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)).
Illustrative neutral request, for internal adaptation only: "You finished your first month with us. If you have a few minutes, we would value an honest review of your experience, positive or negative. Here is the link."
Two cautions. First, the federal rule is a floor, not a ceiling: individual platforms set their own contributed-content and advertising policies, and some prohibit incentives and solicitation practices the federal rule does not reach. Check each platform's current policy page before you launch a program and record the date you checked it. Second, staff incentives tied to review volume create the same pressure as customer incentives. Review material connections and insider relationships
Peptide businesses are small, networked and founder-heavy, which makes insider reviews a live risk rather than a theoretical one. Officers and managers writing about their own business, employees reviewing the clinic that employs them, affiliates earning commission on a protocol, and practitioners who stock the product they praise all sit inside the FTC's material-connection framework. The rule addresses insider consumer reviews and testimonials and the need for clear and conspicuous disclosure unless, for testimonials, the relationship is already clear to the audience ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).
Willful blindness is not a defense. The FTC's guidance notes that a business routinely asking prospective testimonialists how they became interested in the product should not avoid looking at answers that might reveal a material relationship ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).
Build one intake question into every testimonial request: what is your relationship to this business, beyond being a customer. Capture the answer with the quote. Assess health implications before you publish a quote
This is where peptide marketing breaks. A single customer's experience is evidence that one person was satisfied. It is not evidence that a product works. The FTC's endorsement guidance requires advertisers to possess and rely on adequate substantiation, including competent and reliable scientific evidence where appropriate, for claims conveyed through endorsements, and applies that requirement to implied claims as well as express ones ([FTC: Endorsement Guides, 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p204500_endorsement_guides_in_2023.pdf)). Under 16 CFR 255.2(b), an advertisement using a consumer endorsement about a central or key attribute will be read as representing that the endorser's experience is what consumers generally achieve ([FTC: Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P204500%20Guides%20Concerning%20Endors%20and%20Testimonials.pdf)). The same guidance states plainly that consumer endorsements are not themselves competent and reliable scientific evidence, and that a disclosure of expected results can be misleading when the results hold only in limited circumstances the ad does not state.
Run every candidate quote through a short claim-review pass with two readers: a marketer who writes down the claim a reasonable buyer would take from the quote, and a clinical or regulatory reviewer who says whether evidence supports that claim for that product in that context. If the quote implies treatment of a disease, a measurable outcome, or superiority over a drug, it does not ship on a disclaimer. Keep product status, channel eligibility and claim support as separate questions. Protect private information in clinic testimonials
A supplement brand collects customer feedback. A clinic collects something else. Anything originating in a patient encounter, including the fact that a named person is a patient, can be protected health information, and the HIPAA Privacy Rule requires an individual's written authorization before protected health information is used or disclosed for marketing, with limited exceptions ([HHS: Marketing](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html)). Narrow exceptions exist, including face-to-face communications and promotional gifts of nominal value ([HHS: Summary of the HIPAA Privacy Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html)).
Two practical consequences. A patient who posts a public review has not authorized you to republish it in an ad, and responding publicly can itself disclose that the person is a patient. Arrangements in which a covered entity discloses protected health information in exchange for remuneration so another party can market its own product have no exception under the marketing definition ([HHS: Marketing](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html)). Treat authorization language, retention and revocation as items for qualified counsel, not a template download. Maintain the evidence trail
If you cannot reconstruct where a quote came from, you cannot defend it. Keep one record per testimonial containing: original source and URL, date captured, unedited original text, the edited version used, the relationship disclosure answer, any incentive offered, written consent or HIPAA authorization where applicable, the reviewer who cleared the claim, and every placement where the quote ran. Add a withdrawal path, because consent can be revoked and you need to pull assets across ad accounts, landing pages and organic channels, not just the testimonial page.
Pair that with a platform policy register listing each policy page you rely on and the date it was last checked. Policies in sensitive health categories change without notice.
