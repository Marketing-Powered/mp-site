Rehab Advertising: Channels, Policies, Measurement
Rehab advertising is the paid promotion of addiction treatment services to people seeking care, their families, and other relevant audiences, run as a controlled admissions-acquisition system. An accountable program connects channel eligibility, accurate messaging, admissions operations, and privacy-reviewed measurement so appropriate prospective patients reach a process the organization can support. Define a qualified inquiry, name its owner, and decide how that inquiry connects to an admission before choosing a channel or setting a bid.

Agree on what a qualified inquiry means
Picture the Tuesday intake huddle. The admissions lead has the call log. The marketing lead has the platform conversion export. Both are looking at the same morning, and they are not counting the same events. One row is a returned call about a person already in assessment. Another is a two-second hang-up. A third is a parent asking about a service the facility does not offer. Until those rows have a shared meaning, no channel decision is ready.
Before bids, creative, or a vendor review, marketing and admissions should approve a written definition:
A qualified inquiry is a unique, genuine request for treatment information concerning a service the organization offers, within its documented service parameters, with enough information for an authorized admissions team member to determine the appropriate next step.
Adapt the wording to the organization. Do not let a platform conversion setting become the default definition.
Document these distinctions in the same agreement:
* Inquiry versus contact attempt. Several calls about one treatment episode should not automatically become several inquiries.
* Relevant versus clinically appropriate. Marketing relevance is not a clinical eligibility determination.
* Qualified versus financially cleared. Keep service fit and financial clearance as separate stages.
* Unqualified versus unresolved. An unanswered call is not evidence that the caller was unsuitable.
* New inquiry versus an existing relationship. Separate prospective admissions from current-patient, alumni, employment, and vendor contacts.
* Person versus episode. Decide how returning inquiries and separate treatment episodes are counted.
Qualification should describe operational fit. It should not describe a person’s worthiness of care.
Assign an admissions leader to own disposition accuracy and a marketing leader to own acquisition classification. The COO should resolve disagreements that change what executives see in the report. Put the signed definition where both teams can retrieve it, and date the version. When the definition changes, start a new reporting period rather than rewriting history inside an open cohort.
This agreement gives addiction treatment advertising a decision standard. Later choices about channel mix, negatives, creative, and vendor review can be judged against an agreed business event, not against whichever metric looks most favorable that week.
Which addiction treatment advertising channels are allowed
Treat channel eligibility as a documented review, not a permanent whitelist. Platform rules change, and they differ by advertiser, service, destination, and geography. Use the framework below for planning, then confirm the current policy text for the account you intend to run.
### Google Ads for treatment providers
Google restricts the promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Its examples include clinical addiction treatment providers, recovery support services such as sober living environments and mutual help organizations, lead generators or referral agencies, and crisis hotlines. Appearing in that scope does not establish that the advertiser may run ads. [Google addiction services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en)
For United States targeting, Google’s healthcare and medicines policy requires certification as an addiction services provider through the LegitScript Certification program, and it also requires certification with Google. Services that are not eligible for that certification are not allowed to advertise drug and alcohol addiction services on Google. [Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en)
Google states that approved advertisers may promote these services in Australia, Canada, France, Ireland, New Zealand, and the United States, and it points readers to location-specific requirements. Those requirements are not the same in every country. On the same policy page, Australia and France limit this category to government entities, while the United States section uses the LegitScript-plus-Google certification path. Confirm the rule for the geography you will actually target. [Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en)
Promotion of services unrelated to drug and alcohol addiction, including impulse control disorders, behavioral addiction, and nicotine addiction, sits outside this addiction-services policy. That exclusion is not a permission slip for every other healthcare claim. Read the rule that applies to the service being promoted. [Google addiction services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en)
For policy-reviewed paid search, build an eligibility record before launch:
* The legal organization and the services being promoted.
* Destination domains and the specific landing pages.
* Facilities represented, including any facility that will receive transferred inquiries.
* Intended geographic markets, matched to the certification geography.
* Certification and account-approval evidence, with renewal dates.
* The person responsible for keeping that evidence current.
Prepare that file alongside the [LegitScript certification guide](/resources/legitscript-certification-guide). Certification evidence and Google account approval are separate documents. Store both.
### Meta: certification and written permission are separate
Meta requires advertisers who want to run addiction treatment ads targeting people in the United States to be certified with LegitScript and to apply to Meta for permission to advertise. Meta’s standard says advertisers can run these ads in the United States when they are certified with LegitScript and have written permission from Meta. [Meta drug and alcohol addiction treatment ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drug-alcohol-addiction-treatment/)
LegitScript states that its addiction treatment certification is recognized by Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Nextdoor as a way those platforms vet addiction treatment advertisers. Recognition by a certifier is not the same as a completed platform permission. Treat each platform’s own application, creative review, and account status as a separate gate. [LegitScript addiction treatment certification](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/)
Meta’s policy scope includes clinical addiction treatment services, websites that provide information about in-person treatment, online and in-person support groups, and crisis hotlines. It also lists physical rehabilitation, such as kinesiology, among ad types that can run without permission under this particular policy. Do not assume a physical-rehabilitation exception covers drug and alcohol treatment, and do not assume an addiction-treatment permission covers unrelated services. Read the current standard for the ad you plan to run. [Meta drug and alcohol addiction treatment ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drug-alcohol-addiction-treatment/)
Give paid social a defined job: program education, local visibility, or service awareness. Do not assume every audience, event, or optimization option in the account is appropriate. Write about the service rather than assigning a condition to the reader. “Explore outpatient treatment options” describes an offer. Language that presumes knowledge of someone’s substance use does not.
### Other paid placements
A new format is not an exemption from healthcare review. Before testing display, video, connected TV, a directory sponsorship, or another platform, require written answers on advertiser eligibility, geographic limits, audience construction, placement controls, landing-page requirements, data collection and sharing, and how results will be assessed. If eligibility is unresolved, keep the channel out of the launch plan. An open buying interface is not approval.
* Paid search. Planning role: Reach people expressing treatment-related intent. Launch decision: Confirm advertiser, service, destination, and geographic eligibility.
* Paid social. Planning role: Explain programs and support relevant awareness. Launch decision: Confirm certification, platform permission, creative, and data use.
* Display or video. Planning role: Test a defined education or visibility objective. Launch decision: Review placements, audiences, and measurement before funding.
* Sponsored directories. Planning role: Add a separately accountable inquiry source. Launch decision: Review representation, routing, commercial terms, and source labels.
* Organic content and local visibility. Planning role: Support research and location understanding. Launch decision: Manage separately from paid permissions and paid acquisition reporting.
This is an operating framework. It is not a statement that every tactic is permitted for every provider.
### Retargeting needs its own answer
Do not build a treatment acquisition plan around following previous visitors to a treatment site.
Google classifies health as a sensitive interest category. For that category, advertisers cannot use advertiser-curated audiences, including Customer Match and “your data” segments. Google distinguishes those restrictions from certain predefined Google audiences, such as in-market and affinity segments, which the policy says can be used because sensitive user signals are excluded from them. Review the actual targeting method. Do not assume every audience feature follows one rule. [Google health in personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en)
The practical instruction is specific: do not use treatment-site visitor lists, patient-derived lists, or lookalike builds from those lists as a default advertising audience. Recheck the current policy before anyone proposes an exception.
Build the program with named owners
A launch should have a named decision-maker at every handoff. One person may hold several roles. The decisions should still stay distinct.
* Capacity and acquisition priorities. Accountable owner: CEO or COO. Required output: Service-line and location priorities.
* Channel plan and spending. Accountable owner: CMO or marketing lead. Required output: Budget, campaign purpose, and test boundaries.
* Inquiry qualification. Accountable owner: Admissions lead. Required output: Written definitions and disposition rules.
* Privacy and regulated claims. Accountable owner: Privacy or compliance lead, with counsel as needed. Required output: Data-flow and claims decisions.
* Campaign execution. Accountable owner: In-house buyer or agency. Required output: Approved build and change log.
* Measurement architecture. Accountable owner: Analytics or technical owner. Required output: Event map, reconciliation rules, and access controls.
### Step 1: Establish what admissions can support
Owner: COO with admissions.
Document services, locations, populations served, contact coverage, and the operational constraints that should shape advertising. If a program cannot accept inquiries for a service, marketing needs a written instruction, not a message buried in a chat thread.
For multi-location organizations, define where central admissions ends and facility-level responsibility begins. Record both the location initially requested and the location ultimately selected when a transfer occurs.
### Step 2: Approve the qualification rules
Owner: Admissions lead with marketing.
Turn the qualification agreement into a short disposition dictionary. Useful statuses include new and awaiting contact, contact established, qualified inquiry, assessment or next step scheduled, admission confirmed, not progressing with a standardized reason, and unresolved or insufficient information.
Keep detailed clinical information out of marketing-facing fields. Require enough operational information to explain the stage without turning a dashboard into a patient record. Set a review cadence for disputed classifications.
### Step 3: Map eligibility and data flows
Owner: Privacy or compliance lead with the technical owner.
Draw the intended path in plain language: ad interaction, then website or phone contact, then intake record, then qualification, then admission, then restricted reporting. For each handoff, identify what information moves, who receives it, why it is needed, and whether an external system is involved.
Separate internal admissions measurement from ad-platform feedback. A leadership team may need to understand admissions performance internally without sending admission-level information to an advertising platform.
### Step 4: Build campaigns around operational differences
Owner: Marketing lead and media buyer.
Separate campaigns where the distinction changes a decision: service line, geography, brand versus nonbrand demand, or location. Avoid splits that add complexity without a business purpose.
For search, prepare keyword themes, negative-keyword rules, geographic settings, destination pages, and call routing together. Review searches for physical rehabilitation, employment, education, and unrelated services before deciding whether they belong in scope. Keep branded demand visible on its own so executives can see how much reported activity came from people already looking for the organization.
### Step 5: Make the landing page an accurate handoff
Owner: Marketing with admissions and compliance.
The page should state who provides the service, where it is offered, what the next step involves, and how to make contact. Review every factual statement about services, credentials, payment arrangements, and availability against approved organizational information.
Keep the first contact request narrow. Ask whether each field is necessary before collecting it. Test the full mobile path: advertisement, destination, phone action, form submission, confirmation, and routing, including after-hours handling.
### Step 6: Run campaign quality assurance before spending
Owner: Media buyer, with independent approval of launch-critical items.
Campaign quality assurance should cover policy status and documented approvals, final ad copy and every destination, geographic targeting and exclusions, phone numbers and coverage, form delivery and duplicate handling, tracking behavior and unexpected data transmission, and budget limits plus pause authority.
Use synthetic test information. Do not place patient details into test forms or screenshots. Record the tested version. Recheck affected elements after a material change.
### Step 7: Launch against decision rules
Owner: Marketing lead with admissions.
Agree in advance what would justify continuing, investigating, expanding, or pausing. Review spending and delivery alongside qualification completeness, contact handling, and emerging admissions outcomes. Do not force a budget decision from immature admissions data.
Execution detail for paid search belongs on the [rehab PPC service](/services/rehab-ppc) page. This guide sets the operating decisions. It is not a media-buying manual.
Measure inquiries against admissions
Use a stage model that keeps advertising activity distinct from admissions progress: spend, interaction, inquiry, qualified inquiry, next step, admission. Each stage needs a definition, a timestamp, a source of truth, and an owner.
### A dashboard built for decisions
The table below is an original dashboard specification, not a performance report. It contains no results. When a denominator is zero, show the metric as unavailable rather than forcing a value.
* Media spend. Definition to document: Platform cost for the stated period. Decision it supports: Budget pacing.
* Unique inquiries. Definition to document: Deduplicated treatment-related inquiry episodes. Decision it supports: Acquisition volume.
* Qualified inquiries. Definition to document: Episodes meeting the approved definition. Decision it supports: Service relevance.
* Cost per qualified inquiry. Definition to document: Media spend divided by qualified inquiries. Decision it supports: Campaign comparison.
* Qualified-to-admission rate. Definition to document: Admissions divided by qualified inquiries from the same cohort. Decision it supports: Progression analysis.
* Media cost per attributed admission. Definition to document: Cohort media spend divided by admissions attributed under the stated method. Decision it supports: Acquisition economics.
* Disposition completeness. Definition to document: Share of inquiries with an assigned status. Decision it supports: Reporting reliability.
* Attribution coverage. Definition to document: Share of admissions with a usable source under the stated rules. Decision it supports: Measurement visibility.
Keep media-only cost distinct from fully loaded acquisition cost. If agency fees, technology, creative, or other expenses are included, say so on the report.
### Compare two views, not two calendars
For admissions-based PPC reporting, group inquiries by when they entered the acquisition process, then follow their progression. Do not divide this month’s spending by every admission recorded this month if those admissions began in earlier periods. That mixes an acquisition calendar with an operations calendar.
Maintain two views. The acquisition cohort asks what happened to inquiries generated in a defined period. The operational view asks which admissions occurred in a defined period. Both are useful. They answer different questions. Label recent cohorts as still developing, state how long the window stays open, and explain how later admissions are added.
### An illustrative operator decision
Consider a hypothetical organization advertising outpatient and residential services. On the review screen, the outpatient campaign shows a long list of inquiries still awaiting disposition. The residential campaign shows fewer inquiries, but the rows that exist have a status, a next step, and a more complete cohort. A busy admissions office and a quieter residential queue can produce that picture without either campaign being “better.”
That contrast is not evidence that residential advertising is more effective. The operating response is to clear overdue outpatient dispositions, check routing and staffing coverage, compare cohorts with similar time to progress, and examine service availability separately from advertising relevance. Reallocate only when the available evidence supports the decision. This example uses no customer results and no performance figures. It separates a campaign problem from a measurement problem and from an admissions-process problem.
Leave clinical appropriateness to authorized staff. If call review or automated scoring is proposed, define access, processing, retention, and review before it is turned on. Do not attach recordings or transcripts to routine media reports. Keep automated labels distinct from staff-confirmed dispositions, and preserve unresolved cases rather than forcing a positive or negative mark.
Design privacy boundaries before tracking
First-party measurement should start with a map of authorized uses, not a list of tools.
HHS states that if there is no applicable Privacy Rule permission, or if the tracking vendor is not a business associate, individuals’ HIPAA-compliant authorizations are required before protected health information is disclosed to the vendor. The same guidance states that website banners asking users to accept or reject tracking technologies, including cookies, do not constitute a valid HIPAA authorization. [HHS online tracking guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html)
Substance use disorder records may also fall under 42 CFR Part 2. HHS explains that Part 2 applies to federally assisted programs that provide substance use disorder diagnosis, treatment, or referral for treatment, and that some requirements also apply to recipients of those records. Determine whether the organization and the records are in scope. A HIPAA review alone does not answer that question. [HHS Part 2 overview](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/part-2/index.html)
Use these design rules:
* Prefer aggregate reporting when it answers the business question.
* Keep clinical detail in the systems authorized to hold it. Do not copy intake narratives into advertising tools.
* Review what leaves the site, including URLs, event names, form values, identifiers, and integration logs.
* Approve each destination. Permission for an internal report is not permission for a platform upload.
* Document access and retention, including exports, backups, and vendor access.
* Do not treat server-side processing, hashing, or neutral event names as a substitute for reviewing the underlying disclosure.
Avoid sweeping claims that every visit to a public healthcare page is protected health information. HHS’s tracking page notes that on June 20, 2024, a federal court vacated the portion of the guidance that treated an IP address combined with a visit to an unauthenticated public webpage addressing specific health conditions or healthcare providers as sufficient to trigger the obligations described there. HHS states that it is evaluating next steps. The rest of the guidance, including the authorization point above, remains on the page and still needs a careful reading against the organization’s facts. [HHS online tracking guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html)
A useful measurement system has an explicit boundary around what it will not collect or share. This page does not assert that any particular workflow, vendor, or infrastructure arrangement meets HIPAA or Part 2. That determination belongs to the privacy owner and counsel.
What the data cannot prove
Accountability includes a plain list of limits. These limits should sit on the same page as the metrics, not in a footnote nobody reads.
### A platform conversion is not a qualified inquiry
A tracked call, button click, or form event is evidence of that event. It is not an admissions judgment. Keep platform event counts for delivery analysis. Reconcile business outcomes to the agreed intake source of truth.
### Attribution is not proof of causation
A source attached to an admission explains how that admission was credited under a method. It does not establish that advertising alone caused the admission. A person may have encountered the organization through several sources. Use attribution for accountable allocation, and keep the method visible.
### Call duration is not qualification
If duration is used as a screening signal, label it as a proxy. It cannot establish service fit, clinical appropriateness, or whether the caller represents a new inquiry. Do not let it override admissions review.
### Missing records are not negative outcomes
Use “unknown,” “unresolved,” or another explicit category when information is incomplete. Do not count every unclassified inquiry as unsuitable, and do not count every unmatched admission as organic. Report the gap and name the owner who will investigate it.
### Location averages can hide operational differences
Review location-level service scope, capacity, coverage, and data completeness before interpreting a network average. When an inquiry is transferred internally, preserve the original acquisition source and the eventual destination. A transfer should not create a second acquired inquiry.
### More tracking is not automatically better evidence
Collect only what supports an approved decision. A detailed report is not more defensible if its inputs are unauthorized, inconsistently defined, or impossible to reconcile. When person-level linkage is not appropriate, use privacy-reviewed aggregate reporting and state what it cannot establish.
### How paid ads fit drug rehab marketing
Rehab advertising is one component of drug rehab marketing. Keep paid acquisition connected to website content, local visibility, referral development, and admissions communications without blending their reports. Use paid-search questions to identify what the website should explain. Use admissions feedback to improve descriptions of services and next steps. Keep awareness objectives separate from direct admissions objectives.
The same operating model can be adapted to mental healthcare, hospital service lines, and other regulated healthcare organizations. Replace “admission” with the milestone that business actually owns, such as a completed appointment, and rebuild the qualification and privacy rules for that service. Do not copy addiction-specific certification requirements onto every mental health offer. Equally, do not assume a general behavioral-health label keeps a site outside addiction review. LegitScript notes that if a website includes information about substance use disorder treatment, advertising platforms may classify the business under addiction treatment policies even when those services are not the intended advertising focus. [LegitScript certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/faq/)

Before authorizing spending, the same group should be able to say each of the following out loud:
* Admissions and marketing use the same qualification definition.
* Unresolved inquiries remain visible.
* Applicable eligibility and approvals are documented for the services and geographies in market.
* Landing pages accurately represent available services.
* Phone and form routing have been tested with synthetic information.
* Data collection and sharing have defined boundaries.
* Reporting distinguishes events, inquiries, and admissions.

* Someone has explicit authority to pause the campaign.
If a row has no owner, the campaign is not ready. Adding budget will not create the missing decision.

FAQ
Q: Which addiction treatment advertising channels are allowed, and when?
A: Only after the specific advertiser, service, destination, and geography pass the current platform rules. In the United States, Google requires LegitScript certification and Google certification for recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction advertising, and services ineligible for that certification are not allowed to advertise those services on Google. [Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en) Meta requires LegitScript certification and written permission for addiction treatment ads targeting people in the United States. [Meta drug and alcohol addiction treatment ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drug-alcohol-addiction-treatment/) Other formats and platforms need their own written eligibility review. An available ad account is not permission.
Q: Can treatment centers advertise on Google?
A: Eligible advertisers can, subject to Google’s requirements and to the service and destination being advertised. For applicable United States recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction advertising, Google requires LegitScript certification and Google certification. Check the specific service, landing page, and target location before planning a launch. [Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en)
Q: Does LegitScript certification mean every ad will be approved?
A: No. LegitScript states that certification may be required to participate in certain advertising programs, and that it is not the same thing as advertising approval. Platforms set and enforce their own policies and decide whether an advertisement is eligible. [LegitScript addiction treatment certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/faq/)
Q: How should rehab advertising be measured against qualified calls or admissions?
A: Start with unique inquiries and the written qualification definition. Track progression inside acquisition cohorts. Then calculate cost per qualified inquiry and media cost per attributed admission. Show disposition completeness, attribution coverage, and unresolved cases beside those metrics. A qualified call is an inquiry that meets the written definition, not a call that simply lasted past a duration threshold.
Q: What is a reasonable advertising budget?
A: Build a planning estimate from service priorities, operating capacity, and evidence from comparable internal campaigns. Label assumptions clearly. Fund a test with explicit review and stopping rules. Do not import an unsupported industry budget, and do not treat a platform-reported cost per click as a cost per admission.
Q: Is rehab advertising the same as drug rehab marketing?
A: No. Drug rehab marketing is the broader planning discipline, covering paid distribution, website content, local visibility, referrals, and admissions communications. Rehab advertising addresses paid distribution, channel eligibility, campaign execution, and acquisition measurement. Coordinate the work. Do not treat every source as paid demand.
Q: Should admission records be uploaded to advertising platforms?
A: Do not make that a default practice. Design internal admissions reporting separately, then have the privacy owner assess any proposed external disclosure and the applicable platform rules. HHS states that, with limited exceptions, the Privacy Rule requires an individual’s written authorization before protected health information is used or disclosed for marketing. [HHS marketing guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html) Marketing Powered’s founder managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ accounts in behavioral and mental health. That is the founder’s operating history, not a claim about agency client results. The next useful decision may be a campaign change, a qualification rule, a routing repair, or a reporting boundary. Bring eligibility, campaign structure, inquiry definitions, admissions handoffs, and measurement into one view before increasing the budget.
