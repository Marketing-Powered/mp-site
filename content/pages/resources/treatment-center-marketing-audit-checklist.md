Treatment Center Marketing Audit: 30-Point Checklist
A treatment center marketing audit checklist is a scored review of whether spend can be traced to a qualified call, a completed benefits check and an admission, and of whether each step can be measured, acted on and run within current policy. This version has 30 checks in six areas, and each check names an owner and the evidence to pull. Start with the evidence already available, then document gaps.

Read the chain from the admissions desk
Picture a Tuesday evening at the intake desk. An admissions counselor is on a callback. The person seeking care reached a general number after seeing a search ad, then called the number on a local listing. The counselor records a level of care, a geography and a payer question. The call ends without an admission. The next morning, marketing opens the ad platform and sees a conversion. Admissions opens the inquiry log and sees an outcome code, or sometimes no code at all. The two records do not have to be wrong. They often are not the same record.
An audit can review channels without explaining admissions. Check whether the records share consistent definitions and identifiers. If source, qualification and outcome do not persist from the first contact to the admission file, leadership cannot explain acquisition cost. Budget then follows the number a platform can display, which is not the number finance uses for census.
Marketing Powered's founder is a former healthcare CMO who ran marketing for a treatment network that grew from 3 to 24 locations and managed $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts. The founder is a court-certified expert witness in advertising strategy. The audit work here is built around attribution to admission, not just lead. That is a measurement standard, not a claim about any center's results.
Work backward from the admission. Every check maps to one of three questions.
* Measure: Can this stage be seen, and can it be tied to a source with a shared identifier?
* Act: If the number is weak, is there a named owner and a documented way to change it?
* Safe: Can this activity continue under current platform terms, privacy obligations and the center's own risk limits?
Cost per lead compared with acquisition cost is a familiar frame. This audit does not rest on that comparison. It asks where a treatment admissions path breaks: call qualification, verification of benefits, level-of-care fit, payer mix, capacity and follow-up.
When this audit applies
Use the checklist for substance use disorder and mental health programs, including detox, residential, partial hospitalization, intensive outpatient and outpatient. It fits an independent center, a multi-location operator and a hospital behavioral health service line. The same structure can be reused for other regulated service lines if qualification criteria and referral sources are swapped for that line. Broader program context for [substance use disorder treatment providers](/who-we-help/behavioral-health/substance-use-disorder) sits outside this audit page.
Run the review when lead or call volume has moved and admissions have not, when leadership is choosing between an agency and an in-house team, or when a treatment center marketing budget has to be defended with more than a click report. Run it when admissions and marketing disagree and no one owns the reconciliation. Run it before opening a location, adding a level of care, or shifting payer mix. Operators comparing vendors should use the same scorecard on each option, so the comparison is about the chain rather than the pitch.
It is not a clinical quality review and it does not speak to treatment efficacy. It is not legal advice. Compliance items tell a team what to document and what to take to qualified counsel. They do not supply the legal conclusion.
The page owns the audit checklist and the measurement chain behind it. Broader rehab marketing and addiction treatment marketing belong on the treatment center marketing hub, not here.
Score each check and name an owner
Score each check 0, 1 or 2.
* 0: Not in place, or described as in place but not verifiable from an artifact.
* 1: Partly in place, or in place without a review schedule.
* 2: In place, documented, reviewed on a set schedule and evidenced.
Thirty checks produce a maximum of 60. The total is less useful than the shape. Strong scores in measurement and weak scores in call handling tell you more than the total alone.
Suggested lead roles, adjusted to the org chart:
* CEO or COO: budget, capacity, cross-functional decisions.
* CMO or marketing lead: channels, creative, vendor management, reporting design.
* Admissions director: qualification criteria, call handling, verification of benefits, follow-up, non-admit reasons.
* Analytics or web lead: tracking, field maps, call data, site handoffs.
* Compliance or legal: platform terms, privacy, recording, referral and lead arrangements. Outside counsel reviews the legal items. The internal owner makes sure the review happens and is filed.
Pull the artifact, not the recollection. A field map, a script, a vendor contract, a policy memo with a date, or a quarterly report is evidence. "We track that" is not.
Compare sources with an economics worksheet
Do the worksheet before scoring, in one room, with the people who hold different pieces of the chain. Finance brings the spend definition and the net revenue definition. Admissions brings the written qualification criteria and the outcome codes. Marketing brings the media invoices and the source list. Analytics brings the field map from click or call to the inquiry record. The comparison that matters is not a prettier traffic chart. It is two source rows, side by side, with the same definitions, showing which row still has people in it after qualification and verification.
The worksheet uses formulas only. Fields stay blank until your team fills them. This page does not publish benchmark rates. Compare definitions and operating conditions before using external benchmarks. A borrowed rate can describe a different level of care, payer mix, or geography.
Step 1. Define the cohort. Group each inquiry by the date it arrived, not by the admission date. Inquiry-date cohorts keep a later admission from being credited to the wrong period. Set the follow-up window from your own admissions cycle, and write that window on the sheet. Do not borrow a window from another center.
Step 2. Fill the chain by source. Use the verification stage only for inquiries that require insurance verification. Report self-pay, scholarship and other payment pathways on separate rows so they are not forced through a benefits check they never needed.
* S; Definition: Spend you attribute to the source, including media and the tool, agency, or staff cost you choose to allocate; Your value: 
* I; Definition: Inquiries: calls, forms, chats and texts; Your value: 
* Q; Definition: Qualified inquiries under admissions' written criteria; Your value: 
* V; Definition: Verification of benefits completed, not confirmed coverage; Your value: 
* A; Definition: Admissions in the cohort window; Your value: 
* N; Definition: Net revenue per admission, as finance defines expected collections after payer mix; Your value: 
Step 3. Compute rates and costs. Leave a cell blank when the denominator is zero. Do not replace a blank with zero and call it performance.
* Qualification rate = Q / I
* Verification rate = V / Q, only on the insurance-verification pathway
* Admit rate from verified = A / V, only on that pathway
* End-to-end rate = A / I
* Cost per qualified inquiry = S / Q
* Cost per completed benefits check = S / V
* Cost per admission = S / A
* Attributed revenue return after acquisition spend = (A × N - S) / S
That last ratio excludes treatment-delivery costs. It does not establish incremental revenue or profit. It is a worksheet figure for comparing sources under one set of definitions, not a financial statement.
Step 4. Add coverage.
* Source coverage = admissions with a recorded source / total admissions
* Call capture rate = inbound calls with a tracked source and a recorded outcome / total inbound calls
If source coverage is low, channel-level costs above it are estimates. The finding is the gap.
### What the worksheet should show about admissions economics
A useful audit answers four questions in plain language.
* Where the chain narrows. Qualification, verification, or admit after verification. A weak qualification rate warrants checking targeting, messaging and the written criteria. A weak verification rate warrants checking payer mix, staffing and the verification workflow. A weak admit rate after verification warrants checking level-of-care fit, capacity, follow-up and logistical barriers. These are hypotheses, not established causes.
* Which sources hold up past the first stage. A source with many inquiries and a thin downstream path can be more expensive than a source with fewer inquiries and a stronger path to verification and admission. Judge that only after the definitions match.
* What is invisible. The unattributed share is a finding. Do not spread it across channels to make the sheet look complete.
* Whether capacity can absorb demand. If beds, clinician slots, or a given level of care are constrained, more inquiries do not become census. Marketing and operations need the same capacity view before spend increases.
### How to measure the audit against qualified calls and admissions
Use three levels, and label which one a report is using.
* Qualified calls or inquiries give feedback before admission-level results mature. They are only as stable as the written definition. Admissions owns that definition.
* Completed benefits checks are a tighter view of payer-process fit on the insurance pathway. They still include intake-process noise, and they are not a coverage approval.
* Admissions are the outcome leadership is usually trying to explain. They lag, and at small volumes a few cases move the rate.
Judge the audit by the share of spend you can trace to those levels, and by later movement in cost per qualified inquiry and cost per admission on inquiry-date cohorts. Treat early movement as directional until counts support a conclusion. For how source, call and admission records are joined, see [analytics and attribution](/services/analytics-and-attribution).
Stricter criteria can reduce the qualification rate. Changes to the criteria also limit comparisons across periods.
Work through the 30 checks
Score only what an artifact supports. If two people disagree about a score, record both the disagreement and the missing artifact. That disagreement is often the finding.
### Area A: Economics and the measurement chain
* 1; Check: A written definition of a qualified inquiry exists, is owned by admissions, and is the definition used in reporting.; Lead owner: Admissions, CMO; Evidence to pull: Criteria document and last review date
* 2; Check: Every inquiry carries a source, and that source persists to the admission record in the CRM or the adjacent system of record.; Lead owner: Analytics; Evidence to pull: Field map from click or call to the admission record
* 3; Check: Reports show qualified inquiries, completed benefits checks and admissions by source, not only clicks and raw leads.; Lead owner: CMO; Evidence to pull: Last three monthly reports
* 4; Check: Source coverage and call capture rate are calculated and reviewed.; Lead owner: Analytics, Admissions; Evidence to pull: Both metrics for the last full quarter
* 5; Check: Leadership has an agreed cost-per-admission or worksheet-return figure tied to finance's net revenue definition and to capacity.; Lead owner: CEO or COO, Finance; Evidence to pull: Formula, target if one exists, and the owner
If Area A is weak, later channel work is optimizing a partial view.
### Area B: Call handling and intake
Review intake for missed calls, incomplete outcomes and stalled handoffs. This is the stretch marketing reports most often omit.
* 6; Check: Inbound calls are answered or routed against defined hours, including evenings and weekends, and missed calls are measured.; Lead owner: Admissions; Evidence to pull: Missed-call share by hour and day
* 7; Check: Time to first contact on missed calls and form fills is tracked against an internal standard.; Lead owner: Admissions; Evidence to pull: Median and tail time, with the standard written down
* 8; Check: Intake uses one screening script for level of care, geography and payer, and records an outcome code on every call.; Lead owner: Admissions; Evidence to pull: Script and outcome-code list
* 9; Check: Non-admit reasons are coded, for example no benefits, out of network, level-of-care mismatch, declined, or unreachable, and reviewed on a schedule.; Lead owner: Admissions, CMO; Evidence to pull: Reason distribution for the last cohort
* 10; Check: Verification of benefits has a named owner, a documented turnaround expectation and a fallback when verification fails.; Lead owner: Admissions, Finance; Evidence to pull: Workflow document and turnaround log
A fast callback standard that exists only in a meeting note scores 0. A standard with a log scores higher.
### Area C: Paid media and search
Rehab lead generation often enters here as paid search, paid social, or a lead arrangement. Put each source through the worksheet before treating inquiry volume as demand.
* 11; Check: Campaigns are structured by level of care, geography and intent, not one broad bucket.; Lead owner: CMO; Evidence to pull: Account structure export
* 12; Check: Internal reporting connects sources to qualified inquiries and admissions. Any platform conversion upload is separately assessed against applicable privacy requirements and platform terms before activation.; Lead owner: Analytics, Compliance; Evidence to pull: Conversion-action list, plus the upload review note
* 13; Check: Search terms, placements and audiences are reviewed on a set cadence, with negatives maintained. The review includes sensitive-health audience restrictions.; Lead owner: CMO; Evidence to pull: Review log and change history
* 14; Check: Budget allocation reflects downstream rates and capacity, not inquiry cost alone.; Lead owner: CMO, CEO or COO; Evidence to pull: Channel budget beside the worksheet
* 15; Check: Ad and landing-page claims match the services offered and the people the center can admit.; Lead owner: CMO, Admissions; Evidence to pull: Ad review and landing-page notes
Google Ads reports can combine observed and modeled conversions. Reconcile platform reporting with your internal admission records rather than treating conversion totals as verified admissions. [Google Ads conversion modeling](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12443859) describes modeling as a way to estimate conversions Google cannot observe directly, then integrate those estimates with observed conversions in reporting. That is useful for bidding. It is not an admission file.
On check 12, legal permission and platform permission are separate questions. Google's customer data policies say conversions related to sensitive categories, including health or medical information such as purchases of medical services, cannot be used for measurement in enhanced conversions or store sales uploads. The policy applies to advertisers using enhanced conversions for web, enhanced conversions for leads, store sales uploads, or Google-engaged audiences. [Google Ads customer data policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7475709) Confirm the current text before any upload is turned on.
On check 13, health is a sensitive interest category. Google's examples include counseling for mental health issues such as depression, anxiety and addiction. For that category, advertiser-curated audiences cannot be used. The restricted set includes Customer Match, your data segments, audience expansion and lookalike segments. Predefined Google audiences can be used, because Google excludes sensitive user signals from them. [Google Ads health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855) Review the live policy before copying an audience setup from another vertical.
### Area D: Website, landing pages and handoff
Test site findings against intake and capacity data. A faster page does not repair a missing outcome code.

* 17; Check: Per-source tracking numbers are in place and map to the CRM, or the gap is documented.; Lead owner: Analytics; Evidence to pull: Number-pool configuration and field map
* 18; Check: Level-of-care and insurance pages answer questions an admissions counselor would otherwise repeat.; Lead owner: CMO, Admissions; Evidence to pull: Page review against top call questions
* 19; Check: Indexability, canonical tags, schema and key-page inclusion in the sitemap have been reviewed, with a dated note.; Lead owner: Web; Evidence to pull: Crawl summary and date
* 20; Check: Form and chat handoffs reach a monitored queue, with an owner and a failure alert.; Lead owner: Web, Admissions; Evidence to pull: Test submission and time to first response
### Area E: Local presence, reputation and referral demand
* 21; Check: Google Business Profile details are accurate for every location, and name, address and phone match across major listings.; Lead owner: Marketing; Evidence to pull: Listing audit
* 22; Check: Reviews are requested without incentives and without selective solicitation of positive reviews. Responses follow a privacy-safe policy. Scripted language, if restricted, is the center's own safeguard.; Lead owner: Marketing, Compliance; Evidence to pull: Review policy and a sample of responses
* 23; Check: Referral sources are tracked, including hospitals, emergency departments, courts, employers, professionals and detox referral demand into residential or another level of care.; Lead owner: Admissions, CMO; Evidence to pull: Referral source log
* 24; Check: Directory and marketplace listings are checked for accuracy, and any paid listing is held to the same downstream measurement as other sources.; Lead owner: CMO; Evidence to pull: Directory list and per-source rates
* 25; Check: Multi-location operators report by facility and service line, not only in aggregate.; Lead owner: CMO, COO; Evidence to pull: Facility-level report
Google's Maps contribution policy does not allow fake engagement. Merchants may not offer incentives such as payment, discounts, or free goods or services in exchange for a review, a revision, or removal of a negative review. Merchants may not discourage negative reviews or selectively solicit positive reviews. They may ask for content that reflects a genuine experience, without an incentive. [Google Maps prohibited and restricted content](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) Keep clinical detail out of public responses.
### Area F: Marketing compliance, privacy and data control
These five checks produce questions and dated memos. They do not certify a workflow. Policies change. Confirm the current platform page and ask qualified counsel before acting. Marketing Powered is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware. That awareness is a reason to document the review, not a substitute for it. No tool, vendor, or workflow makes a center compliant by itself.
* 26; Check: Applicable advertiser and domain approvals are documented. For US recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction advertising, Google certification status and LegitScript status are on file. Other platforms are assessed separately.; Lead owner: Compliance, CMO; Evidence to pull: Policy review memo with date and certificate status
* 27; Check: Tags, forms, chat and call tools are inventoried for data sent to third parties. Permitted disclosures and required agreements are documented. HIPAA and 42 CFR Part 2 applicability are assessed separately.; Lead owner: Compliance, Analytics; Evidence to pull: Tag inventory and data-flow map
* 28; Check: Recording, transcription, notice, consent, access and retention requirements are documented for each call workflow before the workflow is enabled.; Lead owner: Compliance, Admissions; Evidence to pull: Notice script, recording policy and counsel note
* 29; Check: Lead purchases, call transfers, marketplace contracts and referral compensation have a documented legal review before activation.; Lead owner: Compliance, CEO; Evidence to pull: Contract inventory and review notes
* 30; Check: Ownership, access, retention and vendor handling are documented for CRM, call data, ad accounts and analytics, including who can export the data.; Lead owner: COO, Compliance; Evidence to pull: Data-handling register
For check 26, Google restricts promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Advertisers who have been approved can promote those services in specified locations, including the United States. For US campaigns, Google's healthcare and medicines policy requires certification as an addiction services provider by LegitScript, and also requires Google certification. Not all drug and alcohol addiction services are eligible for LegitScript certification. Those that are not eligible are not allowed to advertise those services on Google. [Google Ads healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) Examples covered by the addiction-services policy include clinical addiction treatment providers, certain recovery support services, lead generators or referral agencies for addiction services, and crisis hotlines for drug and alcohol addiction. Services unrelated to drug and alcohol addiction, such as impulse control disorders, behavioral addiction, or nicotine addiction, are outside that policy. [Google Ads addiction services policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649) Do not extend the LegitScript requirement to every mental health advertiser. Google states that violations of the addiction-services policy will not lead to immediate account suspension without a prior warning, and that a warning is issued at least 7 days before suspension. Other healthcare policies are written differently. Read the policy that matches the ad, and assess other platforms on their own current terms. This page does not treat any non-Google rule as verified.
For check 27, HHS has issued a bulletin on online tracking technologies used by HIPAA covered entities and business associates. The bulletin is not a statement that every public-page visit is protected health information. On June 20, 2024, a federal court vacated the guidance to the extent it said HIPAA obligations are triggered when an online technology connects an IP address with a visit to an unauthenticated public webpage addressing specific health conditions or healthcare providers. The bulletin states that HHS is evaluating next steps, and that tracking technologies on many unauthenticated pages do not have access to protected health information. [HHS guidance on online tracking technologies](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html) Separately, 42 CFR Part 2 protects the confidentiality of substance use disorder patient records for federally assisted programs that provide diagnosis, treatment, or referral for treatment. In general, a Part 2 program may not share information that would identify someone as having or having had a substance use disorder unless Part 2 permits the disclosure. [HHS Part 2 overview](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/part-2/index.html) Ask privacy counsel which regime applies to which system. Do not assume a public marketing page and an authenticated patient portal follow the same rule.
For check 29, federal law addresses prohibited remuneration involving referrals to covered recovery homes, clinical treatment facilities and laboratories, with statutory conditions and exceptions. It does not establish that every paid lead arrangement is unlawful. A clinical treatment facility, as defined in the statute, is a medical setting other than a hospital that provides detoxification, risk reduction, outpatient treatment and care, residential treatment, or rehabilitation for substance use under state licensure or certification. The statute also says it does not occupy the field to the exclusion of state laws on the same subject. [18 U.S.C. § 220](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title18/pdf/USCODE-2024-title18-partI-chap11-sec220.pdf) Document counsel's review of each arrangement. Do not treat a vendor's template as that review.

### Read the pattern, not only the total
Mark each weak check M, A, or S: measure, act, or safe.
* Weak in Area A: Repair measurement before spending to optimize channels. Otherwise the team is tuning the platform's view of success.
* Weak in Area B: Repair intake before buying more demand. Extra inquiries can pass through the same missed calls, missing codes and stalled handoffs.
* Stronger in A and B, weaker in C or D: Investigate targeting and conversion alongside capacity, market demand and data quality. Do not treat the score as proof of a single cause.
* Weak in Area E: Local and referral demand may be unmanaged or unmeasured. Read the referral log before concluding the channel is small.
* Weak in Area F: Escalate before scaling spend. A compliance gap can interrupt the accounts the census plan depends on. The cost is not offset by a channel ratio.
A cluster of M items means the instruments are missing. A cluster of A items means data exists and no one has authority to use it. A cluster of S items means the risk is not what the dashboard suggests.
What this audit cannot prove
An honest review states its limits before anyone uses it to move a budget.
* Small counts are noisy. At low call or admission volume, a few cases move every rate. Report counts beside rates. Do not conclude from one short period.
* Admissions lag. A cohort can look weak because inquiries have not reached a decision. Keep the inquiry date and the follow-up window visible.
* Platform totals are not the admission file. Google Ads reports can combine observed and modeled conversions. Reconcile them with internal admission records. [Google Ads conversion modeling](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12443859)
* Source is a label, not a cause. A person may see an ad, call a listing number and mention a professional referral. Last-touch source on a call is a convenience. Ask intake to record how the person says they found the center, and keep that answer separate from the tracked source.
* Recording and analysis have legal limits. You may not be able to study everything a system can technically capture. Document the permitted scope before enabling a workflow.
* Qualification is a judgment. Stricter criteria can reduce the qualification rate and can hide people who needed a different level of care or a referral elsewhere. Review the criteria with clinical and admissions leadership when the service mix changes.
* The checklist does not measure clinical quality or outcomes. It audits marketing and intake systems. It makes no claim about treatment efficacy.
* Coverage is not the same as profit. Attributed revenue return after acquisition spend ignores treatment-delivery cost and does not show incremental profit.
### Common mistakes
* Starting with the website. Visible fixes are easy to assign. Test them against intake and capacity data before treating them as the census explanation.
* Letting marketing define qualified. Admissions owns what can be admitted. Marketing supplies volume and source detail.
* Reporting only the platform view. If the only report comes from the ad account, the center is grading acquisition with the platform's tools.
* Leaving [lead vendors](/resources/drug-rehab-leads) outside the sheet. Purchased inquiries and marketplace calls belong on the same worksheet, and on the counsel review, before they are treated as a normal channel.
* Auditing once. Without an owner and a reassessment date, the score expires.
* Importing a peer rate. Compare definitions and operating conditions before using an external benchmark.
Prioritize fixes in one working session
Run the work in five stages. Do not assign a calendar length in advance. Timing depends on data access, system complexity and review scope.
Gather evidence. Analytics and admissions pull Area A and Area B artifacts. Marketing pulls account exports. Compliance pulls the vendor list, the tag inventory and any prior counsel notes. If an artifact does not exist, write that down. Absence is a score of 0, not a task for later that quietly disappears.
Complete the worksheet. Marketing, admissions and finance fill one cohort together. Disagreement about "qualified" is part of the finding. Split insurance-verification inquiries from other payment pathways before anyone calculates a verification rate.
Score. A reviewer who does not own the function being scored marks the 30 checks against evidence. The COO is often the right reviewer. An outside reviewer can serve the same role if the internal owner would be grading their own work.
Prioritize. Hold one working session. Put the scored sheet, the cohort worksheet and the compliance memo on the table. The people in the room are the CEO or COO, the admissions director, the marketing lead, the analytics lead and the compliance owner. The session has three outputs: the three highest-leverage fixes, an owner and a reassessment point for each, and a budget statement for the next period that uses the worksheet rather than a platform conversion total. The image of a useful session is not a slide of channel logos. It is a marked-up source table, a call-outcome log and a short list of decisions.
Reassess. Rescore the failed checks. Compare the next inquiry-date cohort with the prior one. Keep counts visible. If volume is too small, say so.
### Use the audit in a rehab marketing plan
The audit should feed a rehab marketing plan with three decisions: what to fix, what to fund and what to stop. It should not open with a channel menu.
Fix before you fund. If attribution or intake is broken, the first dollars in a treatment center marketing budget go to repair. Later dollars pass through the same points.
Fund from downstream rates, with a ceiling set by capacity. Compare addiction marketing channels on cost per qualified inquiry, cost per completed benefits check and cost per admission, adjusted for volume and for how much of the chain is actually observed. If beds or slots are full, holding spend, shifting level of care, or changing payer mix can be the rational move. More inquiries are not automatically more census.
Stop what cannot be measured and is not being tested. Some referral relationships and some brand activity are slow to attribute. Bound them. Name the period, the source code and the way the team will judge them. Do not leave them as permanent exceptions.
This page does not recommend a spend level. Margin, payer mix, capacity and census goals belong to finance and leadership. A figure offered without those inputs would be a guess.

FAQ
Q: What should a marketing audit reveal about admissions economics?
A: It should show where the path from spend to admission narrows, whether that narrowing is qualification, verification, or admit after verification, and which sources still hold up after the first stage. It should show how much of admission volume has a recorded source. It should also show whether capacity limits what additional demand can do. Use the worksheet formulas, keep insurance and other payment pathways separate, and treat the ratios as comparisons under your definitions, not as profit.
Q: How should a treatment center marketing audit checklist be measured against qualified calls or admissions?
A: Measure at three labeled levels. Qualified calls or inquiries are the faster read, before admission-level results mature. Completed benefits checks are the payer-process read on inquiries that require verification. Admissions are the outcome that explains census, and they lag. Judge the audit by the share of spend traceable to those levels and by changes in cost per qualified inquiry and cost per admission on inquiry-date cohorts. Do not treat a platform conversion total as a verified admission count.
Q: Who should own the audit?
A: The CEO or COO should own the decision. Admissions, marketing, analytics and compliance each own their checks. The person who scores a function should not be the only person who runs that function.
Q: Does the checklist work for multi-location or hospital programs?
A: Yes, if the worksheet can be run by facility and service line, and if referral and scheduling steps are added to the chain where they exist. Hospital behavioral health programs often have extra handoffs. The federal referral statute cited above defines a clinical treatment facility as a setting other than a hospital, which is one more reason to take hospital arrangements to counsel rather than assuming the same analysis applies.
Q: Does this replace legal or privacy review?
A: No. Area F identifies what to document and what to ask. It is not legal advice, and it does not establish that a workflow meets HIPAA, Part 2, platform terms, or referral law.
Q: How long does the audit take?
A: Timing depends on data access, system complexity and review scope. A center with one CRM and a complete call log will move faster than a center whose inquiries sit in several inboxes. Do not treat a short review of the ad account as a completed audit.
Q: Where does rehab marketing guidance live?
A: This page is the audit checklist. It does not define rehab marketing or prescribe a general lead-generation program. Use the treatment center marketing hub for that broader subject, and use this scorecard when the question is whether spend can be traced to a qualified call, a completed benefits check and an admission.
