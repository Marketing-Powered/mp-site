Read the site the way admissions does
Diagnosis starts with the first three minutes of a real call, not with a design score. Admissions usually needs four facts before a conversation is worth staffing: which location, which level of care, which payer, and whether the person is asking about detox, residential treatment, or outpatient care. If those answers live only in a phone script or a PDF, the site is adding handle time.
Read the mobile page the way a family member does at night. Is the number usable before the page finishes loading? Does after-hours contact have a defined route, or does it land in a voicemail no one owns? Does the form ask for clinical history the team does not need in order to schedule an assessment? Rehab call quality is an operating question. A long call can be a qualified residential inquiry or a vendor. Duration will not tell you which.

What the commercial build focuses on
This is a commercial web build judged by the conversion path, not by a launch date. The work focuses on service accuracy, credible facility claims, certification-related content review, intake capture, and speed on the phones people actually use. Broader engineering sits with [web development](/services/web-development). The acceptance test on this offer is whether a qualified person can reach the right location and level of care.
LegitScript's addiction treatment certification standards require a conspicuous disclosure of the physical address where services are rendered and of the specific services provided. Photographs must truthfully depict the applicant's facilities, and content must not misrepresent scope or location. [LegitScript](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/) Those rules are a design constraint. A stock exterior, a borrowed address, or a service menu copied across every location is a credibility problem before it is a layout problem.

Routing, verification, and level of care
A usable path is short enough to follow while upset: inquiry, enough detail to route, verification of what can be checked before a clinical conversation, then a scheduled assessment. Detox, residential treatment, and outpatient care should not share one unlabeled button if the buildings, hours, and payer rules differ. Level-of-care acquisition depends on that split. Residential treatment marketing that hides the residential page under a generic "programs" label forces the call to do the sorting.
Insurance verification is usually a form problem, not a branding problem. Ask for the plan name and member ID when the team needs them to schedule, and stop there. Patient intake website design that collects a clinical history on a public page slows the person who is ready to talk and widens the data the organization must protect. After-hours routing needs an owner. A 24-hour number that rings a desk closed at 5 p.m. is not a contact strategy. It is a missed admission window.
Compare two structures before choosing one. A single national form is easier to maintain and worse at routing. Location pages with a shared pattern, local facts, and a defined handoff are harder to govern and easier for admissions to staff. Multi-location operators should prefer the second when census, licensure, or payer mix differs by site.

Policy, consent, and data limits
Google restricts the promotion of recovery-oriented drug and alcohol addiction services. Advertisers who have applied and been approved may promote those services in listed locations, including the United States. Examples include clinical treatment providers, some recovery support services, lead generators or referral agencies, and crisis hotlines. Services for impulse control, behavioral addiction, or nicotine addiction sit outside that policy. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598649?hl=en)
In the United States, advertising those services requires certification as an addiction services provider by LegitScript, plus certification with Google. Not every service is eligible. Services that are not eligible are not allowed to advertise on Google. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) LegitScript states that lead generators, call centers, or marketers who refer patients to other providers for compensation are explicitly disallowed from its addiction treatment certification. [LegitScript](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/) A treatment center site should not be built as a referral storefront.
Health content is a sensitive interest category. Google's examples include counseling for addiction, including alcohol addiction. Advertiser-curated audiences, including Customer Match and remarketing lists, cannot be used for that category. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855?hl=en) The site has to carry the next step, because a remarketing list is not an available recovery tool.
Privacy rules shape the form. LegitScript's standards require compliance with applicable privacy law, and they state that US applicants who collect, store, or transmit protected health information must comply with applicable HIPAA requirements. [LegitScript](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/addiction-treatment-certification/) HHS describes the minimum necessary standard as requiring reasonable steps to limit protected health information to what is needed for the purpose, with exceptions that include treatment disclosures between providers and uses the individual authorizes. [HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html) Public forms should collect what scheduling needs. Clinical detail belongs in systems built to hold it.
Google's Call Ads Supplemental Terms, currently noted as applicable to advertisers in the United States, state that Google does not intend those communication features to create HIPAA obligations and makes no representation that they satisfy HIPAA requirements. A covered entity that would use them in a way involving protected health information is directed not to accept the terms and to request exclusion from monitoring and recording. [Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16914745?hl=en) Call tracking choices are a privacy decision, not a default setting.
Accessibility is part of the same path. WCAG 2.2 is a W3C Recommendation dated 12 December 2024. Its criteria include labels or instructions, consistent help, and a minimum target size, which matter on intake fields and call buttons. [www.w3.org](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) Marketing Powered is LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware. That is not a certification, and it is not a claim that a workflow satisfies HIPAA. Where model-assisted review is used, inference can run on owned local infrastructure. That does not transfer ownership of client data.

FAQ
Q: What should a treatment center website have to convert qualified calls?
A: A phone path that works at the hour people call, location and level-of-care pages that match what is licensed and offered, and routing so detox, residential, and outpatient inquiries do not share one unanswered queue. The intake path should ask for what scheduling needs, with consent language matched to the data actually collected. Labels, help, and tap targets should meet WCAG 2.2 so the form is usable without a mouse and without guessing which field failed. [www.w3.org](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
Q: How should addiction treatment website design be measured against admissions?
A: Against qualified calls and against scheduled assessments or admissions, using definitions admissions already applies. Click-to-call counts and form completions are inputs. Where data use is authorized, join the inquiry to the scheduled assessment or the admission so leadership can see which locations and levels of care the site supports. A page that increases calls and lowers the share admissions can schedule has not improved acquisition.
