Confirm which program fits the activity you actually run
The first working session is a scoping conversation with the people who know the operation: the compliance lead, the person who owns the website, and whoever manages the pharmacy or prescriber relationship. The question on the table is narrow. Does the business perform an in-scope activity? Healthcare Merchant Certification is intended for businesses involved in the online sale, prescribing, facilitation or payment processing of prescription medications, and the program is separate from Addiction Treatment Certification, which applies when a site covers substance use disorder treatment services [LegitScript Healthcare Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/faq/). Scope matters in both directions. Research-use-only chemical businesses, including peptide sellers, are listed as generally not eligible, and a med spa qualifies only if it offers telemedicine that may result in a prescription or processes card-not-present payments specifically for prescription medications [LegitScript Healthcare Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/faq/). Applying under the wrong framing burns a cycle and leaves a record behind.
Inventory entities, domains, partners and jurisdictions
Peptide operations rarely sit inside one clean entity. A single brand can involve a marketing LLC, a 503A compounding partner, a telehealth group practice, several landing domains and a payments relationship, each with different exposure. The inventory step names all of it before anything is submitted, because certification fees are assessed per root domain, with an application fee of $975 and an annual certification fee of $2,150 due only on approval, while subdomains are covered by the root domain certification and businesses under common ownership may be included in a single application [LegitScript Healthcare Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/faq/). Multi-domain, multi-jurisdiction organizations may also be routed to an enterprise path. For background on program mechanics, see [LegitScript certification: cost, rules and timeline](/resources/legitscript-certification).
Build the documentation room before anyone applies
Readiness is a filing problem. We build one organized, indexed evidence set covering licenses, policies, product records and partner agreements, structured so a reviewer can follow it without asking three rounds of questions. For compounded peptide offerings, LegitScript has said review will consider the specific ingredient, formulation, route of administration, compounding facility and supply chain compliance, and that supporting information may include manufacturer and sourcing documentation, certificates of analysis, labeling and delivery-device details [LegitScript on the HHS peptide compounding policy](https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare/hhs-peptide-compounding-policy-what-healthcare-businesses-need-to-know/). That means product records need to match operational reality, down to whether a formulation contains a single active ingredient and what device, if any, ships with it. Documentation discipline for regulated advertisers is covered further in our [LegitScript certification guide](/resources/legitscript-certification-guide).
Resolve readiness gaps instead of hiding them
Every gap gets a named owner, a dated correction and a written status. Expired license on file, a partner agreement that was never countersigned, a product page describing an offering the pharmacy no longer compounds, a claim implying that enforcement discretion is FDA approval: each becomes a tracked item. LegitScript's guidance is explicit that enforcement discretion does not constitute FDA approval or establish that compounded products are safe or effective, and that businesses should review labels, marketing and patient communications accordingly [LegitScript on the HHS peptide compounding policy](https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare/hhs-peptide-compounding-policy-what-healthcare-businesses-need-to-know/). Where an activity cannot be supported, the answer is to change or remove it, not to obscure it. Maintain certification and track platform rules separately
Certification is a standing obligation. Businesses are asked to report significant changes in ownership, operations and partnerships, major website updates, new services, expansion into new jurisdictions, and licensing or regulatory actions [LegitScript Healthcare Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/faq/). Certification is also not advertising permission. LegitScript's updated review approach for qualifying compounded peptide offerings took effect November 2, 2026, and LegitScript states that advertising platforms and payment providers set their own policies, which may differ, and that FDA may revise or withdraw the enforcement discretion policy at any time [LegitScript on the HHS peptide compounding policy](https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare/hhs-peptide-compounding-policy-what-healthcare-businesses-need-to-know/). Google, for its part, allows telemedicine providers accredited by LegitScript's Healthcare Merchant Certification program and requires advertisers to be certified with Google as well [Google Ads healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en-IE).

FAQ
Q: Which LegitScript program applies to our business?
A: It depends on the activity, not the product category. Healthcare Merchant Certification covers businesses that sell or advertise prescription medications online, offer telemedicine that may result in a prescription, process card-not-present payments for prescription drugs, operate in the pharmaceutical supply chain, or connect patients with contracted pharmacies and providers, while sites addressing substance use disorder treatment are pointed toward Addiction Treatment Certification [LegitScript Healthcare Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/faq/). Mixed models need the question answered entity by entity.
Q: Can an agency issue or promise certification?
A: No. Certification is issued by LegitScript against its own standards, and no outside party can commit to an outcome or an approval timeline. The defensible service is readiness: correct program identification, complete evidence, honest resolution of gaps and coordinated submission.
Q: Does the 2026 HHS policy change every peptide application?
A: No. The enforcement discretion policy concerns six specific peptide bulk drug substances compounded by qualifying 503A pharmacies and physician compounders, does not cover 503B outsourcing facilities, and sets conditions including a single active pharmaceutical ingredient and a specified route of administration [LegitScript on the HHS peptide compounding policy](https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare/hhs-peptide-compounding-policy-what-healthcare-businesses-need-to-know/). Offerings outside those conditions are assessed on their own terms.
Q: Will certification unlock advertising and payment access?
A: Not automatically. Certification demonstrates that a business met LegitScript's standards and supports eligibility with participating platforms and payment providers, but each platform applies its own advertising policies and decisions [LegitScript Healthcare Certification FAQ](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/faq/). Plan channel strategy against the current published policy of each platform you intend to use.
Q: What has to be maintained after certification?
A: Accurate reporting of business, website and licensing changes, ongoing monitoring of your broader offerings and practices, and independent tracking of platform and payment-provider rules, since LegitScript continues monitoring certified merchants beyond the covered peptide question [LegitScript on the HHS peptide compounding policy](https://www.legitscript.com/healthcare/hhs-peptide-compounding-policy-what-healthcare-businesses-need-to-know/).
