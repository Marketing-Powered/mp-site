Peptide Marketing Eligibility Audit
Peptide brands often discover the advertising problem after the invoice: creative built, pages shipped, account flagged. A peptide marketing compliance audit runs those checks in the opposite order, assessing each offer, destination, certification and channel before budget moves. The output is a written decision per line item: proceed, remediate, exclude, or escalate for specialist review.

Define the audit boundary before evidence collection starts
The proposed audit opens with a scoping session, typically the owner, whoever holds compliance sign-off, and the person who will inherit the ad accounts. That conversation produces a fixed list: legal entity and jurisdictions, product lines and SKUs in scope, the dispensing or fulfillment model, the channels you actually intend to use, and channel history including prior disapprovals, suspensions and appeal outcomes. Anything outside that list is named and deliberately excluded.
Fixing scope first is not administrative tidiness. Without it, a peptide advertising audit drifts into open-ended compliance consulting, cost stops being predictable, and nobody can say when the work is finished. A bounded diagnostic has a defined input set, a defined output and an end. The scope described on this page is proposed scope, confirmed in writing before work begins.
Build the evidence inventory
The proposed document request covers business and professional licenses, pharmacy or practice credentials, certification status and expiry dates, supplier and product documentation, and the regulatory status you are claiming for each product, whether that is an approved drug, a compounded preparation, a dietary supplement, a cosmetic, or material labeled for research use only. It also captures what is live right now: ad copy, keywords, landing pages, checkout flows and policy pages.
Certification belongs in this inventory, but it is one item in it. Online pharmacies listing prescription drugs must first be accredited through LegitScript, NABP's VIPPS program or the Pharmacy Verified Websites Program, and then apply separately for certification with Google ([Merchant Center healthcare policy](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150151?hl=en)). For the general framework before engaging, start with the [healthcare marketing compliance checklist](/resources/healthcare-marketing-compliance-checklist).
Assess offers and channels as separate questions
Lawful operation, product status, certification, channel eligibility and account standing are five separate questions. Many businesses answer one and assume the rest follow. Platforms assess them independently. Google Ads does not allow promotion of listed unapproved pharmaceuticals and supplements regardless of any claims of legality, and the same policy reaches non-government-approved products marketed as safe or effective for preventing, curing or treating a disease, products subject to government or regulatory action or warning, and products with names confusingly similar to a listed unapproved pharmaceutical, supplement or controlled substance ([Google Ads healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)).
Channel by channel, the rules diverge. Prescription drug terms are restricted in ads, landing pages and keywords, so the ad, the destination and the keyword list each need checking ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)). Shopping runs on its own track: Merchant Center's unapproved substances list extends to substances with similar chemical composition to the listed items, and prescription or behind-the-counter drugs are not allowed outside the United States and Canada ([Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150151?hl=en)). Patient data handling is a different risk category again, covered on the [HIPAA-conscious marketing agency](/services/hipaa-conscious-marketing-agency) page.
Rank findings by action, not by severity
Findings land in a register with one row per offer and channel pair, because the same SKU can be promotable on one surface and ineligible on another. Each row carries a category and the evidence behind it.
* Product line B, Search. Category: Remediate. Basis recorded: Destination page implies treatment of a named condition.
* Product line B, Shopping. Category: Exclude. Basis recorded: Product status documentation not supplied.
* Consultation offer, Search. Category: Proceed. Basis recorded: Claims, destination and geography verified on assessment date.
* Compounded preparation, all channels. Category: Specialist review. Basis recorded: Substance status requires qualified regulatory opinion.
The rows above are an illustrative rubric, not results from a past engagement. Escalation is used honestly: where a product involves a bulk drug substance FDA has placed in its category 2 list of substances that may present significant safety risks in compounding ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)), or where the certification route is unclear, the finding goes to qualified legal or clinical review rather than a marketing opinion. Certification questions often begin with [LegitScript certification cost, rules and timeline](/resources/legitscript-certification).
Deliver a remediation plan, then agree the next engagement
A list of problems is not a deliverable. Every remediate finding is assigned a named owner, commonly a clinical or regulatory reviewer for claims, a developer for destination and checkout changes, and a compliance lead for documentation gaps. Each states the specific evidence missing, the change required, and the checkpoint that confirms the fix before anything is submitted for review. Plans carry re-check dates, because platform policies and certification requirements change and a finding verified months ago is a historical record rather than a current status. If you are weighing internal remediation against a partner, the [healthcare marketing agency vetting and strategy guide](/services/healthcare-marketing-agency) sets out the questions worth asking.
Audit completion and campaign authorization stay separate events. A proceed finding means a line item cleared documented checks on the date it was assessed. It is not a media buy commitment and not a prediction of how a platform reviewer will act. Even where policy is cleared, Google notes that most healthcare ads run as "Eligible (limited)" when the campaign targets allowed locations and the domain is properly certified ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)). Some teams take the register in-house. Others move into build and media work, scoped on its own terms.

Questions peptide teams ask before scoping
### What does the audit cover?
Offers and SKUs in scope, destination pages and checkout flows, claim language, certification status, channel history including past enforcement, and the geographies you intend to target. U.S.-market advertising is the default frame; other jurisdictions are added to scope explicitly.
### Does it determine whether a product is legally marketable?
No. Legal and regulatory status is a determination for qualified counsel and clinical reviewers. The audit documents what is known, identifies the gap and routes it to the appropriate reviewer.
### Does certification make an ad eligible?
No. Certification is a requirement for listing prescription drugs in Merchant Center, and the product itself must still meet the healthcare and medicines policy ([Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150151?hl=en)). On Search, advertisers must separately apply to serve ads for prescription drug services ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)).
### What happens when an offer cannot be promoted?
It is recorded as an exclusion with written rationale. No workaround guidance is provided, and no advice is given on evading platform enforcement.
### What is in the final deliverable?
A findings register with an action category per offer and channel, plus a remediation plan carrying owners, evidence gaps, verification checkpoints and re-check dates.
The Google Ads, Merchant Center and FDA pages cited here were checked on 23 November 2026, and policy pages are rechecked before any finding is issued.
