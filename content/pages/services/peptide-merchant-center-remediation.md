Merchant Center Remediation for Peptide-Related Catalogs
Merchants looking for peptide Merchant Center suspension help are usually facing more than one problem at once: items that are genuinely eligible, items whose feed data contradicts the live product page, and items that do not belong in a promoted catalog at all. Each of those calls for a different response, and the review you submit depends on knowing which is which. This work audits the product data layer first, then documents the correct route.

Identify affected accounts and items
Start by separating what Google actually flagged. Setup and policy issues appear in a banner at the top of Merchant Center and under the Needs attention tab, and a review requested there applies to all issues for the country you sell products in ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13585221)). Product-level issues are handled one item at a time: open the product from the "All products that need attention" table, then choose Request website check or I disagree with the issue ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13585221)). Those buttons may stay inactive until merchant verification is complete and the data source contains at least one product ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13585221)). An item-to-policy ledger records every affected SKU, the policy cited, the destination affected and whether the action sits at item or account level.
Verify listing eligibility before touching the feed
Eligibility is a product question, not a brand question. Google states that some healthcare-related products cannot be listed at all, while others can only be shown in certain locations by merchants who have applied and been approved in their Merchant Center account ([Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150151?hl=en)). Google's named lists are non-exhaustive and extend to substances of similar chemical composition, and the policy disallows herbal and dietary supplements containing active pharmaceutical or dangerous ingredients, products implying they are as effective as prescription drugs, and products subject to government or regulatory action or warning ([Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150151?hl=en)). Check each item's active ingredients against the current list. A collagen Merchant Center review and an injectable research compound are not the same question, and product status conclusions belong with qualified legal or regulatory counsel. The same eligibility discipline runs through [healthcare paid media](/services/paid-media).
Compare the feed against the live product page
Reviewers look at the store, not only the file. For misrepresentation and store transparency issues, Google asks merchants to complete merchant verification, show accurate business details such as address, phone and email that match Merchant Center settings, keep return and shipping policies functional and clear, and ensure prices, availability and product attributes in the data source exactly match the online store ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13585221)). The comparison runs field by field: title, description, ingredient and identity claims, price, availability, terms of sale, and any page language implying a therapeutic effect the product data does not claim. Mismatches are often fixable and can be a cause behind a peptide product feed disapproval. Page-level corrections connect to [healthcare web development](/services/web-development).
Illustrative framework, not live data or real products:
* Collagen powder. Policy cited: None. Level: Item approved. Feed value: In stock, $39. Site value: Out of stock, $44. Action: Correct.
* Topical peptide serum. Policy cited: Misrepresentation. Level: Item. Feed value: "Supports skin repair". Site value: "Clinically proven" claim. Action: Correct, then review.
* Injectable research vial. Policy cited: Healthcare and medicines. Level: Account risk. Feed value: Listed. Site value: Listed. Action: Escalate, likely exclude.
Correct or exclude products honestly
Some items will not survive the audit, and the right answer is removal from the promoted catalog rather than rewriting them into ambiguity. Picture the working session: an ecommerce lead, a regulatory or clinical reviewer and a marketer moving down the ledger row by row, marking each product correct, exclude, or escalate for qualified review. Excluded items stop being submitted to Shopping. Whether they can still be sold through other channels is a separate legal question that belongs to counsel, not to a marketing team. Hidden secondary feeds, reviewer-only landing pages and replacement accounts are not remediation and are not offered here. Claim language is checked against the standards in the [healthcare marketing compliance checklist](/resources/healthcare-marketing-compliance-checklist).
Request the appropriate review, not just any review
Merchant Center asks you to choose between I fixed the issue and I disagree with the issue before requesting review, and Google advises making sure you understand the policy and can support the request before disagreeing ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13585221)). Google also states you may only have one chance to disagree with an issue, that disagreement reviews may take up to 7 business days at account and item level, and that a review may take up to 2 weeks to complete ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13585221)). Pharmacy inventory carries its own gate: prescription listings require accreditation through the LegitScript Healthcare Merchant Certification and Monitoring Program, NABP VIPPS or the Pharmacy Verified Websites Program, followed by a separate application with Google ([Google healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150151?hl=en)). Cost and timeline detail sits in the [LegitScript certification guide](/resources/legitscript-certification).
Prevent catalog drift after listings are restored
In practice, drift comes from product launches, pasted supplier descriptions and seasonal bundles that never pass an eligibility check. That is an operator observation, not measured market data. The control is a gate before any new SKU or edited description reaches the feed, using the same fields reviewers check, plus a dated change log so a future disagreement has evidence behind it. The economics favor prevention: Google states that a one-week cool down period may begin if issues are not resolved by the second review attempt, that the cool down may increase with each unsuccessful review, and that support cannot bypass or shorten it ([Google Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13585221)).
