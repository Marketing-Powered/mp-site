Paid Media for Eligible Peptide Businesses
Advertising permission in this category is decided at the offer level, not at the company level. Marketing Powered works as a peptide advertising agency only after a specific offer, certification status and target market have been confirmed, and this page describes how delivery runs from that point forward. It does not offer blanket approval, reinstatement or any promise that a given peptide product can be advertised.

Confirm approved scope before a campaign plan exists
Scoping starts with the person who owns both the ad budget and the regulatory exposure, usually a clinic operator, pharmacy director, lab commercial lead or consumer-brand marketing head. In a working session we record four things in writing: the exact offer being promoted, the business model behind it, every state or market where ads would serve, and the current certification and account status on each channel. That record matters because Google states that some healthcare content can only be advertised in certain locations by advertisers who have applied and been approved, and that if a campaign targets allowed locations with a properly certified domain the ad is labeled "Eligible (limited)". Google also does not allow promotion of certain products regardless of any claims of legality, including items on its [non-exhaustive unapproved pharmaceuticals and supplements list](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/2423645). If the offer sits there, the honest answer is no plan.
Build permitted demand without audience workarounds
Demand strategy in this vertical is built from intent and geography, not from inferred health signals. Google treats Health as a sensitive interest category, and advertisers promoting products and services in sensitive interest categories cannot use advertiser-curated audiences, while predefined Google audiences remain available because sensitive user signals are excluded from them. That removes the retargeting and list-based tactics most performance teams lean on, so the account has to earn qualified volume through query selection, negative keyword discipline, geographic control tied to licensure, and offer clarity in the ad itself. The structural approach is the same one described on our [healthcare paid media](/services/paid-media) page, applied to a narrower permission set. Marketing Powered was built for Google's sensitive healthcare vertical, where retargeting is not permitted.
Review creative and destinations against evidence limits
Creative review covers the ad, the landing page, the form and every claim between them. Copy must describe the service that is actually permitted, avoid implied clinical efficacy, and stop short of comparative drug claims. Google restricts the use of prescription drug terms in ads, landing pages and keywords, and whether those terms can be used depends on location. Promotion of speculative or experimental medical treatments is also not allowed, outside defined exceptions. Practically, that means the destination page carries the same scrutiny as the ad: matching offer, no dosing or administration instructions, no research-only product positioned for human use. Our approach to ad and landing page review is set out further on the [healthcare PPC](/services/healthcare-ppc) page.
Define acceptable measurement under restricted data conditions
Measurement is specified before launch, not retrofitted. The specification names each conversion event, where it fires, what identifiers are passed, what is deliberately withheld, and who approves changes. Health-related intake data should not flow into ad platforms as event parameters, which pushes serious peptide PPC management toward consented first-party records and offline conversion import from the CRM, where the qualified outcome is confirmed by a human rather than a form submission. Tag and consent configuration gets an independent review before spend begins, because an account can be policy-eligible and still be collecting data it should not collect. The questions used in that review mirror the [healthcare marketing compliance checklist](/resources/healthcare-marketing-compliance-checklist). Marketing Powered operates a LegitScript-aware and HIPAA-aware process and runs owned, local AI infrastructure for HIPAA-aware inference.
Optimize under control, with human approval checkpoints
Automated bidding, asset generation and match-type expansion all create new surface area in a category where enforcement is unforgiving. Google treats unauthorized pharmacy violations as egregious, suspends accounts upon detection without prior warning, and reinstates only in compelling circumstances. So testing runs on a fixed cadence with named approval gates: new keyword themes, new creative and new destinations are reviewed by a person before they serve, and change logs are retained. Automation is used for budget and bid efficiency, not for unsupervised claim generation. Certification status is treated as a live variable rather than a completed task, which is why timelines and scope are tracked alongside the [LegitScript certification](/resources/legitscript-certification) requirements that apply to the business. We do not advise or assist with replacement accounts, cloaking or other enforcement evasion.
Report commercial progress, documented gaps and verified revenue
Reporting separates three things that are usually blended. First, qualified demand: inquiries that match the approved offer and market. Second, documented gaps: disapprovals, geography limits, consent losses and intake handoffs that cost otherwise eligible volume. Third, verified commercial outcome: completed consultations, fulfilled orders or revenue, reported only where authorized data supports the connection. Estimates are labeled as estimates. The operating discipline comes from the founder's history managing $1.5 to $2M per month in Google Ads across 11+ behavioral and mental health accounts, where attribution was tracked to admission, not just lead. Search volume, cost per click and conversion baselines for an individual peptide account are unavailable before launch, not zero.

FAQ
Q: Can every peptide business run ads?
A: No. Eligibility depends on the specific offer, certification status and the locations targeted, and Google prohibits promotion of certain products regardless of any claims of legality. Lawful operation, product status, certification and channel eligibility are four separate questions, and a yes on one does not settle the others.
Q: Which channel should come first?
A: That is a scoping decision, not a fixed recommendation. Sequence follows where permission actually exists today, where intent is highest for the approved offer, and what certification work is already underway. A peptide ad agency that names the channel before reading the offer is guessing.
Q: How is quality measured when events are restricted?
A: Through consented first-party data and offline conversion import, with the qualified outcome confirmed in the CRM rather than inferred from a click. Audience-based measurement is constrained because advertiser-curated audiences are unavailable in sensitive interest categories, so the signal has to come from your own records.
Q: Does automation remove the review requirement?
A: No. Smart bidding and generated assets still produce ads and destinations that are evaluated under the same policies, and enforcement can arrive without warning. Eligible peptide campaign management keeps a human approval point in front of anything new that serves.
Q: What evidence should an engagement include?
A: Documented offer and channel permissions, a sanitized campaign architecture, a creative review record, and a written measurement specification.
