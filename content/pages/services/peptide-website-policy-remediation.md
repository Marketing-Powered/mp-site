Peptide Website and Catalog Policy Remediation Before You Appeal
Peptide website policy remediation begins after a finding already exists: an ad disapproval, a shopping catalog rejection, an account suspension notice or a regulatory letter. The correction has to be visible to ordinary visitors and to reviewers, applied across every linked page, image and feed, and recorded as it is made. This is corrective implementation tied to a documented finding, not a new website build and not a prediction of reinstatement.

Start from the documented finding, not a general cleanup
Work begins with the notice itself. Each line of the disapproval, suspension message or warning letter is translated into a register entry: the issue text, the exact URLs or feed items implicated, the asset type, and the owner of the fix. That register is the scope. Without it, remediation becomes opinion.
Scope matters because reviewers look wider than the page you bought traffic to. Google states that in assessing circumvention it may review information from multiple sources including your ad, website, accounts and third-party sources, and that accounts may be suspended based on adverse regulatory warnings, settlements or rulings about advertising practices ([Google Ads circumventing systems policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15938075?hl=en)). Regulators read the same way. In the Gram Peptides matter, FDA reviewed the firm's website over a period from January to March 2026 before issuing its letter ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)).
Audit the whole destination, not the page reviewers land on
A peptide landing page policy audit that stops at one URL is the most common reason a finding recurs. The destination audit covers linked product and category pages, titles and meta descriptions, structured data fields, image files and alt text, PDFs, blog and education content, redirects and click trackers, and every attribute in the product feed. A peptide catalog compliance review also looks at adjacency, because what sits next to a SKU carries meaning. FDA treated the sale of bacteriostatic water for injection alongside peptide products that require reconstitution as evidence that the water was intended for use in combination for injection ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)). Findings route into the same register, which also tells you where new construction is genuinely needed rather than correction, a distinction covered in [healthcare web development](/services/web-development).
Resolve the inconsistencies that actually created the finding
Most peptide ad destination remediation fails on substance, not wording. A disclaimer does not override the rest of the site. FDA found that despite labeling stating products were "not intended for human consumption, medical use, or veterinary use," evidence from the website established that the products were intended to be drugs for human use, citing product page statements about appetite, glucose handling and body weight ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)).
Correction means aligning the actual facts: what the product is, who the stated buyer is, what the business is permitted to sell, who the legal entity is, and what the offer does. Claims that imply treatment, dosing guidance or human administration are removed rather than softened. Entity names, addresses and contact details are made identical across site, feed and verification records. The sequencing logic is similar to the one in the [healthcare marketing compliance checklist](/resources/healthcare-marketing-compliance-checklist).
Publish one version of the truth for users and reviewers
Every change is published publicly, to all traffic. Google defines cloaking as showing different content on a website to different people or to Google in order to hide things that break its rules, and it permits only limited variation such as language, offers or geography, with the promoted product or service remaining the same for everyone ([Google Ads circumventing systems policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15938075?hl=en)). The same policy treats creating replacement accounts after a suspension decision and submitting false information in verification programs as circumvention, and describes those violations as egregious, with suspension on detection and reinstatement only in compelling circumstances.
So the launch gate is explicit: a documented remediation scope, no concealed content, no reviewer-only page variants, no alternate review domains, no replacement accounts. Verify the work and capture dated evidence
Verification is a second pass, not a status email. Dated public captures are taken before and after each change, feeds are resubmitted and re-checked, structured data is validated against visible content, and crawlers are confirmed to reach every page that was corrected. Syndicated copies, cached versions and marketplace listings are retested, because an old description living on a partner site can reproduce the original finding.
The evidence standard here comes from courtroom habits rather than marketing ones. Hand off a review packet that states what remains unresolved
The deliverable is a packet: the issue-to-URL register with status per item, before and after captures with dates, feed and structured data check results, the qualified claims review, and a plain list of what was not fixed and why. That last section is the point. Some items are not website defects. Google's unapproved substances policy states that products subject to any government or regulatory action or warning are not permitted regardless of claims of legality ([Google Ads healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15595718?hl=en-IE)). A packet that says so protects budget that would otherwise fund an unwinnable appeal.
The record also supports a regulatory response. FDA asked the recipient to describe, in writing within fifteen working days, the specific corrective steps taken, steps to prevent recurrence, and copies of related documentation ([FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/gram-peptides-721806-03312026)).
