Private Cloud AI Deployment With Defined Data Boundaries
A private cloud AI deployment can describe at least three different architectures, and the word "private" tells a buyer almost nothing about which one is on the table. What matters is who operates the model serving layer, which account holds the data, and what your organization can export when the relationship ends. This page is a scoping framework for those questions, not a product pitch.

Select the service model before anyone writes configuration
Three patterns are sold under one label. Dedicated AI hosting reserves compute that someone operates on your behalf. A customer-operated deployment runs models inside your own cloud account and VPC, with your team holding identity, logging and network control. Private connectivity to a managed model service is a third thing: AWS documents interface VPC endpoints powered by PrivateLink that let you reach Amazon Bedrock as if it were inside your VPC, without an internet gateway, NAT device, VPN or Direct Connect connection, and instances do not need public IP addresses ([AWS Bedrock VPC interface endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/vpc-interface-endpoints.html)). That is a private network path to infrastructure the provider still runs. Examples on this page use AWS documentation; other providers document equivalent controls differently, so verify each against its own documentation.
Illustrative comparison. Confirm every row against your provider's current documentation and your signed agreements.
* Operates model serving. Dedicated AI hosting: Hosting operator. Customer-operated in your VPC: Your team. Private endpoint to managed inference: Cloud provider.
* Account holding data at rest. Dedicated AI hosting: Operator account. Customer-operated in your VPC: Your account. Private endpoint to managed inference: Your account plus provider services.
* Who sets identity and network rules. Dedicated AI hosting: Shared, by contract. Customer-operated in your VPC: Your team. Private endpoint to managed inference: Your team, within provider limits.
* Typically exportable at exit. Dedicated AI hosting: Config, data, artifacts named in contract. Customer-operated in your VPC: Everything in your account. Private endpoint to managed inference: Your data, prompts and logs, not the model service.
Map the complete data path, not just the prompt
A VPC AI deployment is only as private as its least examined artifact. Inventory prompts and system instructions, retrieval sources, embeddings and the vector store, outputs, evaluation datasets, application logs, platform and billing telemetry, backups and snapshots, and every administrator or support account that can read any of it. Read provider statements instead of assuming them. AWS describes a Model Deployment Account per model provider in each Region, owned and operated by the Amazon Bedrock service team, and states that model providers have no access to those accounts, to Bedrock logs, or to customer prompts and completions ([AWS Bedrock data protection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html)). Record statements like that in a provider and retention register with the date verified, alongside [data custody and processing boundaries](/ai-edge/data-sovereignty).
Set identity and network controls that someone can audit
Private connectivity does not replace access control. AWS is explicit that you remain responsible for control over your content and for the security configuration of the services you use, and recommends individual users under IAM Identity Center or IAM with only the permissions their duties require, multi-factor authentication, SSL/TLS for communication with AWS resources, and API and user activity logging through CloudTrail ([AWS Bedrock data protection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html)). Endpoint policy matters too: an endpoint policy names the principals, actions and resources permitted through that path, and the default endpoint policy allows full access to the service ([AWS Bedrock VPC interface endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/vpc-interface-endpoints.html)). Decide who holds break-glass credentials, where secrets live, and who may change network rules. Those choices define [owned AI systems and deployment boundaries](/ai-edge/owned-ai-systems).
Plan cost and capacity against stated workload assumptions
Cost arguments collapse when assumptions stay unwritten. Put the numbers your team already has on one page: expected requests per day, average input and output length, peak concurrency, acceptable latency, retention periods, and how much idle reserved capacity the design implies. Then price categories rather than a headline rate. Inference or GPU time, storage for vectors, logs and backups, cross-zone and egress transfer, observability tooling, and the engineering hours to patch, monitor and respond are all line items someone pays for. Label every projection an estimate until a production month has been measured. Utilization, not list price, usually decides between reserved capacity and metered managed inference, and the same arithmetic applies when comparing cloud capacity to [supported local processing](/ai-edge/local-processing).
Prove isolation and recoverability before go-live
Design documents are not evidence. Before a secure cloud LLM deployment carries real traffic, run tests and keep the records. Confirm that calls from outside the permitted network path fail. Confirm that the endpoint policy denies actions outside the approved list. Restore a backup into a clean environment and time it. Enumerate every outbound connection the system may make, including telemetry, package mirrors, model updates and third-party APIs, and justify each. Keep a change-control record showing who approved the configuration. Private deployment by itself does not establish security or regulatory compliance; tested controls, documented data flows and a named accountable operator are what counsel and auditors ask to see. That is the practical core of [AI governance and approval controls](/ai-edge/ai-governance).
Contract for operation and exit at the same time
Exit terms are easier to negotiate before you depend on the system. Name the account owner of record, the roles with operational access, every provider and subprocessor in the path with its retention terms, and the escalation route when inference fails at 2 a.m. Define what leaves with you: infrastructure-as-code, configuration, prompts and templates, evaluation sets, fine-tuning datasets and adapter weights where licensing permits, retrieval sources, and logs, each in a stated format, with deletion evidence and a notice period. A vendor that cannot describe its own exit process has answered the question. Questions buyers ask during scoping
### Does a private endpoint mean the model runs in our VPC?
No. An interface VPC endpoint gives your VPC a private route to the service, reachable without an internet gateway, NAT device, VPN or Direct Connect connection ([AWS Bedrock VPC interface endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/vpc-interface-endpoints.html)). The model still runs on provider-operated infrastructure. Running models inside your own account is a different pattern with different responsibilities and costs.
### Can we use our own cloud account?
A customer-account deployment is one of the three models above, and it often fits when your security team requires direct control of identity, logging and network rules. Whether it is right depends on your identity provider, regional requirements, model licensing and the operations staffing available. That is a scoping decision rather than a default.
### Who can access prompts, logs and backups?
Whoever your configuration and provider agreements allow, which is why the data path map and the provider register exist. AWS advises never putting confidential information into tags or free-form fields such as a Name field, because that data may be used in billing or diagnostic logs ([AWS Bedrock data protection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html)). The same caution applies to prompt metadata in any private AI cloud.
### What information and access are needed to scope this work?
The workload and its data types, your cloud accounts and regions, current network and identity patterns, logging and retention requirements, regulatory constraints your counsel has defined, and the approval process a change must clear. An account and network diagram, even a rough one, shortens the first conversation considerably.
### What will our organization own and be able to export at the end?
Whatever the agreement names, which is why the export list belongs in the contract rather than a closing conversation. That list usually covers configuration and infrastructure code, prompts, retrieval sources, evaluation data, logs and the model artifacts your licenses permit you to move, with formats and a deletion attestation specified in advance.
