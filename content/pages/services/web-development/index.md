Who This Work Is For
A healthcare website has several jobs at once. It has to explain services in language a patient or family understands, show the right location with the right hours and contact path, and move someone from interest to an inquiry without friction. It also has to hold up to the organization's review process, its accessibility obligations, and its measurement needs.
We build for organizations where those jobs are hard: [hospitals and health systems](/who-we-help/hospitals-and-health-systems) with many service lines and locations, multi-location operators who need consistent structure with local relevance, and behavioral and mental health providers whose visitors are often under stress when they arrive. The homepage summarizes this under [connected marketing](/#services); the pages below go deeper.
Our Web Development Process
### Discovery
We begin with the current site: structure, traffic by page, inquiry paths, forms, tracking, performance, and the URLs that earn visits today. We interview the people who answer the phone and manage locations, because they know where the site creates work. The output is a scope with priorities, not a template.
### Information architecture and location structure
Service lines, conditions, locations, and providers each need a place in the structure, and the relationships between them need to be easy to follow. For multi-location organizations we design a location-page pattern that carries consistent elements (address, hours, services offered, contact path, accessibility information) while allowing local content. [Scaling multi-location campaigns](/problems/scale-multi-location-campaigns) depends on that structure, so we design it with the campaign plan in view.
### Design and build
Design work is covered on the [website design and build](/services/web-development/website-design-and-build) page. The build prioritizes page speed, accessibility, maintainability by your team, and clean handoffs to the CRM and call tracking. Conversion-focused development is covered under [CRO development](/services/web-development/cro-development), and campaign landing pages under [landing page development](/services/web-development/landing-page-development).
### Launch
Before launch, every form path is tested end to end, every redirect is checked, tracking is validated against the CRM, and the pages are reviewed on a phone at a narrow viewport. After launch we watch crawl coverage, errors, and inquiry volume against the pre-launch baseline.
URL Preservation and Redirects
Existing URLs that earn visits are an asset. We keep them where we can. Where a URL has to change, we record the old and new address and ship the redirect in the same release, one hop, to the page that best replaces the old one. We do not rename pages to make the address match a new heading. Our [technical SEO and performance](/services/web-development/technical-seo-and-performance) page covers the crawl and index side of a migration.
Intake Paths and Forms
The inquiry form on a marketing site should ask for what the intake team needs to make contact and nothing more: a name, an organization where relevant, an email address, an optional phone number. It should carry a plain note asking visitors not to include patient or other sensitive information. Detailed screening belongs in the conversation that follows, inside the systems built to hold it.
Forms, tracked phone numbers, and chat all write to the CRM through the connections described on our [integrations and tracking](/services/web-development/integrations-and-tracking) page, so the source of every inquiry is recorded without the intake team re-keying it.
Accessibility and Performance as Requirements
Accessibility is a design requirement, not a review step. We work to the [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) as the reference: readable contrast, keyboard operability, visible focus, labeled form fields, accessible error messages, and content that reflows at narrow widths without horizontal scrolling. Decorative images carry empty alternative text; informative images describe what they show.
Performance is treated the same way. Reserved image dimensions, limited third-party scripts, and measured Core Web Vitals are part of the build definition, because a slow page loses the people who need it most.
Tracking Handoff
A website that cannot be measured cannot be improved. Analytics events, tag management, consent behavior, and call tracking are configured as part of the build and validated before launch, then handed to whoever owns measurement in your organization with documentation. Our [analytics and attribution](/services/analytics-and-attribution) service picks up from there if you want us to run it.
Who Owns What After Launch
We write down ownership before the build starts. The client owns the domain, the hosting account, the content, and the data. We own the delivery of what we were engaged to build, the documentation, and, where the engagement includes it, maintenance for a defined period. Content updates, hosting renewals, and measurement reviews each have a named owner on the client side or in the engagement, so nothing waits on an assumption.

He managed about $1.5 million to $2 million per month in Google Ads spend. That is prior executive experience, not agency client work. It is why we treat the website as part of the acquisition and intake operation rather than a brochure.

FAQ
Q: Which organizations do you build websites for?
A: Hospitals and health systems, multi-location organizations, and behavioral and mental health providers. The common requirement is a site that has to present many services or locations clearly and move visitors to an inquiry the intake team can act on.
Q: Will you keep our existing URLs?
A: Where they earn visits, yes. Where a change is needed, the old and new addresses are recorded and the redirect ships in the same release. We do not rename pages to match new headings.
Q: What goes on an inquiry form?
A: The fields the intake team needs to make contact: name, organization where relevant, email, and an optional phone number, with a note asking visitors not to include patient or sensitive information. Screening questions belong in the follow-up conversation and the systems built for it.
Q: How do you handle accessibility?
A: As a build requirement, using the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines as the reference for contrast, keyboard use, focus visibility, form labels, error messages, and reflow at narrow widths. Accessibility is tested before launch, not reviewed afterward.
Q: Who maintains the site after launch?
A: Ownership is written down before the build: the client owns the domain, hosting, content, and data; maintenance is either a defined part of the engagement or handed to the client's team with documentation.
